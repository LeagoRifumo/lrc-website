Joby Reports Third Quarter 2026 Financial Results
●Company raised full-year 2026 revenue outlook +15%
SANTA CRUZ, Calif.—November 4, 2026—Joby Aviation, Inc. (NYSE:JOBY), a company developing electric air taxis for commercial passenger service, today issued its Third Quarter 2026 Shareholder Letter (https://cdn.sanity.io/files/h5mp19kq/production/57e01cc66b8fe8b0357f1446f62e4ec428978c1a.pdf) detailing the company’s operational and financial results for the quarter ending September 30, 2026. The company will host a live audio webcast of its conference call to discuss the results at 2:00 p.m. PT (5:00 p.m. ET) today.
Highlights include:
●First flights completed in Texas and Florida as part of White House-backed eIPP program, designed to accelerate commercialization of air taxis
●Wide range of “for credit” testing completed as part of fifth and final stage of FAA type certification
●Manufacturing output significantly increased, with 17 aircraft now in production
●Record quarter for Blade, flying 44,000+ passengers; Q3 2026 revenue of $55 million primarily from Blade
●Full Year 2026 revenue outlook raised 15% to a range of $135 million to $140 million
●Joby’s J208 autonomous aircraft completed the first ever fully autonomous flight across the U.S.
●Joby’s autonomy focused contracts with the U.S. Air Force were expanded to ~$37 million
●Planned acquisition of Resonant Sciences, an EBITDA-positive defense technology company with $100 million-plus in trailing twelve-month revenue, to significantly expand Joby’s defense business, adding a fast-growing company with established programs, classified capabilities and scaled manufacturing operations
●$2.2 billion in cash, cash equivalents, and short-term investments as of September 30, 2026
Commenting on Joby’s third quarter results, JoeBen Bevirt, founder and CEO, said:
“This quarter, we made important progress on the fifth and final stage of certification, completing a wide range of ‘for credit’ testing with the FAA. We significantly increased manufacturing output while improving efficiency, and demonstrated our electric air taxi in key early U.S. markets through the White House-backed eIPP program. Our J208 aircraft also completed what we believe to be the first fully autonomous flight of a civilian aircraft across the United States, and this morning we announced an expansion of our autonomy-focused contracts with the U.S. Air Force to nearly $37 million.”
“Our core mission is - and always has been - to bring electric vertical flight to passengers around the world. We designed our aircraft from the ground up for that mission, and our engineering, manufacturing and certification efforts are focused on bringing it to market. The progress we’re making gives me every confidence that Joby will continue to lead the way.”
Outlook
Joby’s current outlook incorporates investment toward certification of our eVTOL aircraft, expansion of manufacturing, and advancement of commercial preparations including participation in the U.S. eVTOL Integration Pilot Program. The company increased its full year 2026 total revenue outlook to a range of $135 million to $140 million. For the second half of 2026, Joby anticipates use of cash, cash equivalents and short-term investments to be between $385 million and $415 million.

About Joby Aviation
Joby Aviation, Inc. (NYSE: JOBY) is a next-generation aviation company based in California. Its aircraft, propulsion and autonomy technologies span both commercial aviation and defense applications. Joby also operates Blade, a leading urban air mobility business providing passenger transportation services in the United States and Europe. Joby intends to operate its fast, quiet and convenient air taxi service in cities around the world and sell its aircraft to other operators and partners. To learn more, visit www.jobyaviation.com.

Launching eIPP Operations in Texas and Florida WE BEGAN FLIGHTS under the White House-backed eVTOL Integration Pilot Program (eIPP) in Texas in September followed by demonstrations in Florida, completing flights in Tampa and Orlando. The eIPP has the potential to significantly accelerate our path to commercial service by enabling us to demonstrate the operational maturity, acoustic performance and airspace integration needed to launch service. By flying real-world routes between urban locations and major airports, we are generating operational data that will help the FAA develop the safety rules needed for commercial air taxi service at scale. Working closely with the FAA, we see a path under the eIPP to progress from pilot-only flights to flights carrying non-paying passengers and, eventually, paying passengers as certification progresses. JOBY’S FLIGHTS IN TEXAS marked the launch of the eIPP program. Over a week of flying, we completed missions between Alliance Airport in Fort Worth, key sites across the region and DFW International Airport. Our approach into DFW took us through the “spine,” the airspace between two active runways, demonstrating our operational maturity and building on years of research with the FAA and NASA, including simulations of air taxi operations at DFW. We celebrated the launch of the program at Perot Field, with a vertical takeoff and landing flight demonstration attended by Transportation Secretary Sean Duffy and Senator Ted Cruz. We’re establishing a long- term presence at the airport, where a newly leased 45,000-square-foot facility will serve as our home base for the Dallas-Fort Worth market. We also brought our aircraft to Toyota Motor's North American HQ in Plano, Texas, where it executed a precision landing on the campus — an unmistakable demonstration of the flexibility enabled by vertical takeoff and landing. THE U.S. AIR FORCE HAS AWARDED additional funding to accelerate our autonomous flight technology, bringing its total commitment from nearly $20 million in previously awarded contracts to nearly $37 million, and raising the contract ceiling for operational testing from $5 million to $50 million. Awarded through AFWERX, the Air Force’s innovation arm, the funding supports continued development of our autonomy software and expanded operational testing in the Pacific as the Air Force moves toward sustained autonomous logistics operations. The contract expansion builds on our nationwide J208 flights, part of our 2026 Electric Skies campaign. In the first-ever autonomous flight across the United States, our Superpilot™ autonomy system flew a Cessna Caravan 3,199 miles from Concord, California, to North Carolina’s Outer Banks, completing the first coast-to-coast autonomous flight by a civilian aircraft. The system completed the journey with zero disengagements and no control inputs from its onboard safety pilot. The aircraft then returned to Concord, bringing the full tour to more than 6,500 miles across 24 states. Our dual-use technology is designed to support autonomous flight in civilian skies and contested military environments, combining advanced sensors, onboard computing and automated flight controls. A ground-based operator monitors each flight and can update routes and communicate with air traffic control in real time. Our planned acquisition of Resonant Sciences is expected to further strengthen this foundation and advance our broader strategy for autonomous logistics and cargo operations across defense, emergency response and commercial markets. DURING THE QUARTER, we entered into a definitive agreement to acquire Resonant Sciences, a defense technology company based in Dayton, Ohio. The acquisition is expected to bring immediate scale to Joby’s defense business through the addition of a rapidly growing, EBITDA-positive company supporting active U.S. national security programs. Resonant’s approximately 250 employees bring deep expertise in sensing, secure communications, electronic countermeasures and low-observability technology. The company combines classified infrastructure and established production operations with longstanding customer relationships and its electronics, apertures and radomes, are already qualified across more than 20 commercial and defense airframes. Resonant’s revenue grew approximately 40% year- over-year to more than $100 million in the trailing twelve months through July 2026, with high-teens adjusted EBITDA margins in recent periods. Bookings in the first half of 2026 exceeded three times the prior-year level, while backlog more than doubled year-over-year. We believe that combining our aircraft platforms, propulsion and autonomy with Resonant’s sensing and mission systems will allow us to pursue opportunities neither company could address alone, with an early focus on integrating our autonomy technology and Resonant’s radio-frequency sensing and signal processing to develop autonomous systems for defense customers. The acquisition is designed to support growth in defense while preserving our commercial aviation organization’s focus on certifying our aircraft, scaling manufacturing and launching our commercial air taxi service. Under Co-Founder and CEO J. Micah North, Resonant will become our dedicated defense business, and we expect to bring our existing turbine-electric, hydrogen-electric and autonomy initiatives into this organization that is already equipped to serve defense customers. This would give those programs the dedicated leadership, infrastructure and production capabilities needed to grow, allowing our commercial team to remain focused on certifying, manufacturing and launching our electric air taxi. We expect the acquisition to close in early 2027, subject to receipt of regulatory approvals and other customary closing conditions. Third Quarter 2026 Financial Summary IN THE THIRD QUARTER OF 2026, revenue totaled $55.1 million. Our net loss of $211.8 million primarily reflected a net operating loss of $278.1 million partially offset by other income of $66.5 million. Operating expenses for the quarter totaled $333.2 million and reflected costs to support certification and manufacturing of our aircraft and costs associated with the operation of our Blade passenger business. Expenses included stock-based compensation of $54.7 million and depreciation and amortization of $12.1 million. Other income included $16.7 million of interest and other income, net, as well as a $49.8 million non- cash gain from the revaluation of warrants, earnout shares and contingent consideration. Net loss in the third quarter of 2026 decreased by $189.4 million compared with the third quarter of 2025, primarily driven by a $286.0 million improvement in other income. This included a $278.9 million favorable change in the fair value of warrants, earnout shares, and contingent consideration. Net loss also benefited from a $3.6 million increase in gross profit, reflecting a $32.5 million increase in revenue, partially offset by a $28.9 million increase in cost of revenue, both primarily associated with our Blade business. These improvements were partially offset by higher operating expenses driven by a $59.4 million increase in research and development costs, reflecting continued investment to support certification and manufacturing readiness, and a $40.6 million increase in selling, general and administrative expenses. Compared with the second quarter of 2026, our third quarter of 2026 net loss decreased by $33.6 million from $245.4 million in the prior quarter. Other income was $50.9 million higher than in the prior quarter, primarily reflecting a non-cash revaluation gain on our warrants, earnout shares, and contingent consideration compared to a loss in the prior quarter. The $17.2 million increase in loss from operations primarily reflected increased investment to support certification, manufacturing, and the Blade business, as well as higher legal and other expenses. Adjusted EBITDA in the third quarter of 2026 was a loss of $207.9 million, primarily reflecting employee costs and support associated with the development, certification and manufacturing of the aircraft and Blade operations. The Adjusted EBITDA loss increased by $75.1 million compared with the third quarter of 2025 and by $10.8 million compared with the second quarter of 2026, reflecting continued investment in our aircraft development, certification, manufacturing and commercial preparations. Adjusted EBITDA is a non-GAAP metric defined as net income (loss) before interest income, interest expense, income tax expense (benefit), depreciation and amortization expense, stock- based compensation expense, loss on common stock issuance in private placement, impact from revaluation of non-operating derivative liabilities, acquisition transaction costs, and other income or costs which are not directly related to ongoing core operations. Please see the section titled “Non-GAAP Financial Measures” for a reconciliation of Net Loss to Adjusted EBITDA. We ended the third quarter of 2026 with $2.2 billion in cash, cash equivalents, and investments in marketable securities which includes approximately $128 million in net proceeds from our at-the-market equity program during the quarter. During Q3 2026, our use of cash, cash equivalents and short-term investments was $195 million, keeping us on track with our previously provided guidance for the second half of 2026. Strong Blade performance enables us to again raise our full-year revenue guidance to a range of $135 million to $140 million, from the earlier outlook of $115 million to $125 million. For the second half of 2026, we continue to expect our use of cash, cash equivalents and short-term investments to be between $385 million and $415 million. This reflects investment toward certification of our eVTOL aircraft, expansion of manufacturing, and advancement of commercial preparations including participation in the U.S. eVTOL Integration Pilot Program.
