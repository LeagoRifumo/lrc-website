EXHIBIT 99.1
Allied Gold Reports Q3 2026 Results, Begins Production at the Kurmuk Mine and Delivers Strong Performance at the Côte D’Ivoire Complex
TORONTO, Nov. 04, 2026 (GLOBE NEWSWIRE) -- Allied Gold Corporation (TSX: AAUC) (NYSE: AAUC) (“Allied” or the “Company”) herein provides its financial and operational results for the third quarter of 2026. The Company produced 91,195 ounces of gold during the quarter, representing a 5% increase over the comparable prior-year period, at all-in sustaining costs (“AISC”)(1) of $2,378 per ounce sold. As previously disclosed, production is expected to be weighted toward the second half of the year, particularly the fourth quarter, with production increases expected at Sadiola. Production at the Kurmuk Mine will add to expected production increases.
At Kurmuk, commissioning activities began in the third quarter, and more recently, ore has been introduced into the plant. The mine is now in production and will ramp up into the end of the year. This cornerstone asset is expected to deliver a significant increase in production at attractive operating costs, while enhancing the scale, quality and longevity of the Company’s portfolio. As the lowest-cost operation in the portfolio, Kurmuk is expected to drive a disproportionate increase in cash flow relative to production. Exploration activities continued to extend known mineralized systems and advance new targets across the portfolio, most notably at Kurmuk.

Operational Highlights
Third Quarter Production: The Company produced 91,195 ounces of gold in the third quarter, representing a 5% increase over the comparable prior-year period.
Third Quarter Sales: Gold sales were 92,532 ounces, with minor timing differences from production resulting from the scheduling of shipments and the sale of inventory.
Performance by Asset:
At Sadiola, production of 42,291 ounces in the third quarter compares favourably to plan and to production for the prior year quarter. The Company has begun implementing initiatives to improve production and reduce costs, including amended mining contractor arrangements with commitments on fleet reliability and equipment availability. Early results have been favourable, and fourth-quarter operations are expected to improve further with higher-grade fresh and oxide ore in the mining sequence, with improvements continuing into 2027.
At Bonikro, production of 28,644 ounces in the third quarter was 30% higher than in the comparable prior-year period, driven by higher feed grades and improved recoveries from Stage 5, with AISC(1) of $1,670 per ounce sold, the lowest among the Company’s operating mines.

Settlements under the Company’s gold collar program reduced average revenue by $371 per ounce in the quarter. The collars expire in December 2026, after which the Company expects greater exposure to spot gold prices, subject to deliveries under its existing stream and gold prepay arrangements.
2026 Production Outlook: Production for the nine months ended September 30, 2026 was 284,640 gold ounces, a 9% increase over the comparable prior-year period, and the fourth quarter is expected to be the Company’s highest-producing quarter of the year. Fourth-quarter production is expected to benefit from the initial contribution from the Kurmuk Mine, the lowest-cost operation in the Company’s portfolio, a return to more normalized operations at Sadiola and continued momentum at the CDI Complex, which is well positioned to meet planned annual production. For 2026, the Company’s operating mines are expected to produce 375,000 to 400,000 gold ounces, consistent with their historical production profile, and Kurmuk is expected to contribute 25,000 to 35,000 gold ounces in its partial first year, for a total of 400,000 to 435,000 gold ounces. At the midpoint of the range, this represents approximately a 10% increase over 2025 production.
2027 and Longer-Term Production Outlook: Annual production from the Company’s current operating mines is expected to remain consistent through 2027 at the previously disclosed level of 375,000 to 400,000 ounces, excluding Kurmuk. With Kurmuk expected to contribute an additional 240,000 to 270,000 ounces in 2027, total production is forecast to increase to 615,000 to 670,000 ounces, approximately 54% above the midpoint of the 2026 production outlook. Looking beyond 2027, Kurmuk is expected to produce approximately 300,000 gold ounces in 2028 at industry-leading costs. Together with the phased expansion at Sadiola and continued growth at the CDI Complex, this supports the Company’s path to sustainable annual production of approximately 800,000 gold ounces by 2029, supporting a meaningful step change in cash flow generation.
Advancement of Key Growth Initiatives
Kurmuk Development: At Kurmuk, commissioning activities started in the third quarter. Development of the mine continued to meet key execution milestones during the quarter, including connection to the national grid, first ore to the crushing circuit and build-up of ore stockpiles. Commissioning progressed across the crushing, reclaim, milling and raw water systems, supported by the completion of the initial grid power connection and the availability of medium-voltage power in September 2026. Mining activities also advanced successfully, and ore stockpiles of approximately 1.5 million tonnes have been established to support three months of plant feed ahead of the production ramp-up. The grade of the stockpiled ore is higher than the Mineral Reserve grade. More recently, ore has been introduced into the plant. The mine is now in production. Kurmuk is expected to operate at industry-leading costs, supported by a 20-year power purchase agreement with a fixed energy charge of $0.04 per kWh.
Kurmuk Exploration: On October 6, 2026, the Company reported results from 149 holes totalling 28,003 metres drilled at Kurmuk since October 2025. Highlights included 13.86 metres at 10.07 g/t Au and 13.77 metres at 9.15 g/t Au in hole ASDD227 at Ashashire, where the deposit remains open below a depth of 425 metres; 16.1 metres at 2.9 g/t Au and 14.4 metres at 3.4 g/t Au in hole DMDD788 at Dish Mountain; and 80.09 metres at 1.53 g/t Au in hole TSDD052 at the Kiftet prospect on the 9-kilometre Tsenge trend. Reported intercepts are drilled lengths. An initial Mineral Resource for Kiftet and Hiccup Hill is expected in the first half of 2027, and drilling is expected to contribute to the Company’s annual Mineral Resource update in early 2027. The Company’s five-year objective for Kurmuk is to grow Mineral Resources to more than 5 million ounces, including at least 0.5 million ounces within 10 kilometres of the mill, to sustain or exceed initial production of approximately 300,000 ounces per year and extend mine life beyond 15 years.
Sadiola Expansion: Following the termination of the corporate transaction process, the Company has resumed its evaluation of the next modules of its phased, modular optimization and expansion program at Sadiola, which targets a long-term production platform of 300,000 to 400,000 gold ounces per year. The Phase 1 crushing and milling circuit reached full production in the second quarter of 2026, and construction of the pre-leach thickener is ongoing, with commissioning targeted for the second quarter of 2027. The thickener is expected to enable the plant to process more than 90% of fresh ore, improve operating flexibility and reduce reagent consumption, supporting a sustainable operating platform of 200,000 to 230,000 gold ounces per year before further expansion. Phased early works for the 7 Mt/y expansion are planned to begin in late 2026, targeting operational capacity in the first half of 2029, and the staged energy program is expected to reduce AISC(1) by $150 to $200 per ounce once fully implemented.
CDI Complex Mine Life: The Mineral Reserve and Mineral Resource update for Agbaou announced on July 29, 2026 increased Proven and Probable Mineral Reserves by more than 60% over the year-end 2025 estimates and extended the Agbaou mine life, based on Proven and Probable Mineral Reserves only, to 2030. The Bonikro mine life has been extended to 2036, supported by initial Proven and Probable Mineral Reserves of approximately 585,000 ounces at Oumé and the inclusion of Stage 6 at Bonikro, which added approximately 101,000 ounces. Together with ongoing exploration across the CDI Complex, these extensions support the Company’s strategic objective of at least 200,000 gold ounces per year from the CDI Complex for over 10 years. Studies are advancing to increase the Bonikro plant’s processing capacity to 3.0 to 3.2 Mt/y, building on ongoing throughput upgrades of approximately 0.5 Mt/y intended to process low-grade stockpiles at a rate of 15,000 to 20,000 gold ounces per year beginning in late 2026 to early 2027.
Portfolio Exploration: A total of 232 holes comprising 31,976 metres were drilled across the Company’s properties in the third quarter, including 13,678 metres at Kurmuk, 5,587 metres at Sadiola and 12,711 metres at Agbaou, Hiré and Oumé in the CDI Complex. Fourth-quarter programs will continue to test extensions at Ashashire, Dish Mountain and the Tsenge trend at Kurmuk and at Sadiola Main, Tambali North and the FE2 Trend at Sadiola, and will test for near-pit oxide and fresh mineralization at Agbaou and Hiré.
Capital Allocation and Shareholder Returns
As the Company enters a period of significantly increased free cash flow generation, its capital allocation priorities include continuing to invest in growth, maintaining a strong balance sheet and returning capital to shareholders. The Company is progressing a shareholder returns policy as part of this capital allocation framework and intends to evaluate the full range of cash return options, including a base dividend anticipated to start in 2027 and a share buyback program.
In regard to the share buyback program, and subject to the approval of the Toronto Stock Exchange (“TSX”), the Company intends to launch a normal course issuer bid to purchase for cancellation up to 10% of the public float of its common shares through the facilities of the TSX, alternative Canadian trading systems or the New York Stock Exchange, at the prevailing market price at the time of acquisition. The actual number of common shares that may be purchased pursuant to the normal course issuer bid will be determined by management of the Company in compliance with the rules of the TSX. The Company intends to file a notice of intention with the TSX in due course and will announce a date for the commencement of the normal course issuer bid following acceptance of the notice by the TSX.
The Company believes that, from time to time, the market price of its common shares may not fully reflect their underlying value. At such times, the Company may purchase common shares under the normal course issuer bid where it considers such purchases to be an attractive investment and an appropriate use of corporate funds, and to be in the best interests of the Company and its shareholders.
In determining the timing and amount of any purchases under the share buyback program, the Company will take into account the trading liquidity of its common shares and the concentration of its shareholder base, with the objective of maintaining an orderly and liquid market for its common shares without undue concentration.

Sadiola Expansion Project
The Company has been advancing a modular optimization and expansion program at Sadiola, aimed at establishing a production platform of 300,000 to 400,000 ounces per year. Key components of the program include the crushing circuit expansion to efficiently process a higher proportion of fresh ore that would represent approximately 60% of mill feed, power infrastructure improvements, the addition of a pre-leach thickener, all part of a series of modular plant upgrades that can be implemented sequentially to increase throughput toward 7 Mt/y and ultimately 9 Mt/y. This strategy also enables the recovery improvement project and the energy program to be implemented progressively as throughput capacity expands, thereby improving capital efficiency and returns. The first module of this strategy, the crushing circuit expansion, was brought into full production in the second quarter of 2026. Further optimizations to the processing circuit, including instrumentation and automation upgrades, continued during the third quarter of 2026. Together, these initiatives are expected to improve operating performance, enhance overall processing rates, and reduce operating costs.
The pre-leach thickener, currently under construction with commissioning targeted for the second quarter of 2027, is expected to enable the plant to process more than 90% fresh ore, improve operating flexibility and reduce reagent consumption. These improvements are intended to support a sustainable operating platform of 200,000 to 230,000 gold ounces per year before further expansion.
The Company continues to advance the feasibility study and associated engineering for the 7 Mt/y expansion, as well as the engineering and design for the subsequent expansion steps. In parallel, Allied continues to advance studies to increase recoveries for fresh ore, including test work and engineering for flotation, Albion and other processing alternatives, as well as undertaking design and execution engineering for the new solar farm earthworks.
The total capital expenditures for the 7 Mt/y phase of the processing plant scope are forecast at approximately $200 million including engineering and construction of a permanent two-stage crushing plant and a grinding mill in the second line of the Sadiola plant. The Company is advancing an updated feasibility study and execution strategy that will update the capital requirements for the 7Mt/y by early 2027 which also incorporates the advancement of certain bulk earthworks and civil works originally envisaged under the subsequent 9 Mt/y expansion, allowing these activities to be undertaken as part of the 7 Mt/y development program to improve construction sequencing and minimize future disruption to operations. The phased execution program is planned to commence early works in late 2026, targeting 7 Mt/y operational capacity in the first half of 2029. The subsequent 9 Mt/y expansion phase is expected to include further crushing and wet plant upgrades to achieve the ultimate planned processing capacity. Further, the Company continues to evaluate processing optimizations which would increase recoveries. These improvements are not included in the production plan at the present time, and once implemented are expected to increase production, cash flows and improve value.
Alongside the Sadiola growth strategy, the Company is advancing a staged and scalable energy program for the asset. Additional diesel generators and control systems were initially installed to support the start of operations of the Phase 1 expansion. These are to be followed by a hybrid power solution that adds permanent diesel medium-speed thermal units using heavy fuel oil (“HFO”) and a photovoltaic plant with battery energy storage systems (“BESS”), progressively reducing reliance on diesel generation and establishing a scalable power supply capable of supporting the subsequent 7 Mt/y and 9 Mt/y expansions. The program is intended to give Sadiola a flexible power solution that can meet its ultimate power needs while being self-reliant, efficient and cost-effective. The current development strategy targets approximately 41 MW of generation capacity to support the 7 Mt/y expansion, and the timing and configuration of the HFO generation program are being evaluated to align power availability with the potential first-quarter 2029 expansion milestone.
The initial photovoltaic plant and BESS are projected to reduce energy costs by up to 20% compared to current costs, and additional photovoltaic and BESS capacity, together with medium-speed thermal generators, is projected to reduce energy costs further, by up to 45%. Once the power program is fully implemented, the reduction in AISC(1) is estimated at $150 to $200 per ounce. Cost improvements are expected to be modest in 2026, to increase gradually with the deployment of solar and battery storage systems in late 2027 and to increase meaningfully with the introduction of medium-speed thermal generation, currently targeted for 2029.
Sadiola Exploration
Since acquiring Sadiola in 2021, the Company has identified more than 15 million tonnes of economic oxide mineralization within the near-mine footprint, adding to the oxide resource base that is critical to the existing and planned processing infrastructure. The Company’s current oxide Mineral Reserves comprise 27 million tonnes containing 804,000 ounces.
During the third quarter of 2026, exploratory and resource drilling programs were conducted on the Sadiola licence, with a total of 53 holes drilled, comprising 5,587 metres, utilizing core and RC drilling. Year to date, 267 exploration holes comprising 34,002 metres were drilled over the Sadiola Property. Resource and exploratory drilling programs continued and were expanded at Tambali North and infill drilling commenced at FE1.
An IP survey, designed to trace the Sekekoto West host geology, to the north and to the south, is in progress, with a secondary goal of providing sufficient data to create a 3D resistivity image of the on-trend S12 deposit, which will be used to assist resource modelling of this highly prospective karsted zone. First-pass IP surveys were completed in the quarter with a set of strike-parallel lines, over the S12 Deposit, commencing in late September and continuing into October.
At Tambali North, a program was designed to follow-up on historic oxide gold mineralization and test a model that suggests that the Tambali Deposit mineralization continued to the north into the Sadiola Main pit and that historic waste piles, lying above the north-northeast extension of the Tambali North mineralization, are locally gold-bearing. This program initially comprised six short drill lines spaced 200 metres apart. Results to date have been positive and the Company has advanced infill drilling at a 100 metre line spacing at Tambali North. For the third quarter of 2026, 188 holes totalling 20,726 metres complete with 35 holes, comprising 3,676 metres were drilled.
Drilling continued at Sadiola Main during the quarter with 7 geotech drill holes, totalling 1,475 metres. These holes are designed to support the long-term development of the Sadiola Deposit. Additional holes are planned to test for depth extensions, to the northern end of the Sadiola Main Zone, and the northeast-trending cross-structures, while gathering additional geological data to better define bedding-parallel mineralization across the entire deposit area.
For the remainder of 2026, Sadiola will see continued efforts with four drills dedicated to testing the extension of the gold-mineralized structures at Sadiola Main, Tambali North and along the FE2 Trend at FE1. Oxide ore is favoured in the short term as it provides the plant with relatively inexpensive, high-quality ounces. The horizontal and down-dip/down-plunge limits of these systems are still open and as such, expectations of new discoveries and additions to the mineral inventory are high.

Bonikro Mine Life Extension
As previously discussed, the updated integrated production plan for Bonikro incorporates production from Hiré, Oumé, and Bonikro and demonstrates a mine life extension to 2036 based on the 2025 Proven and Probable Mineral Reserves, supporting average production of approximately 120,000 gold ounces per year.
The Company is advancing studies to increase the Bonikro plant’s processing capacity to 3.0–3.2 Mt/y. The expansion is expected to provide additional operating flexibility, support future production opportunities, and facilitate the incorporation of additional mineral inventory from exploration sources across the district. This increase is expected to build on the previously guided and ongoing processing plant throughput upgrades of approximately 0.5 million tonnes of ore per annum, intended to bring forward the processing of low-grade stockpiles at a rate of 15,000 to 20,000 gold ounces per annum, beginning in late 2026 to early 2027.
In combination with the recently reported extension of mine life for Agbaou based on Proven and Probable Mineral Reserves to 2030, the new integrated Bonikro LOM plan and the ongoing exploration efforts in the CDI Complex, supports the Company’s strategic objective of at least 200,000 gold ounces per year for over 10 years (thereby also increasing the minimum level of cumulative production from 180,000 gold ounces per year to 200,000 gold ounces per year).

Oumé Exploration
Following the successful Oumé exploration program, which resulted in the declaration of initial Proven and Probable Mineral Reserves containing approximately 585,000 ounces of gold, the Company expanded its exploration efforts to test for extensions to the Oumé gold system. Drilling in the third quarter continued over the Dougbafla Junction target with 14 holes totalling 729 metres completed with 1255 metres of drilling completed over this target in 2026. One, 462-metre hole was also drilled to test for the north strike extension of the Dougbafla North Deposit. Year to date, 220 holes totalling 10,130 metres were drilled in the Oumé area. In the fourth quarter, three, wide spaced holes are planned to further test the contacts of a northeast-trending felsic intrusion. In-fill soil sampling and prospecting are planned proximal to the Bandama Zone and southeast of the structure, that hosts the Dougbafla East Zone, located immediately east of the Oumé deposit area outside of the Allied Concessions.

Agbaou Mine Life Extension
As previously disclosed, the Company updated the Mineral Reserves and Mineral Resources for Agbaou, resulting in a 60% increase in the Proven and Probable Reserves. As a result, the mine life for Agbaou based on Mineral Reserves has been extended to 2030, which, together with the previously disclosed update on the extension of the Bonikro mine life and the ongoing exploration efforts in the CDI Complex, supports the Company’s strategic objective of 200,000 gold ounces per year for over 10 years for these assets.
Agbaou Exploration
At Agbaou, the Company actively pursues opportunities to extend the mine life by increasing Mineral Reserves through sustained drilling and other exploration efforts. During the quarter, 52 holes totalling 8,237 metres were completed including approximately 4,660 metres drilled to test for extensions to zones within the Agbaou pits. Drilling is continuing to test for both deeper and near-pit oxide gold mineralization into the fourth quarter of 2026. A total of 4 holes comprising 1,209 metres were completed over the Agbaou South target. The remainder of the drilling focus is on waste dump sterilization testing, with 32 holes comprising 3,246 metres drilled. Year to date 137 holes totalling 25,064 metres were drilled in the Agbaou area.
For the fourth quarter of 2026, additional drill programs are anticipated in the Agbaou pit for both oxide and fresh mineralization and west of South Sat 3 for oxides.
Kurmuk
At Kurmuk, commissioning activities started in the third quarter. Development of the mine continued to meet key execution milestones during the quarter, including connection to the national grid, first ore to the crushing circuit and the build-up of ore stockpiles. Commissioning progressed across the crushing, reclaim, milling and raw water systems, supported by the completion of the initial grid power connection and the availability of medium-voltage power in September 2026. Mining activities are now sufficient to feed the plant, with mining occurring at expected production run rates. Ore stockpiles of approximately 1.5 million tonnes have been established to support three months of plant feed during the production ramp-up. The grade of the stockpiled ore is higher than the Mineral Reserve grade. Exploration programs continued to test extensions of known deposits and evaluate new mineralized zones, with the objective of defining a mineral inventory exceeding 5 million ounces. Subsequent to quarter-end, the crushing and supporting infrastructure were completed, ore was introduced into the plant, and the mine commenced production.
As previously disclosed, production is expected to ramp up into the end of the year, with total production of between 25,000 and 35,000 gold ounces and an accelerated ramp-up to nameplate and commercial production expected during this period. Production in the first quarter of 2027 is expected to increase sequentially month over month, with quarterly production of 55,000 to 65,000 gold ounces and a monthly average production of approximately 20,000 gold ounces. The Kurmuk Mine is expected to produce between 240,000 and 270,000 gold ounces in 2027, which at the higher end, exceeds previous guidance, and approximately 300,000 gold ounces in 2028. The mine is expected to target an average of approximately 300,000 gold ounces per year during the first four years and 240,000 gold ounces per year over the life of the mine, with industry-leading cost performance. Further, the objective of the ongoing exploration program is not only to extend mine life but also to find higher-grade, near-surface ore, to extend the number of years averaging 300,000 gold ounces per year.
As the project advanced, and as previously disclosed, the Company completed a review of the capacity of the processing plant in consideration of the ore inventory and the exploration progress at Dish, Ashashire and Tsenge. Allied made a strategic decision to maximize the operational flexibility for Kurmuk since the start of operations with an average processing capacity of up to 6.4 Mt/y. This increased flexibility was incorporated into the execution project, with subsequent modifications and upgrades to the leaching circuit expected to be deployed in the future to sustain throughput and increase fresh ore recoveries. These enhancements and optimizations are expected to make Kurmuk a stronger, de-risked operation, providing upside and operational flexibility, aligning with the Company’s long-term strategy of maximizing value at each of its assets.
Kurmuk Project Exploration
At both Dish Mountain and Ashashire, drilling continues to intersect lateral and vertical extensions of the deposits, with the limits of the mineralized system remaining open. At Tsenge, near-term exploration is focused on defining the extent of higher-grade zones and updating the mineralization models with a medium-term exploration goal of evaluating the entire 9-kilometre strike length of the gold-in-soil anomalies at Tsenge. A significant amount of highly successful trenching continues to be carried out at Tsenge, confirming the bedrock expression of the mineralized lenses that are coincident and proximal the regional, to the gold-in-soil anomalies aligned along this 9 km long trend. A second-pass drill program completed over the Urchin Prospect, located adjacent to the Ashashire haul road, yielded positive results that will require follow-up drilling.
