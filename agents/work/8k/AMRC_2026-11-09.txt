Ameresco Reports Third Quarter 2026 Financial Results
Third Quarter Results Reflected Strong Growth Across Major Business Lines, Margin Expansion and Robust Business Development
39% Total Backlog Growth to Record $7.2 Billion
$875 Million in New Project Awards Led by Strong Momentum in Europe and Federal
$737 Million Converted to Contracted Backlog from Awarded
Adjusting Guidance to Reflect Adoption of New JV Accounting Methodology
Third Quarter 2026 Financial Highlights:
•Revenues of $580.3 million
•Net income attributable to common shareholders of $31.9 million
•GAAP EPS of $0.59
•Non-GAAP EPS $0.88
•Adjusted EBITDA of $91.7 million
FRAMINGHAM, MA – November 9, 2026 – Ameresco, Inc. (NYSE:AMRC), a leading energy infrastructure company, today announced financial results for the third quarter ended September 30, 2026. The Company also furnished supplemental information in conjunction with this press release in a Current Report on Form 8-K. The supplemental information, which includes Non-GAAP financial measures, has been posted to the “Investors” section of the Company’s website at www.ameresco.com. Reconciliations of Non-GAAP measures to the appropriate GAAP measures are included herein. All financial result comparisons made are against the prior year period unless otherwise noted.
“Our third quarter results represented another quarter of strong growth and disciplined execution. We achieved revenue increases across each of our major business lines, expanded margins and continued to build both awarded and contracted backlogs. The $875 million in new projects we were awarded in the third quarter brought Ameresco’s total project backlog to a record $7.2 billion. This also was an excellent quarter for project contract conversion with $737
million converting from awarded into contracted backlog, building visibility as we head into 2027,” CEO George Sakellaris commented.
“The strength of our business model is reflected in the diversity of our solutions, markets and customers. We secured over $675 million in additional awards driven by strength in Federal infrastructure and Europe. We also added data center energy infrastructure awards of $200 million representing an expanded scope of work for Ameresco at an existing project. In addition, our recurring O&M and Energy Asset businesses continue to grow at an impressive pace, further enhancing long-term revenue visibility and reinforcing the resilience of our business model,” Mr. Sakellaris concluded.

Total revenue increased 10% to $580.3 million, reflecting broad-based growth across Projects, Energy Assets and O&M and continued strong execution on project backlog conversion. Project revenue increased 9% to $446.0 million, while Energy Asset revenue grew 21% to $75.4 million, as we continued to expand our portfolio of owned operating assets. O&M revenue increased 20% to $37.0 million, driven by the continued addition of new long-term operations and maintenance contracts. Gross margin expanded to 18.7%, with meaningful improvement on both a sequential and year-over-year basis. Margin improvement was driven by a combination of a favorable business mix, strong execution and a decrease in depreciation related to an adjustment to our energy assets’ useful lives.
Net income attributable to common shareholders was $31.9 million, or $0.59 per diluted share, while Non-GAAP EPS was $0.88. Adjusted EBITDA increased 24% to $91.7 million. In Q3 we adopted HLBV accounting related to the Neogenyx Fuels transaction. HLBV accounting allocates income or loss among partners based on the change in the amounts each partner would receive if the entity were hypothetically liquidated at book value at each reporting date. This method is commonly used in tax equity and complex partnership structures. Given the
inherent variability within the HLBV calculation quarter-to-quarter, to facilitate comparability, our Non-GAAP EPS and adjusted EBITDA now reflect 100% of the operating results of our consolidated joint ventures, most notably Ameresco Sunel Energy ("AMRC Sunel JV"), a significant part of our Europe segment, and Neogenyx Fuels, which constitutes substantially all of the business reported in our Renewable Fuels segment. Results of these joint ventures are included in the Non-GAAP Financial Measures below.
Earnings for the quarter also included a large tax benefit related to our planned adoption of a tax-based accounting policy as discussed last quarter. This better aligns earnings recognition with the period in which investment tax credits are generated, rather than allocating the benefit over the life of the related assets.

“Our rapidly growing project backlog together with the strength of our recurring revenue businesses position us for continued growth in 2027 and beyond. Ameresco remains well positioned to benefit from the emerging power infrastructure opportunities within the data center and C&I landscape, while we continue to see positive trends in our traditional markets serving the federal government, municipalities, universities, schools, and hospitals. About Ameresco, Inc.
Ameresco, Inc. (NYSE: AMRC) is a leading energy infrastructure company delivering integrated solutions to create reliable power and modernize infrastructure. The company’s Power Infrastructure business integrates energy resources across behind-the-meter and utility-scale systems. Its Buildings & Public Infrastructure business modernizes the built environment with smart, connected solutions that optimize performance and enhance resilience. Ameresco is a trusted full lifecycle partner, delivering over $15 billion in solutions and contracting over 5 GW of energy resources since its founding in 2000. Headquartered in Massachusetts, Ameresco serves public and private sector customers across North America and Europe. Learn more at www.ameresco.com.

Exhibit A: Non-GAAP Financial Measures
We use the Non-GAAP financial measures defined and discussed below to provide investors and others with useful supplemental information to our financial results prepared in accordance with GAAP. These Non-GAAP financial measures should not be considered as an alternative to any measure of financial performance calculated and presented in accordance with GAAP. For a reconciliation of these Non-GAAP measures to the most directly comparable financial measures prepared in accordance with GAAP, please see Non-GAAP Financial Measures and Non-GAAP Financial Guidance in the tables above.
We understand that, although measures similar to these Non-GAAP financial measures are frequently used by investors and securities analysts in their evaluation of companies, they have limitations as analytical tools, and investors should not consider them in isolation or as a substitute for the most directly comparable GAAP financial measures or an analysis of our
results of operations as reported under GAAP. To properly and prudently evaluate our business, we encourage investors to review our GAAP financial statements included above, and not to rely on any single financial measure to evaluate our business.
Adjusted EBITDA and Adjusted EBITDA Margin
We define adjusted EBITDA as net income before income tax (benefit) provision, interest and other expenses net, depreciation and amortization of intangible assets, accretion of asset retirement obligations, stock-based compensation expense, energy asset and goodwill impairment, contingent consideration, restructuring and other charges, gain or loss on sale of equity investment, and gain or loss upon deconsolidation of a variable interest entity. We define adjusted EBITDA margin as adjusted EBITDA stated as a percentage of revenue.
We believe adjusted EBITDA and adjusted EBITDA margin are useful to investors in evaluating our operating performance for the following reasons: (i) adjusted EBITDA and similar Non-GAAP measures are widely used by investors to measure a company's operating performance without regard to items that can vary substantially from company to company depending upon financing and accounting methods, book values of assets, capital structures and the methods by which assets were acquired, (ii) securities analysts often use adjusted EBITDA and similar Non-GAAP measures as supplemental measures to evaluate the overall operating performance of companies, and (iii) by comparing our adjusted EBITDA across historical periods and with that of other companies, investors can evaluate our operating results without the additional variations resulting from certain items that may vary based on financing and accounting methods, capital structures, asset values and acquisition methods.
Our management uses adjusted EBITDA and adjusted EBITDA margin (i) as measures of operating performance, because they do not include the impact of items that we do not consider indicative of our core operating performance, (ii) for planning purposes, including the preparation of our annual operating budget, (iii) to allocate resources to enhance the financial performance of the business, (iv) to evaluate the effectiveness of our business strategies, and (v) in communications with the board of directors and investors concerning our financial performance.
Non-GAAP Net Income and Non-GAAP EPS
We define Non-GAAP net income and Non-GAAP earnings per share (Non-GAAP EPS) to exclude certain discrete items that management does not consider representative of our ongoing operations, including energy asset and goodwill impairment, contingent consideration, restructuring and other charges, gain or loss on sale of equity investment, and gain or loss upon deconsolidation of a variable interest entity. Non-GAAP EPS is equal to Non-GAAP net income divided by diluted shares outstanding. We consider Non-GAAP net income and Non-GAAP EPS to be important indicators of our operational strength and performance of our business because they eliminate the effects of events that are not part of the Company's core operations.
Non-GAAP Adjusted Cash from Operations
We define Non-GAAP adjusted cash from operations as cash flows from operating activities plus proceeds from Federal ESPC projects. Federal ESPC projects are treated as financing cash flows under GAAP. These cash flows, however, correspond to benefits generated by the underlying assets and projects. Thus, we believe that adjusting operating cash flow to include the cash generated by our Federal ESPC projects provides investors with a useful measure for evaluating the cash generating ability of our core operating business. Our management uses
Non-GAAP adjusted cash from operations as a measure of liquidity because it captures all sources of cash associated with our operations.
