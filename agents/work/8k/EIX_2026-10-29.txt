Edison International Reports Third Quarter 2026 Results
•Third-quarter 2026 GAAP EPS of $1.88; core EPS of $1.92
•SCE has hardened approximately 90% of its distribution system in high fire risk areas and continues targeted investment to reduce remaining risk
•Durable wildfire reform that includes a clear liquidity source and a true shareholder liability cap not connected to the Wildfire Fund remains a priority
•Reaffirmed 2026 core EPS guidance of $5.90 to $6.20
•Continued confidence in delivering 5% to 7% core EPS growth from 2025-2030
ROSEMEAD, Calif., Oct. 29, 2026 — Edison International (NYSE: EIX) today reported third-quarter net income of $724 million, or $1.88 per share, compared to net income of $832 million, or $2.16 per share, in the third quarter of last year. As adjusted, third-quarter core earnings were $738 million, or $1.92 per share, compared to core earnings of $901 million, or $2.34 per share, in the third quarter of last year.
Southern California Edison’s third-quarter 2026 core earnings per share (EPS) decreased year over year, primarily due to earnings recognized in the third quarter of 2025 upon implementation of the 2025 GRC final decision, which was retroactive to Jan. 1, 2025, partially offset by higher revenue resulting from the escalation mechanism authorized in the 2025 GRC final decision.
Edison International Parent and Other’s third-quarter 2026 core loss per share decreased year over year, primarily due to lower preferred stock dividends, partially offset by higher interest expense.
“Edison International posted another quarter of strong operational performance, with a focus on community safety and customer affordability,” said Pedro J. Pizarro, president and CEO of Edison International. “We continue advancing our efforts toward outcomes that serve customers, communities, and capital providers.”
Pizarro added, “California needs lasting wildfire reform to help protect customer affordability and support investment in a safer, more reliable grid. We are working with stakeholders toward a long-term solution that will enable the needed investments at a reasonable cost of capital.”

About Edison International
Edison International (NYSE: EIX) is one of the nation’s largest electric utility holding companies, focused on providing clean, reliable energy and energy services. Headquartered in Rosemead, California, Edison International is the parent company of Southern California Edison, a utility delivering electricity to 15 million people across Southern, Central and Coastal California.
Appendix
Use of Non-GAAP Financial Measures
Edison International’s earnings are prepared in accordance with generally accepted accounting principles used in the United States and represent the company’s earnings as reported to the Securities and Exchange Commission. Our management uses core earnings and core earnings per share ("EPS") internally for financial planning and for analysis of performance of Edison International and Southern California Edison. We also use core earnings and core EPS when communicating with analysts and investors regarding our earnings results to facilitate comparisons of the Company’s performance from period to period. Financial measures referred to as net income, basic EPS, core earnings, or core EPS also apply to the description of earnings or earnings per share.
Core earnings and core EPS are non-GAAP financial measures and may not be comparable to those of other companies. Core earnings and core EPS are defined as basic earnings and basic EPS excluding income or loss from discontinued operations and income or loss from significant discrete items that management does not consider representative of ongoing earnings. Basic earnings and losses refer to net income or losses attributable to Edison International shareholders. Core earnings are reconciled to basic earnings in the attached tables. The impact of participating securities (vested awards that earn dividend equivalents that may participate in undistributed earnings with common stock) for the principal operating subsidiary is not material to the principal operating subsidiary’s EPS and is therefore reflected in the results of the Edison International holding company, which is included in Edison International Parent and Other.

Prepared Remarks of Edison International CEO and CFO
Third Quarter 2026 Earnings Teleconference
October 29, 2026, 1:30 p.m. (PT)
Pedro Pizarro, President and Chief Executive Officer, Edison International
My comments today will focus on four areas: our strong performance; the need for durable wildfire reform; SCE’s continued progress making the grid safer and more reliable; and the utility’s work to support communities impacted by the Eaton Fire.
Starting with earnings, Edison International’s third-quarter core EPS was $1.92, bringing year-to-date core EPS to $4.89. Based on strong year-to-date performance and our outlook for the remainder of the year, we are reaffirming our 2026 core EPS guidance range of $5.90 to $6.20. Aaron will discuss our financial performance in his remarks.
We know that durable wildfire liability reform is a key focus for investors and, moreover, it is essential to manage customer costs and support the world’s fourth-largest economy. We were disappointed when California’s legislative session concluded without action on a durable, long-term solution. It was evident that capital providers also felt that way given the market reaction. Since the legislative session ended, we and the other California IOUs have been engaging with a broad group of stakeholders to improve understanding of the consequences of inaction and advocate for a special legislative session with a sense of urgency. The objective is to achieve a long-term solution that includes a clear and durable liquidity source for funding claims resolution and a true shareholder liability cap that is not connected to the Wildfire Fund. While we are focused on these key items, we recognize that other pieces may be needed to develop a package that gains support from stakeholders and ultimately the Senate, the Assembly, and the Governor in time to send a bill to the Governor by the constitutional deadline of November 15.
If we do not succeed in 2026, we must continue the effort with a new Governor and Legislature in 2027. While we remain confident that California policymakers will ultimately
1
take action given the dire and escalating impacts on customers and the economy, delay into 2027 will have significant and costly consequences. We evaluate our investments through a disciplined benefit-cost lens, balancing the needs of the system with affordability for customers. The uncertainty in the present environment is increasing the cost of financing these investments. If Edison’s credit rating were to fall below investment grade, that would add further costs at a time when affordability is paramount for customers.
SCE will file its 2029 General Rate Case in about six months. Increased financing costs would influence the investments requested, with priority given to core work in safety, reliability, and compliance. Without durable wildfire reform in place, SCE would request less capital than it otherwise would for projects that enable electrification, the clean energy transition, and economic growth. Higher financing costs could similarly affect decisions over the next couple of years about incremental capital investment in areas such as grid readiness for load growth and supply resources in SCE’s service area.
Turning to SCE’s progress in making the grid safer and more reliable, the utility has reached substantial wildfire mitigation and grid hardening milestones, including hardening approximately 90% of its distribution system in high fire risk areas. SCE continues to focus on driving out risk through targeted infrastructure investment, comprehensive inspections and remediation, and vegetation management. We continue to enhance our data-driven and risk-informed approach, integrating insights from multiple wildfire risk and climate models to give us a more complete view of risk and help target investment where it can provide the greatest safety benefit and risk reduction. The capital investments approved in SCE’s 2025 GRC, including covered conductor, targeted undergrounding, and asset replacement, will make the grid safer and more resilient.
Conditions on the ground for the rest of 2026 currently appear consistent with a normal year, and there is potential for El Niño to contribute to earlier rainfall. Looking over the much longer term, Edison International unveiled its most recent white paper at Climate Week NYC last month. Titled “Adapting Today: Energizing a More Resilient Tomorrow,” it highlights expected 2050 weather impacts across floods, droughts, sea level rise, extreme heat, and wildfires; illustrates the long-term planning and investments needed; and
2
emphasizes the need for partnership across communities, government, and the private sector to prepare California’s economy.
Regardless of the weather outlook, SCE maintains flexibility in its work plan so it can conduct additional inspections and remediation where changing conditions indicate those activities will provide the greatest safety benefit. To support those decisions, the utility has expanded the information available from weather stations, high-definition cameras, fuel sampling, inspections, and advanced modeling capabilities. Together, these tools help SCE target resources and take additional action where conditions warrant. They complement the substantial physical hardening SCE has already completed, including more than 7,200 miles of covered conductor. That work provides a stronger foundation. More precise data and more advanced technologies, including AI-enhanced inspections and AI-enabled cameras, are helping SCE address the risk that remains by identifying maintenance needs, threats, and potential equipment failures earlier.
In parallel, the Office of Energy Infrastructure Safety continues to evolve its wildfire mitigation plan guidelines and put in place more structured and in-depth requirements in select areas. That scrutiny is an important part of continuously strengthening California investor-owned utilities’ wildfire mitigation programs and reducing risk for customers and communities.
I’m pleased to note that in approving SCE’s 2026 through 2028 Wildfire Mitigation Plan, OEIS highlighted SCE’s transmission high fire risk-informed inspection program and described it as the most comprehensive among the large IOUs. The agency noted that SCE conducts both detailed ground and aerial inspections annually on approximately 77% of its transmission assets in high fire risk areas, with inspection frequency tailored to the consequence and risk profile of each location. SCE is committed to building on both its successes and opportunities for improvement to maintain a rigorous, layered approach to reducing risk and enhancing execution of its wildfire programs.
Turning to the Eaton Fire, we have said that, absent additional evidence, SCE believes that it is likely that its equipment was associated with the ignition. During the quarter, the Los Angeles County Fire Department published redacted reports from its investigation. Those

The prudency standard established under California law evaluates whether the utility’s actions were consistent with those of a reasonable operator across a spectrum of possible practices. The CPUC can also consider factors within and beyond the utility’s control that may have exacerbated the costs of the fire. It is important to distinguish the prudency standard from the substantially different standards associated with felony liability or willful disregard. Those standards require intentionality and recklessness. They involve a fundamentally different level of conduct and intent that we are fully confident our company did not exhibit.
