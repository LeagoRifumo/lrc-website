Fervo Energy Reports Third Quarter 2026 Results
Houston, TX - November 9, 2026 - Fervo Energy Company (“Fervo” or the “Company”) (NASDAQ: FRVO),
global pioneer of next-generation geothermal energy, today reported financial and operational results for the third
quarter ended September 30, 2026.
"In the third quarter, Cape Station achieved a major milestone, reaching first power and contractual commercial
operation," said Tim Latimer, CEO and Co-founder of Fervo. "We also signed what we believe is the largest
enhanced geothermal power purchase agreement to date with Google, creating a pathway to scaling our
partnership to nearly a gigawatt. With commercial operations underway and significant demand for our
technology, we are focused on delivering the remaining Phase I GeoBlocks on schedule and building toward
geothermal as a reliable, always-on, low-cost source of power."
BUSINESS AND OPERATIONAL HIGHLIGHTS
•Declared commercial operation at Cape Station Phase I GeoBlock 1 on September 30, ahead of its contractual
commercial operation date. The 33-megawatt GeoBlock 1 is operating in line with expectations for
production and power delivered under its power purchase agreement (“PPA”).
•GeoBlock 2 has achieved grid sync and is in its early operations phase, delivering test energy to the grid.
GeoBlock 3 is mechanically complete and is in cold commissioning. GeoBlocks 2 and 3 remain on schedule
and are expected to achieve commercial operation by January 1, 2027, which will complete Cape Station
Phase I.
•In partnership with Google, Fervo signed what it believes is the largest enhanced geothermal power purchase
agreement to date. The 15-year, grid-connected, take-or-pay agreement supplies 396 megawatts, with first
power expected mid-2028. Fervo is also committed to offering Google an option to expand its offtake by
approximately 600 megawatts, for a total of nearly 1 gigawatt.
•In connection with this agreement, Fervo progressed construction across Cape Station GeoBlocks 4 through
11, now referred to as Cape Google 400. These GeoBlocks are expected to provide approximately 400
megawatts of power under Fervo’s PPA with Google beginning in 2028.
•Received an updated heat-initially-in-place study supporting approximately 8.4 gigawatts of capacity
potential at Cape Station, nearly double the prior estimate of 4.3 gigawatts. The study was completed by
DeGolyer and MacNaughton, an independent consulting firm.
•Selected for two potential awards from the U.S. Department of Energy totaling approximately $20.0 million,
including funding for appraisal wells in Idaho and Nevada.
FINANCIAL HIGHLIGHTS
•Reported Q3 2026 net income of $11.7 million and net loss attributable to common stockholders of $20.8
million, or $(0.07) per share. Net income reflected $15.4 million of interest income and two non-recurring
items: an $11.8 million non-cash gain on interest rate swaps and a $29.9 million tax benefit from tax credits
earned as Cape Phase I assets were placed in service.
•Reported Q3 2026 operating loss of $43.7 million, including general and administrative expense of $39.4
million compared to $27.4 million in Q2 2026, reflecting higher stock-based compensation, public company
costs, and headcount growth.
•Reported Q3 2026 capital expenditures of $256.7 million, compared to $226.5 million in Q2 2026, primarily
related to activities at Cape Station.
•Expect fourth quarter 2026 capital expenditures of approximately $450.0 million to $500.0 million, bringing
expectations for the second half of 2026 to $700.0 million to $750.0 million, compared to prior expectations
of $850.0 million to $900.0 million. Efficiencies in GeoBlock construction and commissioning have allowed
certain spending on Cape Google 400 to occur later in the construction cycle, with no change to expected
commercial operation dates or total expected investment.
•Placed GeoBlock 1 in service on September 30, transferring approximately $514.3 million from construction-
in-process to property, plant and equipment. This amount is not the standalone cost of GeoBlock 1. It includes
a larger share of Phase I wellfield costs and shared infrastructure serving all three GeoBlocks, as well as
capitalized interest and financing costs. Expected total cost for GeoBlocks 1 through 3 remains approximately
$7,000 per kilowatt.
•Continue to anticipate fourth quarter 2026 revenue in line with prior expectations, which will be the first full
quarter of revenue from Cape Station. Revenue in 2027 remains unchanged from the Company’s prior
outlook.
•Ended Q3 2026 with unrestricted cash and cash equivalents of $1.9 billion and current and long-term debt of
$301.6 million.
BUSINESS UPDATES
Commercial
In partnership with Google, Fervo entered into what it understands to be the largest enhanced geothermal power
purchase agreement signed to date. The agreement is a 15-year, grid-connected, take-or-pay contract for 396
megawatts across eight GeoBlocks, with fixed terms. First power is expected from these GeoBlocks in mid-2028,
with full capacity in operation targeted by year-end 2028.
Through this agreement, Fervo has committed to offering Google the option to expand its offtake by
approximately 600 megawatts for a total of nearly 1 gigawatt. The 600-megawatt expansion, if accepted by
Google, would be the first qualifying project under the Geothermal Framework Agreement that Fervo executed in
March 2026, which establishes a development framework for up to 3 gigawatts of geothermal capacity through
2033.
For the 600-megawatt expansion, Fervo and Google are pursuing behind-the-meter, direct-to-load delivery under
Utah's SB132 framework, which could allow Fervo to directly power a potential regional data center in Beaver
County, Utah.
With the signing of the Google agreement, Fervo now has 1,054 megawatts of capacity under binding power
purchase agreements, representing approximately $11.9 billion in contracted revenue, compared to 658
megawatts and approximately $7.2 billion at the end of the second quarter of 2026.
Operations and Construction
On September 30, Fervo declared commercial operation for GeoBlock 1 ahead of its contractual deadline.
GeoBlock 1 is delivering power under its power purchase agreement and operating in line with expectations. This
is the first of three 33-megawatt GeoBlocks that make up Cape Station Phase I’s approximately 100 megawatts of
capacity.
GeoBlocks 2 and 3 have achieved mechanical completion. GeoBlock 2 has achieved grid sync, a major milestone,
which marks the end of hot commissioning. The plant is now in its early operations phase, exporting test energy
to the grid, and GeoBlock 3 is close behind in cold commissioning, where Fervo is testing all of the mechanical
and electrical systems prior to introducing brine. Both are expected to reach contractual commercial operation by
January 1, 2027.
Additionally, Fervo began installing a small, on-site solar array at Cape Station Phase I that it will use to provide
station auxiliary power.
Cape Google 400, which is expected to fulfill Fervo’s approximately 400-megawatt power purchase agreement
with Google, continues to advance on both the surface and subsurface. On the surface, the first set of 50-megawatt
turbines has passed factory acceptance testing, and major equipment, including recuperators and over 60 percent
of air-cooled condensers, has been delivered to site. In the subsurface, drilling for these GeoBlocks is
approximately 18 percent complete.
Cape Google 400 incorporates the Company’s 3.0 well design, with longer laterals, larger-diameter casing, and a
deeper target depth than Cape Phase I, engineered to increase power output per well while reducing installed cost
per kilowatt. Fervo continues to target an installed cost of $5,500 per kilowatt for Cape Google 400, on the path to
its long-term target of $3,000 per kilowatt.
Portfolio
Fervo's development pipeline continued to advance during the third quarter. Capacity in operation reached 36
megawatts, up from 3 megawatts at the end of Q2, while capacity under construction now stands at 466
megawatts, encompassing GeoBlocks 2 through 11. Ready-to-build capacity remains at 550 megawatts.
Advanced Development remains at 3.0 gigawatts and Early Development grew to approximately 54.7 gigawatts,
up from 48.6 gigawatts. The increase reflects, in part, a new independent heat-initially-in-place study of Cape
Station, commissioned by Fervo in Q3 2026 and conducted by DeGolyer and MacNaughton. The study supports
approximately 8.4 gigawatts of capacity potential, nearly double the prior estimate of 4.3 gigawatts, driven by the
greater drilling depths of the Company’s 3.0 well design and incremental lease acquisitions. New appraisal wells
planned in Nevada and Idaho were selected in the quarter for two potential awards from the U.S. Department of
Energy totaling approximately $20.0 million, subject to definitive funding agreements.
Fervo's current land portfolio spans approximately 670,000 acres.
Financing
As of September 30, 2026, Fervo had unrestricted cash and cash equivalents of $1.9 billion and current and long-
term debt of $301.6 million.

ABOUT FERVO
Fervo Energy (NASDAQ: FRVO) is a modern power company built around one of the market’s most important
needs: affordable, dependable new power supply. Through the large-scale deployment of enhanced geothermal
systems, Fervo has established a repeatable, industrial approach to building utility-scale power. The Company is
transforming geothermal into a clean, reliable, cost-competitive solution designed to meet rising demand from AI
hyperscalers, utilities, and a more electricity-intensive economy. For more information, visit
www.fervoenergy.com.
