Harrow Announces Third Quarter 2026 Financial Results
Third Quarter 2026 and Selected Highlights:
•Record third quarter revenue of $99.3 million, up 41% sequentially and 39% year over year
•Branded revenue of $83.5 million, up 62% year over year and approximately 84% of total revenue
•GAAP net income of $9.8 million, including a $5.0 million litigation settlement gain, compared with a net loss of $17.3 million in the second quarter of 2026 and net income of $1.0 million in the third quarter of 2025
•Adjusted EBITDA (a non-GAAP measure) of $21.2 million, compared with $(1.2) million in the second quarter of 2026 and $22.8 million in the third quarter of 2025
•Reaffirmed full-year 2026 revenue guidance of $350 million to $365 million; updated full-year 2026 Adjusted EBITDA guidance to more than $40 million, including fourth quarter Adjusted EBITDA of more than $33 million, compared with prior Adjusted EBITDA guidance of $80 million to $100 million
•Cash and cash equivalents of $75.3 million as of September 30, 2026
NASHVILLE, Tenn., November 9, 2026 – Harrow (Nasdaq: HROW), a leading provider of ophthalmic disease management solutions in North America, announced results for the third quarter ended September 30, 2026. The Company also posted its third-quarter Letter to Stockholders and corporate presentation in the “Investors” section of its website at harrow.com.
“The third quarter delivered the second-half acceleration we anticipated,” said Mark L. Baum, Chairman and Chief Executive Officer of Harrow. “Branded products drove record revenue, growing 62% year over year. VEVYE delivered record quarterly revenue, supported by continued prescription growth, improving economics, and increasing productivity from our expanded sales force. TRIESENCE delivered a record quarter, while IHEEZO unit demand grew 21% year over year. Looking to the fourth quarter, we expect to benefit from continued VEVYE and TRIESENCE growth, IHEEZO’s historically strongest period, and TYRVAYA’s initial revenue contribution. Achieving our full-year revenue guidance of $350 million to $365 million will require strong execution, but we continue to believe it is achievable.”
Mr. Baum continued, “We expect the fourth quarter to be Harrow’s strongest on record and that second-half revenue will exceed the high end of what we contemplated in March. That said, our updated Adjusted EBITDA outlook reflects both the first half operating shortfall against our original plan and additional spending to accelerate our G-MELT NDA submission, advance important development programs, and integrate TYRVAYA. We expect more than $33 million in fourth-quarter Adjusted EBITDA, bringing our full-year outlook to more than $40 million. To be clear: our amended Adjusted EBITDA outlook is a floor, and we believe there is opportunity to exceed it.”

Pipeline and Regulatory Highlights:
•IHEEZO QUELL Study: Announced topline results from QUELL, a Phase 4, randomized, double-masked study of 231 patients undergoing intravitreal injections. While QUELL did not meet its prespecified primary endpoint of non-inferiority, successful ocular surface anesthesia was achieved in 55.2% (63 of 115) of IHEEZO patients versus 59.7% (68 of 113) receiving proparacaine drops plus a subconjunctival lidocaine injection1. Successful anesthesia rates were lower than anticipated in both treatment groups. However, secondary findings included lower reported cumulative pain through 24 hours with IHEEZO (2.9 versus 3.7), less ocular surface staining (mean increase of 1.7 versus 3.2), and fewer adverse events (27% versus 47%). No serious adverse events occurred in either group. These findings provide additional important information about patient comfort and tolerability. Full results are expected to be presented at the Hawaiian Eye & Retina Conference in January 2027.

•G-MELT: Completed a pre-NDA meeting with the FDA; the Company expects to submit its NDA before the end of this year, meaningfully earlier than previously anticipated.
•TRIESENCE: A multicenter Phase 3 study evaluating TRIESENCE for the treatment of ocular inflammation and pain following cataract surgery continues to enroll, with initial data expected in early 2027.
•NATACYN®: An investigator-initiated study evaluating fungal involvement in blepharitis and the potential benefits of NATACYN is ongoing, with initial data expected in the fourth quarter of 2026.

Non-GAAP Financial Measures
In addition to the Company’s results of operations determined in accordance with U.S. generally accepted accounting principles (GAAP), which are presented and discussed above, management also utilizes Adjusted EBITDA, an unaudited financial measure that is not calculated in accordance with GAAP, to evaluate the Company’s financial results and performance and to plan and forecast future periods. Adjusted EBITDA is considered a “non-GAAP” financial measure within the meaning of Regulation G promulgated by the SEC. Management believes that this non-GAAP financial measure reflects an additional way of viewing aspects of the Company’s operations that, when viewed with GAAP results, provides a more complete understanding of the Company’s results of operations and the factors and trends affecting its business. Management believes Adjusted EBITDA provides meaningful supplemental information regarding the Company’s performance because (i) it allows for greater transparency with respect to key metrics used by management in its financial and operational decision-making; (ii) it excludes the impact of non-cash or, when specified, non-recurring items that are not directly attributable to the Company’s core operating performance and that may obscure trends in the Company’s core operating performance; and (iii) it is used by institutional investors and the analyst community to help analyze the Company’s results. However, Adjusted EBITDA, and any other non-GAAP financial measure should be considered as a supplement to, and not as a substitute for, or superior to, the corresponding measures calculated in accordance with GAAP. Further, non-GAAP financial measures used by the Company and the way they are calculated may differ from the non-GAAP financial measures or the calculations of the same non-GAAP financial measures used by other companies, including the Company’s competitors.
Adjusted EBITDA
The Company defines Adjusted EBITDA as net income (loss), excluding the effects of stock-based compensation and expenses, interest, taxes, depreciation, amortization, loss on extinguishment of debt, litigation settlement gains and, if any and when specified, other non-recurring income or expense items. Management believes that the most directly comparable GAAP financial measure to Adjusted EBITDA is net income (loss). Adjusted EBITDA has limitations and should not be considered as an alternative to gross profit or net income (loss) as a measure of operating performance or to net cash provided by (used in) operating, investing, or financing activities as a measure of ability to meet cash needs.

29th Quarterly Letter to Stockholders
November 9, 2026
Dear Harrow Stockholders:
The second-half ramp we anticipated materialized as Harrow reported record third quarter revenue of $99.3 million, up 41% sequentially and 39% year over year. Branded products led the way, providing $83.5 million in revenue, up 62% year over year and approximately 84% of our total revenue. GAAP net income was $9.8 million, which included a $5.0 million litigation settlement gain, and Adjusted EBITDA was $21.2 million.
Reflecting on 2026 Guidance
When providing 2026 guidance, our intention was to avoid setting ambitious targets and then falling short. We guided to $350–365 million in revenue for the year, including $133–153 million in the first half. We ultimately delivered $114.9 million in the first half, about $28 million below the first-half midpoint, and a meaningful miss — which I acknowledge.
Here is what we got right, what we missed, and what we have changed and what we are changing:
VEVYE® net revenues were the primary driver of our first-half miss. Aside from minor misses on some of our smaller products, assumptions we made about VEVYE revenue under our expanded commercial coverage proved too optimistic, especially around our projected gross-to-net (GTN) vs. actual GTN — and that is what hit us hard.
We have since changed the economics of each VEVYE prescription, and current results confirm that our adjustments are working. Since making these changes, net revenue per VEVYE prescription is improving in line with our internal expectations, a trend that we expect to continue, especially after we begin to access the recent coverage win (discussed more below).
With the above said, there are broader points worth highlighting: We deliberately built one of the most diverse portfolios in ophthalmology. This allows us to offer more solutions to eye care professionals and their patients. I view our product breadth as an important long-term feature, not a bug. The challenge, from a forecasting perspective, particularly as we introduce new products and expand into adjacent markets, is predicting precisely when and how much each product will contribute in a given 90 day period. Too often during a quarter, weakness in one product is not overcome by strength in another. I have seen this type of quarterly choppiness smooth out over annual periods and I am confident that annual guidance numbers will better serve my intent to set appropriate revenue performance expectations. Therefore, as we provide 2027 guidance during our fourth quarter conference call early next year, I intend to go back to our "annual revenue greater than X" approach.
We plan to provide annual guidance and report clearly each quarter on our progress and the risks to that outlook. Our historical annual revenue growth trajectory speaks for itself. That said, today we have more resources, more and better talent supporting the business, a phenomenal and growing product pipeline — all providing profitable revenue generation from assets with far greater potential than at any other time in our history.
Turning to the fourth quarter, we are also now in what we expect to be our strongest quarter of the year. Here are additional points that support my confidence in our business:
•VEVYE is gaining momentum. Business-rule changes we made earlier this year, along with a more productive and maturing expanded sales force, drove record quarterly revenue, bringing performance back closer to our original 2026 model. We are still behind where we had initially hoped to be from a revenue perspective, but are quickly gaining ground as GTN improves.
Separately, our new coverage win (10+ million commercial lives) is expected to take effect January 1, strengthening our 2027 outlook.
•IHEEZO is heading into its strongest period. The team's new retina account wins and deeper penetration of existing accounts have more than offset the units we lost when IHEEZO's pass-through status expired. With a permanent product-specific J-Code in the office setting of care, strong reimbursement, and a growing customer base, we expect a record fourth quarter and a record year for IHEEZO unit demand.
•TRIESENCE revenues are climbing. TRIESENCE® topped $10 million in quarterly revenue for the first time since we acquired and relaunched it. Surgical adoption is rising, and we expect a strong fourth quarter. This is sticky revenue. As I've said on prior calls, my experience is that practices who integrate products into surgical workflows, and experience outstanding clinical outcomes, tend to continue to use — and even expand use — as opposed to trimming or ceasing use. This can drive a long-term cycle of growth which is what I expect from TRIESENCE, given its relatively low penetration in its largest market opportunity.
•BYOOVIZ went wide before it went deep. The headline in the third quarter was a revenue miss for BYOOVIZ compared to our launch expectations. However, more accounts tried BYOOVIZ than we modeled. Demand picked up in September and has carried into the fourth quarter. Taken together, we expect the third and fourth quarters, in the aggregate, to put us in range of achieving our internal BYOOVIZ model for the full year.
•Specialty and compounding should grow. The Specialty Products and Dry Eye Disease teams recently merged, creating a fortified Ocular Surface Disease team. We now have a much larger sales force promoting VEVYE as its primary product, but also promoting VERKAZIA® and NATACYN® — which is already correlating to growing prescription volumes. We expect the fourth quarter to be the strongest period for our specialty and compounded products.
•TYRVAYA starts contributing. Our recently completed acquisition of TYRVAYA® should make its first revenue contribution in the fourth quarter, strengthening our Ocular Surface Disease franchise.
Today, we are reaffirming our full-year revenue guidance of $350–365 million. Hitting our revenue target requires fourth-quarter revenue of about $136–151 million. That is sequential revenue growth of roughly 37–52%, and it will take strong execution across the business. For context, our revenue grew about 30% on average from the third quarter to the fourth over the last two years (36% in 2024 and 24% in 2025). This year, while our established products are growing on a stronger trajectory, supported by our expanded sales force and broader commercial coverage, we have two new revenue sources: (i) a first quarter of TYRVAYA, and (ii) growth in BYOOVIZ.
We also see reasonable scenarios in which we finish modestly below the low end of our revenue range, mostly because of order timing and year-end purchasing. Based on what we see today (i.e. second-half revenue to exceed the high end of what we contemplated in March), we believe the low end is achievable, and maintaining revenue guidance is the right call.
We are lowering our Adjusted EBITDA outlook from $80 million to $100 million to a floor of "more than $40 million," including more than $33 million in the fourth quarter. This is a significant change, and I want to explain why we are making it.
Our first-half revenue shortfall, driven by VEVYE, combined with lower-than-expected gross margins throughout this year, put us behind our original Adjusted EBITDA expectations. Even so, we still saw a path to achieving our original guidance through a strong second half, supported by accelerating revenue growth and a favorable mix of higher-margin products. The third quarter demonstrated some of that acceleration, delivering $21.2 million in Adjusted EBITDA and bringing our year-to-date total to $7.3
million, but third quarter profitability came in below our model. This shortfall primarily reflected our decisions to accelerate several important R&D initiatives and certain cash-pay-related expenses. As a result, we are still short of where we needed to be to hit our full-year number. Reaching the low end of our original guidance would still require close to $73 million in fourth-quarter Adjusted EBITDA, an exceptionally strong finish, even considering the momentum we are seeing across our business.
As we evaluated the balance of the year, we also made several decisions to accelerate important initiatives and strengthen the business heading into 2027. Some of these decisions will affect cash, while others, such as the TYRVAYA transition, will affect fourth quarter profitability. Our revised outlook reflects the shortfall against our original plan, the continued acceleration of strategic initiatives, and additional fourth quarter considerations:
•G-MELT™. Following our pre-NDA meeting, we now plan to submit the G-MELT NDA this year, ahead of the first-half 2027 timeline we discussed in August. A 2026 submission brings the FDA application fee, along with other submission-related costs, into the fourth quarter. Our new outlook assumes those costs are incurred this year.
•R&D Expenses. We accelerated several development programs during the year, including the IHEEZO QUELL study that we just completed, the ongoing phase 3 TRIESENCE study, and the continued advancement of YOCHIL, which increased R&D expense above our original plan in the third-quarter and will continue to affect our expense profile in the fourth quarter.
•TYRVAYA®. Following the October 1 acquisition, we are onboarding our new colleagues and transitioning operations from Viatris. These activities, along with purchase accounting associated with acquired inventory, will affect fourth-quarter profitability.
•Royalties. We expect certain product specific royalties to step up in the fourth quarter as we surpass annual revenue thresholds ahead of our 2026 model, triggering a slightly increased royalty tier associated with sales of the product. This is an obligation we are pleased to incur, as it reflects the continued growth and success of the product.
Taken together, lower-than-anticipated gross margins, our Q3 profitability shortfall, and additional Q4 strategic initiatives have changed our expectations for full-year Adjusted EBITDA. Nevertheless, incurring these costs is the right long-term call for our stockholders. Rather than depend on an unusually favorable fourth-quarter revenue mix to overcome these additional costs, we believe the prudent approach is to establish an Adjusted EBITDA floor that reflects our current outlook and the remaining variability in fourth-quarter expenses. Importantly, our outlook calls for more than $33 million in fourth-quarter Adjusted EBITDA, which would represent our strongest quarterly performance of the year. Once again — this is a floor, not a ceiling, and we believe there is opportunity to exceed it as we execute through the balance of the year.
We expect to enter 2027 with a much stronger foundation, increased commercial visibility, and growing momentum across our business. Our commercial investments are translating into economic accomplishment; VEVYE's expanding coverage and improving prescription economics are creating opportunities to increase prescription volume and profit per prescription; and our retina and surgical portfolios continue to gain traction. With TYRVAYA, a larger sales organization, new clinical evidence, and an anticipated G-MELT NDA submission (ahead of schedule), we are well positioned for continued growth in 2027 and beyond.
Four Focused Commercial Teams
Upon closing the TYRVAYA acquisition, we reorganized our commercial operations into four focused teams, each built around shared prescribers, care settings, and purchasing dynamics. Ocular Surface Disease, now our largest team (after adding approximately 40 experienced personnel from Viatris), combines our dry eye and specialty organizations to promote our ocular surface portfolio to the medical
optometric and front of the eye ophthalmologist prescriber communities. Retina focuses on retina specialists and multi-specialty practices for in-office procedures. Surgical serves cataract and refractive surgeons with a comprehensive peri-operative portfolio, with G-MELT expected to expand that offering upon FDA approval. Finally, Access+ is focused on our compounded products and supporting customers across all three branded teams. You can see the breakdown in our updated corporate presentation.
The goal is simple: put the right products with the right representatives in front of the right customers. By organizing around shared customer relationships, we can leverage our growing portfolio more efficiently, make every sales call more productive, and provide a better experience for the eye care professionals we serve.
HarrowRx: Improving Prescription Workflow Management
Our branded sales teams are now supported by HarrowRx, a single specialty pharmacy platform launched on November 1, 2026. HarrowRx is integrated with the electronic medical record (EMR) systems prescribers already use, so physicians can prescribe or order any of Harrow’s products, along with new services and features we intend to add over the coming months and into 2027.
Instead of choosing among multiple specialty pharmacies, hubs, and savings programs and remembering which applies to each product, Harrow customers can now send every Harrow prescription to one destination. HarrowRx then directs each prescription or order to the appropriate channel: prescriptions for our pharmacy-benefit products are supported and dispensed through our pharmacy partner network, and orders for our buy-and-bill products are routed to our distribution partner for support and fulfillment. HarrowRx simplifies how practices work with Harrow, making it easier to do business with us.
Building a Leading Ocular Surface Disease Franchise
Dry Eye Disease
VEVYE and TYRVAYA approach dry eye through different mechanisms and routes of administration, giving physicians two distinct options for patients. They also fit well together commercially. Our expanded Ocular Surface Disease team can now discuss both products with many of the same prescribers, allowing prescribers to reach for Harrow products to treat more of their dry eye patients.
VEVYE is our core dry eye offering and it continues to perform, delivering a record third quarter of $39.7 million in revenue, up 35% sequentially and 75% year over year. That performance reflected continued prescription growth, greater productivity from our expanded sales force, and improving net product pricing (NPP).
This year, we more than doubled the field organization supporting VEVYE, and we are now seeing the return on that investment. Consistent engagement with eye care professionals, or more "feet on the street" is driving adoption as newer representatives establish relationships and become more productive in their territories. Importantly, we believe there is substantial opportunity ahead as the team continues to mature.
Equally important is the progress we are making in translating that demand into sustainable net revenue. IHEEZO
IHEEZO generated $25.3 million in third-quarter revenue, up 62% sequentially and 15% year over year. Unit demand totaled 62,210, down approximately 5% from our record second quarter. We generally recognize revenue when product is shipped to a distributor, while reported unit demand reflects units ordered by or shipped to practices, not necessarily units used during the quarter. These figures can, and in our case typically do, differ based on the timing of distributor shipments and purchasing activity at the practice level.
The third quarter has historically been IHEEZO's seasonally slowest period, as summer vacations and fewer procedure days affect activity across many practices. This year, the effect was slightly amplified during the third quarter due to increased second quarter stocking activity by our physician office customers, which was elevated beyond our initial estimates. These were purchases of our discontinued single unit presentation of IHEEZO. That said, we do not view this modest sequential decline as indicative of a change in IHEEZO's underlying growth trajectory.
When comparing the third quarter of this year with the prior year, unit demand increased 21% year over year, and our customer base expanded markedly. As we think about the balance of the year, we are now in IHEEZO's historically strongest quarter.
Here is our read on the initial topline results of QUELL: Recall that QUELL is a Phase 4, randomized, double-masked study of IHEEZO in 231 patients receiving intravitreal injections. We designed QUELL to answer the practical questions retina specialists ask us: How well does it anesthetize the eye? Can it
make the experience more comfortable for patients? And can it simplify the procedure and improve efficiency for retina practices? We also didn't make it easy on ourselves. We chose a demanding comparator: proparacaine drops plus a subconjunctival lidocaine injection, a potent injectable anesthetic. We wanted to understand how a topical gel, requiring no additional anesthetic injection, would perform against a regimen that includes one.
QUELL did not meet its pre-specified primary endpoint because successful anesthesia rates were lower than anticipated in both treatment groups. The primary endpoint was successful ocular surface anesthesia, defined as a pain score of 0, meaning no pain, or 1, meaning pressure only, both immediately before and immediately after the injection. Successful ocular surface anesthesia was achieved in 55.2% (63 of 115) of IHEEZO patients versus 59.7% (68 of 113) receiving the injectable anesthesia1. The numbers were close, but we assumed that 88% of patients in each group would achieve successful anesthesia. Unfortunately, the actual rates could not produce a confidence interval narrow enough to fit within the 12-point margin we pre-specified.
The primary endpoint, however, tells only part of the story because patients experience the procedure — the pain associated with it and how their eye feels afterward. And on those measures, QUELL's exploratory findings were encouraging for IHEEZO:
•IHEEZO patients reported less pain in the hours post procedure, including lower cumulative pain through 24 hours (2.9 for the IHEEZO arm versus 3.7 for the injectable arm).
•IHEEZO patients also showed less ocular surface staining in the treated eye (mean increase of 1.7 for the IHEEZO arm versus 3.2 with routine anesthesia, with no change in the untreated eye), suggesting less damage to the ocular surface.
•IHEEZO patients' dryness symptoms improved from baseline by 24 hours.
•Fewer IHEEZO patients experienced adverse events (27% versus 47%).
For retina patients undergoing repeated injections, these findings provide valuable new insights into comfort and tolerability. For busy retina practices, the potential to avoid an additional anesthetic injection could also simplify workflow. I believe QUELL has given us important new clinical evidence that will help retina specialists evaluate IHEEZO's role in their practices, and we look forward to presenting the full results at the Hawaiian Eye & Retina Conference in January 2027.
