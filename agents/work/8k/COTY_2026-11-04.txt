COTY ANNOUNCES FIRST QUARTER FISCAL YEAR 2027 RESULTS
Q1 Profit and EPS Ahead of Guidance; Modest Sales Decline of 1%, with LFL Sales Down 2%
Top 6 Core Prestige Brands Back to Growth
Operating Cash Flow Up $65 Million YoY to $130 Million, with Free Cash Flow of $85 Million
Extended Marc Jacobs License, with 40-Year Duration, Reaffirms Coty as Beauty Partner of Choice for Luxury
NEW YORK -- November 4, 2026 -- Coty Inc. (NYSE: COTY) (Paris: COTY) ("Coty" or "the Company") today announced its results for the first quarter of fiscal year 2027, ended September 30, 2026.
"Our first quarter results were ahead of expectations on sales, adjusted EBITDA, and EPS, alongside a significant improvement in free cash flow, supported by the focus of Coty.Curated,” said Markus Strobel, Executive Chairman and Interim Chief Executive Officer. "Our like-for-like sales decline narrowed to 2%, broadly in line with the fourth quarter and a clear improvement from the declines of 6% and 4% in the first and second halves of fiscal 2026, reflecting improvements in key markets like the U.S., Asia Pacific, and Latin America. While our progress in the first quarter is encouraging, we have more work to do to translate green shoots into a broad-based recovery.
"Our Coty.Curated framework is beginning to deliver results. Over the past year, we have increasingly concentrated investment behind our Top 6 core Prestige brands, which represented 73% of Prestige sales in Q1. These brands moved from a 6% like-for-like decline in the first half of fiscal 2026 to 5% like-for-like growth in the first quarter, with five of the six growing and combined sell-out returning to growth. Our first half launches, including BOSS Bottled Beyond for Her, Burberry Goddess Amber Vanilla, Mood Stones by Kylie Jenner, and Marc Jacobs Beauty makeup, are resonating with consumers and tracking ahead of plan.
"I am proud that we have extended the Marc Jacobs license, securing a 40-year license duration for the brand. Since our partnership began in 2003, Coty has built multiple iconic fragrances, with Marc Jacobs fragrances now ranking among the Top 10 to 15 prestige female fragrances across the U.S., U.K., Canada, and Australia. The recent launch of Marc Jacobs Beauty makeup is also off to a strong start across these markets. The multi-decade agreement provides Coty with a strong platform to build on that momentum while enhancing the long-term visibility of Coty’s Prestige portfolio. This extension is also a powerful testament to Coty’s position as a partner of choice for the world’s leading luxury fashion and lifestyle brands.
"In Consumer Beauty, the like-for-like decline narrowed to 3% from 8% in the first half of fiscal 2026. CoverGirl and Sally Hansen in the U.S. and Rimmel in the U.K. are narrowing their retail sales gap to their categories. Sally Hansen regained the number-one position among U.S. mass nail brands in the overall nail category.
"The prestige and mass beauty markets continue to grow despite macroeconomic uncertainty, while our overall sell-out still trails the market in both divisions. Our priority is to translate investment into stronger sell-out and market share gains, with market share embedded in how we reward performance across the organization.
"We remain committed to completing the strategic review by the end of calendar 2026. Alongside it, we are designing the organization and cost structure to improve execution and prioritize reinvestment behind our brands. We will continue to build on our strengths: a portfolio of leading brands, strong category positions, and a differentiated end-to-end global platform. As we’ve previously shared, FY27 will be a transition year. We expect the recovery to be uneven, and implementation will take time. We know confidence is earned through consistent delivery, and our goal is to steadily improve execution and translate it into shareholder value in the years ahead."
Soraya Benchikh, Chief Financial Officer, said, "I joined Coty at a pivotal moment, with clear priorities to deliver quality growth, improve productivity to fund disciplined reinvestment, and strengthen cash generation and conversion. The first quarter shows encouraging early progress on each, although sustainable improvement will take time. We delivered adjusted EBITDA ahead of our guidance, supported by better-than-expected sales and cost control, while continuing to invest in our brands. The year-over-year decline in adjusted EBITDA moderated to 7% from steeper declines in fiscal 2026.
"On cash, better payables and receivables performance helped improve free cash flow to $85 million. Our free cash flow, together with the initial $250 million of Gucci Beauty proceeds, reduced net debt by over $300 million since June.
"Our objective is to create value over time through a virtuous cycle: focused investment drives quality growth, while better gross margins and a more efficient cost base strengthen earnings. Converting those earnings into cash
1
supports deleveraging and reinvestment in the next phase of sustainable growth. This is the model we are focused on executing with discipline and accountability, quarter after quarter."
*Top 6 Core Prestige Brands include Hugo Boss, Burberry, Calvin Klein, Marc Jacobs, Chloé, and Kylie Cosmetics. Top 4 Core Consumer Beauty Brands include CoverGirl, Rimmel, Sally Hansen, and adidas.

Three Months Ended September 30, 2026, Summary Results
For the three months ended September 30, 2026, compared to the three months ended September 30, 2025:
•Net revenue of $1,555.2 million decreased 1% on a reported basis. On a like-for-like (LFL) basis, net revenue declined 2%.
•Prestige net revenue of $1,058.4 million, representing 68% of the Company's total sales, declined 1% on a reported and LFL basis.
•Consumer Beauty net revenue of $496.8 million, representing 32% of the Company's total sales, decreased 2% on a reported basis and 3% on a LFL basis.
•Reported and adjusted gross margin of 63.3% decreased 120 basis points year-over-year. The decline reflected pressure from higher freight and material costs related to the Middle East conflict, alongside higher excess and obsolescence reflecting elevated demand forecasts.
•Reported operating income of $170.4 million declined from $185.0 million in the prior year. Reported operating margin of 11.0% compared to 11.7% in the prior year. Adjusted operating income of $220.5 million declined from $240.5 million in the prior year, reflecting lower sales and gross profit. Adjusted operating margin of 14.2% contracted by 100 basis points year-over-year.
•Reported net income of $106.2 million increased from $64.6 million in the prior year. Reported net income margin of 6.8% compared to 4.1% in the prior year. Adjusted net income of $146.1 million improved from $106.0 million in the prior year. Adjusted net income margin of 9.4% improved from 6.7% in the prior year. Reported and adjusted net income included a $14.8 million benefit from the mark-to-market on the equity swap, compared with a $26.5 million negative impact from the mark-to-market on the equity swap in the prior-year quarter.
•Adjusted EBITDA of $274.2 million decreased 7% from $296.1 million. Adjusted EBITDA margin of 17.6% decreased by 120 basis points.
2
•Reported earnings per share of $0.12 compared with $0.07 in the prior year. Adjusted earnings per share of $0.16 compared with $0.12 in the prior year. Adjusted earnings per share included a $0.01 benefit from the mark-to-market on the equity swap, compared with a $0.03 negative impact from the mark-to-market on the equity swap in the prior-year quarter.
•Cash flow from operating activities was $130.4 million, compared with $65.2 million in the prior-year period. Free cash flow was $84.6 million, compared with $11.2 million in the prior-year period.
•Total debt of $2,787.0 million on September 30, 2026, decreased from $3,088.2 million as of June 30, 2026. This resulted in a total debt to net income ratio of 5.1x. Financial net debt of $2,576.4 million decreased from $2,912.1 million on June 30, 2026. This resulted in a financial leverage ratio (net debt to adjusted EBITDA) of 3.1x.
Noteworthy Developments:
•Coty continues to implement its Coty.Curated framework to support sharper focus and stronger execution across the portfolio.
•Coty’s Prestige strategy is anchored in its Top 6 core Prestige brands, Burberry, Hugo Boss, Calvin Klein, Marc Jacobs, Chloé, and Kylie Cosmetics, which collectively returned to growth in Q1, up 5%. Key launches continued to perform well, including BOSS Bottled Beyond for Her, Mood Stones by Kylie Jenner, and Marc Jacobs makeup, which contributed approximately 2% of the growth in these Top 6 core brands.
•Coty extended the Marc Jacobs license, securing a 40-year license duration for the brand and building on a partnership that began in 2003. The agreement reinforces Coty’s position as a partner of choice for the world’s leading luxury fashion brands and increases the long-term visibility of its Prestige portfolio.
•Coty sharpened investment behind its four core Consumer Beauty brands, CoverGirl, Rimmel, Sally Hansen, and adidas. In the U.S., CoverGirl continued to narrow its category gap in both value and units, while Sally Hansen outperformed the category in units and regained its position as the #1 mass nail brand.
•Coty released its Fiscal Year 2026 (FY26) Sustainability Report under its Beauty That Lasts strategy on October 21, 2026. The report details advances across focus areas, including validation of Coty’s Net Zero 2050 target, achieving its water withdrawal reduction target, decreasing virgin plastic use, and investment in employees’ digital and leadership skills.
Pipeline for FY27 and Beyond:
Prestige Plans
•Building on the fully incremental launch of BOSS Bottled Beyond for Her to grow Hugo Boss’s female fragrance business, while focusing investment on the brand’s core franchises
•Amplifying Burberry’s fragrance and makeup momentum with higher marketing investment to deepen consumer engagement, while strengthening the brand’s presence in ultra-premium fragrance with the Prorsum collection’s launch at select retailers. Burberry’s Goddess Amber Vanilla fragrance extension is already a Top 5 female launch and performing ahead of expectations, supported by its distinctive packaging and ingredient profile
•Building Marc Jacobs Beauty into a multi-category brand with the successful launch of Marc Jacobs Beauty makeup online and in hundreds of Sephora stores across the U.S., U.K., Australia, and Canada with retail sales exceeding targets, while simultaneously driving a halo on the core fragrance business
•Fueling continued growth in Kylie Cosmetics’ makeup and fragrance sales through the strong incremental launch of the Mood Stones by Kylie Jenner fragrance collection and new lip kits, resulting in double-digit brand LFL sales growth in Q1
•Advancing additional launches and strengthening the base business across multiple brands including Calvin Klein, Chloé, and Davidoff, alongside the planned debut of Etro fragrances in the second half and Swarovski fragrances in CY27
3
Consumer Beauty Plans
•Focusing Color the Future on fewer, higher-impact launches, including CoverGirl's Clean Fresh Yummy Big Oil Stick, Rimmel's Oh My Gloss! Slip Stick, Max Factor's Lasting Blur, and seasonal shade stories from Sally Hansen's Miracle Gel and INSTA-DRI
•Implementing Future of Scenting to sharpen focus across the mass fragrance portfolio and drive more consistent performance, with continued scaling of adidas fragrances globally
Outlook
Consumer demand for beauty remains resilient, with more moderate but continued growth in fragrances and cosmetics. Coty continues to advance its Coty.Curated strategic framework, focusing on core brands and markets, reducing portfolio complexity, increasing agility through organizational simplification, and identifying savings opportunities across the P&L to support increased investment in consumer engagement while also strengthening profitability.
Coty expects 2Q27 LFL revenue to decline by a low-single-digit percentage, broadly consistent with the last two quarters. As part of this outlook, the Company anticipates continued LFL sales growth in its Top 6 core Prestige brands. On a reported basis, Coty expects a low-single-digit percentage headwind to Q2 revenues from foreign exchange (FX).
Coty anticipates Q2 adjusted EBITDA to decline by a high-single-digit percentage, consistent with the trends in Q1. This is expected to result in adjusted EPS, excluding the equity swap, of $0.16 to $0.18 per share.
Supported by the seasonally strong business dynamics in the first half, as well as continued disciplined capital expenditure and working capital management, Coty continues to expect free cash flow for the first half of FY27 of over $300 million.
FY27 is expected to be a transition year as Coty completes its strategic review and advances the early-stage implementation of Coty.Curated. Accordingly, at this stage Coty is providing guidance for Q2. Following the expected completion of the Company's strategic review by the end of CY26, Coty expects to provide a broader outlook supported by greater visibility into Coty's growth and profitability trajectory.

Non-GAAP Financial Measures
To supplement the financial measures prepared in accordance with GAAP, we use non-GAAP financial measures for Coty Inc. including Adjusted operating income (loss), Adjusted EBITDA, Adjusted net income (loss), and Adjusted net income (loss) attributable to Coty Inc. to common stockholders (collectively, the “Adjusted Performance Measures”). The reconciliations of these non-GAAP financial measures to the most directly comparable financial measures calculated and presented in accordance with GAAP are shown in tables below. These non-GAAP financial measures should not be considered in isolation from, or as a substitute for or superior to, financial measures reported in accordance with GAAP. Moreover, these non-GAAP financial measures have limitations in that they do not reflect all the items associated with the operations of the business as determined in accordance with GAAP. Other companies, including companies in the beauty industry, may calculate similarly titled non-GAAP financial measures differently than we do, limiting the usefulness of those measures for comparative purposes.
Despite the limitations of these non-GAAP financial measures, our management uses the Adjusted Performance Measures as key metrics in the evaluation of our performance and annual budgets and to benchmark performance of our business against our competitors. The following are examples of how these Adjusted Performance Measures are utilized by our management:
•strategic plans and annual budgets are prepared using the Adjusted Performance Measures;

•senior management receives a monthly analysis comparing budget to actual operating results that is prepared using the Adjusted Performance Measures; and
•senior management’s annual compensation is calculated, in part, by using some of the Adjusted Performance Measures.
In addition, our financial covenant compliance calculations under our debt agreements are substantially derived from these Adjusted Performance Measures.
Our management believes that Adjusted Performance Measures are useful to investors in their assessment of our operating performance and the valuation of the Company. In addition, these non-GAAP financial measures address questions we routinely receive from analysts and investors and, in order to ensure that all investors have access to the same data, our management has determined that it is appropriate to make this data available to all investors. The Adjusted Performance Measures exclude the impact of certain items (as further described below) and provide supplemental information regarding our operating performance. By disclosing these non-GAAP financial measures, our management intends to provide investors with a supplemental comparison of our operating results and trends for the periods presented. Our management believes these measures are also useful to investors as such measures allow investors to evaluate our performance using the same metrics that our management uses to evaluate past performance and prospects for future performance. We provide disclosure of the effects of these non-GAAP financial measures by presenting the corresponding measure prepared in conformity with GAAP in our financial statements, and by providing a reconciliation to the corresponding GAAP measure so that investors may understand the adjustments made in arriving at the non-GAAP financial measures and use the information to perform their own analyses.
Adjusted operating income/(loss) /Adjusted EBITDA excludes restructuring costs and business structure realignment programs, amortization, acquisition- and divestiture-related costs and acquisition accounting impacts, stock-based compensation, and asset impairment charges and other adjustments as described below. For adjusted EBITDA, in addition to the preceding, we exclude adjusted depreciation as defined below. We do not consider these items to be reflective of our core operating performance due to the variability of such items from period-to-period in terms of size, nature and significance. They are primarily incurred to realign our operating structure and integrate new acquisitions, and implement divestitures of components of our business, and fluctuate based on specific facts and circumstances. Additionally, Adjusted net income (loss) attributable to Coty Inc. and Adjusted net income (loss) attributable to Coty Inc. per common share are adjusted for certain interest and other (income) expense items, as described below, and the related tax effects of each of the items used to derive Adjusted net income (loss) as such charges are not used by our management in assessing our operating performance period-to-period.
Adjusted Performance Measures reflect adjustments based on the following items:
•Costs related to acquisition and divestiture activities: The Company has excluded acquisition- and divestiture-related costs and the accounting impacts such as those related to transaction costs and costs associated with the revaluation of acquired inventory in connection with business combinations because these costs are unique to each transaction. Additionally, for divestitures, the Company excludes write-offs of assets that are no longer recoverable and contract related costs due to the divestiture. The nature and amount of such costs vary significantly based on the size and timing of the acquisitions and divestitures, and the maturities of the businesses being acquired or divested. Also, the size, complexity and/or volume of past transactions, which often drives the magnitude of such expenses, may not be indicative of the size, complexity and/or volume of any future acquisitions or divestitures.
•Restructuring and other business realignment costs: The Company has excluded costs associated with restructuring and business structure realignment programs to allow for comparable financial results to historical operations and forward-looking guidance. In addition, the nature and amount of such charges vary significantly based on the size and timing of the programs. By excluding the referenced expenses from the non-GAAP financial measures, management is able to further evaluate the Company's ability to utilize existing assets and estimate their long-term value. Furthermore, our management believes that the adjustment of these items supplements the GAAP information with a measure that can be used to assess the sustainability of our operating performance.
•Asset impairment charges: The Company has excluded the impact of asset impairments as such non-cash amounts are inconsistent in amount and frequency and are significantly impacted by the timing and/or size of acquisitions. Our management believes that the adjustment of these items supplements the GAAP information with a measure that can be used to assess the sustainability of our operating performance.
•Amortization expense: The Company has excluded the impact of amortization of finite-lived intangible assets, as such non-cash amounts are inconsistent in amount and frequency and are significantly impacted

Our management believes that the adjustment of these items supplements the GAAP information with a measure that can be used to assess the sustainability of our operating performance. •Gain or loss on brand exit, sale and early license termination: The Company has excluded the impact of gain or loss on brand exit, sale and early license termination as such amounts are inconsistent in amount and frequency and are significantly impacted by the size of the brand exit, sale and early license termination.

Consequently, our management believes that the adjustment of these items supplements the GAAP information with a measure that can be used to assess the sustainability of our operating performance.
•Stock-based compensation: Although stock-based compensation is a key incentive offered to our employees, we have excluded the effect of these expenses from the calculation of adjusted operating income and adjusted EBITDA. This is due to their primarily non-cash nature; in addition, the amount and timing of these expenses may be highly variable and unpredictable, which may negatively affect comparability between periods.
•Depreciation and Adjusted depreciation: Our adjusted operating income (loss) excludes the impact of accelerated depreciation for certain restructuring projects that affect the expected useful lives of Property, Plant and Equipment, as such charges vary significantly based on the size and timing of the programs. Further, we have excluded adjusted depreciation, which represents depreciation expense net of accelerated depreciation charges, from our adjusted EBITDA. Our management believes that the adjustment of these items supplements the GAAP information with a measure that can be used to assess the sustainability of our operating performance.
•Other (income) expense: The Company has excluded the impact of pension curtailment (gains) and losses and pension settlements as such events are triggered by our restructuring and other business realignment activities and the amount of such charges vary significantly based on the size and timing of the programs. Further, we have excluded realized and unrealized gains and losses on the investment in Wella, as our management believes these unrealized gains and losses do not reflect our underlying ongoing business, and the adjustment of such impact helps investors and others compare and analyze performance from period to period. Such transactions do not reflect our operating results and we have excluded the impact as our management believes that the adjustment of these items supplements the GAAP information with a measure that can be used to assess the sustainability of our operating performance.
