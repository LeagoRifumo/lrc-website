CREDIT ACCEPTANCE ANNOUNCES THIRD QUARTER 2026 RESULTS
Southfield, Michigan – October 29, 2026 – Credit Acceptance Corporation (Nasdaq: CACC) (referred to as the “Company”, “Credit Acceptance”, “we”, “our”, or “us”) today announced a consolidated net loss of $56.6 million, or $5.43 per diluted share, for the three months ended September 30, 2026, compared to consolidated net income of $108.2 million, or $9.43 per diluted share, for the same period in 2025. Adjusted net income, a non-GAAP financial measure, for the three months ended September 30, 2026 was $135.5 million, or $13.01 per diluted share, compared to $117.9 million, or $10.28 per diluted share, for the same period in 2025.
Third Quarter 2026 Additional Financial Highlights
•Economic profit, a non-GAAP financial measure, was $50.7 million, or $4.87 per diluted share, compared to $43.0 million, or $3.75 per diluted share, in the third quarter of 2025.
•Consumer Loan assignment volume reached 110,010 units and $1.4 billion, up 37.7% and 51.0%, respectively, compared to the third quarter of 2025, while unit volume per active dealer increased by 15.2% compared to the third quarter of 2025.
•Forecasted net cash flows from our loan portfolio declined 2.5% ($291.2 million), compared to a decline of 0.5% ($58.6 million) in the third quarter of 2025, primarily reflecting the impact of an adjustment to our methodology for forecasting the amount of future net cash flows.
•Average balance of our loan portfolio increased to $8.1 billion, up 1.1% compared with the third quarter of 2025.
•Share repurchases totaled 269,150 shares (2.6% of the shares outstanding at the beginning of the quarter) for an aggregate cost of $161.6 million.
•Total liquidity stood at $1.2 billion as of September 30, 2026. Additionally, we completed a $600.0 million asset-backed financing, matching the largest asset-backed financing in our history and achieved our lowest credit spreads since late 2021.
“During the third quarter, Consumer Loan unit and dollar volume grew 38% and 51%, respectively, both economic profit and adjusted net income experienced double-digit growth, and the number of active dealers reached an all-time high,” said Vinayak Hegde, Chief Executive Officer. “One of the most encouraging aspects of this growth is that rather than relying on a single lever, our momentum is driven by our four-pronged strategy of customer obsession, segmentation, leveraging data and AI, and disciplined operating rhythms and practices. We believe execution of this strategy strengthens our forecasting, pricing, and operating processes and positions the Company to make more informed capital-allocation decisions.”
Third Quarter 2026 Company Highlights
•Enrolled 1,350 new dealers in our programs and had a record 12,093 active dealers during the quarter, reflecting continued engagement across our dealer network.
•$37.3 million in dealer holdback paid to dealers.
•Made continued progress executing our product roadmap, including the following initiatives, which contributed to increased volume growth:
•Scorecard update: Launched a new scorecard that uses refreshed and additional data attributes to enhance our risk-assessment capabilities by credit profile, dealer, vehicle, and deal structure.
•Segmentation: Reorganized our sales force to improve alignment by dealer type, origination channel, and geography, with targeted teams for independent dealers, franchise dealers, and national dealer groups.
•Reduced friction for dealers: Continued integration improvement with the aggregator platforms and enhanced the deal optimization tool, which reduced friction for dealers who use Dealertrack, RouteOne, and DealerCenter, and resulted in higher volume with franchise and large independent dealers.
•Settled claims related to a longstanding multi-state investigation and the New York state attorney general lawsuit without any admission of fault or wrongdoing by the Company and on terms not requiring the Company to record additional charges beyond amounts previously accrued and disclosed.
•Named one of PEOPLE magazine’s 100 Companies That Care® by Great Place to Work® and PEOPLE magazine, for the fifth consecutive year, with a #11 ranking.

Consumer Loan Metrics
Dealers assign retail installment contracts (referred to as “Consumer Loans”) to Credit Acceptance. At the time a Consumer Loan is submitted to us for assignment, we forecast future expected cash flows from the Consumer Loan. Based on the amount and timing of these forecasts and expected expense levels, an advance or one-time purchase payment is made to the related dealer at a price designed to maximize economic profit, a non-GAAP financial measure that considers our return on capital, our cost of capital, and the amount of capital invested.
We use a statistical model to estimate the expected collection rate for each Consumer Loan at the time of assignment. We continue to evaluate the expected collection rate for each Consumer Loan subsequent to assignment. Our evaluation becomes more accurate as the Consumer Loans age, as we use actual performance data in our forecast. By comparing our current expected collection rate for each Consumer Loan with the rate we projected at the time of assignment, we are able to assess the accuracy of our initial forecast. During the third quarter of 2026, based on our analysis of historical Consumer Loan performance data and market trends, we adjusted our methodology for forecasting future net cash flows from our loan portfolio. The adjustment reduced forecasted collection rates for Consumer Loans assigned to us in 2022 through 2026 to the levels we now expect for the life of these Consumer Loans. Prior to our making the adjustment in the third quarter of 2026, we had observed small but consistent quarterly underperformance of these Consumer Loans relative to forecasted cash flows and had made corresponding adjustments to our forecast each quarter. The underperformance of the Consumer Loans assigned to us in 2022 through 2026 had been most pronounced in the case of Consumer Loans in lower scorecard bands. Most of the impact of the forecast adjustment we made in the third quarter of 2026 is related to Consumer Loans assigned to us before the scorecard change we introduced in the second quarter of 2026.

Forecasting collection rates accurately at loan inception is difficult. With this in mind, we establish advance rates that are intended to allow us to achieve acceptable levels of profitability across our portfolio, even if collection rates are less than we initially forecast.

The risk of a material change in our forecasted collection rate declines as the Consumer Loans age. Because Consumer Loans assigned in 2022 and prior years represent less than 10% of total forecasted future net cash flows from Consumer Loans, changes in the forecasted collection rate for those loans would generally be expected to have a relatively modest impact on total forecasted future net cash flows. In contrast, Consumer Loans assigned since 2022 represent a larger portion of expected future net cash flows, and a significant portion of their total forecasted collections has not yet been realized. Accordingly, changes in the forecasted collection rate for those more recent loans would generally be expected to have a more significant impact on total forecasted future net cash flows.
The spread as of September 30, 2026 ranges from 11.8% to 24.2%, on an annual basis, for Consumer Loans assigned over the last 10 years. The spreads with respect to 2019 and 2020 Consumer Loans have been positively impacted by Consumer Loan performance, which has exceeded our initial estimates by a greater margin than the other years presented. The spreads with respect to Consumer Loans assigned to us in 2021 through 2026 have been negatively impacted by Consumer Loan performance, which has been lower than our initial estimates by a greater margin than the other years presented. The lower spread for 2026 Consumer Loans relative to 2025 Consumer Loans as of September 30, 2026 reflects the net impact of two offsetting factors. A lower initial spread on 2026 Consumer Loans, primarily due to a higher advance rate, decreased the spread from 2025 to 2026. The impact of the lower initial spread was partially offset by Consumer Loan performance, as the performance of 2025 Consumer Loans has been lower than our initial estimates by a greater margin than 2026 Consumer Loans.

•A decrease in salaries and wages expense of 11.5% ($9.8 million), primarily due to (i) a reduction in headcount, (ii) a decrease in fringe benefits, primarily due to lower medical claims and a lower number of team members participating in the plan, and (iii) a decrease in stock-based compensation expense, primarily due to the retirements of senior leaders in 2026. Floating Yield Adjustment
The net loan income (finance charge revenue less provision for credit losses expense) that we recognize over the life of a loan equals the cash we collect from the underlying Consumer Loan less the cash we pay to the dealer. We believe the economics of our business are best exhibited by recognizing loan revenue on a level-yield basis over the life of the loan based on expected future net cash flows. The purpose of this non-GAAP adjustment is to provide insight into our business by showing this level yield measure of income. Under GAAP, contractual amounts due in excess of the loan receivable balance at the time of assignment will be reflected as interest income, while contractual amounts due that are not expected to be collected are reflected in the provision for credit losses. Our non-GAAP floating yield adjustment recognizes the net effects of contractual interest income and expected credit losses in a single measure of finance charge revenue, consistent with how we manage our business. The floating yield adjustment recognizes revenue on a level-yield basis based upon expected future net cash flows, with any changes in expected future net cash flows, which are recognized immediately under GAAP as provision for credit losses, recognized over the remaining forecast period (up to 120 months after the origination date of the underlying Consumer Loans) for each individual dealer loan and purchased loan. The floating yield adjustment does not accelerate revenue recognition. Rather, it reduces revenue by taking amounts that are reported under GAAP as provision for credit losses and instead treating them as reductions of revenue over time.
Under the GAAP methodology we employ, which is known as the current expected credit loss model, or CECL, we are required to recognize:
•a significant provision for credit losses expense at the time of the loan’s assignment to us for contractual net cash flows we do not expect to realize; and
•finance charge revenue in subsequent periods that is significantly in excess of our expected yield.
Due to the GAAP treatment of contractual net cash flows we do not expect to realize at the time of loan assignment (i.e. significant expense at the time of loan assignment, which is offset by higher revenue in subsequent periods), we do not believe the GAAP methodology we employ provides sufficient transparency into the economics of our business, including our results of operations, financial condition, and financial leverage. Our floating yield adjustment enables us to provide measures of income that are not impacted by GAAP’s treatment of contractual net cash flows we do not expect to realize at the time of loan assignment. We believe the floating yield adjustment is presented in a manner which reflects both the economic reality of our business and how the business is managed and provides valuable supplemental information to help investors better understand our business, executive compensation, liquidity, and capital resources.
