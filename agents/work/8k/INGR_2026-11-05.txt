INGREDION INCORPORATED REPORTS THIRD QUARTER 2026 RESULTS
•T&HS delivered its 10th consecutive quarter of net sales volume growth, with share gains in strategic categories, including continued clean label growth globally
•Third quarter 2026 reported and adjusted* operating income both decreased 19% compared to the third quarter 2025
•Third quarter 2026 reported and adjusted diluted EPS were $2.23 and $2.28, compared with $2.61 and $2.75 in the third quarter 2025
•Adjusting full-year guidance for reported diluted EPS to be in the range of $8.04 to $8.49 and adjusted diluted EPS to be in the range of $9.25 to $9.70
WESTCHESTER, Ill., November 5, 2026 – Ingredion Incorporated (NYSE: INGR), a leading global provider of ingredient solutions to the food and beverage manufacturing industry, today reported its third quarter 2026 results.
“Our third quarter results fell short of our expectations, largely due to Argo’s slower than anticipated financial recovery, with incremental pressure from higher input costs and consumer softness,” said Jim Zallie, chairman, president and CEO of Ingredion. “While production at Argo remained stable for a second consecutive quarter and inventories are largely rebuilt, high manufacturing costs coupled with softer food and beverage demand impacted profitable volume growth. With plant reliability improved, our focus has shifted to sustainably lowering operating costs and improving volume growth. While we expect these actions to improve earnings, the recovery is taking longer than we expected.”
“For Texture and Healthful Solutions, net sales volume growth was a bright spot again this quarter. Volume grew for the tenth consecutive quarter, supported by continued strong demand for clean label globally as customers reformulate for simpler labels, affordability and nutrition. However, higher tapioca costs and manufacturing input cost inflation negatively impacted profit growth in the segment. In Food & Industrial Ingredients—LATAM, stronger volume from brewing and beverage customers in Mexico and Brazil was offset by foreign exchange impacts from a stronger Mexican peso and the impact to our Andean operations from the August earthquake in Colombia.”
“As a result, we are lowering our full-year outlook, primarily to reflect the slower earnings recovery in Food & Industrial Ingredients—U.S./CAN, along with the impacts of higher input costs and softer demand.”
“Looking ahead, we are taking action to improve financial performance through operating cost discipline and spend management, while we continue to invest in our specialty growth platforms. As we progress the integration planning for the pending acquisition of Tate & Lyle, we are building the foundation for an even stronger company with greater opportunities for growth, innovation and shareholder value creation in 2027 and beyond.”

Updated Full-Year 2026 Outlook
The Company now expects its full-year 2026 reported diluted EPS to be in the range of $8.04 to $8.49, and its adjusted diluted EPS to be in the range of $9.25 to $9.70.
The Company now expects full-year 2026 net sales to be flat, reflecting volume growth and favorable foreign exchange, offset by lower price mix as well as the impact of the previously mentioned sale of its majority stake in the Pakistan business. Excluding results of the Pakistan business, net sales is expected to be flat to up low single digits.
Reported operating income is expected to be down low double digits, with adjusted operating income now expected to be down mid-teens for full-year 2026, which reflects the operational challenges in F&II–U.S./CAN.
The 2026 full-year outlook further assumes the following: Texture & Healthful Solutions operating income is now expected to be flat to up low single-digits, driven by sales volume growth, offset by expected higher input cost inflation; Food & Industrial Ingredients–LATAM operating income is now anticipated to be down mid-to-high single-digits, reflecting the continued strength of the Mexican peso; Food & Industrial Ingredients–U.S./CAN operating income is now expected to be down 35-40%, driven by operating costs at Argo; and All Other’s operating loss is still anticipated to be approximately $(15) million, which reflects the absence of a second half earnings contribution from the sale of the Pakistan business.

About Ingredion
Ingredion Incorporated (NYSE: INGR), headquartered in the suburbs of Chicago, is a leading global ingredient solutions provider serving customers in more than 120 countries. With 2025 annual net sales of approximately $7.2 billion, the Company turns grains, fruits, vegetables and other plant-based materials into value-added ingredient solutions for the food, beverage, animal nutrition, brewing and industrial markets. With Ingredion Idea Labs® innovation centers located around the world and more than 11,000 employees, the Company co-creates with customers and fulfills its purpose of bringing the potential of people, nature and technology together to make life better. Visit ingredion.com for more information and the latest Company news.

II. Non-GAAP Information
To supplement the consolidated financial results prepared in accordance with U.S. generally accepted accounting principles (“GAAP”), non-GAAP historical financial measures are used, which exclude certain GAAP items such as acquisition/integration costs, restructuring costs, impairment charges, net (gain) on sale of business, Mexico tax item, and other specified items. The term “adjusted” is generally used when referring to these non-GAAP financial measures.
Management uses non-GAAP financial measures internally for strategic decision making, forecasting future results and evaluating current performance. By disclosing non-GAAP financial measures, management intends to provide investors with a more meaningful, consistent comparison of the Company’s operating results and trends for the periods presented. These non-GAAP financial measures are used in addition to and in conjunction with results presented in accordance with GAAP and reflect an additional way of viewing aspects of the Company’s operations that, when viewed with its GAAP results, provide a more complete understanding of factors and trends affecting its business. Expected financial measures may not reflect certain future charges, costs and/or gains that are inherently difficult to predict and estimate due to their unknown timing, effect and/or significance. Non-GAAP adjustments are generally made to adjusted financial measures, which increases management’s confidence in its ability to forecast adjusted financial measures than in its ability to forecast GAAP financial measures. These non-GAAP measures, including non-GAAP expected measures, should be considered as a supplement to, and not as a substitute for, or superior to, the corresponding measures calculated in accordance with GAAP.
Non-GAAP financial measures are not prepared in accordance with GAAP; therefore, the Company’s non-GAAP information is not necessarily comparable to similarly titled measures presented by other companies.
