Klarna Reports Third Quarter 2026 Results New York, November 17, 2026 — Klarna Group plc (NYSE: KLAR), the everyday finance network, today reported third quarter 2026 results. GMV reached $36.1 billion (+10% YoY), revenue $1.0 billion (+12% YoY), transaction margin dollars (TMD) $371 million (+32% YoY), and adjusted operating income $21 million (+$35m YoY). "Q3 was our fourth straight quarter with revenue above 1 billion dollars and positive adjusted operating income. We are keeping more of each dollar we process through our everyday finance network: transaction margin is now 37% of revenue, up from 31% a year ago. Looking ahead, we’re finishing the year strong as we are running on track with, or ahead of, all our full year outlook.” Sebastian Siemiatkowski, CEO & Co-Founder, Klarna Klarna’s full earnings release is available at the Quarterly Results section of its website at https:// investors.klarna.com/financials/quarterly-results/ Q3 2026 Financial Highlights ●​ GMV: $36.1 billion (+10% YoY; U.S. +24% YoY) ●​ Revenue: $1,009 million (+12% YoY) ●​ Transaction Margin Dollars: $371 million (+32% YoY) ●​ Adjusted operating income: $21 million (+$35m YoY) ●​ Net income: $(65) million, an improvement of $30m versus Q3 2025 ●​ Provisions for credit losses: 0.56% of GMV versus 0.72% of GMV in Q3 2025 ●​ Active consumers: 122 million (+7% YoY) ●​ Merchants: 1.35 million (+59% YoY) ●​ Earnings per share: $(0.19), an improvement of $0.06 versus Q3 2025 More consumers, deeper engagement, bigger purchases​ We added 8 million active consumers year over year to reach 122 million, and they are doing more with us: average revenue per active consumer rose 21% to $34.0. The Klarna Card volume doubled and subscription revenue grew 530% in Q3. Our charge-card equivalent, Pay Later, remains the entry point for consumers, who then increasingly move on to bigger purchases with Fair Financing. In the US Fair Financing grew 73% to $3.6 billion. They also use Klarna for a wider range of purchases: Events & Services rose to 14% of GMV from 9% and Home & Electronics to 22% from 21%, while Apparel & Accessories fell to 32% from 36%. More merchants, more categories, a stronger network​ Our merchant network is our main customer acquisition channel. Merchants rose 59% year over year to 1.35 million, driven by default-on integrations with the world’s largest payment service providers. In October, Clover, Fiserv’s small-business platform, went live at 100,000 U.S. in-store locations, and we extended the partnership to Fiserv’s Commerce Hub; in
Europe, we updated our agreement with Mollie, which serves 350,000 businesses. We are also expanding available payment methods with existing merchants. Fair Financing availability at merchants grew to 283,000 merchants, or 21%, up from 150,000 a year ago. Southwest Airlines and J.Crew went live in the U.S., and Marks & Spencer’s launch means every major U.K. department store now offers Klarna. Each merchant we add gives consumers more reasons to use Klarna, and each consumer we add makes Klarna more valuable to merchants. Lower costs, wider margins, more from every dollar​ Our economics improve as the network grows, and we manage Klarna to optimize transaction margin dollars (TMD), the revenue we retain after processing, credit and funding costs. TMD grew 32% to $371 million, much faster than revenue, as transaction costs grew just 3%, even as GMV grew 10%. Provisions for credit losses improved to 0.56% of GMV, from 0.72% a year ago, reflecting continued improvements in underwriting and a larger share of loans offloaded. TMD margin is now 36.8% of revenue, up 5.7 percentage points year over year. The U.S. is the biggest driver, with TMD margin rising from 6% to 18% over the past 12 months, following the path of our mature markets, where TMD margin sits close to 60%. For the fourth quarter, this implies GMV of $42.6 – $44.6 billion, revenue of $1,017 – $1,097 million, TMD of $414 – $444 million and adjusted operating income of $100 – $120 million. Our capital-light approach As a bank, Klarna benefits from a scalable funding base that supports continued growth. Non-IFRS measures and reconciliations We use certain non-IFRS financial measures to supplement our consolidated financial statements, which are presented in accordance with IFRS. These non-IFRS financial measures include TMD, Transaction margin, Adjusted operating income (loss), Adjusted operating expenses and Adjusted operating margin. We use these non-IFRS financial measures to facilitate the review of our operational performance and as a basis for strategic planning. We also present period-over-period changes in certain metrics on like-for-like (LfL) basis, which is calculated by adjusting the metric for the impact of foreign currency fluctuations. The impact of foreign currency fluctuations is calculated by translating the reported amounts in the current period using the exchange rates in use during the comparative prior period. TMD and Transaction margin are key performance measures used by our management to measure our ability to attain efficiency and scale and to grow these metrics over time. They measure our success in growing revenue while effectively managing our processing and servicing costs, provision for credit losses and funding costs. In addition, by excluding certain items that are nonrecurring or not reflective of the performance of our normal course of business, we believe that Adjusted operating expenses, Adjusted operating income (loss) and Adjusted operating margin provide meaningful supplemental information regarding our performance. These non-IFRS measures should be considered in addition to, not as a substitute for or in isolation from, our financial results prepared in accordance with IFRS. Other companies, including companies in our industry, may calculate these non-IFRS (or similar non-GAAP) financial measures differently or not at all, which reduces their usefulness as comparative measures. TMD is defined as total revenue less total transaction costs, consisting of processing and servicing, provision for credit losses and funding costs. Transaction margin is calculated by dividing TMD by our total revenue. Adjusted operating income (loss) is defined as operating profit (loss) excluding (i) depreciation, amortization and impairments, (ii) share-based payments expense, (iii) restructuring costs and expenses related to preparation for an initial public offering. Adjusted operating expenses are defined as operating expenses excluding (i) depreciation, amortization and impairments, (ii) share- based payments expense and (iii) restructuring costs and expenses related to preparation for an initial public offering. Adjusted operating margin is defined as Adjusted operating income (loss) divided by our total revenue. Depreciation, amortization and impairments below include amounts recorded within Technology and product development expenses in our consolidated statements of profit and loss.
