RAPID CITY, S.D. — Nov. 4, 2026 — Black Hills Corp. (NYSE: BKH) today announced financial results for the third quarter ended Sept. 30, 2026. Net income available for common stock and earnings per share, diluted (EPS) for the three and nine months ended Sept. 30, 2026, compared to the three and nine months ended Sept. 30, 2025, were:

Third-quarter GAAP EPS was $0.54 compared to $0.34 in the same period in 2025. Third-quarter adjusted EPS was $0.57, compared to $0.45 in the same period in 2025. Adjusted EPS financial results benefited from $0.15 per share of new rates and rider recovery and $0.06 per share of weather favorability, which more than offset higher operating expenses and financing and depreciation costs driven by capital investment and new assets in service.
Year-to-date GAAP EPS was $2.76 compared to $2.58 in the same period in 2025. Year-to-date adjusted EPS was $2.89, compared to $2.68 in the same period in 2025. Adjusted EPS financial results benefited from $0.63 per share of new rates and rider recovery and customer growth, which more than offset the unfavorable impacts of $0.12 per share from mild weather, and financing and depreciation costs driven by capital investment and new assets in service.
“We reported excellent third-quarter results as our team achieved significant progress on our strategic initiatives,” said Linn Evans, president and CEO of Black Hills Corp. “We delivered strong earnings and maintained our solid investment-grade credit ratings.
“We are excited to have reached agreements to serve Google's planned data center in Wyoming, as previously announced. We expect earnings to begin mid-2027 and increase to approximately $150 million of net income in 2030, consisting of returns on investment of $1.8 billion in 564 MW of new generation, and microgrid management fees based on contracted customer load. Additionally, we have requested Certificates of Public Convenience and Necessity (CPCNs) for $686 million of electric transmission expansion investments in Wyoming to serve our large-load opportunities. In total, additional investment of $2.5 billion in generation and transmission investment to serve large-load data center demand in Wyoming represents significant upside to our current plan.
“Our 99-MW Lange II generation facility is on track to be in service in the fourth quarter, with construction in the final stages in South Dakota to replace aging infrastructure and support updated reserve margins. We continued to make regulatory progress during the quarter, obtaining approval for new rates for Arkansas Gas and South Dakota Electric.
“Given strong execution by our team in serving our customers and advancing our regulatory and growth initiatives, we expect to deliver in the upper half of our 4% to 6% long-term EPS growth target supported by our current plan for 2026 to 2030. With significant upside to
serve large-load growth, we look forward to providing a comprehensive update for 2027 to 2031 as part of our year-end earnings release,” concluded Evans.
Merger with NorthWestern Energy Group, Inc.
•
On Aug. 19, 2025, Black Hills Corp. and NorthWestern Energy announced a tax-free, all-stock merger. The transaction is expected to close by year-end 2026, subject to receipt of a satisfactory final regulatory approval from the Montana Public Service Commission. All other remaining approvals and conditions for closing have been received or satisfied, including approvals by shareholders of both companies, the completion of the waiting period on the Hart-Scott-Rodino Act, and approvals by the Federal Energy Regulatory Commission and regulatory commissions in Nebraska and South Dakota.
THIRD-QUARTER 2026 HIGHLIGHTS AND RECENT UPDATES
Electric Utilities
•
On Nov. 4, South Dakota Electric received approval from the South Dakota Public Utilities Commission (SDPUC) for a settlement of its rate review request filed Feb. 19, 2026. The black-box settlement provides $27.4 million in new annual revenue based on a weighted average cost of capital of 7.58%. The settlement also includes a four-year moratorium period, in which South Dakota Electric will not increase base rates, absent an extraordinary event, until August 2030. The moratorium excludes South Dakota Electric’s additional regulatory mechanisms. New rates will be effective in December 2026, and will replace interim rates which were effective in August 2026.
•
On Oct. 7, South Dakota Electric received approval from the Wyoming Public Service Commission (WPSC) for a settlement of its rate review request filed March 18, 2026. The settlement provides $4.0 million in new annual revenue based on a capital structure of 53% equity and 47% debt and a return on equity of 9.60%, with new rates effective Dec. 1, 2026.
•
On Sept. 30, Black Hills signed definitive agreements with terms through 2048 with Google and other third-party partners to serve a planned data center project to be constructed in Cheyenne, Wyoming. The project is contracted to be served through 2.7 GW of resources, which includes reserve margins for project load. The company plans to invest $1.8 billion in 564 MW of new company-owned generation and manage the output of 2.1 GW of third-party contracted resources. Under the company’s flexible large-load tariff in Wyoming, return on investment and management fees on contracted resources are expected to add $150 million of net income in 2030, with earnings beginning in mid- and late-2027, respectively. The project is forecasted to generate unlevered free cash flow of approximately $2.4 billion, after netting out $1.8 billion of investment, during the contract term through 2048.
•
In 2026 to date, Wyoming Electric requested approval from the WPSC for a new large-load transmission recovery tariff and CPCNs for two transmission expansion projects with total investment of $686 million to serve the growing energy needs of Wyoming Electric's large-load customers.
o
On June 19, Wyoming Electric filed a request with the WPSC to establish a new Large Customer Transmission Cost Adjustment Mechanism (LCTCAM) tariff. The tariff provides a framework to directly recover transmission investment costs from LPCS customers who are served by, and benefit from, the transmission facilities. The LCTCAM is designed to ensure that customers not directly served by those facilities are protected from bearing those costs. The company expects a decision by the WPSC in the first quarter of 2027.
o
On Sept. 1, Wyoming Electric filed a request with the WPSC for a CPCN to expand infrastructure in Cheyenne, Wyoming. The $80 million project, named the South Cheyenne Transmission Project, includes new substations and nearly 15 miles of transmission lines and upgrades to be in service in multiple phases in late 2027 through 2028. Recovery is expected to be requested under the LCTCAM, which is currently pending with the WPSC. The company expects a decision by the WPSC in mid-2027.
o
On Nov. 2, Wyoming Electric filed a request with the WPSC for a CPCN to expand transmission capacity across the Cheyenne region and key interconnection points. The $606 million project, named the Aeolus Transmission Project, includes two new 230-kilovolt transmission lines, with upgrade capabilities to 345 kilovolts, spanning approximately 125 miles. Pending approval by the WPSC, completion of the project is expected by mid-2029. Recovery is expected to be requested under the LCTCAM, which is currently pending with the WPSC. The company expects a decision by the WPSC in mid-2027.
•
During the third quarter, South Dakota Electric continued construction of its Lange II project which is anticipated to be in service during the fourth quarter of 2026. The addition of these resources will replace generation facilities planned for retirement and support updated planning reserve margin requirements. On Aug. 4, South Dakota Electric filed a request with the South Dakota Public Utilities Commission (SDPUC) for recovery of costs related to the 99 MW, $320 million gas-fired generation project through a rider mechanism.
•
Year to date, Wyoming Electric recorded four new all-time customer load peaks driven primarily by growth in large-load data center demand. The new peaks advance a track record of 20 consecutive years of increasing electric demand in the Cheyenne, Wyoming region. The most recent peak of 439 MW on July 20, 2026, represents an increase of 16% over the 2025 peak of 379 MW on June 20, 2025.
2
•
On Sept. 11, Colorado Electric filed its 2026 Clean Energy Plan with the Colorado Public Utilities Commission (CPUC). The updated Electric Resource Plan builds on the company’s 2022 Clean Energy Plan. The plan includes a 25-year planning horizon, and reliably achieves Colorado’s requirement to reduce greenhouse gas emissions by 80% by 2030, based on 2005 levels. To meet these objectives, the plan proposes the addition of 100 MW of wind energy, 200 MW of battery energy storage systems, and 125 MW of firm, dispatchable natural gas-fired generation.
•
In July, Wyoming Electric and South Dakota Electric completed regulatory requirements for wildfire liability protections outlined in legislation enacted in 2025 and early 2026, respectively. On July 9, 2026, Wyoming Electric received approval from the WPSC of the company’s Wildfire Mitigation Plan. On July 2, 2026, South Dakota Electric submitted the company’s plan to the SDPUC and was posted by the commission, aligning the company’s wildfire mitigation plans with the legislation requirements.
•
On June 30, Wyoming Electric submitted its 2026 Integrated Resource Plan to the WPSC based on a 20-year planning period, including a near-term period through 2033, which identified a near-term capacity shortfall of 95 MW beginning in 2027. Based on its forecasts and analysis, Wyoming Electric recommends the addition of 36 MW of new natural gas-fired reciprocating internal combustion engines (RICE), 50 MW of battery storage, and energy market purchases to meet the identified resource need. Wyoming Electric's IRP does not address Large Power Contract Service (LPCS) tariff capacity needs, which are handled separately under customer-specific agreements.
•
On June 12, Colorado Electric filed a rate review request with the CPUC seeking approval to recover approximately $184 million of critical investments since its last rate review in 2024. The rate review requested $26.7 million of new annual revenue based on a capital structure of 51% equity and 49% debt and a return on equity of 10.5%. The company is seeking new rates in the first quarter of 2027.
Gas Utilities
•
On Oct. 12, Arkansas Gas received final approval from the Arkansas Public Service Commission of new rates to recover approximately $147 million of system investments for its natural gas pipeline infrastructure since its last general rate filing in 2023. The new rates provide $17.9 million of new annual revenue based on a capital structure of 47.5% equity and 52.5% debt and a return on equity of 9.8%. New rates were effective Oct. 12, 2026.
Corporate and Other
•
On Oct. 28, Black Hills’ board of directors approved a quarterly dividend of $0.703 per share payable on Dec. 1, 2026, to common shareholders of record at the close of business on Nov. 17, 2026. On an annualized basis, the dividend represents 56 consecutive years of increases, the second-longest track record in the electric and natural gas industry.
•
Year to date, the company issued a total of 1.0 million shares of new common stock under its at-the-market equity offering program for net proceeds of $69 million.
2026 ADJUSTED EARNINGS GUIDANCE REAFFIRMED
Black Hills reaffirms its guidance for 2026 adjusted EPS* to be in the range of $4.25 to $4.45, based on the following assumptions:
•
Normal weather conditions within our utility service territories;
•
Constructive and timely outcomes of utility regulatory dockets;
•
Excludes merger-related costs;
•
Excludes mark-to-market adjustments;
•
Increase in operations and maintenance expense (excludes merger-related costs, depreciation and amortization, and taxes other than income taxes) of approximately 3.5% off 2025 of $580 million;
•
Equity issuance between $50 million and $70 million; and
•
An effective tax rate of approximately 14% for the full year.
This guidance excludes the expected merger with NorthWestern Energy, which is expected to close by year-end 2026.
* The 2026 Adjusted EPS guidance shown above is a forward-looking, non-GAAP financial measure. The company is not able to provide comparable GAAP EPS guidance due to items that are not considered representative of the company's underlying operating performance that cannot be reasonably quantified for the full-year period. These items include merger-related costs the company expects to incur in 2026, in addition to any other unplanned items that may affect GAAP results in 2026.
