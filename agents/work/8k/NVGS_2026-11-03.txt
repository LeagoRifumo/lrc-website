Other Highlights and Developments
Fleet Operational Update
The average daily time charter equivalent (“TCE”) rate across the fleet was $30,314 for the three months ended September 30, 2026, compared to $30,966 for the three months ended September 30, 2025, and $33,946 for the three months ended June 30, 2026.
Utilization across the fleet was 87.9% for the three months ended September 30, 2026, compared to 89.3% for the three months ended September 30, 2025, and 90.8% for the three months ended June 30, 2026.
We continue to monitor the ongoing geopolitical situation in the Middle East. During the three months ended September 30, 2026, none of our vessels operated in, or transited through, the Arabian Gulf or the Strait of Hormuz, and we have not experienced any significant operational impact on our vessels as a result.
Customer buying patterns shifted during the third quarter of 2026. In the second quarter, customers had increased precautionary purchases to secure cargoes from North America and other regions amid disruption to Middle East supply. Early in the third quarter however, reports of potential diplomatic progress between the United States and Iran led many customers to defer cargo purchases and adopt a wait-and-see approach through July and August 2026. This temporary pause in buying impacted demand for handysize tonnage, and utilization eased to an average of around 85% during those two months.
As it became clear that conditions in the Strait of Hormuz had not materially improved, customers resumed purchasing, and demand for our vessels strengthened. Utilization recovered to 94% in September 2026, and remained firm into October 2026.
The same uncertainty continued to dampen customers’ willingness to commit to longer-term charter arrangements during the three months ended September 30, 2026. Charterers instead favored shorter, spot-oriented employment.
The ongoing disruption to transits through the Strait of Hormuz in the third quarter of 2026 constrained the availability of hydrocarbon products from the Middle East and supported demand for North American exports. Replacing Middle East volumes with North American supply increased ton-mile demand as longer voyages to end-markets require more vessels to transport the same quantities. El Niño-related low rainfall provided further support by prompting the Panama Canal Authority to cut daily booking slots twice during the third quarter; first to 34 and then to 32 transits. These reductions increased waiting times for canal transits and led many vessels traveling from North America to take the longer route to Asia via the Cape of Good Hope. Firmer demand for very large gas carriers ("VLGCs") and midsize vessels also provided support to handysize LPG.
As of September 30, 2026, we had 27 vessels engaged under time charters, 19 vessels on spot voyage charters and contracts of affreightment ("COAs"), and two vessels operating in the independently managed Unigas Pool. For the 12-month period commencing October 1, 2026, approximately 34% of our available days are covered by time charter contracts. For the same forward-looking 12-month period, our midsize vessels are exclusively on time charters, approximately 59% of our fully refrigerated vessels and 30% of our semi-refrigerated vessels are on time charters, whereas 93% of our ethylene-capable handysize vessels are expected to be employed in the spot voyage market.
The handysize 12‑month forward‑looking market assessment for semi‑refrigerated vessels increased by $20,000 per calendar month (“pcm”) from the end of the second quarter of 2026, to $995,000 pcm at the end of the third quarter of 2026.
The handysize 12‑month forward‑looking market assessment for fully refrigerated vessels was $850,000 pcm at the end of the second quarter of 2026 and remained unchanged at the end of the third quarter of 2026.
The handysize 12-month forward-looking market assessment for ethylene-capable vessels decreased by $100,000 pcm from the end of the second quarter of 2026, to $1,000,000 pcm at the end of the third quarter of 2026.
Ethylene Export Terminal
We own a 50% share in an ethylene export marine terminal at Morgan’s Point, Texas (the “Ethylene Export Terminal”) through a joint venture (the "Export Terminal Joint Venture") with Enterprise Products Partners. The Ethylene Export Terminal includes an ethylene cryogenic storage tank with a capacity of 30,000 metric tons ("mts"), and has a nameplate capacity to export at least 1.55 million mts of ethylene per year and load ethylene-capable gas carriers at rates of 1,000 mts per hour.
Our share of the results of our equity investment in the Ethylene Export Terminal was a gain of $2.2 million for the three months ended September 30, 2026, compared to a gain of $3.3 million for the three months ended September 30, 2025, and a gain of $7.1 million for the three months ended June 30, 2026. The year-on-year decrease is primarily due to lower average realized rates on spot volumes achieved in the third quarter of 2026 compared to the third quarter of 2025.
The Ethylene Export Terminal throughput for the three months ended September 30, 2026, was 264,398 mts, compared to 270,502 mts for the three months ended September 30, 2025, and 374,278 mts for the three months ended June 30, 2026. Throughput for the third quarter of 2026 was lower than the first and second quarters of 2026 as international end users reduced inventories built during the second quarter of 2026. Throughput was also constrained because the Ethylene Export Terminal cannot operate above nameplate capacity for extended periods, particularly during periods of elevated summer temperatures.

The gain of $7.1 million and 374,278 mts of throughput seen in the second quarter of 2026 was the result of a wide international price arbitrage driven by the strong demand for U.S. ethylene in both Europe and Asia.
Since January 2026, four new customers have signed offtake contracts for available ethylene volumes from our Ethylene Export Terminal, and we continue to expect that additional capacity will be contracted during the remainder of 2026 and into 2027. Ongoing geopolitical uncertainties continue to affect customers’ willingness to commit to long-term contracts and, until further offtake contracts are signed, available volumes will be sold on a spot contract basis.

Legal Updates
In February 2025, as part of an investigation into allegations of corruption, Muhamad Kerry Adrianto and certain other business partners and executives of PT Pertamina (Persero), Indonesia's state-owned energy company (“Pertamina”), were arrested by Indonesian authorities. The allegations related to the mismanagement of crude oil and oil refinery products at Pertamina between 2018 and 2023. The legal proceedings linked with the investigation by local authorities related to nine individuals and concluded in February 2026, with all nine defendants being found guilty. Mr. Adrianto was given a custodial sentence of 15 years, a fine of around $60,000 and was ordered to pay compensation of approximately $173 million.
On March 5, 2026, Mr. Adrianto lodged an appeal to his sentence with the High Court in Jakarta, Indonesia. On June 10, 2026, the High Court issued its appeal judgment, materially rejecting Mr. Adrianto's appeal, with his fine being reduced from around $60,000 to approximately $30,000 and the compensation order being increased from approximately $173 million to around $840 million. On June 22, 2026, Mr. Adrianto filed an appeal against the High Court's appeal decision to the Indonesian Supreme Court. The Company understands that the Supreme Court rejected the appeal on October 14, 2026, however, the judgment has not yet been received and the Company continues to monitor developments.
We are not aware of any link or connection between the Company or PTNK, our Indonesian joint venture, and the investigation or its findings other than through Mr. Adrianto, who served as a director of PTNK until September 2025, when he was replaced as a director of PTNK.
Following the above, we continue to believe that the events surrounding Mr. Adrianto will not have a material impact on the Company or our operations.

** Non-GAAP Financial Measure - Time charter equivalent ("TCE") - TCE is a measure of the average daily revenue performance of a vessel. TCE is not calculated in accordance with U.S. GAAP. For all charters, we calculate TCE by dividing total operating revenues (excluding revenues from the Unigas Pool), less any voyage expenses, by the number of earning days for the relevant period. Under a time charter, the charterer pays substantially all of the vessel's voyage-related expenses, whereas for voyage charters, also known as spot market charters, we pay all voyage expenses and charge our customers for these costs through our sales invoicing. TCE is a shipping industry performance measure used primarily to compare period-to-period changes in a company’s performance despite changes in the mix of charter types (i.e., voyage charters, time charters and contracts of affreightment) under which vessels may be employed. We include average daily TCE as we believe it provides additional meaningful information. Our calculation of TCE may not be comparable to that reported by other companies.
