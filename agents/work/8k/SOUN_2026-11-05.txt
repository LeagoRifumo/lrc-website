SoundHound AI Reports Record Q3 Revenue and
Guides to Sustained Adjusted EBITDA Profitability within 2H 2027
SANTA CLARA, Calif.--SoundHound AI, Inc. (Nasdaq: SOUN), a global leader in voice and agentic AI, today reported its financial results for the third quarter 2026.
"This is a milestone moment for SoundHound. We announced another record quarter, closed our acquisition of LivePerson, and guided that the company will be adjusted EBITDA profitable within the second half of 2027,” said Keyvan Mohajer, CEO and Co-Founder of SoundHound AI. “Our OASYS platform now leads its category and is positioned to become the operating system of the new agentic era, strengthened by technology from six pioneering businesses we have combined under SoundHound AI.”
Financial Highlights
•Third quarter reported revenue was $62.4 million, an increase of 48.5% year-over-year.
•Third quarter GAAP gross margin was 34.1%; non-GAAP gross margin was 50.3%.
•Third quarter GAAP net loss was $(46.4) million; non-GAAP net loss was $(21.2) million.
•Third quarter adjusted EBITDA was a loss of $(20.7) million.
•Third quarter GAAP earnings per share was a loss of $(0.10); non-GAAP earnings per share was a loss of $(0.05).
"Since closing the LivePerson acquisition, we have moved swiftly and decisively to integrate the businesses and realize substantial cost and revenue synergies. At the same time, we are improving our quality of earnings by shifting upfront license deals to hosted structures and exiting non-core, dilutive business lines, making our revenue more stable and predictable,” said John Collins, CFO of SoundHound AI. “These business transformations provide the operating leverage necessary to reach sustainable, profitable growth within the second half of 2027."
Business Highlights
LivePerson Update
•Closed the acquisition of LivePerson on September 4th, retiring all but a negligible portion of the debt, which will be repaid at maturity in the fourth quarter.
•Coordinated outreach to LivePerson's top 90 enterprise accounts representing over $100 million in ARR, receiving overwhelmingly positive feedback regarding financial stability and the addition of enterprise-grade voice and agentic AI capabilities.
•Renewals and upsells included a major US healthcare benefits provider, a leading European broadcaster and telecommunications provider, and a leading UK water utility.
Enterprise and Customer Care
•A leading academic health system selected SoundHound across both its hospital services and its health plan.
•Randox, a health testing provider serving UK and global populations, selected SoundHound for customer care.
•A major US electric utility signed a new contract to bring AI to its back-office functions.
•A British multinational telecommunications company added additional services.
Auto, Devices, and Voice Commerce
•Signed a 7-figure minimum-guarantee agreement with a tier one Chinese automotive supplier for voice AI and edge capabilities.
•Signed a new deal with Greaves Electric, a leading electric two-wheeler maker, for an embedded voice assistant.
•Launched OASYS Edge, an on-device architecture bringing LLM-powered voice AI agents directly onto local device hardware in vehicles, TVs, and smart devices.
•Signed a deal with a global electronics manufacturer LG to debut a new commerce channel across approximately 20 million smart TVs in the US.
•Signed direct voice commerce partnerships with a large coffeehouse brand and a growing fast-casual restaurant chain.
Consumer Brands, Hospitality and Physical Operations
•A multinational food and beverage company renewed its enterprise HR deployment.
•Resorts World Las Vegas, a global resort operator, expanded from an on-property digital concierge to an external-facing autonomous booking agent.
•A household-name pizza chain added SoundHound AI ordering to nearly 100 additional restaurant locations.
•Won a major global consumer products company as a new end customer through our partnership with Alta Resources, a business process outsourcing and customer management partner.

Business Outlook
•SoundHound has identified significant annualized cost synergies of $120 million, with over $80 million in actions scheduled to take effect before January 1, 2027, and the full $120 million run-rate expected in 2027.
•The Company has been strategically non-renewing and exiting certain negative gross margin contracts inherited from prior acquisitions and expects to continue doing so, with a positive impact on both gross margin and EBITDA.
•SoundHound will use the self-learning feature of OASYS to prioritize a hosted-service model and reduce upfront licensing, which has historically resulted in fluctuating point-in-time revenue contributions in prior quarters. The expected impact is more predictable revenue and long-term, sustainable ARR growth.
•With the impact of the actions above, SoundHound expects the 2026 revenue to be in the range of $250M - $260M, and 2027 revenue in the range of $350M - $400M.
•SoundHound expects to reach sustained adjusted EBITDA positive within the second half of 2027, even at the lower-end of its revenue guidance.

About SoundHound AI
SoundHound AI is a voice and agentic AI company that enables businesses to deliver natural, end-to-end conversational experiences across digital and physical channels, including phones, kiosks, chat, smart devices, drive-thrus, TVs, in-vehicle, and more. Its agentic platform, OASYS, is a self-learning, orchestrated AI system where organizations can build and deploy conversational AI agents to handle transactions, tasks, and workflows on behalf of customers and employees. Built on proprietary technology backed by 800+ patents and years of AI research, SoundHound serves leading brands across industries including automotive, financial services, healthcare, retail, telecommunications, and more. It powers millions of products and processes billions of interactions annually for enterprise customers worldwide. For more information, visit: www.soundhound.com

Non-GAAP Measures of Financial Performance
To supplement the company’s financial statements, which are presented on the basis of U.S. generally accepted accounting principles (GAAP), the following non-GAAP measures of financial performance are included in this release: non-GAAP gross profit, non-GAAP gross margin, adjusted EBITDA, non-GAAP net loss and non-GAAP earnings per share.
The company believes that providing this non-GAAP information in addition to the GAAP financial information allows investors to view the financial results in the way the company views its operating results. The company also believes that providing this information allows investors to not only better understand the company's financial performance, but also, better evaluate the information used by management to evaluate and measure such performance.
As such, the company believes that disclosing non-GAAP financial measures to the readers of its financial statements provides the reader with useful supplemental information that allows for greater transparency in the review of the company’s financial and operational performance.
The company defines its non-GAAP measures by excluding certain items:
The company arrives at non-GAAP gross profit and non-GAAP gross margin by excluding (i) amortization of intangibles (including acquired intangible assets), (ii) stock-based compensation and related payroll taxes, and (iii) acquisition-related costs
The company arrives at adjusted EBITDA by excluding (i) total other income/(expense), net, (ii) income taxes, (iii) depreciation and amortization expense (including acquired intangible assets), (iv) amortization of capitalized commissions, (v) stock-based compensation and related payroll taxes, (vi) change in fair value of contingent acquisition liabilities, (vii) litigation settlement expenses and (viii) acquisition-related costs.
The company arrives at non-GAAP net loss and non-GAAP net loss per share by excluding (i) depreciation and amortization expense (including acquired intangible assets), (ii) amortization of capitalized commissions, (iii) stock-based compensation and related payroll taxes, (iv) change in fair value of contingent acquisition liabilities, (v) change in fair value of derivative, (vi) litigation settlement expenses, (vii) acquisition-related costs and (viii) income tax effects related to acquisitions.
Reconciliations of GAAP to these adjusted non-GAAP financial measures are included in the tables below. When analyzing the company's operating results, investors should not consider non-GAAP measures as substitutes for the comparable financial measures prepared in accordance with GAAP.
To the extent that the company presents any forward-looking non-GAAP financial measures, the company does not present a quantitative reconciliation of such measures to the most directly comparable GAAP financial measure (or otherwise present such forward-looking GAAP measures) because it is impractical to do so.
