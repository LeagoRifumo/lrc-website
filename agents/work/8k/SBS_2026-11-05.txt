distributed 16,282,170 16,603,880 16,678,077 16,597,024Earnings Release 3Q26 November 5, 2026 • 3Q26 core EPS¹,² of R$0.31 versus R$0.38 in 3Q25 • R$4.5 bn Capex in 3Q26, +13.8% y/y CEO Message In the third quarter of 2026, Sabesp continued to advance the priorities shaping the Company’s future. Our first priority is universalization, delivered safely, followed by the continued improvement of our customers’ experience. We continued to make progress on universalization, investing R$4.5 billion in the quarter and approximately R$12 billion in the first nine months of 2026. As of September, we had achieved 118%³ of the 2024-2026 U-Factor target for new water units, 103%4 for new sewage units and 89% for units connected to sewage treatment. The benefits of these investments are already visible: in the São Paulo Metropolitan Region, the average annual organic load discharged into the Tietê River has declined by approximately 30% in comparison to 2025. We are pursuing this agenda without compromising safety: our continued focus on strengthening safety standards and field oversight is delivering results, with the Lost-Time Injury Frequency Rate declining by 50% among our employees and 62% among contractors year-over-year. Customer experience, also continued to improve as our Commercial Plan advanced. Since March 2026, complaints through Ombudsman and other critical channels have declined by 51%, while average waiting time at customer service agencies has fallen by 70%, reinforcing the progress we are making in building a more customer-centric Company. We remain focused on executing our transformation with discipline: expanding access to essential water and sanitation services safely, improving our customers’ experience and the quality of our operations, and creating sustainable long-term value. Consolidated Results Sabesp’s operating performance in the quarter reflected the expansion of its customer base and progress on its universalization agenda. Adjusted Net Revenue² increased 9.0% year-over-year, driven by tariff adjustments and higher volumes, supported by new units added through universalization. This was partially offset by rainier weather conditions, which affected consumption patterns and resulted in an unfavorable customer category and consumption-band mix. Adjusted EBITDA² increased 13.8% year-over-year, despite the execution of the Commercial Plan, which had a R$179 million impact in the quarter. The initiatives implemented under the plan have contributed to lower complaints and shorter waiting times at customer service agencies. Adjusted Net Income² decreased 13.8% year-over-year, mainly reflecting a weaker net financial result, as 3Q25 benefited from a non-recurring R$1.5 billion gain from court-ordered payments (precatórios), while interest expenses increased on a larger debt balance associated with the financing of our accelerated investment program. CapEx reached R$4.5 billion in 3Q26, up 13.8% year-over-year, bringing investments in the first nine months of the year to approximately R$12 billion, 15% higher year-over-year. The contracted CapEx backlog reached R$44.2 billion, with the sequential increase driven by the contracting of Universalization Waves 2 and 3, while Wave 4, covering the São Paulo Metropolitan Area, remains in bidding. Additionally, the accounting policies set out below relate to new transactions that did not exist in the year ended December 31, 2025. 3.1 Business combination A business combination occurs when the Company obtains control of another entity or operation. Such transactions are accounted for using the acquisition method whereby the identifiable assets acquired and liabilities assumed are recognized at their respective fair values as of the acquisition date. The difference between the consideration transferred and the fair value of the net assets acquired may result in the recognition of goodwill or a gain on a bargain purchase. Acquisition related costs are recognized as an expense as incurred. To determine whether an acquisition constitutes a business, the Company assesses whether the acquired set includes inputs and processes capable of generating goods or services, revenue, or other economic benefits. In other words, the acquisition of standalone assets is not sufficient; the acquired elements must have the capability to operate and generate outputs. In making this assessment, the Company considers whether the acquired processes are critical to the continuation or development of the activities and whether they are supported by the resources necessary for their execution, such as systems, technology, specialized knowledge, or a skilled workforce. As of the acquisition date, the Company also assesses the assets acquired and liabilities assumed in order to classify them based on their contractual, economic, and legal characteristics. Any contingent consideration is initially recognized at fair value, and subsequent changes in its measurement are accounted for in accordance with CPC 48 / IFRS 9 and recognized in profit or loss. When the consideration transferred exceeds the fair value of the net assets acquired, the excess is recognized as goodwill. Such goodwill is subsequently tested for impairment on a recurring basis and allocated to the cash-generating units expected to benefit from the synergies arising from the acquisition. If the acquisition relates to a concession arrangement, the excess is recognized as an intangible asset and amortized over the term of the concession contract. When a portion of a cash-generating unit is disposed of, the corresponding portion of goodwill is included in the carrying amount of the operation when determining the gain or loss on disposal. In business combinations achieved in stages, the equity interest previously held by the Company is remeasured to fair value on the date control is obtained, with any resulting gain or loss recognized in profit or loss for the period.
