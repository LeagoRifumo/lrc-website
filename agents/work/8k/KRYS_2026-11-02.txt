Krystal Biotech Announces Third Quarter 2026 Financial and Operating Results
$127.3 million in 3Q VYJUVEK global revenue and $1.1 billion since launch
VYJUVEK pricing negotiations in Germany and Italy completed
KB113 clinical data demonstrate regression in more than 80% of assessed basal cell carcinoma lesions in patients with Gorlin syndrome
Strong balance sheet, ending the quarter with $1.2 billion in cash and investments
Board authorizes share repurchase program of up to $400 million
PITTSBURGH, November 2, 2026 (GLOBE NEWSWIRE) – Krystal Biotech, Inc. (the “Company” or “Krystal”) (NASDAQ: KRYS) today reported financial results for the third quarter ended September 30, 2026 and provided a business update.
“Continued growth in VYJUVEK revenue is supporting profitability and funding the advancement of our pipeline,” said Krish S. Krishnan, Chairman and Chief Executive Officer of Krystal Biotech. “Our financial strength allows us to invest fully in these programs and our global commercial expansion while returning capital to shareholders. The $400 million share repurchase program, under which we expect to repurchase shares opportunistically over the next two years, reflects our confidence in Krystal’s long-term growth prospects and our commitment to disciplined capital allocation.”
VYJUVEK® (beremagene geperpavec-svdt, or B-VEC)
for the Treatment of Dystrophic Epidermolysis Bullosa (DEB)
The Company recorded $127.3 million in global VYJUVEK net product revenue for the third quarter of 2026, an increase of 30% compared to the prior year third quarter. Gross margin for the quarter was 94%.
Patient demand in the United States continues to grow with over 760 reimbursement approvals secured for VYJUVEK nationwide. Steady expansion of the U.S. prescriber base is supporting continued adoption across the country.
The Company is also expanding its reach across its initial launch markets of Germany, France, and Japan, converting high physician interest and engagement into patient starts and broadening the prescriber base to bring VYJUVEK treatment closer to patients.
Pricing negotiations are now complete in both Germany and Italy, and the Company expects to launch VYJUVEK in Italy in early 2027. Pricing negotiations are ongoing in multiple European countries and the United Kingdom, with decisions expected in Spain later this year, and in France no earlier than 2027. The Company has also submitted a marketing authorization application for VYJUVEK in Australia and expects to submit an application for marketing authorization in Switzerland before year end.
Dermatology
KB111 for the treatment of Hailey-Hailey disease (HHD)
Enrollment is complete and dosing is ongoing in HALITE-1, the Company’s open-label, single-arm study evaluating the safety of KB111, administered once weekly for 12 weeks, in patients with HHD. HHD is a genetic disease characterized by painful rash and blistering in skin folds. In addition to pain, patients with HHD reporting debilitating symptoms of itch, burning, infections, and body odor, as well as severe, negative impacts on quality of life and psychological distress. The prevalence of HHD is not well characterized and is most commonly estimated at roughly 1 per 50,000, although underreporting is possible.
A total of five patients have been enrolled in HALITE-1. The Company expects to report interim study results before the end of the year. Details of the study can be found at www.clinicaltrials.gov under NCT identifier: NCT07717346.
The Company has also completed development of its HHD-specific severity scale for the clinical evaluation of KB111. The Company expects to meet with the FDA in the first quarter of 2027, following completion of scale validation, to discuss study results, the scale, and study designs to enable a registrational study start in 2027.
KB113 for the treatment of Gorlin syndrome (GS)
In October, the Company announced a positive clinical update supporting initial proof of concept for KB113 in the treatment of basal cell carcinoma (BCC) in patients with GS. GS is a genetic disease characterized by a greatly increased risk of developing BCCs. Patients with Gorlin syndrome can develop BCCs as early as infancy and may have hundreds of BCCs over their lifetimes requiring frequent and potentially disfiguring surgical procedures. Prevalence data for GS is limited but available data suggest the number of patients with GS in the United States could exceed 10,000.
In the Company’s three patient pilot, multicenter, single-arm, open-label study, more than 80% of assessed BCC lesions showed investigator-assessed clinical regression. Regression was observed in both injected and non-injected lesions, consistent with an abscopal effect. Histologic assessment also confirmed complete clearance of an injected BCC lesion in each patient. KB113 was well tolerated, with all reported adverse events mild or moderate in severity, transient, and resolving during the study.
Building on these positive initial results, the Company has initiated discussions with the FDA regarding the advanced clinical development of KB113 for the treatment of BCCs in patients with GS. The Company will provide an update on its clinical development plans following alignment with the FDA.
Ophthalmology
KB803 for the treatment and prevention of corneal abrasions in DEB patients
The Company has enrolled three additional patients in its registrational IOLITE study, including one child aged one year. The additional enrollment replaces two patients who withdrew for reasons unrelated to safety and provides supporting evidence for the potential treatment of corneal abrasions in DEB patients from birth. The Company now expects the last
patient visit in February 2027 with top-line results reported shortly thereafter. The primary efficacy endpoint is the change in the average number of days per month with corneal abrasion symptoms while receiving KB803 versus placebo. Details about the study can be found at www.clinicaltrials.gov under NCT identifier: NCT07016750.
KB801 for the treatment of neurotrophic keratitis (NK)
Enrollment is ongoing in EMERALD-1, the Company’s registrational, 1:1 randomized, double-masked, multicenter, placebo-controlled study evaluating KB801 for the treatment of NK. The Company has activated 22 sites across the United States and is close to activating sites in France, Spain and the United Kingdom. Patient enrollment has progressed slower than initially anticipated, reflecting the study's narrowly defined eligibility criteria and the challenges of enrolling patients in a placebo-controlled trial in an indication with an available approved therapy. The Company now expects to complete enrollment of approximately 60 patients in EMERALD-1 and report top-line results in 2027. Details about the study can be found at www.clinicaltrials.gov under NCT identifier: NCT06999733.
Respiratory
KB407 for the treatment of cystic fibrosis (CF)
Three patients are currently being dosed in the Company’s ongoing open-label, single-arm study evaluating the safety of repeat-dose KB407 administration over 24 weeks in patients with CF who are ineligible for, do not tolerate, or do not benefit from modulator therapy. The Company expects to enroll and dose three additional patients before year end, with interim study results now anticipated in the first half of 2027. Earlier this year, the Company announced positive interim clinical findings demonstrating successful delivery and expression of wild-type CFTR protein in the lungs of patients treated with KB407. Details about the study can be found at www.clinicaltrials.gov under NCT identifier: NCT05504837.
In the third quarter, the Company was selected for the FDA’s Complex Innovative Trial Design (CID) Meeting Program, advancing ongoing discussions regarding the Company’s innovative registrational study design. The CID Meeting Program is intended to facilitate and advance the use of complex and novel clinical trial designs and provides the opportunity for increased interaction with the FDA. The Company will share the design and associated statistical analysis of the registrational study following alignment with the FDA and is presently on track to initiate the registrational study in 2027.
KB408 for the treatment of alpha-1 antitrypsin deficiency (AATD) lung disease
The Company continues to enroll in repeat-dose Cohort 2B of SERPENTINE-1, the Company’s open-label dose escalation study evaluating KB408 in adult patients with AATD with a Pi*ZZ or a Pi*ZNull genotype. Cohort 2B is designed to evaluate the safety and tolerability of repeat KB408 dosing at the same dose level that was previously shown to safely deliver SERPINA1 to the lungs of AATD patients after a single dose. Details of the study can be found at www.clinicaltrials.gov under NCT identifier: NCT06049082. The Company expects to report interim study results in 2027.
Oncology
Inhaled KB707 for the treatment of non-small cell lung cancer (NSCLC)
KYANITE-1 is an ongoing Phase 1/2 open-label, multi-center, dose escalation and expansion study evaluating inhaled KB707, as monotherapy or in combination with standard of care therapies, in patients with locally advanced or metastatic solid tumors involving the lungs.
Interim clinical results from the KYANITE-1 dose expansion cohort evaluating inhaled KB707 in combination with pembrolizumab were presented at the American Society for Clinical Oncology 2026 Annual Meeting in May and demonstrated antitumor activity in patients with advanced NSCLC, with an objective response rate (ORR) of 31% and a disease control rate (DCR) of 75%. Multiple patients from this cohort remain on treatment and in follow-up with sustained disease control.
The Company is currently enrolling patients with advanced NSCLC in a global KYANITE-1 dose expansion cohort evaluating inhaled KB707 in combination with chemotherapy. An initial dose-optimization phase evaluated the safety and tolerability of the combination regimen in the first 10 patients and informed the selection of the chemotherapy dose for subsequent enrollment. Sites have also been activated in France and Spain. The Company expects to enroll approximately 70 patients to be dosed at the selected combination regimen by mid-2027.
Details of the KYANITE-1 study can be found at www.clinicaltrials.gov under NCT identifier: NCT06228326.

About VYJUVEK
VYJUVEK is a non-invasive, topical, redosable genetic medicine designed to deliver two copies of the COL7A1 gene when applied directly to DEB wounds. VYJUVEK was designed to treat DEB at the molecular level by providing the patient’s skin cells the template to make normal COL7 protein, thereby addressing the fundamental disease-causing mechanism. VYJUVEK is approved in the United States, Europe, and Japan.
U.S. INDICATION
VYJUVEK is a herpes-simplex virus type 1 (HSV-1) vector-based gene therapy indicated for the treatment of wounds in adult and pediatric patients with dystrophic epidermolysis bullosa with mutation(s) in the collagen type VII alpha 1 chain (COL7A1) gene.
IMPORTANT SAFETY INFORMATION
Adverse Reactions
The most common adverse drug reactions (incidence >5%) were itching, chills, redness, rash, cough, and runny nose. These are not all the possible side effects with VYJUVEK. Call your healthcare provider for medical advice about side effects.
To report SUSPECTED ADVERSE REACTIONS, contact Krystal Biotech, Inc. at 1-844-557-9782 or FDA at 1-800-FDA-1088 or http://www.fda.gov/medwatch.
Contraindications
None.
Warnings and Precautions
VYJUVEK gel may be applied by a healthcare provider, a caregiver, or the patient.
After treatment, patients and caregivers should be careful not to touch treated wounds and dressings until the next dressing change.
Wash hands and wear protective gloves when changing wound dressings. Disinfect bandages from the first dressing change with a virucidal agent, and dispose of the disinfected bandages in a separate sealed plastic bag in household waste. Dispose of the subsequent used dressings in a sealed plastic bag in household waste.
Patients should avoid touching or scratching wound sites or wound dressings.
In the event of an accidental exposure flush with clean water for at least 15 minutes.
For more information, see full U.S. Prescribing Information.
