AGCO REPORTS THIRD-QUARTER RESULTS
•Net sales of $2.3 billion, down 8.6% year-over-year
•Reported earnings per share of $0.75 and adjusted earnings per share(1) of $1.05
•Full-year adjusted earnings per share outlook of approximately $5.50
DULUTH, GA – October 29 – AGCO (NYSE: AGCO) reported net sales of $2.3 billion for the third quarter ended September 30, 2026, a decrease of 8.6% compared to the third quarter of 2025. Reported net income was $0.75 per share for the quarter and adjusted net income(1) was $1.05 per share. These results compare to reported net income of $4.09 per share and adjusted net income(1) of $1.35 per share for the third quarter of 2025. Excluding favorable foreign currency translation of 0.1%, net sales in the quarter decreased 8.7% compared to the third quarter of 2025.
“AGCO delivered resilient third-quarter results in what is the weakest global industry demand environment in more than a decade,” said Eric Hansotia, AGCO’s Chairman, President and CEO. “While elevated input costs and uncertainty around commodity markets and the trade environment pushed industry demand below the last cyclical trough, we aligned production with demand, managed dealer inventories and maintained cost discipline. We also advanced our Farmer-First strategy by strengthening the market position of our premium brands and expanding precision agriculture solutions that help farmers automate tasks, address labor constraints and get more from their equipment.”
Hansotia continued, “Lower farm income and uncertainty across global agricultural markets continued to weigh on farmer sentiment, with conditions softening further in EME and Latin America. We remain focused on what we can control: helping farmers improve their economics while strengthening our cost structure. Our fuel-efficient tractors, AI-enabled targeted spraying systems and autonomous solutions are designed to lower operating costs and improve returns for farmers. These actions are helping us navigate current conditions and position AGCO for the recovery.”
Net sales for the first nine months of 2026 were approximately $7.2 billion, an increase of 0.7% compared to the same period in 2025. For the first nine months of 2026, reported net income was $2.59 per share and adjusted net income(1) was $3.42 per share. These results compare to reported net income of $8.45 per share and adjusted net income(1) of $3.11 per share for the same period in 2025. Excluding favorable foreign currency translation of 3.4%, net sales in the first nine months of 2026 decreased 2.7% compared to the same period in 2025.

North American industry retail tractor sales were 9% lower in the first nine months of 2026 compared to the same period in 2025 with the largest change occurring in higher-horsepower categories. Combine unit sales were 10% lower year-over-year during the same period. Commodity prices and evolving conditions surrounding trade and export demand continued to influence farm profitability. As a result, farmers remained cautious with spending, and industry demand is expected to remain below historical mid-cycle levels through the remainder of 2026.
Brazil industry retail tractor sales were 17% lower in the first nine months of 2026 compared to the same period in 2025 reflecting reduced demand for larger equipment. Farm profitability remained under pressure due to higher interest rates, constrained credit conditions and soft farm economics. Broader economic and policy uncertainty weighed on farmer confidence and led to delayed equipment purchases.
Western Europe industry retail tractor sales were 1% lower during the first nine months of 2026 compared to the same period in 2025 with the third quarter down 9% compared to the third quarter of 2025. Spain, Germany, France and Italy showed the largest third quarter declines. Hot and dry weather across portions of Western Europe created yield variability and weighed on farmer sentiment in the third quarter. While relatively healthy livestock and dairy economics and an aging equipment fleet continue to support replacement demand, softer crop economics, elevated input costs and economic developments have constrained equipment purchasing activity. As a result, industry demand is expected to remain challenged for the remainder of 2026.

North America
North American region net sales increased 35.1% during the third quarter of 2026 compared to the third quarter of 2025, excluding the impact of unfavorable currency translation. Higher unit sales across most equipment categories, particularly high-horsepower and mid-range tractors and parts, supported the increase in sales. Income from operations for the third quarter of 2026 was $13.8 million compared to a loss from operations of $42.0 million in the same period in 2025, primarily due to higher sales and production volumes and the benefit of approximately $13.0 million of certain IEEPA tariff refunds recognized during the period.
Latin America
Net sales in the Latin America region decreased 19.1% during the third quarter of 2026 compared to the third quarter of 2025, excluding the impact of favorable currency translation. Softer industry demand resulted in lower sales. Income from operations for the third quarter of 2026 was $13.0 million lower compared to the same period in 2025. This decrease was primarily the result of significantly lower sales and production volumes.
Europe/Middle East
Net sales in the Europe/Middle East region decreased 18.1% during the third quarter of 2026 compared to the third quarter of 2025, excluding the impact of unfavorable currency translation. Sales declines were primarily driven by weaker industry demand, with the most pronounced decreases in Germany, France, Italy and Spain. Income from operations in the third quarter of 2026 was $115.1 million lower compared to the same period in 2025, primarily due to significantly lower sales and production levels.
Asia/Pacific/Africa
Asia/Pacific/Africa region net sales increased 9.2% during the third quarter of 2026 compared to the third quarter of 2025, excluding favorable currency translation impacts. Higher sales in Australia were partially offset by lower sales across many of the Asian and African markets. Income from operations in the third quarter of 2026 was $17.2 million higher compared to the same period in 2025, primarily due to higher sales and a favorable product mix.
Outlook
AGCO’s net sales for 2026 are expected to be from $9.8 to $10.0 billion. Adjusted operating margins are projected to be about 7.0% reflecting continued emphasis on pricing discipline, cost management and operational alignment. Production volumes are planned to align dealer inventory with market demand, while cost controls and positive pricing continue to support performance. Based on these assumptions, 2026 adjusted earnings per share is targeted at approximately $5.50. These estimates reflect tariff policies as of October 29, 2026, together with AGCO’s established mitigation actions and sourcing strategies. Any changes to tariff policies or related responses could affect these projections.

Certain other entries remain subject to refund procedures that CBP has not yet defined. Refunds received remain subject to CBP review and to the outcome of the administration’s appeal described below, and we could be required to repay some or all of the amounts received. The administration has appealed the CIT's refund order to the U.S. Court of Appeals for the Federal Circuit, contesting both the scope of the refund obligation and the reliquidation of finally liquidated entries for importers who have not filed individual lawsuits. About AGCO
AGCO (NYSE: AGCO) is a global leader in agricultural machinery and precision agriculture technologies. AGCO's high-performance equipment and smart farming solutions, including brand-agnostic retrofit technologies and autonomous offerings, empower farmers to drive productivity while sustainably feeding the world. 1. SEGMENT REPORTING
The Company has four operating segments which are also its reportable segments which consist of the North America, Latin America, Europe/Middle East and Asia/Pacific/Africa regions. Effective January 1, 2026, the Company realigned its organizational structure to support its Farmer‑First transformation initiatives in North America. As a result, the Company’s Mexico operations were transferred from the North America segment to the South America segment, which was renamed Latin America. Segment information for all prior periods presented has been retrospectively adjusted to reflect this change. The Company’s reportable segments are geography based and distribute a full range of agricultural machinery and precision agriculture technology. The Company’s Chief Operating Decision Maker (“CODM”), Eric P. Hansotia, Chairman of the Board, President and Chief Executive Officer, evaluates segment performance primarily based on income from operations. The CODM utilizes income from operations to evaluate each segment’s performance including the allocation of resources. Sales for each segment are based on the location of the third-party customer. The Company’s selling, general and administrative expenses and engineering expenses are generally charged to each segment based on the region and division where the expenses are incurred. As a result, the components of income (loss) from operations for one segment may not be comparable to another segment.
