H&R Block Reports Fiscal 2027 First Quarter Results and Reaffirms Fiscal 2027 Outlook
— Revenue Increased 10% —
— Repurchased $100 Million of Shares as Part of Full-Year Capital Return Strategy —
KANSAS CITY, Mo. - H&R Block, Inc. (NYSE: HRB) (the "Company") today released financial results1 for its fiscal 2027 first quarter ended September 30, 2026.
"We are pleased with our start to fiscal 2027, including revenue growth of more than 10%," said Curtis Campbell, president and chief executive officer. "At the same time, we continue advancing strategic initiatives designed to enhance the client experience and support future growth. Our focus remains on executing our strategy and positioning H&R Block for long-term success."
Fiscal 2027 First Quarter Results and Key Financial Metrics
"Our financial position remains strong, and we continue to execute our disciplined capital allocation priorities," said Tiffany Mason, chief financial officer. "During the first quarter, we invested in the business to support future growth while returning capital to shareholders through dividends and share repurchases. We remain confident in the assumptions underlying our fiscal 2027 outlook and are reaffirming our guidance."
The Company reminds readers that its business is highly seasonal, and first quarter results consistently reflect this pattern. Historically, this period contributes modestly to annual revenue and typically generates a net loss.
For the first quarter, the Company delivered total revenue of $224.3 million, an increase of $20.8 million, or 10.2%, versus the prior year. The increase was primarily the result of growth in international services, higher tax protection and identity services, an increase in small business services, and growth in U.S. tax preparation revenues.
Total operating expenses of $456.2 million increased by $45.6 million, or 11.1%, versus the prior year. The increase was primarily due to higher field compensation and benefits, increased general corporate expenses, and higher occupancy and equipment costs.
During the first quarter, the Company recognized $8.0 million of severance and related costs associated with the previously announced seasonal office leadership transition, which were recorded in field compensation and benefits. This charge has been excluded from adjusted results.
Net loss from continuing operations increased by $22.8 million, or 13.8%, to ($188.1) million, and loss per share from continuing operations2 increased 21.4% to ($1.53).
Adjusted net loss from continuing operations2 increased by $14.9 million, or 9.5%, to ($172.1) million, and adjusted loss per share from continuing operations2 increased 16.7% to ($1.40), due to a higher net loss and fewer shares outstanding as a result of share repurchases.

Capital Allocation
The Company reported the following related to its capital structure:
•The Company announced today that its Board of Directors declared a quarterly cash dividend of $0.46 per share which will be paid on January 6, 2027, to shareholders of record as of December 3, 2026.
•The Company repurchased and retired 2.2 million shares at an aggregate price of $100.3 million, or $45.68 per share, in the first quarter. It has approximately $500 million remaining on its $1.5 billion share repurchase program.
•Year-to-date, the Company has returned $156.9 million to shareholders in the form of dividends and share repurchases.
H&R Block has paid quarterly dividends consecutively since the Company became public in 1962. Since 2016, the Company has returned more than $5.3 billion to shareholders in the form of dividends and share repurchases, buying back over 49% of its shares outstanding3.
Fiscal Year 2027 Outlook Reaffirmed
The Company continues to expect:
•Revenue to be in the range of $4.11 to $4.16 billion.
•Adjusted EBITDA4 to be in the range of $1.11 to $1.14 billion.
•Effective tax rate to be approximately 23%.
•Adjusted Diluted Earnings Per Share4 to be in the range of $6.04 to $6.24.

Non-GAAP Financial Information
Non-GAAP financial measures should not be considered as a substitute for, or superior to, measures of financial performance prepared in accordance with GAAP. Because these measures are not measures of financial performance under GAAP and are susceptible to varying calculations, they may not be comparable to similarly titled measures for other companies.
We consider our non-GAAP financial measures to be performance measures and a useful metric for management and investors to evaluate and compare the ongoing operating performance of our business. We make adjustments for certain non-GAAP financial measures related to amortization of intangibles from acquisitions, goodwill impairments, severance and related costs of discrete strategic initiatives, and material discrete tax impacts of IRS examination settlements. We may consider whether other significant items that arise in the future should be excluded from our non-GAAP financial measures, such as future organizational restructuring initiatives and legal settlements, when such items are not indicative of our ongoing operating performance.
We measure the performance of our business using a variety of metrics, including earnings before interest, taxes, depreciation and amortization (EBITDA) from continuing operations, adjusted EBITDA from continuing operations, adjusted net income from continuing operations, and adjusted diluted earnings per share from continuing operations. We also use EBITDA from continuing operations and pretax income from continuing operations, each subject to permitted adjustments, as performance metrics in incentive compensation calculations for our employees.
