WHITE MOUNTAINS REPORTS THIRD QUARTER RESULTS
HAMILTON, Bermuda (November 5, 2026) - White Mountains Insurance Group, Ltd. (NYSE: WTM) reported book value per share of $2,243 as of September 30, 2026, a decrease of 1% for the third quarter of 2026 and an increase of 3% for the first nine months of 2026, including dividends.
Liam Caffrey, CEO, commented, “Book value per share was down 1% in the quarter. Higher interest rates and a lower share price at MediaAlpha drove unrealized investment losses, which offset an otherwise solid quarter for operating results. Ark produced a 75% combined ratio and $386 million of gross written premiums, leading to strong growth in tangible book value. Kudu grew adjusted EBITDA, but its trailing 12 months return on equity declined to 8% as higher interest rates reduced the fair value of its portfolio. HG Global generated a record $19 million of gross written premiums in the quarter, but book value returns were negatively impacted by higher interest rates. Distinguished had a solid quarter, growing both managed premiums and ScaleCo adjusted EBITDA. WTM Partners experienced good top and bottom-line growth. MediaAlpha's share price declined 24% in the quarter, producing $54 million of mark-to-market losses for our remaining position. Our consolidated investment portfolio, excluding MediaAlpha, was flat overall, with fixed income weighing down the result. Undeployed capital remains at roughly $0.8 billion.”
Comprehensive income (loss) attributable to common shareholders was $(39) million and $134 million in the third quarter and first nine months of 2026 compared to $114 million and $272 million in the third quarter and first nine months of 2025. Results in the third quarter and first nine months of 2026 included $(69) million and $92 million of net realized and unrealized investment gains (losses) compared to $76 million and $250 million in the third quarter and first nine months of 2025. Results in the third quarter and first nine months of 2026 also included $(54) million and $(60) million of unrealized investment gains (losses) from White Mountains’s investment in MediaAlpha compared to $8 million and $2 million in the third quarter and first nine months of 2025.
Ark/WM Outrigger
Ark’s combined ratio was 75% and 83% in the third quarter and first nine months of 2026 compared to 76% and 84% in the third quarter and first nine months of 2025. Ark’s combined ratio in the third quarter and first nine months of 2026 included two points and four points of catastrophe losses, driven primarily by losses related to the war in Iran. This compares to minimal catastrophe losses in the third quarter of 2025 and seven points of catastrophe losses in the first nine months of 2025, driven primarily by losses related to the California wildfires. Ark’s combined ratio included four points and five points of net favorable prior year development in the third quarter and first nine months of 2026, driven primarily by the property and specialty lines of business. This compares to zero points and five points of net favorable prior year development in the third quarter and first nine months of 2025, also driven primarily by the property and specialty lines of business.
Ark has ongoing exposure to the war in Iran, primarily through the specialty and marine & energy lines of business. In the third quarter and first nine months of 2026, Ark recorded estimated losses of $10 million and $52 million (net of reinsurance and reinstatement premiums) related to the war.
Hurricane Isaias made U.S. landfall in October 2026. Ark does not expect its losses related to the storm to be significant.

Ark reported gross written premiums of $386 million and $2,254 million, net written premiums of $338 million and $1,466 million and net earned premiums of $510 million and $1,256 million in the third quarter and first nine months of 2026 compared to gross written premiums of $366 million and $2,289 million, net written premiums of $283 million and $1,510 million and net earned premiums of $499 million and $1,202 million in the third quarter and first nine months of 2025. The increase in gross written premiums in the third quarter of 2026 was driven primarily by a change in the timing of recognition of certain delegated authority business as well as growth in the specialty and accident & health lines of business, partially offset by reduced writings in property as a result of softening market conditions. The timing of recognition of certain delegated authority business had no impact on the timing of recognition of Ark’s earned premiums. The decrease in gross written premiums in the first nine months of 2026 was driven primarily by reduced writings in property as a result of the softening market conditions, partially offset by growth in the specialty, casualty and accident & health lines of business.
Ark reported pre-tax income of $64 million and $149 million in the third quarter and first nine months of 2026 compared to $97 million and $240 million in the third quarter and first nine months of 2025. Ark’s results included net realized and unrealized investment gains (losses) of $(40) million and $(43) million in the third quarter and first nine months of 2026 compared to $18 million and $99 million in the third quarter and first nine months of 2025.
Ark reported book value of $1,673 million as of September 30, 2026, an increase of 4% in the third quarter of 2026 and 8% in the first nine months of 2026, including dividends. Ark reported tangible book value of $1,825 million as of September 30, 2026, an increase of 5% in the third quarter of 2026 and 12% in the first nine months of 2026, including dividends. Ark’s book value includes goodwill and other intangible assets, net of tax, and White Mountains’s contingent consideration liability, which are excluded from Ark’s tangible book value.
Ian Beaton, CEO of Ark, said, “We have enjoyed good results for the first three quarters of 2026. Ark's combined ratio was 75% for the third quarter and 83% year to date, both one point better than prior year. Gross written premiums were $2.3 billion year to date, slightly down year-over-year as growth in specialty and other classes was offset by continued headwinds in the property market.”
During the fourth quarter of 2025, Ark renewed Outrigger Re Ltd. for the 2026 underwriting year, but WM Outrigger Re did not participate. Accordingly, WM Outrigger Re’s results are not material to White Mountains’s consolidated financial statements for the third quarter and first nine months of 2026.
Kudu
Kudu reported total revenues of $(5) million, pre-tax loss of $15 million and adjusted EBITDA of $18 million in the third quarter of 2026 compared to total revenues of $54 million, pre-tax income of $44 million and adjusted EBITDA of $15 million in the third quarter of 2025. Total revenues, pre-tax income (loss) and adjusted EBITDA included $21 million of net investment income in the third quarter of 2026 compared to $18 million in the third quarter of 2025. Total revenues and pre-tax income (loss) also included $(26) million of net realized and unrealized investment gains (losses) in the third quarter of 2026 compared to $36 million in the third quarter of 2025. The decrease in net realized and unrealized investment gains was driven by a decrease in the fair value of Kudu’s participation contracts, primarily due to the impact of higher interest rates on discount rates, partially offset by growth in assets under management at several managers. On a trailing 12 months basis, return on equity was 8% as of September 30, 2026, down from 15% as of June 30, 2026, primarily due to lower unrealized investment gains.

Kudu reported total revenues of $127 million, pre-tax income of $93 million and adjusted EBITDA of $50 million in the first nine months of 2026 compared to total revenues of $139 million, pre-tax income of $108 million and adjusted EBITDA of $47 million in the first nine months of 2025. Total revenues, pre-tax income and adjusted EBITDA included $61 million of net investment income in the first nine months of 2026 compared to $57 million in the first nine months of 2025. Total revenues and pre-tax income also included $66 million of net realized and unrealized investment gains in the first nine months of 2026 compared to $81 million in the first nine months of 2025. The decrease in net realized and unrealized investment gains was driven primarily by the impact of higher interest rates on discount rates and foreign currency movements on the fair value of Kudu’s participation contracts, partially offset by step-ups in valuation related to certain sale transactions.
Rob Jakacki, CEO of Kudu, said, “The fair value of our portfolio was down 2% in the quarter as interest rates spiked; however, our underlying portfolio fundamentals remain sound, supported by the continued strength and resilience of our partner firms. Trailing 12 months adjusted EBITDA grew to $69 million, aided by two new acquisitions this year. We remain focused on disciplined capital deployment, prudent portfolio management and positioning the business to capitalize on opportunities in the marketplace.”
HG Global
HG Global reported gross written premiums of $19 million and $39 million and earned premiums of $9 million and $24 million in the third quarter and first nine months of 2026 compared to gross written premiums of $16 million and $42 million and earned premiums of $8 million and $23 million in the third quarter and first nine months of 2025. HG Global’s total par value of policies assumed was $929 million and $2,264 million in the third quarter and first nine months of 2026 compared to $850 million and $2,208 million in the third quarter and first nine months of 2025. HG Global’s total gross pricing was 208 and 170 basis points in the third quarter and first nine months of 2026 compared to 187 and 189 basis points in the third quarter and first nine months of 2025.
HG Global reported pre-tax income (loss) of $(28) million and $(7) million in the third quarter and first nine months of 2026 compared to $22 million and $64 million in the third quarter and first nine months of 2025. HG Global’s results included net realized and unrealized investment gains (losses) of $(29) million and $(36) million in the third quarter and first nine months of 2026 compared to $7 million and $20 million in the third quarter and first nine months of 2025. Investment losses in the third quarter and first nine months of 2026 were driven by the impact of higher interest rates leading to unrealized losses on HG Global’s fixed income portfolio.
The fair value of the BAM surplus notes decreased to $336 million as of September 30, 2026 compared to $353 million as of June 30, 2026. The decrease was driven by a $16 million decrease in fair value and an $8 million cash payment of principal and interest, partially offset by $7 million of accrued interest. The decrease in fair value was driven primarily by the impact of higher interest rates on the discount rate.
Kevin Pearson, President of HG Global, said, “HG Global had a record third quarter for production with gross written premiums of $19 million, up 21% year-over-year, driven primarily by performance in the primary market. The spike in interest rates resulted in $29 million of unrealized investment losses and a decrease in the fair value of the BAM surplus notes, but the underlying fundamentals of these assets remain unchanged.”
We encourage you to read BAM’s third quarter statutory financial statements and operating supplement, which will be available on BAM’s website at https://bambonds.com/about-bam/credit-rating-and-financial-information/.

Distinguished
Distinguished reported managed premiums of $159 million and $480 million, commission and fee revenues of $55 million and $151 million, pre-tax loss of $13 million and $41 million and ScaleCo adjusted EBITDA of $5 million and $22 million for the third quarter and first nine months of 2026. Distinguished reported managed premiums of $43 million, commission and fee revenues of $14 million, pre-tax loss of $3 million and ScaleCo adjusted EBITDA of $1 million for the period from September 2, 2025 through September 30, 2025. On a trailing 12 months basis, Distinguished reported managed premiums of $625 million and ScaleCo adjusted EBITDA of $30 million.
Jason Rotman, President of Distinguished, said “We had another solid quarter. On a trailing 12 months basis, managed premiums grew 5% quarter-over-quarter to a new high of $625 million, and ScaleCo adjusted EBITDA grew 5% to $30 million. We enjoyed particularly strong growth in our environmental and surety programs, partially offset by continued headwinds in our umbrella program. GrowthCo continues to mature nicely, and the pipeline of new opportunities remains robust.”
WTM Partners
WTM Partners reported total revenues of $107 million and $243 million, pre-tax income of $1 million and $3 million and adjusted EBITDA of $9 million and $19 million in the third quarter and first nine months of 2026 compared to total revenues of $54 million and $97 million, pre-tax loss of $1 million and $3 million and adjusted EBITDA of $2 million and $4 million in the third quarter and first nine months of 2025. The increases were driven primarily by organic growth at Enterprise Solutions and the acquisitions of Basesix and Hawkeye Electric in the second quarter of 2026.
John Daly, CEO of WTM Partners, said “WTM Partners had a solid quarter generating $9 million of adjusted EBITDA. Enterprise Solutions, including recently acquired Hawkeye Electric, generated solid growth and made good progress on integration initiatives. Our other recent acquisition, low voltage systems integration platform Basesix, has performed ahead of expectations. We have roughly $200 million of equity capital deployed into these businesses and are working an active pipeline of new platform and tuck-in opportunities.”
MediaAlpha
As of September 30, 2026, White Mountains owned 17.9 million shares of MediaAlpha, representing a 29% basic ownership interest based on the total class A and class B common shares outstanding. As of September 30, 2026, MediaAlpha’s share price was $9.57 per share, which decreased from $12.57 per share as of June 30, 2026. The carrying value of White Mountains’s investment in MediaAlpha was $171 million as of September 30, 2026 compared to $225 million as of June 30, 2026.
On November 4, 2026, White Mountains agreed to sell 5.5 million shares of MediaAlpha for $13.90 per share. White Mountains will own 12.4 million shares of MediaAlpha after the sale, representing a 21% ownership interest based on the class A and class B common shares outstanding as of October 26, 2026. At this level of ownership, each $1.00 per share increase or decrease in the share price of MediaAlpha will result in an approximate $5.00 per share increase or decrease in White Mountains’s book value per share.
We encourage you to read MediaAlpha’s third quarter earnings release and related shareholder letter, which are available on MediaAlpha’s investor relations website at https://investors.mediaalpha.com.

Other Operations
White Mountains’s Other Operations reported pre-tax income (loss) of $(33) million and $5 million in the third quarter and first nine months of 2026 compared to $(24) million and $(45) million in the third quarter and first nine months of 2025. The results of White Mountains’s Other Operations for the third quarter of 2026 were largely driven by the decline in MediaAlpha’s share price. Unrealized investment gains (losses) from White Mountains’s investment in MediaAlpha were $(54) million and $(60) million in the third quarter and first nine months of 2026 compared to $8 million and $2 million in the third quarter and first nine months of 2025. Excluding MediaAlpha, net realized and unrealized investment gains were $25 million and $105 million in the third quarter and first nine months of 2026 compared to $15 million and $50 million in the third quarter and first nine months of 2025. Net investment income was $14 million and $43 million in the third quarter and first nine months of 2026 compared to $6 million and $25 million in the third quarter and first nine months of 2025. White Mountains’s Other Operations reported general and administrative expenses of $28 million and $118 million in the third quarter and first nine months of 2026 compared to $66 million and $170 million in the third quarter and first nine months of 2025. The decrease in general and administrative expenses in the third quarter and first nine months of 2026 was driven primarily by lower incentive compensation costs and loss and loss adjustment expenses in 2026 compared to 2025.
Share Repurchases
In the first nine months of 2026, White Mountains repurchased and retired 103,827 of its common shares for $217 million at an average share price of $2,088.39, or 93% of White Mountains’s September 30, 2026 book value per share. Share repurchases in the third quarter of 2026 were minimal.
Investments
The total consolidated portfolio return was -0.6% in the third quarter of 2026. Excluding MediaAlpha, the total consolidated portfolio return was flat in the third quarter of 2026. The total consolidated portfolio return was 2.1% in the third quarter of 2025. Excluding MediaAlpha, the total consolidated portfolio return was 2.0% in the third quarter of 2025.
The total consolidated portfolio return was 3.0% in the first nine months of 2026. Excluding MediaAlpha, the total consolidated portfolio return was 3.9% in the first nine months of 2026. The total consolidated portfolio return was 6.6% in the first nine months of 2025. Excluding MediaAlpha, the total consolidated portfolio return was 6.8% in the first nine months of 2025.
Mark Plourde, President of White Mountains Advisors, said, “Excluding MediaAlpha, the total portfolio was flat in the quarter. Results were impacted by mark-to-market losses due to rising interest rates, offset by net investment income. Our short duration fixed income portfolio returned -0.7%, ahead of the Bloomberg Intermediate Aggregate Index return of -2.6%. Excluding MediaAlpha, the equity portfolio returned 0.8%, behind the S&P 500 Index return of 2.3%. Equity results were driven by lower relative returns from our market neutral positions and certain other long-term investments.”

Regulation G
This earnings release includes non-GAAP financial measures that have been reconciled from their most comparable GAAP financial measures.
•Ark’s tangible book value and growth in tangible book value are non-GAAP financial measures.
Tangible book value is a non-GAAP financial measure derived by adjusting GAAP book value to exclude (i) goodwill and other intangible assets, (ii) the deferred tax liability on other intangible assets and (iii) the contingent consideration liability. The contingent consideration liability represents the estimated fair value of the additional shares that could be earned by management rollover shareholders if and to the extent that White Mountains achieves certain multiple of invested capital return thresholds. If earned, these additional shares would result in a reallocation of economics among Ark’s shareholders, which is reflected in the fair value of the contingent consideration liability recorded by White Mountains, but would have no impact on Ark’s stand-alone book value or tangible book value.
Growth in tangible book value equals the change in tangible book value plus dividends for the period divided by beginning tangible book value.
White Mountains believes that these non-GAAP financial measures are useful to management and investors in evaluating Ark’s enterprise value.

•Kudu’s EBITDA, adjusted EBITDA, annualized adjusted EBITDA, annualized revenue and cash revenue yield are non-GAAP financial measures.
EBITDA is a non-GAAP financial measure that adds back interest expense on debt, income tax (expense) benefit, depreciation and amortization of other intangible assets to GAAP net income (loss).
Adjusted EBITDA is a non-GAAP financial measure that excludes certain other items in GAAP net income (loss) in addition to those added back to calculate EBITDA. The items relate to (i) net realized and unrealized investment gains (losses) on Kudu’s revenue and earnings participation contracts, (ii) non-cash equity-based compensation expense and (iii) transaction expenses. A description of each item follows:
•Net realized and unrealized investment gains (losses) - Represents net unrealized investment gains and losses recorded on Kudu’s revenue and earnings participation contracts, which are recorded at fair value under GAAP, and realized investment gains and losses from participation contracts sold during the period.

•Non-cash equity-based compensation expense - Represents non-cash expenses related to Kudu’s management compensation that are settled with equity units in Kudu.
•Transaction expenses - Represents costs directly related to Kudu’s mergers and acquisitions activity, such as external lawyer, banker, consulting and placement agent fees, which are not capitalized and are expensed under GAAP.
Annualized adjusted EBITDA is a non-GAAP financial measure that (i) annualizes partial year revenues related to Kudu’s revenue and earnings participation contracts acquired during the previous 12-month period and (ii) removes partial year revenues related to revenue and earnings participation contracts sold during the previous 12-month period.
Annualized revenue is a non-GAAP financial measure that adds the adjustments for annualized adjusted EBITDA to GAAP net investment income.
Cash revenue yield is a non-GAAP financial measure that is derived using annualized revenue as a percentage of total net capital drawn and invested. The most directly comparable GAAP financial measure is net investment income revenue yield, which is derived using GAAP net investment income as a percentage of total net capital drawn and invested.
