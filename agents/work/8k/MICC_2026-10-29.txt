Peter ter Kulve, CEO:
“We delivered strong performance in Q3, with volume-led growth ahead of the growing ice cream category. This performance extends our track record of share gains; reflecting the strength of our brands, the impact of our innovation, and continued improvements in execution.
Sales of our leading brands have accelerated, powered by innovation. Magnum and Heartbrand grew double-digit with standout performance from Magnum La Pistache - the season's number one ice cream innovation - and a reinvigorated Solero achieved exceptional growth in Europe. Ben & Jerry's maintained momentum from Q2, again growing more than 9%, and Cornetto grew sales high single-digit. Yasso, our low-calorie, high protein frozen Greek yogurt in the US, continued its strong trajectory with more than 30% growth.
All regions continued to grow, including the US, our biggest market. Improved execution and operational rigour enabled us to capitalise on strong demand, especially in Europe, and win share. Our productivity programme remains firmly on track, helping offset inflationary cost pressures and creating capacity to reinvest behind our brands, capabilities, and future growth.
Looking ahead, whilst the external environment remains uncertain, the fundamentals of our business are strong. Our leading brands are gaining momentum, and we continue to be laser focused on executing our growth strategy and productivity programme. We reaffirm our full year outlook for underlying margin improvement and raise our growth outlook to approximately 5.5%.”
Full Year 2026 Outlook
Our focus continues to be executing our growth strategy and productivity programme. We have upgraded our outlook for organic sales growth to approximately 5.5%, compared to our prior outlook of 3% to 5%.
Whilst we are mindful of the continued uncertainty in the global environment, Adjusted EBITDA margin improvement is still expected to be 40 to 60bps (on a comparable perimeter basis with 2025). Productivity savings and operating leverage are anticipated to offset higher costs, primarily driven by oil and energy prices. Reported improvement in Adjusted EBITDA margin is expected to be 0 to 20bps, mainly due to the impact of the acquisition of the India business.
Performance review Q3 2026
Revenue was €2.5 billion (Q3 2025: €2.2 billion) with Organic Sales Growth (OSG) for the quarter +9.6%. Volume was +7.3% higher and price was up +2.2%. All three regions contributed to growth, with Europe & ANZ +8.9%, Americas +3.9%, and AMEA +17.6%.
Reported revenue was +12.1% higher than last year, including +2.6% impact from acquisitions in India and Portugal and -0.3% foreign currency translation effects (FX). FX related mostly to the strengthening of the euro against the Turkish Lira.
Leading brands growing sales double-digit:
Magnum delivered strong double-digit growth, driven by Türkiye, France, the UK, and Mexico, underpinned by continued momentum from innovations extending the brand across flavours, formats, and occasions.
Ben & Jerry’s again grew more than 9%, bringing new consumers onboard, with exciting new flavours and by unlocking more occasions through additional formats in the US and Europe.
Cornetto delivered high-single-digit growth, supported by new product introductions, including Cornetto Max Pistachio in Europe and fruit sorbet variants in Asia.
Heartbrand grew double-digit, driven by continued momentum from licencing partnerships, including Hello Kitty and Bluey in the US, and reinvigorating Solero in Europe.
Yasso delivered more than 30% growth, with further expansion of handheld formats and pints in the US, serving the increasing market focus on wellness.
Innovation driving growth with more than 10% revenue year-to-date
Our growth continued to be powered by innovation aligned around four focus areas:
Core superiority: We further strengthened our core portfolio through quality upgrades and on-trend new flavours. Magnum La Pistache has been the standout; outperforming the competition, and our own expectations, as the top ice cream innovation this year across Europe, with exceptional performance in the UK, Germany, and France.
Portfolio architecture: Across markets, we continued to tailor recipes and review pack-price architecture according to local consumer preferences, including shifting our portfolio in India to new, improved, dairy recipes. Partnerships continued to support growth, with Hello Kitty and Bluey for Popsicle in the US, and Minecraft in Europe.
More occasions: Through our premium brands, we unlocked more consumption occasions by introducing new formats, including the Magnum cone and sandwich in Türkiye. Ben & Jerry’s delivered many successful ice cream innovations with sticks ranked as three of the top ten in the US, and sandwiches within the top five in Europe.
Category expansion: We continued to develop and grow the category with the expansion of Yasso, our higher-protein, lower-calorie offering in the US, from sticks to pints and further rollout of “Hydro: ICE”, our new hydration and energy proposition, in the Netherlands, Spain, and Portugal.
Distribution growing with rollout of more freezer cabinets
Our frontline-first model and ownership culture has continued to drive growth with improved distribution and increased availability across all channels. The At-Home channel grew high-single-digit, supported by our dedicated sales force and occasion-based innovations. The Away-from-Home channel delivered double-digit growth. We deployed more freezer cabinets in key markets, focused on the higher growth AMEA region, and are also introducing new innovative formats. Digital commerce maintained double-digit growth, led by scaling omnichannel customers and partnerships with rapid delivery platforms.
Productivity programme remains on track
Our productivity programme, started in 2024, remains on track to deliver planned cumulative savings of €500 million by 2028. We have continued to improve factory efficiencies, debottleneck our supply chain and reduce waste, especially in the US and Europe.
Our global technology transformation is entering the implementation phase. The first deployment wave is scheduled to start in November 2026, covering the UK and Ireland, Hungary, and Romania. We are on track to exit remaining Transitional Service Agreements (TSAs) by the end of 2027.

About The Magnum Ice Cream Company
The Magnum Ice Cream Company N.V. EURONEXT: MICC / NYSE: MICC / LSE: MICC is the world's leading ice cream business. Home to four of the world's five largest ice cream brands: Magnum, Ben & Jerry's, Cornetto and the Heartbrand, our portfolio delights consumers in 80 markets around the world. Headquartered in Amsterdam, The Netherlands, we have a global team of around 18,000 employees, a network of 32 factories, 13 R&D centres, and a fleet of three million freezer cabinets. Perimeter
Our acquisitions in India and Portugal, completed on 30 March 2026 and 1 April 2026 respectively, have been included in the Group perimeter since 1 April. Consistent with the definition of OSG, their growth has been reflected in reported OSG, OVG, and OPG from the date of business combination.
Turkish Competition Authority Investigation Update
The Turkish Competition Authority (TCA) opened an investigation into our subsidiary in Türkiye focused on the use of freezer cabinets in small retail outlets in May. During this time the TCA announced an interim measure that, at retail points with closed net sales areas of 100 square metres or less, where no other cabinet directly accessible to consumers is present, 30% of the total capacity of each TMICC-owned freezer cabinet shall be allocated to competing products or left empty. We have been complying fully and continue to cooperate with the TCA throughout this process. The investigation is ongoing.

Non-IFRS Financial Measures Definitions
The information in this announcement contains certain measures not defined by, or calculated in accordance with, IFRS, including Organic Sales Growth (OSG), Organic Price Growth (OPG), Organic Volume Growth (OVG), Adjusted EBITDA and Adjusted EBITDA margin. The non-IFRS financial measures presented in this announcement may not be comparable to other similarly titled measures used by other companies, have limitations as analytical tools and should not be considered in isolation, or as a substitute for, financial information presented in compliance with IFRS. The definition and reconciliation with IFRS measures are presented in Appendix A.
Appendix A Definitions and Reconciliation of non-IFRS Financial measures
The sections below provide reconciliations of the closest measures prepared in accordance with IFRS to the non-IFRS measures used by the Group.
Constant currency
The Group uses “constant rate” and “organic” measures primarily for internal performance analysis and targeting purposes. The Group presents certain items, percentages and movements, using constant exchange rates, which do not include the impact of fluctuations in foreign currency exchange rates. Constant currency values are calculated by translating both the current and the prior period local currency amounts using the prior year average exchange rates into euro, except for the local currency of entities that operate in hyperinflationary economies. These currencies are translated into euro using the prior year closing exchange rate before the application of IAS 29.
OSG, OVG, OPG
OSG refers to the increase in revenue for the period, excluding any change in revenue resulting from disposals, changes in currency and price growth in excess of 26%. in hyperinflationary economies. Inflation of 26% per year compounded over three years is one of the key indicators within IAS 29 to assess whether an economy is deemed to be hyperinflationary. The impact of disposals is excluded from OSG for a period of 12 calendar months from the applicable closing date. OSG includes increases or decreases in sales of an acquired business immediately following the business combination, unless a reliable historical baseline is not available for the 12 months prior to the acquisition, in which case sales during the first 12 months of the acquisition are excluded from OSG. The Group believes this measure provides valuable additional information on the organic sales performance of the business and it is a key measure used internally.
OVG is part of OSG and means, for the applicable period, the increase in revenue in such period calculated as the sum of: (i) the increase in revenue attributable to the volume of products sold; and (ii) the increase in revenue attributable to the composition of products sold during such period. OVG therefore excludes any impact on OSG due to changes in prices.
OPG is part of OSG and means, for the applicable period, the increase in revenue attributable to changes in prices during the period. OPG therefore excludes the impact to OSG due to (i) the volume of products sold; and (ii) the composition of products sold during the period. In determining changes in price, the Group excludes the impact of price growth in excess of 26% per year in hyperinflationary economies as explained in OSG above.

Adjusted EBITDA and Adjusted EBITDA margin
Adjusted EBITDA is defined as operating profit before the impact of adjusting items within operating profit and before the impact of depreciation and amortisation. Adjusted EBITDA margin is calculated as Adjusted EBITDA divided by revenue for the period. Those measures are used to evaluate the performance of the Group and its segments. Items are classified as adjusting due to their nature and/or frequency of occurrence. The Group’s management believes this measure provides useful information in understanding and evaluating the Group’s operating results.
