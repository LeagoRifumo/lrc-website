Overview
We are an offshore shallow-water drilling contractor providing worldwide offshore drilling services to the oil and gas industry. Our primary business is the ownership, contracting and operation of jack-up rigs for operations in shallow-water areas (i.e., in water depths up to approximately 400 feet), including the provision of related equipment and work crews to conduct oil and gas drilling and workover operations for exploration and production customers. As of September 30, 2026, our fleet consisted of 29 premium jack-up rigs, all of which were delivered in 2008 or later, and five additional rigs that we acquired jointly, with our 50/50 joint venture partner. In October 2026, one of our rigs was involved in a marine incident offshore Gabon resulting in the sinking of the rig. See ‘Recent Developments’ below for additional information.
Recent Developments
Incident on the Prospector 5
On October 24, 2026, the Company's jack-up drilling rig, Prospector 5, was involved in an incident while conducting preloading operations offshore Gabon in preparation for a planned drilling campaign. The incident resulted in a loss of stability and the subsequent sinking of the rig. At the time of the incident, 115 personnel were onboard the rig. As of the date of issuance of these financial statements, 110 individuals have been safely accounted for, four individuals have been confirmed deceased, and search and rescue efforts remain ongoing for the one individual who remains unaccounted for. The Company activated its emergency response procedures following the incident and continues to work with local authorities, its customer, and other relevant parties in response to the event. Investigations into the cause of the incident are ongoing.
The Company is evaluating the consequences of the incident, including the resulting write-off of the rig, potential insurance recoveries, potential losses, contractual impacts, legal matters, or claims that may arise and other related matters. As of the date of issuance of these financial statements, the ultimate financial impact of the incident cannot be reasonably estimated. Because the incident occurred subsequent to September 30, 2026, it is classified as a non-recognized subsequent event under ASC 855 and as such, no adjustments have been made to the accompanying consolidated financial statements.
The Company maintains insurance coverage for physical damage to drilling units and certain related liabilities, subject to applicable policy terms, conditions, limits and deductibles. The Company has hull and machinery insurance coverage of $200 million for Prospector 5. The rig has been declared a total loss, and the hull and machinery insurance claims leader has recommended payment by participating underwriters of the insured value of Prospector 5, subject to applicable policy terms and conditions. The 2014 built Prospector 5 had a depreciated net book value of $94.8 million as of September 30, 2026. The Company also maintains other insurance policies, including protection and indemnity (P&I) coverage, but does not maintain loss of hire coverage. However, the Company can provide no assurance regarding the amount or timing of any insurance recoveries or the ultimate costs, liabilities or other consequences that may result from this incident or final outcome of the ongoing investigations of the incident.

On September 18, 2026, the Company completed the sale of its 51% equity ownership interest in Perfomex and Perfomex II to its existing partner for a consideration of $5.3 million and $3.4 million, respectively, to simplify its corporate and operating structure in Mexico. Operating revenues
Total operating revenues decreased by $19.0 million to $742.4 million for the nine months ended September 30, 2026 compared to $761.4 million for the same period in 2025. The decrease in total operating revenues is due to a decrease in dayrate revenue of $67.7 million, partially offset by an increase in bareboat charter revenue of $41.3 million and management contract revenue of $7.4 million.
The decrease in dayrate revenues was primarily due to a decrease in the weighted average earned dayrates during the nine months ended September 30, 2026, as compared to the same period in 2025, a decrease in operating days in the Americas related to one rig following its sanction related contract termination in November 2025, and a decrease in operating days in the Middle East related to one rig following the end of its contract in April 2026.
These decreases were partially offset by an increase in operating days, primarily attributable to five rigs that operated during the nine months ended September 30, 2026 with no comparable operations in the prior-year period, one rig in West Africa that generated dayrate revenue in 2026 compared to bareboat charter revenue in 2025, and one rig that operated in both periods but achieved a higher number of operating days in 2026.
The increase in bareboat charter revenue was primarily due to a higher number of operating days in the period, driven by two of the rigs acquired in January 2026, which were operating under a bareboat charter agreement for much of the nine months ended September 30, 2026 and two of our rigs for which contracted operations were suspended for a significant period in 2025.
The increase in management contract revenue was primarily due to a higher number of rigs operating under management contract agreements during the nine months ended September 30, 2026 compared to the same period in 2025, as the operations of certain of our rigs under management contract agreements were suspended for a significant period during the nine months ended September 30,

Gain on disposals
Gain on disposals increased by $0.2 million to $0.4 million for the nine months ended September 30, 2026 compared to $0.2 million for the same period in 2025. Total operating expenses
Total operating expenses, consisting of rig operating and maintenance expenses, depreciation of non-current assets and general and administrative expenses, increased by $154.4 million to $661.3 million for the nine months ended September 30, 2026 compared to $506.9 million for the same period in 2025.
Rig operating and maintenance expenses are costs associated with owning jack-up drilling rigs, including rigs in operation and idle rigs, the cost of rig personnel, insurance, consumables and supplies, equipment rental, and third-party maintenance and service costs. Rig operating and maintenance expenses increased by $129.8 million to $490.3 million for the nine months ended September 30, 2026 compared to $360.5 million for the same period in 2025.
The increase is primarily due to an increase in repair and maintenance expenses related to one rig preparing for its contract in the Americas which commenced operations in September 2026. The increase was also driven by: (i) operating costs incurred by the five rigs acquired in January 2026, (ii) a $20.6 million provision for credit losses, (iii) an increase in activity from two rigs in the Americas and one rig in the Middle East and North Africa region and (iv) operating costs incurred by one rig in West Africa in 2026 compared to operating under a bareboat charter agreement in 2025, offset by a period of approximately 5 months where the rig was temporarily suspended and the operating and maintenance expenses were incurred by the Company, whereas such expenses were recognized by Perfomex before and after the suspension period.
These increases were partially offset by lower expenses from one rig in West Africa and two in the Americas. The decrease for the rig in West Africa was primarily due to a decrease in amortization of deferred costs. The decrease for one of the rigs in the Americas resulted from its temporary suspension during the six months ended June 30, 2025, when operating and maintenance expenses were incurred by the Company, whereas such expenses were recognized by Perfomex before and after the suspension period. The other rig in the Americas incurred lower costs following its sanction related contract termination in November 2025.
Depreciation of non-current assets, primarily our jack-up drilling rigs, increased by $19.5 million to $129.7 million for the nine months ended September 30, 2026, compared to $110.2 million for the same period in 2025. The increase was primarily driven by the acquisition of five jack-up drilling rigs in January 2026 and higher depreciable asset balances resulting from additions to capitalized drilling equipment and long-term maintenance projects.
General and administrative expenses include personnel and facilities costs related to our management and our administrative and support departments. General and administrative expenses increased by $5.1 million to $41.3 million for the nine months ended September 30, 2026 compared to $36.2 million for the same period in 2025. The increase is primarily due to an increase in personnel costs and professional fees.

Note 3 - Recently Issued Accounting Standards
Adoption of new accounting standards
In November 2024, the Financial Accounting Standards Board (“FASB”) issued Accounting Standards Update (“ASU”) 2024-04 Debt—Debt with Conversion and Other Options (Subtopic 470-20). This ASU clarifies the requirements for determining whether certain settlements of convertible debt instruments should be accounted for as an induced conversion. Specifically, an inducement offer is required to provide the debt holder with, at a minimum, the form and amount of consideration issuable under the conversion privileges. An entity should assess whether this criterion is satisfied as of the date the inducement offer is accepted by the holder. If the convertible debt had been exchanged or modified (without being deemed substantially different) within the one-year period leading up to the offer acceptance date, an entity should compare the terms provided in the inducement offer with the terms that existed one year before the offer acceptance date. This ASU was adopted prospectively and is effective for the Company from January 1, 2026.
