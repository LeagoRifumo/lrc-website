WARNER MUSIC GROUP CORP. REPORTS RESULTS FOR FISCAL FOURTH QUARTER AND FULL YEAR ENDED SEPTEMBER 30, 2026
Financial Highlights
•Quarterly and Annual Revenue Reached All-Time Highs, with Full Year High-Single Digit Revenue Growth and Double-Digit Adjusted OIBDA and Adjusted EPS Growth
•Quarterly Recorded Music Subscription Streaming Growth Accelerated to 15% Driven by Per Subscriber Minimum Increases, Positive Industry Trends and Continued Global Market Share Gains
•Overdelivered on FY26 Margin Expansion Guidance of 150-200 Basis Points Driven by Operating Leverage and Cost-Savings Delivery
•2027 Outlook Supported by Healthy Industry and Market Share Trends, Improved Contractual Terms with DSPs and New and Incremental Growth Vectors including AI Licensing

NEW YORK, New York, November 19, 2026—Warner Music Group Corp. today announced its fourth-quarter and full-year financial results for the periods ended September 30, 2026.
“Our Q4 and full-year results demonstrate the success of a multi-year, transformational strategy that sets a new bar for WMG,” said Robert Kyncl, CEO, Warner Music Group. “Our creative prowess paired with a disciplined, return-focused capital allocation approach, and tech-enabled capabilities, positions us to drive sustainable, long-term value for our artists, songwriters, and shareholders in fiscal 2027 and beyond.”
“With revenue reaching an all-time high, led by double-digit subscription streaming growth and margin expansion ahead of our expectation, we delivered against our financial goals in 2026,” said Lou Dickler, Acting CFO. “Supported by contractual per-subscriber minimum increases, sustained global market share gains, and rigorous cost discipline, we enter fiscal 2027 with substantial momentum and a clear roadmap for continued growth.”

Fourth-Quarter Results
Revenue was up 7.1% (or 7.5% in constant currency). Consistent with prior quarters, Recorded Music revenue growth was unfavorably impacted by the termination of the distribution agreement with BMG (the “BMG Termination”), which resulted in $7 million less Recorded Music digital revenue compared to the prior-year quarter. Excluding the BMG Termination, total revenue was up 7.5% (or 7.9% in constant currency).
Digital revenue increased 13.2% (or 13.1% in constant currency) and streaming revenue increased 13.7% (or 13.5% in constant currency). Recorded Music streaming revenue increased 13.4% (or 13.2% in constant currency); however, adjusted for the $7 million impact of the BMG Termination, Recorded Music streaming revenue was up 14.3% (or 14.0% in constant currency) driven by continued market growth, including upside from price and wholesale rate increases, as well as market share gains. Music Publishing streaming revenue increased 15.1% (the same in constant currency). Revenue increases in the quarter were also driven by growth in Recorded Music physical and licensing revenue, partially offset by lower Recorded Music artist services and expanded-rights revenue and Music Publishing performance and mechanical revenue. Music Publishing synchronization revenue remained constant.
Operating income increased by $177 million to $320 million in the quarter from $143 million in the prior-year quarter, primarily due to revenue mix and a $107 million decrease in restructuring and non-cash impairment charges compared to the prior-year quarter, and a $7 million decrease in amortization expense, as well as factors affecting Adjusted OIBDA discussed below, partially offset by a $21 million increase in non-cash stock-based compensation costs due to accelerated expense recognition associated with executive transition and involuntary terminations coupled with higher vesting expense of outstanding awards.
Adjusted OIBDA increased 17.8% from $405 million to $477 million (or 17.2% in constant currency) and Adjusted OIBDA margin increased 2.1 percentage points to 23.8% from 21.7% (or 1.9 percentage points from 21.9% in constant currency), primarily driven by strong operating performance, revenue mix and savings from the Company’s restructuring plans, a portion of which has been reinvested into the Company’s business.
Net income increased by $70 million to $179 million from $109 million in the prior-year quarter. The increase was primarily due to the factors affecting operating income described above, and the impact of exchange rates on the Company’s Euro-denominated debt resulting in a gain of $7 million compared to a loss of $1 million in the prior-year quarter, partially offset by a loss of $1 million on intercompany loans compared to a gain of $13 million in the prior-year quarter. The increase was also partially offset by a tax expense of $98 million in the quarter compared to a benefit of $3 million in the prior-year quarter driven by higher pre-tax income and tax expense recognized in connection with a change in our indefinite reinvestment assertion.
Basic and Diluted earnings per share were $0.34 for both the Class A and Class B shareholders due to net income attributable to the Company in the quarter of $179 million.

As of September 30, 2026, the Company reported a cash balance of $589 million, total debt of $4.704 billion and net debt (defined as total debt, net of deferred financing costs, premiums and discounts, minus cash and equivalents) of $4.115 billion, compared to $3.833 billion at the end of the prior year. Total debt as of September 30, 2026 includes $303 million of subsidiary debt acquired in the Company’s acquisition of Tempo Music Holdings, LLC (“Tempo Music”) and $363 million in loans outstanding under the Beethoven JV. The debt is secured only by certain music rights owned by Tempo Music and the Beethoven JV, respectively, and is nonrecourse to the Company and its subsidiaries, other than Tempo Music and the Beethoven JV, respectively.
Cash provided by operating activities decreased 32% to $158 million from $231 million in the prior-year quarter. The decrease was largely the result of timing of working capital, including the timing of payments related to the Company’s restructuring plans and contractual digital advances, partially offset by strong operating performance. Capital expenditures decreased to $25 million, from $28 million in the prior-year quarter. Free Cash Flow, as defined below, decreased $70 million to $133 million from $203 million in the prior-year quarter.
Full-Year Results
Total revenue increased 10.9% (or 8.9% in constant currency). As previously disclosed, Recorded Music revenue growth was impacted by a $12 million digital revenue settlement in the year and $4 million for performance obligations satisfied in previous periods in the prior year (the “DSP True-Up and Settlement Payments”) as well as $16 million in digital revenue from the settlement of certain infringement cases in the prior year (the “Copyright Settlement”). In addition, revenue growth was unfavorably impacted by the BMG Termination, which resulted in a $29 million decrease in Recorded Music digital revenue compared to the prior year. Music Publishing revenue was impacted by $17 million of revenue in the prior year recognized in connection with the historical matched royalties that were processed to date by the Mechanical Licensing Collective (the “MLC Historical Matched Royalties”). Adjusted for these items, total revenue was up 11.8% (or 9.8% in constant currency).
Digital revenue increased 12.5% (or 10.4% in constant currency) and streaming revenue increased 13.4% (or 11.2% in constant currency). Recorded Music streaming revenue increased 13.5% (or 11.1% in constant currency). Adjusted for the impact of the DSP True-Up and Settlement Payments of $12 million in the current year and $4 million in the prior year, and the impact of the BMG Termination of $29 million in the prior year, Recorded Music streaming revenue increased 14.2% (or 11.8% in constant currency) driven by continued market growth, including upside from price and wholesale rate increases, as well as market share gains. Music Publishing streaming revenue increased 13.0% (or 11.5% in constant currency). Adjusted for the $17 million impact of the MLC Historical Matched Royalties, Music Publishing streaming revenue increased 15.5% (or 13.9% in constant currency). Revenue increases in the year were also driven by growth in Recorded Music artist services and expanded-rights, physical and licensing revenue and Music Publishing performance, synchronization and mechanical revenue.
Operating income increased by $483 million to $1,177 million from $694 million in the prior year. The increase in operating income was primarily due to revenue mix, savings from the Company’s strategic restructuring plans, a portion of which has been reinvested in the Company’s business, and factors that led to the increase in Adjusted OIBDA discussed below, partially offset by a $25 million increase in amortization expense and a $9 million increase in depreciation expense.
Adjusted OIBDA increased 22.7% to $1,770 million from $1,443 million (or 19.4% in constant currency). Adjusted OIBDA margin increased 2.3 percentage points to 23.8% from 21.5% in the prior year (or 2.1 percentage points from 21.7% in constant currency). The increases in Adjusted OIBDA and Adjusted OIBDA margin include the impact of the DSP True-Up and Settlement Payments of $7 million in the current year and $3 million in the prior year, and the impact of the Copyright Settlement of $9 million, the MLC Historical Matched Royalties of $4 million and the BMG Termination of $2 million in the prior year. Excluding the impact of these items, Adjusted OIBDA increased 23.7% (or 20.4% in constant currency) and Adjusted OIBDA margin increased 2.2 percentage points to 23.7% from 21.5% in the prior year (or 2.1 percentage points from 21.6% in constant currency), primarily due to revenue mix and cost savings from the Company’s restructuring plans, a portion of which has been reinvested in the Company’s business, partially offset by unfavorable movements in foreign currency exchange rates of approximately $8 million.
Net income increased by $365 million to $735 million from $370 million in the prior year. The increase in net income was primarily due to the factors described above, partially offset by higher income tax expense of $189 million, driven by higher pre-tax income and tax expense recognized in connection with a change in our indefinite reinvestment assertion in the current year as compared to the tax benefit recognized in the prior year from impairment of EMP.

Fourth-Quarter Results
Recorded Music revenue was up 6.8% (or 7.3% in constant currency), driven by growth across digital, physical, and licensing revenue, partially offset by a decrease in artist services and expanded rights revenue. Excluding the $7 million impact of the BMG Termination in the prior-year quarter, Recorded Music revenue increased 7.3% (or 7.8% in constant currency). Digital revenue was up 12.9% (or 12.8% in constant currency), and streaming revenue was up 13.4% (or 13.2% in constant currency). Adjusted for the $7 million impact of the BMG Termination, Recorded Music streaming revenue was up 14.3% (or 14.0% in constant currency). Streaming revenue reflects growth in subscription revenue of 14.9% (or 14.7% in constant currency), and in ad-supported revenue of 9.1% (or 8.6% in constant currency). Subscription revenue, adjusted by $3 million for the BMG Termination, increased 15.4% (or 15.2% in constant currency). Ad-supported revenue, adjusted by $4 million for the BMG Termination, increased 11.0% (or 10.5% in constant currency). The increase in subscription revenue reflects continued market growth, including upside from price and wholesale rate increases, as well as market share gains and other items such as audits and accruals, including the impact of a $7 million DSP audit payment for historical periods. The increase in ad-supported revenue reflects market share gains in the quarter as well as improved deal economics. Physical revenue increased 28.5% (or 30.5% in constant currency) primarily driven by strong releases in the quarter. Licensing revenue increased 11.9% (the same in constant currency), due to an increase in licensing deals. Artist services and expanded-rights revenue decreased 21.4% (or 19.9% in constant currency) attributable to higher merchandising revenue in the prior-year quarter, primarily from the Company’s partnership with Oasis. Major sellers in the quarter included Madonna, Number_i, Charli XCX, Bruno Mars and Linkin Park.
Recorded Music operating income increased $157 million to $365 million, up from $208 million in the prior-year quarter and operating margin was up 8.7 percentage points to 22.3% versus 13.6% in the prior-year quarter. The increases in operating income and operating income margin were driven by revenue mix, a $75 million decrease in restructuring and non-cash impairment charges compared to the prior-year quarter and a $10 million decrease in amortization expense, as well as the factors affecting Adjusted OIBDA discussed below.

Adjusted OIBDA increased 19.2% (or 18.8% in constant currency), to $423 million from $355 million in the prior-year quarter, and Adjusted OIBDA margin was up 2.7 percentage points to 25.8% from 23.1% in the prior-year quarter (or 2.5 percentage points from 23.3% in constant currency). The increases in Adjusted OIBDA and Adjusted OIBDA margin were primarily driven by revenue growth and strong operating performance, and savings from the Company’s restructuring plans, a portion of which has been reinvested in the Company’s business.
Full-Year Results
Recorded Music revenue was up 10.7% (or 8.7% in constant currency), driven by growth across digital, artist services and expanded-rights, physical and licensing revenue. Excluding the impacts of the DSP True-Up and Settlement Payments of $12 million in the current year and $4 million in the prior year, and the impact of the BMG Termination of $29 million and the Copyright Settlement of $16 million in the prior year, Recorded Music revenue was up 11.5% (or 9.5% in constant currency). Digital revenue was up 12.4% (or 10.1% in constant currency), largely driven by growth in streaming revenue of 13.5% (or 11.1% in constant currency), partially offset by a decrease in download and other digital revenue of 29.2% (or 30.0% in constant currency). Adjusted for the impacts of the DSP True-Up and Settlement Payments of $12 million in the current year and $4 million in the prior year, and the impact of the BMG Termination of $29 million and the Copyright Settlement of $16 million in the prior year, digital revenue was up 13.7% (or 11.3% in constant currency), and streaming revenue was up 14.2% (or 11.8% in constant currency). Streaming revenue reflects growth in subscription revenue of 14.8% (or 12.4% in constant currency) and in ad-supported revenue of 9.5% (or 7.5% in constant currency). Subscription revenue, adjusted for the impacts of the DSP True-Up and Settlement Payments of $12 million in the current year and $4 million in the prior year, and the impact of the BMG Termination of $18 million in the prior year, increased 15.4% (or 12.8% in constant currency). Ad-supported revenue, adjusted by $11 million for the BMG Termination, increased 10.8% (or 8.8% in constant currency). The increase in subscription revenue reflects market growth, including upside from price and wholesale increases, market share gains and strong chart performance, and the increase in ad-supported revenue reflects market share gains and an overall strong performance in the year as well as improved deal economics. Adjusted for the impact of the Copyright Settlement of $16 million in the prior year, download and other digital revenue decreased 13.7% (or 14.9% in constant currency). Artist services and expanded-rights revenue increased 4.9% (or 3.8% in constant currency) attributable to higher concert promotion revenue primarily in France and Spain. Physical revenue increased 12.5% (or 11.5% in constant currency) driven by strong releases in the year as well as catalog and carryover success. Licensing revenue increased 5.5% (or 3.2% in constant currency), primarily driven by other copyright-infringement settlements. Major sellers in the year included Alex Warren, Sombr, Bruno Mars, Linkin Park, and Teddy Swims.
Recorded Music operating income increased to $1,308 million from $850 million in the prior year, and operating margin was up 6.1 percentage points to 21.8% from 15.7% in the prior year. The increase in operating income was primarily due to the factors affecting Adjusted OIBDA discussed below, as well as a $151 million decrease in restructuring and non-cash impairment charges, a $19 million decrease in non-cash stock-based compensation expense and other related costs, a $10 million decrease in depreciation expense, and a $4 million decrease in executive transition costs, partially offset by an $8 million increase in amortization expense.
Adjusted OIBDA increased 22.1% to $1,549 million from $1,269 million (or 18.9% in constant currency) and Adjusted OIBDA margin was up 2.4 percentage points to 25.9% from 23.5% (or 2.3 percentage points from 23.7% in constant currency). The increases in Adjusted OIBDA and Adjusted OIBDA margin include $7 million from the DSP True-Up and Settlement Payments in the year, partially offset by $9 million for the Copyright Settlement, $3 million from the DSP True-Up and Settlement Payments and $2 million from the BMG Termination in the prior year. Excluding the impact of these items, Adjusted OIBDA increased 22.9% (or 19.7% in constant currency) and Adjusted OIBDA margin increased 2.4 percentage points to 25.8% from 23.4% (or 2.2 percentage points from 23.6% in constant currency) attributable to strong operating performance, revenue growth and savings from the Company’s restructuring plans, a portion of which has been reinvested in the Company’s business, partially offset by unfavorable movements in foreign currency exchange rates of approximately $7 million.

Music Publishing revenue increased 7.7% (or 7.4% in constant currency) driven by higher digital revenue, partially offset by lower performance and mechanical revenue. The increases in Adjusted OIBDA and Adjusted OIBDA margin were primarily driven by revenue growth.

About Warner Music Group
With a legacy extending back over 200 years, Warner Music Group today is home to an unparalleled family of creative artists, songwriters, and companies that are moving culture across the globe. At the core of WMG’s Recorded Music division are four of the most iconic companies in history: Atlantic, Elektra, Parlophone and Warner Records. They are joined by renowned labels such as TenThousand Projects, 300 Entertainment, Asylum, Big Beat, Canvasback, East West, Erato, FFRR, Fueled by Ramen, Nonesuch, Reprise, Rhino, Roadrunner, Sire, Spinnin’ Records, Warner Classics and Warner Records Nashville. Warner Chappell Music - which traces its origins back to the founding of Chappell & Company in 1811 - is one of the world's leading music publishers, with a catalog of more than one million copyrights spanning every musical genre from the standards of the Great American Songbook to the biggest hits of the 21st century.

Supplemental Disclosures Regarding Non-GAAP Financial Measures
We evaluate our operating performance based on several factors, including the following non-GAAP financial measures:
Adjusted OIBDA and Adjusted OIBDA margin
We allocate resources and evaluate performance based on several factors, including Adjusted OIBDA. We define Adjusted OIBDA as operating income (loss) adjusted to exclude the following non-operational, discrete, or non-cash items to facilitate period-over-period comparability: (i) non-cash depreciation of tangible assets, (ii) non-cash amortization of intangible assets, (iii) non-cash stock-based compensation and other related expenses, (iv) gains or losses on divestitures, (v) expenses related to restructuring, impairment, and transformation initiatives, which include costs
14
associated with the Company’s financial transformation initiative to design and implement new information technology and upgrade our finance infrastructure, and (vi) executive transition costs. We define Adjusted OIBDA margin as Adjusted OIBDA as a percentage of revenue. Items excluded are not viewed to contribute directly to management’s evaluation of operating results. We consider Adjusted OIBDA and Adjusted OIBDA margin to be important indicators of the operational strengths and performance of our businesses. However, a limitation of the use of Adjusted OIBDA and Adjusted OIBDA margin as performance measures is that they do not reflect the periodic costs of certain capitalized tangible and intangible assets used in generating revenues in our businesses. Accordingly, Adjusted OIBDA and Adjusted OIBDA margin should be considered in addition to, not as a substitute for, operating income (loss), operating income (loss) margin, net income (loss) attributable to Warner Music Group Corp. and other measures of financial performance reported in accordance with United States generally accepted accounting principles (“U.S. GAAP”). In addition, our definitions of Adjusted OIBDA and Adjusted OIBDA margin may differ from similarly titled measures used by other companies.
Adjusted Net Income and Adjusted EPS
We define Adjusted Net Income as net income (loss) attributable to Warner Music Group Corp. adjusted to exclude the following non-operational, discrete, or non-cash items to facilitate period-over-period comparability: (i) non-cash amortization of intangible assets, (ii) expenses related to restructuring and transformation initiatives, which include costs associated with the Company’s financial transformation initiative to design and implement new information technology and upgrade our finance infrastructure, (iii) gains or losses on divestitures, (iv) non-cash stock-based compensation, (v) loss on extinguishment of debt, (vi) executive transition costs, (vii) other (income) expenses, and (viii) certain discrete tax items. Excluding the impact of certain discrete tax items, these exclusions are then further adjusted to account for tax effects. Adjusted Net Income should be considered in addition to, not as a substitute for, net income (loss) attributable to Warner Music Group Corp. and other measures of financial performance reported in accordance with U.S. GAAP. We use Adjusted Net Income to calculate Adjusted Earnings (Loss) Per Share (“EPS”), which we define as Adjusted Net Income divided by the basic weighted-average shares outstanding for the period. Our definition of Adjusted Net Income and Adjusted EPS may differ from similarly titled measures used by other companies.

Constant Currency
Because exchange rates are an important factor in understanding period-to-period comparisons, we believe the presentation of revenue on a constant-currency basis in addition to reported revenue helps improve the ability to understand our operating results and evaluate our performance in comparison to prior periods. Constant-currency information compares results between periods as if exchange rates had remained constant period over period. We use results on a constant-currency basis as one measure to evaluate our performance. We calculate constant-currency results by applying current-year foreign currency exchange rates to prior-year results. However, a limitation of the use of the constant-currency results as a performance measure is that it does not reflect the impact of exchange rates on our revenue. These results should be considered in addition to, not as a substitute for, results reported in accordance with U.S. GAAP. Results on a constant-currency basis, as we present them, may not be comparable to similarly titled measures used by other companies and are not a measure of performance presented in accordance with U.S. GAAP.

Free Cash Flow
Our definition of Free Cash Flow is defined as cash flow provided by operating activities less capital expenditures. We use Free Cash Flow, among other measures, to evaluate our operating performance. Management believes Free Cash Flow provides investors with an important perspective on the cash available to fund our debt service requirements, ongoing working capital requirements, capital expenditure requirements, strategic acquisitions and investments, and any dividends, prepayments of debt or repurchases or retirement of our outstanding debt or notes in open market purchases, privately negotiated purchases, any repurchases of our common stock or otherwise. As a result, Free Cash Flow is a significant measure of our ability to generate long-term value. It is useful for investors to know whether this ability is being enhanced or degraded as a result of our operating performance. We believe the presentation of Free Cash Flow is relevant and useful for investors because it allows investors to view performance in a manner similar to the method management uses.
Free Cash Flow is not a measure of performance calculated in accordance with U.S. GAAP and therefore it should not be considered in isolation of, or as a substitute for, net income (loss) as an indicator of operating performance or cash flow provided by operating activities as a measure of liquidity. Free Cash Flow, as we calculate it, may not be comparable to similarly titled measures employed by other companies. In addition, Free Cash Flow does not necessarily represent funds available for discretionary use and is not necessarily a measure of our ability to fund our cash needs. Because Free Cash Flow deducts capital expenditures from “net cash provided by operating activities” (the most directly comparable U.S. GAAP financial measure), users of this information should consider the types of events and transactions that are not reflected.
