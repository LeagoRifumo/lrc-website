Vermilion Energy Inc. Reports Q3 2026 Results and Releases 2027 Budget with Planned 4% Dividend Increase
CALGARY, AB, Nov. 4, 2026 /CNW/ -- Vermilion Energy Inc. ("Vermilion", "We", "Our", "Us" or the "Company") (TSX: VET) (NYSE: VET) is pleased to report operating and condensed financial results for the three and nine months ended September 30, 2026.
The unaudited interim financial statements and management discussion and analysis for the three and nine months ended September 30, 2026 will be available on the System for Electronic Document Analysis and Retrieval Plus ("SEDAR+") at www.sedarplus.ca, on EDGAR at www.sec.gov/edgar.shtml, and on Vermilion's website at www.vermilionenergy.com.
Highlights
Q3 2026 Results
Vermilion remains on track to deliver its upwardly revised full-year 2026 production guidance of approximately 122,400 boe/d within its capital budget of $620 million. Third quarter production averaged 117,349 boe/d(9) (71% natural gas), reflecting planned maintenance in Ireland, Germany, and Canada. The 32-day turnaround in Ireland was safely completed on-time and on-budget, reflecting Vermilion's track record of operational excellence, with production fully restored in September 2026.
Generated $237 million ($1.55/basic share)(2) of fund flows from operations ("FFO")(1) and over $90 million of free cash flow ("FCF")(6), fully funding $146 million of exploration and development ("E&D") capital expenditures(3).
Reduced net debt(7) by approximately $60 million to $1.17 billion at September 30, 2026, bringing net debt reduction to approximately $900 million over the past 18 months.
Returned $41 million to shareholders through dividends and share buybacks, including $21 million in dividends and $20 million of share repurchases, twice the value repurchased through the first half of 2026.
Realized an average natural gas sales price of $5.60/mcf, more than triple the AECO benchmark, reflecting structural exposure to premium international gas markets and portfolio diversification. Month ahead TTF averaged $25/MMBtu in the quarter and current prices for key European gas markets remain elevated.
Reported net loss of $35 million ($0.23/basic share), driven by unrealized losses on derivative instruments, partially offset by strong European gas and crude oil pricing. The unrealized losses on derivative instruments primarily reflect significant increases in forward oil and European gas prices during the quarter. These higher forward prices negatively impact current reported earnings through mark-to-market valuation of existing hedges, while improving Vermilion's revenue and FFO outlook.
In Germany, the Company completed the acquisition of producing assets, while bringing the Wisselshorst Z1a well (0.64 net) on production in July 2026. The acquired assets and production from Wisselshorst position Vermilion's German business for production of approximately 7,500 boe/d in Q4 2026(12), with current operating netbacks(10) supporting robust excess free cash flow ("EFCF")(6) generation.
In the Deep Basin, the Company had multiple wells ranked among the most prolific in Alberta during the quarter, including a condensate-rich well that produced 12.6 mmcf/d of natural gas and over 400 bbls/d of condensate in the month of August. In addition, the Company drilled an Upper Mannville well to nearly 7,800 metres, representing the longest Upper Mannville well drilled by industry to-date.
In the Montney, the Company brought a four-well pad in Alberta on production, piloting a new completion approach to reduce sand and water recovery. These wells have been impacted by third-party downtime, which has extended into Q4 2026, but have produced in-line with expectations when capacity is available.
In Canada, unit operating expenses are down approximately 10% year-over-year, driven by realized synergies and Vermilion's continued focus on operational excellence. At a corporate level, Vermilion continues to see lower general and administration and interest expenses, down approximately 30% year-over-year.
2027 Budget
Vermilion's Board of Directors has approved an E&D capital budget of $675 to $725 million for 2027. The program builds on the Company's operational momentum and includes drilling and infrastructure investment in Germany and the Netherlands, continued development of the Montney and a steady three-rig program targeting high-return, liquids-rich gas opportunities in the Deep Basin.
The Company expects to deliver annual average production of 122,000 to 126,000 boe/d, approximately 71% natural gas, while maintaining its focus on capital efficiency and EFCF generation. This guidance range represents 2% absolute growth and 5% production per share growth from 2026. Of note, $150 million of the 2027 E&D investments are forecasted to add production starting 2028, consistent with our five-year plan presented in Q4 2025.
Vermilion plans to invest approximately $385 million of E&D capital in its liquids-rich gas assets in the Montney and Deep Basin in 2027. In the Deep Basin, the Company plans to drill 41 (36.3 net) wells, with minimal infrastructure requirements, while continuing strategic investments to further strengthen depth of inventory. In the Montney, the Company plans to drill 13 (13.0 net) wells and continue expanding owned infrastructure.
Vermilion plans to invest approximately $315 million across its International assets in 2027, an increase of over 50% compared to 2026, allocating capital to high-return drilling prospects and growth infrastructure. In Germany, the Company plans to drill two (1.3 net) follow-up wells at Wisselshorst, while also continuing to invest in the development of key infrastructure. In the Netherlands, the Company plans to drill two (1.0 net) wells, targeting larger structures than those pursued in recent drilling programs, while in Australia, Vermilion plans to commence a two-well (2.0 net) drilling program in the second half of 2027.
Targeting a 4% increase in the quarterly cash dividend to $0.14 per share, effective with the Q1 2027 dividend payable on March 31, 2027, subject to approval by the Company's Board of Directors in March 2027.
Outlook
Q4 2026 production is expected to average 121,000 boe/d, with full-year production of approximately 122,400 boe/d on E&D capital expenditures of approximately $620 million(12). In Canada, the Company has elected to defer several new well start-ups and restrict gas production to align with expected higher pricing later in Q4 2026. Vermilion's return of capital framework targeting 40% to 60% of EFCF to shareholders remains in place as it enables flexible capital allocation to maximize shareholder value during a time of high market volatility.
Declared a quarterly cash dividend of $0.135 per common share, payable on December 31, 2026, to shareholders of record on December 15, 2026.

(1) | Fund flows from operations (FFO) is a total of segments and non-GAAP financial measure most directly comparable to net (loss) earnings and is calculated as sales less royalties, transportation expense, operating expense, G&A expense, corporate income tax (recovery) expense, PRRT expense, interest expense, equity based compensation settled in cash, realized (gain) loss on derivatives, realized foreign exchange (gain) loss, and realized other (income) expense. The measure is used by management to assess the contribution of each business unit to Vermilion's ability to generate income necessary to pay dividends, repay debt, fund asset retirement obligations, and make capital investments. FFO does not have a standardized meaning under IFRS® Accounting Standards and therefore may not be comparable to similar measures provided by other issuers. More information and a reconciliation to net (loss) earnings, the most directly comparable primary financial statement measure, can be found in the "Non-GAAP and Other Specified Financial Measures" section of this document. Fund flows from continuing operations and fund flows from discontinued operations are calculated in the same manner as FFO and are most directly comparable to net (loss) earnings from continuing operations and net earnings (loss) discontinued operations, respectively.
(2) | Fund flows from operations per basic and diluted share are non-GAAP ratios calculated by dividing fund flows from operations (a total of segments measure and non-GAAP financial measure) by basic weighted average shares outstanding and diluted weighted average shares outstanding, respectively. Diluted weighted average shares outstanding includes incremental shares issuable under Vermilion's equity-based compensation plans, as determined using the treasury stock method. Management uses these measures to assess operating performance on a per-share basis, including the impact of issued and potentially issuable common shares. More information on FFO and a reconciliation to net (loss) earnings, the most directly comparable primary financial statement measure, can be found in the "Non-GAAP and Other Specified Financial Measures" section of this document. Fund flows from continuing operations and discontinued operations per basic and diluted share are calculated on the same basis.
(3) | Capital expenditures is a non-GAAP financial measure most directly comparable to cash flows used in investing activities and is calculated as the sum of drilling and development costs and exploration and evaluation costs. Management considers capital expenditures to be a useful measure of our investment in our existing asset base. Capital expenditures does not have a standardized meaning under IFRS Accounting Standards and therefore may not be comparable to similar measures provided by other issuers. More information and a reconciliation to cash flows used in investing activities, the most directly comparable primary financial statement measure, can be found in the "Non-GAAP and Other Specified Financial Measures" section of this document. Capital expenditures is also referred to as E&D capital expenditures.
(4) | Acquisitions is a non-GAAP financial measure and is not a standardized financial measure under IFRS Accounting Standards and therefore may not be comparable to similar measures disclosed by other issuers. Acquisitions is calculated as the sum of acquisitions, net of cash acquired, acquisitions of securities and net acquired working capital (deficit). Management believes that including these components provides a useful measure of the economic investment associated with our acquisition activity and is most directly comparable to cash flows used in investing activities. More information and a reconciliation to acquisitions, net of cash acquired and acquisition of securities, the most directly comparable primary financial statement measure, can be found in the "Non-GAAP and Other Specified Financial Measures" section of this document.
(5) | Dispositions is a non-GAAP financial measure and is not a standardized financial measure under IFRS Accounting Standards and therefore may not be comparable to similar measures disclosed by other issuers. Dispositions is calculated as the sum of dispositions, and disposition of securities. Management believes that including these components provides a useful measure of the proceeds associated with our disposition activities and is most directly comparable to cash flows used in investing activities. More information and a reconciliation to dispositions and disposition of securities, the most directly comparable primary financial statement measures, can be found in the "Non-GAAP and Other Specified Financial Measures" section of this document.
(6) | Free cash flow (FCF) and excess free cash flow (EFCF) are non-GAAP financial measures most directly comparable to cash flows from operating activities. FCF is calculated as FFO less drilling and development costs and exploration and evaluation costs and EFCF is calculated as FCF less payments on lease obligations and asset retirement obligations settled. FCF is used by management to determine the funding available for investing and financing activities including payment of dividends, repayment of long-term debt, reallocation into existing business units and deployment into new ventures. EFCF is used by management to determine the funding available to return to shareholders after costs attributable to normal business operations. FCF and EFCF do not have standardized meanings under IFRS Accounting Standards and therefore may not be comparable to similar measures provided by other issuers. More information and a reconciliation to cash flows from operating activities, the most directly comparable primary financial statement measure, can be found in the "Non-GAAP and Other Specified Financial Measures" section of this document.

Message to Shareholders
Vermilion is delivering on our plan to structurally improve excess free cash flow ("EFCF")(1), with operational momentum continuing to build as we progress the key milestones communicated during our Q4 2025 Investor Day. The rate of change in profitability is being reflected in operational results and outlook, with improvements to our capital efficiencies, base declines and controllable cost structure. Our internationally diversified portfolio is benefiting from elevated pricing for European gas, Canadian condensate and Dated Brent oil. The combination of delivering on production and cost targets while benefiting from higher prices has enabled both increased shareholder returns and debt reduction.
The third quarter of 2026 was focused on the execution of planned maintenance activities, together with the continued integration of the recently acquired German assets. All planned maintenance activities were completed on schedule and without incident, reflecting Vermilion's commitment to safety and operational excellence. With maintenance work complete, production fully restored and the German integration progressing, Vermilion expects European gas production of approximately 100 mmcf/d in Q4 2026(2). At the corporate level, annual production is forecasted to be approximately 122,400 boe/d(2), above the original mid-point of guidance despite unplanned Australia downtime and profitability-driven shut-ins and deferrals of Canadian gas wells.
Strong operational execution was complemented by increased shareholder returns and further debt reduction. Following the announced increase of Vermilion's return of capital target to 40% to 60% of EFCF, the Company paid $21 million in dividends and repurchased $20 million of shares during the quarter, twice the value repurchased through the first half of the year. Through the first nine months of 2026, Vermilion has returned $92 million to shareholders while reducing net debt(3) by nearly $180 million, which represents $1.76/basic share(4) of combined debt reduction and shareholder returns year-to-date.
Following the close of the largest cash acquisition in the Company's history, Vermilion has reduced net debt by approximately $900 million while growing production per share by approximately 50%. Fourth quarter fund flows from operations ("FFO")(5) is currently forecasted to be significantly higher than Q3 2026, supported by restored production and higher European gas and liquids volumes, as well as elevated European gas prices. With operational tailwinds and commodity price support, the Company is well positioned to accelerate debt reduction and shareholder returns.
Vermilion is also pleased to announce a 2027 E&D capital budget of $675 million to $725 million, with associated production guidance of 122,000 to 126,000 boe/d, which represents 2% absolute growth and 5% production per share growth from 2026(2). The 2027 program builds on the Company's operational momentum, with increased drilling activity across the International business, a three-rig Deep Basin program and continued advancement of the Mica Montney development. The program also includes strategic infrastructure investment in Germany and the Montney that will support production growth well beyond 2027. The 2027 budget includes the full capital investment for the two-well Australia drilling program, where production is expected to commence in early 2028. In total, 2027 capital includes approximately $150 million of investment that will facilitate future growth but will not contribute to 2027 production. As these projects are completed, Vermilion expects corporate capital investment in 2028 to return toward 2026 levels while maintaining the production and capital objectives established at the Q4 2025 Investor Day. Our EFCF estimate has increased by $200 million compared to the Investor Day outlook, as we are now projected to generate $1.9 billion over the five year period. This revised estimate factors in the outperformance in 2026 along with 2027 budget and strip pricing, while maintaining the original assumptions between 2028 and 2030. The Company also plans to increase the base dividend by 4% in 2027 to an annual level of $0.56/share, representing the sixth consecutive year of dividend increases, consistent with its commitment to sustainable, ratable dividend growth. Vermilion's dividend payout(6) remains among the lowest in the Canadian E&P sector at sub-10% of FFO, offering capacity for future increases.
Q3 2026 Review
In Q3 2026, Vermilion generated $237 million of FFO on E&D capital expenditures of $146 million, resulting in free cash flow ("FCF")(1) of over $90 million that was primarily allocated to the balance sheet and shareholder returns. Net debt was reduced by approximately $60 million, while shareholder returns increased quarter-over-quarter to over $40 million, with dividends of $21 million and the repurchase of $20 million of shares. During the quarter, the Company recorded non-cash, price-related losses on risk management contracts of $245 million ($188 million net of taxes), driven by higher forward oil and European gas prices. These mark-to-market losses impacted current reported earnings but do not reflect the underlying cash generation of the business, while higher forward prices improve Vermilion's future revenue and FFO outlook.
During the quarter, Vermilion continued to advance key operational initiatives across its international portfolio. In Germany, the Company closed the acquisition of producing assets announced earlier this year, while bringing the Wisselshorst Z1a well (0.64 net) on production in July 2026. Together, the acquired assets and production from Wisselshorst position Vermilion's German business for production of approximately 7,500 boe/d in Q4 2026(2). The Wisselshorst well produced gross volumes of 3 to 5 mmcf/d in the quarter, in-line with expectations. Construction on the debottlenecking pipeline is planned for early 2027, and is expected to increase productive capacity to 15 to 17 mmcf/d by the end of 2027. European gas production during the quarter was impacted by planned turnaround activity in Ireland and non-operated downtime in Germany, temporarily reducing consolidated European gas production to 81 mmcf/d. All work was completed safely and on schedule, and production has been fully restored, positioning Vermilion for fourth quarter European gas production of approximately 100 mmcf/d(2). Subsequent to the quarter, the Company spudded one well (0.5 net) in the Netherlands, with first production expected in the first half of 2027.
In Canada, optimized capital allocation within the portfolio enabled the Company to add five net wells in 2026 while managing to the mid-point of our corporate E&D capital guidance. Across our Canadian business, unit operating expenses are down approximately 10% year-over-year, driven by realized synergies and Vermilion's continued focus on operational excellence. In Q3 2026, Vermilion set a new industry benchmark for well length in the Deep Basin and successfully implemented pilots to reduce well costs in the Alberta Montney. In the Deep Basin, the Company had multiple wells ranked among the most prolific in Alberta during the quarter, including a condensate-rich well that produced 12.6 mmcf/d of natural gas and over 400 bbls/d of condensate in the month of August. In addition, the Company drilled the longest Upper Mannville well by industry to-date at nearly 7,800 metres (4.8 miles), illustrating the technical competence of our teams, while improving returns on invested capital. This well exemplifies Vermilion's continuous push to improve risk-adjusted economic outcomes. Deep Basin production was temporarily lower during the quarter due to planned third-party maintenance and the timing of drilling activity, with activity expected to increase in the fourth quarter. In addition, the Company elected to defer several new well start-ups and restrict gas production to align with expected higher pricing later in Q4 2026. In the Montney, the Company brought a four-well pad in Alberta on production, piloting a new completion approach to reduce sand and water recovery. Subsequent to the quarter, the Company established a new drilling pace benchmark on a pad expected to be completed and brought online in the coming months. Vermilion remains focused on translating these operating improvements into repeatable reductions to our $8.2 million per well DCET cost. The wells brought online in Q3 2026 have been impacted by third-party downtime, which has extended into Q4 2026, but have produced in-line with expectations when capacity is available.
2027 Budget and Guidance
Vermilion's Board of Directors has approved an E&D capital budget of $675 million to $725 million for 2027, which includes $150 million of investment that will facilitate future growth, but will not contribute to 2027 production. The Company expects to deliver annual average production of 122,000 to 126,000 boe/d, approximately 71% natural gas, while maintaining its focus on capital efficiency and EFCF generation. European gas production is expected to be in-line with 2026 as natural declines at Corrib are offset primarily with year-over-year growth in Germany. With a successful 2027 Wisselshorst drill program, European gas production is expected to increase by mid-2028, with production from new discoveries brought online and the debottlenecking pipeline from the Wisselshorst discovery well complete. Vermilion estimates stay-flat capital at $565 million per year, which backstops our capital allocation and shareholder return plan looking through 2027 and beyond.
Canada
Vermilion plans to invest approximately $385 million of E&D capital in its liquids-rich gas assets in the Montney and Deep Basin in 2027. In the Deep Basin, the Company plans to operate a steady three-rig program and drill 41 (36.3 net) wells, with minimal infrastructure requirements and drill capital primarily directed to proven developments. In the Montney, the Company plans to drill 13 (13.0 net) wells and continue expanding owned infrastructure. The revised timing of third-party gas processing infrastructure in Northeast British Columbia is expected to shift the Company's 28,000 boe/d target production rate to early 2029 from mid-2028. Montney volumes are forecasted to grow by 10% in 2027, while generating positive EFCF on current pricing. The capital required to achieve the 28,000 boe/d target production rate continues to decline, with approximately $45 million of remaining infrastructure spend and approximately 28 wells to drill between 2027 and 2028. In the interim, Vermilion will reallocate capital to the Deep Basin, where recent wells have materially outperformed expectations, demonstrating the strength of the Company's diversified approach. Once the Montney reaches its target production rate, annual drilling is expected to moderate to approximately eight wells to maintain production, with no further material infrastructure expansion anticipated.
International
Vermilion plans to invest approximately $315 million across its International assets in 2027, focused on advancing high-value European natural gas opportunities and returning to drilling in Australia. In Germany, the Company plans to build on the increased scale and strong economics of its expanded business. The 2027 program includes two (1.3 net) follow-up wells at Wisselshorst, with spud planned for the first half of the year, along with continued development of key infrastructure. This includes a debottlenecking pipeline that adds a connection to the broader gas distribution network to support higher production from the discovery. The two (1.3 net) follow-up wells at Wisselshorst will be drilled back-to-back using the same drilling rig, and the Company expects cost savings of approximately 20% along with 30% improvement in cycle time compared to previous drilling programs. In the Netherlands, the Company plans to drill two (1.0 net) wells, targeting larger structures than those pursued in recent drilling programs. In Australia, Vermilion plans to commence a two-well (2.0 net) drilling program in the second half of 2027, aligning with a broader industry multi-well drilling program to reduce mobilization and demobilization costs. The 2027 budget includes the full capital associated with the Australia program, with production expected to commence in early 2028. The Company will also continue to fund economic workover and optimization projects across the International portfolio.
Financial Outlook and Return of Capital
Vermilion remains confident in its ability to achieve or exceed the five-year plan communicated at the Investor Day in Q4 2025, based on operational momentum through 2026, as well as improving capital efficiency and a line of sight to lower base decline. As the Company executes strategic capital investments and increases operational scale across its key growth assets, unit operating, transportation, general and administration, and interest costs are expected to reduce by approximately 5% to 10% over the five-year plan compared to 2026 levels(2), driving enhanced profitability and EFCF generation. The Company's capital allocation priorities remain unchanged, with a target to return 40% to 60% of EFCF to shareholders through sustainable and growing base dividends and share buybacks, while continuing to strengthen the balance sheet. Since 2022, Vermilion has repurchased and cancelled more than 22 million shares, supporting per-share production growth of over 50%. The lower share count enables the targeted 4% increase in the quarterly dividend per share in 2027 to result in little to no increase in cash outlay compared to the 2026 dividend, while the Company's dividend payout remains below 10% of fund flows from operations. The increased quarterly cash dividend of $0.14 per share is expected to be effective with the Q1 2027 dividend payable on March 31, 2027, subject to approval by the Company's Board of Directors in March 2027.

Fund flows from operations per basic and diluted share: FFO per basic share and diluted share are non-GAAP ratios. Fund flows from operations per basic share is calculated by dividing fund flows from operations (total of segments measure) by the basic weighted average shares outstanding as defined under IFRS Accounting Standards. Fund flows from operations per diluted share is calculated by dividing fund flows from operations by the sum of basic weighted average shares outstanding and incremental shares issuable under the equity based compensation plans as determined using the treasury stock method. About Vermilion
