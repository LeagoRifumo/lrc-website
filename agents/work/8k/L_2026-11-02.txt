LOEWS CORPORATION REPORTS NET INCOME OF $306 MILLION
FOR THE THIRD QUARTER OF 2026
New York, NY, November 2, 2026: Loews Corporation (NYSE: L) today released its third quarter 2026 financial results.
Third Quarter 2026 highlights:
Loews Corporation reported net income of $306 million, or $1.50 per share, in the third quarter of 2026, compared to $504 million, or $2.43 per share, in the third quarter of 2025. The following are key highlights of our third quarter results:
•CNA Financial Corporation’s (NYSE: CNA) net income attributable to Loews Corporation decreased year-over-year primarily due to an unfavorable impact of $125 million (after-tax and noncontrolling interest) in 2026 from its annual long-term care reserve assumption updates, as well as lower Property and Casualty underlying underwriting results and higher catastrophe losses. The unfavorable long-term care reserve charge will almost entirely be offset by higher investment yields from recent investment portfolio actions. This anticipated benefit will flow through investment income over time.
•Boardwalk Pipelines’ net income decreased year-over-year primarily due to higher operating expenses, including $14 million (after-tax) of charges related to net unrealized losses at Continuum and an impairment charge associated with the relocation of Boardwalk’s corporate office. Excluding those items, results improved year-over-year, benefiting from higher gas transportation revenues.
•Loews Hotels’ net income increased year-over-year primarily due to a higher overall average daily rate and an increase in occupied room nights across most of the portfolio, partially offset by lower equity income from joint ventures.
•Corporate segment net income decreased year-over-year primarily due to lower investment income from the parent company trading portfolio.
•Book value per share decreased to $90.24 as of September 30, 2026, from $90.71 as of December 31, 2025.
•Book value per share, excluding AOCI, increased to $100.67 as of September 30, 2026, from $95.89 as of December 31, 2025.
•On September 30, 2026, the parent company had $4.4 billion of cash and investments and $1.8 billion of debt.
•Loews Corporation repurchased 1.6 million shares of its common stock during the third quarter of 2026 for a total cost of $167 million.

Definitions of Non-GAAP Measures and Reconciliation of GAAP Measures to Non-GAAP Measures:
CNA Financial Corporation
Core income is calculated by excluding from CNA’s net income attributable to Loews Corporation the after-tax effects of investment gains or losses and gains or losses resulting from pension settlement transactions. In addition, core income excludes the effects of noncontrolling interests. The calculation of core income excludes investment gains or losses because they are generally driven by economic factors that are not necessarily reflective of CNA’s primary insurance operations. The calculation of core income excludes gains or losses resulting from pension settlement transactions as they result from decisions regarding CNA’s defined benefit pension plans which are unrelated to its primary insurance operations.

In evaluating the results of Property & Casualty operations, CNA utilizes the loss ratio, the underlying loss ratio, the expense ratio, the dividend ratio, the combined ratio and the underlying combined ratio. These ratios are calculated using GAAP financial results. The loss ratio is the percentage of net incurred claim and claim adjustment expenses to net earned premiums. The underlying loss ratio excludes the impact of catastrophe-related reinstatement premiums, catastrophe losses and development-related items from the loss ratio. Development-related items represent net prior year loss reserve and premium development, and include the effects of interest accretion and change in allowance for uncollectible reinsurance. The expense ratio is the percentage of insurance underwriting and acquisition expenses, including the amortization of deferred acquisition costs, to net earned premiums. The dividend ratio is the ratio of policyholders’ dividends incurred to net earned premiums. The combined ratio is the sum of the loss ratio, the expense ratio and the dividend ratio. The underlying combined ratio is the sum of the underlying loss ratio, the expense ratio and the dividend ratio. The underlying loss ratio and the underlying combined ratio are deemed to be non-GAAP financial measures, and management believes some investors may find these ratios useful to evaluate CNA’s underwriting performance since they remove the impact of catastrophes which are unpredictable as to timing and amount, and development-related items as they are not indicative of current year underwriting performance.

Loews Corporation Third Quarter 2026 Earnings Remarks
Ben Tisch, CEO:
Loews reported net income of $306 million, or $1.50 per share, in the third quarter, compared with $504 million, or $2.43 per share, a year ago. The largest driver of the year-over-year decline was CNA's annual long-term care reserve review, which resulted in a $125 million reduction in net income attributable to Loews. I'd like to spend a few minutes discussing this charge because, while it’s meaningful from a GAAP earnings perspective, we expect the economic impact to be closer to breakeven.
CNA increased its long-term care reserves by $173 million pre-tax, primarily reflecting higher inflation expectations on future claim costs. While that increase is certainly not welcome, there's an important accounting nuance here that I think is worth understanding.
Historically, when evaluating our long-term care reserves, we would update our assumptions for both future claim inflation and the investment yields we expect to earn on the assets backing those liabilities. That approach made intuitive economic sense. Over long periods of time, interest rates and inflation tend to move together, and the inflation in long-term care claims has historically been broadly correlated with the consumer price index. Higher inflation might mean higher future claims, but it also generally means higher interest rates and, consequently, higher portfolio yields on the assets backing those claims.
That relationship changed with the adoption of LDTI accounting. Under the new rules, while we continue to update our assumptions for future claim inflation, we are no longer permitted to reflect changes in expected portfolio yields in the calculation of our reserves. Instead, we must use the investment return assumptions established when LDTI was adopted.
Think about what that means in today's environment. We're required to recognize the impact of higher expected future claims, but we're not permitted to recognize the offsetting benefit of the considerably higher investment yields available to us today. In other words, for income statement purposes, one side of the economic equation gets updated while the other remains frozen in time. The impact of inflation changes is felt immediately, while the impact of
Page 1 of 10
investment yields is dribbled in as earned over time. It's an asymmetry that investors should understand when evaluating our long-term care results.
And this isn't just a theoretical argument. As I discussed last quarter, CNA has been deliberately extending the duration of the investment portfolio backing its long-term care liabilities, locking in today's higher yields for longer periods while simultaneously improving credit quality. During 2026, CNA completed more than $1.3 billion of these trades, exchanging shorter-duration, BBB-rated bonds yielding 5.5% for longer-duration, A-rated bonds yielding 6.2%.
When we consider these investment actions, along with the higher yields available on future reinvestments, we believe the additional investment income should substantially, if not entirely, offset the higher expected claims identified in this year's reserve review. Said another way, while the accounting requires us to recognize a charge today, we expect the ultimate economic impact to be little to none.
We remain confident that our long-term care block is appropriately reserved. CNA maintains a substantial $1.3 billion statutory margin and continues to improve the economics of the runoff through rate increases, investment actions and policy buyouts. Active policy counts have declined by 50% since 2015 and are expected to fall another 40% over the next decade. We believe reserves have reached a plateau and should gradually decline over the coming years as the block continues to run off and the benefits of the portfolio changes will be felt through net investment income over time.
Turning to CNA's property and casualty business, the market remains competitive. The underlying loss ratio was 64.1%, consistent with the first half of the year but about two points higher than the third quarter of 2025, driven in part by elevated loss cost trends in longer-tail casualty lines.
It is worth remembering, however, that underwriting margin is only one part of the economics of these businesses. In longer-tail casualty lines, premiums can be invested for years before claims are ultimately paid. At today's investment yields, these businesses can continue to generate attractive returns on equity even at lower underwriting margins. As a result, capital is likely to remain plentiful and competition strong, and I would not count on a meaningful

improvement in the pricing environment in the near term. This is a time for underwriting discipline, not for chasing growth, and CNA is managing the business accordingly.
At Boardwalk, the backdrop could hardly be more different. The business continues to benefit from higher transportation rates, increased pipeline utilization and strong demand for natural gas infrastructure. During the quarter, Boardwalk's revenue backlog grew by almost $600 million, largely as a result of the new Carthage Crossing project.
For an expected investment of approximately $90 million, Boardwalk will add compression to increase capacity on its existing system from Carthage, Texas, to northern Louisiana. This is exactly the kind of opportunity we like: deploying incremental capital into an existing system, with long-term contractual visibility and an investment-grade counterparty. Projects like this can generate highly attractive returns while carrying considerably less execution risk than a greenfield pipeline. Speaking of greenfield pipes, while we have nothing to report today, I can say that demand and activity continue at a robust pace, and I’d expect us to add to the existing $3.6 billion project backlog in the quarters and years ahead.
Loews Hotels had another very good quarter, with Adjusted EBITDA increasing 25% to $86 million. The improvement reflected stronger performance across most of the portfolio, particularly in Miami and Arlington.
Construction of Americana by Loews Hotels in Arlington continues to progress on time and on budget. Arlington is a market where our strategy is working exactly as designed: locating hotels next to major demand generators and pairing that leisure demand with substantial meeting space that attracts group business. Americana will add a third hotel to an already successful campus and expand our ability to serve both group and leisure travelers in a market we know well.
Looking ahead, we remain excited about the growth opportunities at Boardwalk and the addition of Americana to our Arlington campus. While the insurance market remains soft, we are confident in CNA's ability to grow profitably without compromising underwriting discipline.
The diversity of our portfolio remains a significant advantage. Our decentralized model allows each management team to focus on running its business, while our strong balance sheet gives us the flexibility to allocate capital where we see the most attractive long-term returns.

For the third quarter of 2026, Loews reported net income of $306 million, or $1.50 per share, compared with $504 million, or $2.43 per share, in the prior-year quarter. The year-over-year decline primarily reflected a $125 million after-tax impact from CNA’s annual long-term care reserve review. Declines in CNA’s P&C underwriting income and parent company net investment income also pressured results, partially offset by strong growth at Loews Hotels.
Book value per share was $90.24, compared with $90.71 at year-end 2025. Higher interest rates during the quarter increased unrealized losses on CNA’s fixed income investments, which are reflected in AOCI. Excluding AOCI, book value per share increased to $100.67 at the end of the third quarter of 2026, compared with $95.89 at year-end 2025.
CNA contributed $180 million of net income to Loews, compared with $371 million in the third quarter of 2025. The company’s annual reserve assumption review in the third quarter resulted in a $173 million pre-tax increase to its GAAP reserves, reducing Loews’s net income by $125 million. This charge primarily reflected higher near-term cost-of-care inflation and unfavorable morbidity, partially offset by better-than-expected premium rate actions.
From an economic perspective, the unfavorable assumption changes are expected to be almost entirely offset by higher investment yields from recent investment portfolio extension trades. The distinction is timing: GAAP accounting requires CNA to recognize the reserve charge immediately, while the expected benefit of higher investment yields will flow through investment income over time.
CNA’s P&C underwriting income declined over the prior year’s third quarter, driven by a 4.2-point increase in the combined ratio to 97.0%. The underlying loss ratio rose 2.2 points, and the expense ratio increased 0.6 points, although both were in line with the first half of the year. Catastrophes were 1.3 points higher than in the prior-year period, which was a particularly light quarter. The P&C net prior-period development impact on the combined ratio was flat in the current quarter. Unfavorable development in excess casualty and professional and management liability was offset by favorable development in workers’ compensation , property, surety and international.

These earnings remarks contain financial measures that are not in accordance with accounting principles generally accepted in the United States of America ("GAAP"). Management believes some investors may find these measures useful to evaluate our and our subsidiaries’ financial performance. CNA utilizes underlying loss ratio and underlying combined ratio, Boardwalk Pipelines utilizes earnings before interest, income tax expense, depreciation and amortization (“EBITDA”) and Loews Hotels & Co utilizes Adjusted EBITDA. These non-GAAP measures are defined and reconciled to the most comparable GAAP measures.

Definitions of Non-GAAP Measures and Reconciliation of GAAP Measures to Non-GAAP Measures
CNA Financial Corporation
In evaluating the results of Property & Casualty operations, CNA utilizes the loss ratio, the underlying loss ratio, the expense ratio, the dividend ratio, the combined ratio and the underlying combined ratio. These ratios are calculated using GAAP financial results. The loss ratio is the percentage of net incurred claim and claim adjustment expenses to net earned premiums. The underlying loss ratio excludes the impact of catastrophe-related reinstatement premiums, catastrophe losses and development-related items from the loss ratio. Development-related items represent net prior year loss reserve and premium development, and include the effects of interest accretion and change in allowance for uncollectible reinsurance. The expense ratio is the percentage of insurance underwriting and acquisition expenses, including the amortization of deferred acquisition costs, to net earned premiums. The dividend ratio is the ratio of policyholders’ dividends incurred to net earned premiums. The combined ratio is the sum of the loss ratio, the expense ratio and the dividend ratio. The underlying combined ratio is the sum of the underlying loss ratio, the expense ratio and the dividend ratio. The underlying loss ratio and the underlying combined ratio are deemed to be non-GAAP financial measures, and management believes some investors may find these ratios useful to evaluate CNA’s underwriting performance since they remove the impact of catastrophes which are unpredictable as to timing and amount, and development-related items as they are not indicative of current year underwriting performance.

Loews Hotels & Co
Adjusted EBITDA is calculated by excluding from Loews Hotels & Co’s EBITDA, the noncontrolling interest share of EBITDA adjustments, gains or losses on asset acquisitions and dispositions, asset impairments, and equity method income, and including Loews Hotels & Co’s pro rata Adjusted EBITDA of equity method investments. Pro rata Adjusted EBITDA of equity method investments is calculated by applying Loews Hotels & Co’s ownership percentage to the underlying equity method investment’s components of Adjusted EBITDA and excluding distributions in excess of basis.
