Quarter-on-quarter performance
Profits decreased S/ 128.3 million QoQ, mainly due to a S/ 84.8 million increase in impairment losses on loans, largely reflecting provisions related to the El Niño phenomenon (FEN) at Interbank, a S/ 52.3 million decrease in other income, a S/ 27.5 million negative variation in translation result and a S/ 19.3 million increase in other expenses. These effects were partially offset by increases of S/ 26.4 million in net interest and similar income and S/ 13.6 million in insurance results, and a S/ 18.2 million decrease in income tax.
The S/ 84.8 million QoQ increase in impairment losses on loans was driven by S/ 185 million of proactive FEN-related provisions recorded at Interbank, comprising S/81 mn of expert-criteria provisions already held and S/104 mn of additional provisions. Excluding El Niño Phenomenon-related provisions, impairment losses on loans would have decreased by approximately S/ 19 million QoQ to S/ 255 million.
Net interest and similar income increased by S/ 26.4 million, primarily driven by higher interest income. The growth in interest income was mainly attributable to Interbank, which contributed an additional S/ 45.3 million, while lower interest income at Interseguro reduced the increase by S/ 14.3 million.
The S/ 52.3 million decrease in other income was mainly driven by lower results in our wealth management business, reflecting more moderate portfolio performance compared to 2Q26.
The S/ 13.6 million improvement in insurance results was mainly driven by a S/ 19.3 million increase in Individual Life, reflecting adjustments to lapse assumptions. This was partially offset by decreases of S/ 3.6 million in Annuities, due to higher insurance finance expenses from higher disability and survivorship claims, and S/ 2.0 million in Retail Insurance.
The S/ 27.5 million decrease in translation result reflected a move from a gain of S/ 21.9 million in 2Q26 to a loss of S/ 5.6 million in 3Q26, of which S/ 9.1 million corresponds to our banking business.
Finally, the S/ 19.3 million increase in other expenses was mainly driven by higher expenses in our banking and insurance businesses, which increased by S/ 13.4 million and S/ 5.0 million, respectively.
Year-on-year performance
Profits increased S/ 0.8 million YoY, primarily driven by a S/ 77.0 million reduction in impairment of financial investments and increases of S/ 57.1 million in net interest and similar income, S/ 32.3 million in other income and S/ 10.0 million in fee income from financial services. These effects were partially offset by a S/ 102.3 million increase in impairment losses on loans, largely reflecting provisions related to the El Niño phenomenon (FEN) at Interbank, a S/ 64.9 million increase in other expenses and a S/ 10.9 million decrease in translation result.
The S/ 102.3 million increase in impairment losses on loans mainly reflected S/ 185 million of proactive FEN-related provisions recorded at Interbank, comprising S/81 mn of expert-criteria provisions already held and S/104 mn of additional provisions. Excluding El Niño Phenomenon-related provisions, impairment losses on loans would have been approximately S/ 255 million, in line with the S/ 257 million recorded in 3Q25.
Net interest and similar income increased by S/ 57.1 million, driven by a S/ 93.6 million increase in interest and similar income, partially offset by a S/ 36.4 million increase in interest expenses. Higher interest income was mainly explained by increases of S/ 72.7 million at Interbank and S/ 24.5 million at Interseguro, while the increase in interest expenses was mainly attributable to Interseguro, which rose S/ 29.5 million.
The S/ 32.3 million YoY increase in other income was driven by higher contributions at Interbank (+S/ 22.9 million), Interseguro (+S/ 9.9 million) and Inteligo (+S/ 7.5 million), the latter reflecting positive portfolio results.
The S/ 77.0 million decrease in impairment of financial investments reflects the impairment losses recorded by our insurance business in 3Q25, mainly related to Rutas de Lima, which did not repeat in 3Q26.
Other expenses increased by S/ 64.9 million, mainly reflecting higher expenses at Interbank (+S/ 62.6 million, with administrative expenses up 14.6% and salaries and employee benefits up 9.5%) and Interseguro (+S/ 19.0 million).
The S/ 10.9 million decrease in translation result, from a gain of S/ 5.3 million in 3Q25 to a loss of S/ 5.6 million in 3Q26, is mostly explained by decreases at Interbank (S/ 5.6 million) and Interseguro (S/ 3.9 million).
Finally, income tax decreased by S/ 2.6 million, while income before translation result and income tax increased 1.6%.

QoQ Performance
Net interest and similar income increased 5.9% QoQ and 6.9% YoY, driven by higher interest and similar income and lower funding costs. NIM increased 20 bps QoQ to 5.1%, while declining 10 bps YoY. The quarterly expansion was accompanied by a 20 bps increase in the average yield on assets and a 10 bps reduction in the cost of funding.
Meanwhile, risk-adjusted NIM declined 10 bps QoQ and 40 bps YoY to 3.4%, reflecting a higher cost of risk (2.7%, compared to 2.1% in 2Q26 and 3Q25), including FEN-related provisions recorded during the quarter. Excluding El Niño Phenomenon-related provisions, risk-adjusted NIM would have reached 3.9% in 3Q26.
Net interest and similar income increased 5.9% QoQ, or S/ 57.2 million, driven by higher income from loans and lower interest expense, which more than offset the decline in income from financial investments and cash and due from banks and inter-bank funds.
Loan-related income increased by S/ 52.3 million QoQ, or 4.1%, driven by continued growth in retail and commercial loan portfolios. In addition, the average loan yield increased to 9.7%, from 9.6% in 2Q26.
Income from financial investments decreased by S/ 3.5 million QoQ, or 2.5%, mainly reflecting a lower contribution from sovereign bonds and certificates of deposit issued by the Central Reserve Bank.
Income from cash and due from banks and inter-bank funds decreased by S/ 3.4 million QoQ, or 3.8%.
On the funding side, interest expense declined 2.3% QoQ, as lower costs associated with due to banks and correspondents and inter-bank funds, down 11.8%, and bonds, notes and other obligations, down 3.4%, more than offset a 0.5% increase in expenses related to deposits and obligations.
As a result, average interest-earning assets increased 1.1% QoQ, while the average yield on interest-earning assets increased 20 basis points to 7.7%.
Interest and similar expense decreased 2.3% QoQ (S/ 11.8 million), reflecting lower expenses on due to banks and correspondents and on bonds, notes and other obligations, partially offset by higher expenses on deposits and obligations.
Interest expense on deposits and obligations increased by S/ 1.8 million QoQ (+0.5%), despite a 1.5% increase in deposit balances, as the cost of deposits declined 10 basis points to 2.3%.
Interest expense on due to banks and correspondents declined by S/ 10.1 million QoQ (-11.8%), in line with an 11.1% reduction in average balances.
Interest expense on bonds, notes and other obligations decreased by S/ 3.4 million QoQ (-3.4%), while average balances remained broadly stable during the quarter.
Average interest-bearing liabilities increased 1.4% QoQ. As a result, the average cost of interest-bearing liabilities declined 10 basis points to 2.9%, from 3.0% in 2Q26. The lower cost of funding reflected a greater contribution from customer deposits within the funding structure and a lower reliance on due to banks and correspondents. This contributed to the increase in net interest and similar income and the expansion in NIM during the quarter.
YoY Performance
Net interest and similar income increased 6.9% YoY, or S/ 66.6 million, supported by higher interest income from loans and cash and due from banks and inter-bank funds, partially offset by a lower contribution from financial investments.
Interest income from loans increased by S/ 49.1 million YoY, or 3.9%, supported by higher average loan balances, partially offset by a 30 basis point decline in the average loan yield, from 10.0% to 9.7%.
Interest income from cash and due from banks and inter-bank funds increased by S/ 27.5 million YoY, or 45.5%, mainly driven by a greater contribution from interest-bearing liquidity balances during the period.
In contrast, income from financial investments decreased by S/ 3.8 million YoY, or 2.7%, reflecting lower yields and a lower contribution from sovereign bonds and certificates of deposit.
As a result, average interest-earning assets increased 8.4% YoY, or S/ 6,212.9 million, while the average yield on interest-earning assets declined 20 basis points to 7.7%, from 7.9% in 3Q25.
Interest and similar expense increased 1.2% YoY, mainly driven by higher expenses on deposits and obligations (+5.2%) and on bonds, notes and other obligations (+19.3%), partially offset by lower expenses on due to banks and correspondents (-25.5%).
Interest expense on deposits and obligations increased by S/ 16.3 million YoY, as an 11.2% increase in average deposit balances more than offset a 20 basispoint reduction in the cost of deposits, from 2.5% in 3Q25 to 2.3% in 3Q26.
Interest expense on due to banks and correspondents decreased by S/ 26.0 million YoY, in line with a 27.5% reduction in average balances.
In contrast, interest expense on bonds, notes and other obligations increased by S/ 15.8 million YoY (+19.3%), alongside a 24.2% increase in average balances, including the USD 500 million senior bond issued in January 2026 as part of the refinancing strategy for the bond maturing in October 2026.
As a result, the average cost of interest-bearing liabilities declined 20 basis points YoY to 2.9%, despite the higher weight of bonds, notes and other obligations in the funding mix. The lower cost of funding reflected a greater contribution from customer deposits within the funding structure and a lower reliance on due to banks and correspondents.
IMPAIRMENT LOSS ON LOANS, NET OF RECOVERIES
Impairment allowance for loans increased 7.7% QoQ.
The quarterly performance reflected an increase in impairment losses on loans of S/ 84.8 million, including proactive FEN-related provisions. Interbank has set aside S/185 mn in total for El Niño, comprising S/81 mn of expert-criteria provisions already held and S/104 mn of additional provisions.
As a result, the cost of risk increased to 2.7%, from 2.1% in 2Q26. Excluding El Niño Phenomenon-related provisions, the cost of risk would have stood at 1.9% during the quarter.

REGULATORY CAPITAL
The bank’s total capital ratio was 15.1% as of 3Q26, below the 15.5% reported in 2Q26 and the 15.8% recorded in 3Q25.
Core Equity Tier 1 (CET1) stood at 11.6% as of 3Q26, below the 11.9% reported in 2Q26 and the 12.1% recorded in 3Q25.
Both remain comfortably above regulatory minimum requirements, including applicable buffers and additional capital allocated to cover other risks, in line with SBS requirements.
The regulatory minimum total capital ratio is 10.0%. In addition, the regulator requires additional capital buffers for conservation, economic cycle, market concentration and other risks.
As of 3Q26, the additional buffer requirement totaled 3.6%, resulting in a total regulatory limit of 13.6%, well below the bank's 15.1% total capital ratio.
Risk-weighted assets (RWA) increased 4.2% QoQ, mainly reflecting higher credit-risk capital requirements associated with loan growth.
At the same time, total regulatory capital increased 1.7% QoQ, supported by earnings generated during the quarter, although partially offset by higher unrealized losses on FVOCI investments and IFS share buybacks. As a result, the total capital ratio declined from 15.5% in 2Q26 to 15.1% in 3Q26, while remaining above regulatory requirements.
On a year‑on‑year basis, the total capital ratio declined from 15.8% in 3Q25 to 15.1% in 3Q26, as the 4.3% increase in total regulatory capital was more than offset by higher risk‑weighted assets (+9.2% YoY).
The growth in regulatory capital was mainly supported by increases in Tier 1 capital (+4.4% YoY) and Tier 2 capital (+4.1% YoY), although partially offset by higher unrealized losses on FVOCI investments and IFS share buybacks.

Wealth Management
In the wealth management segment, digital engagement continued to gain traction during 3Q26. Interfondos’ digital users represented 41.6% of total users, a significant increase from 30.2% in 2Q26. This performance reflects sustained momentum in client adoption of digital investment tools and advisory services.
Digital transaction penetration remain strong across key platforms. In Inteligo SAB (brokerage) channel, the share of digital transactions represented 49.8%, compared with 42.8% in 2Q26 and 37.7% in 3Q25.
