To Our Shareholders
Bitcoin built the foundation. Power created the opportunity. Our strategy now is simple: maximize the value of every
megawatt ("MW") we control.
In Q3 2026, we started transforming the platform we have assembled into a business designed to produce durable,
contracted returns. Over the past several years, MARA has built a difficult-to-replicate portfolio of powered assets,
operating infrastructure, and technical capability. Our priority now is to convert those advantages into signed customer
agreements, completed projects, and durable free cash flow.
The Quarter: Progress on Many Fronts
A lease does not start with a signature. It starts with power, a buildable site, a capable partner, and a financeable customer.
This quarter, we advanced each of those pieces.
•Sites and development: Since the end of last quarter, we have made great progress and our discussions with
tenants are in advanced stages. Our engineering and operations teams have hosted prospective tenants across
nearly all of our sites and we feel particularly proud of the development team's progress at Kearney, where pre-
development is well advanced. While we are targeting signing two leases before year-end, market conditions are
fluid and that timing could slip into next year. We will continue to advance the power, permitting, site, and
financing work required to support these agreements and look forward to updating the market as we have news
to share.
•Power: At Long Ridge, we recently received FERC approval, and we expect to close the transaction in the
coming weeks, subject to customary conditions. We are excited to add a highly efficient natural gas plant to our
portfolio that is currently authorized to sell 485 MW and expected to increase to the full 505 MW nameplate
capacity in the first half of 2027. Long Ridge will not only immediately strengthen our financial profile with
contracted cash flows from power generation but also provides us over 1,600 contiguous acres with access to
power, water, fiber, and rail, supporting a premier data center campus. Together with our existing Bitcoin mining
operations at the site, we will have over 1 GW of total potential capacity. We look forward to completing the
transaction and beginning the next phase of Long Ridge’s development as part of our strategy to expand into data
center infrastructure. In addition, we advanced our power portfolio by securing the rights to develop a site in
Matagorda County, Texas with up to 2 gigawatts ("GW") of power capacity. The site was recently classified as a
studied load by ERCOT in the Batch Zero process and we are working with the utility to bring power online. Any
payments to the sellers are contingent on power being approved and, to date, we have made none.
•Exaion signed a four-year $79M GPU-as-a-service contract on 100% take-or-pay terms with its first US
customer. Customer demand has built a pipeline of over $3 billion in total contract value, with approximately $1
billion of that targeted for delivery by the end of 2027. These are indications of demand and are not yet contracted
or committed.
•After quarter-end, our Board of Directors authorized a share repurchase program of up to $500M of our
common stock, which reflects the Board’s and management’s confidence in our long-term strategy and gives us
an additional tool to enhance shareholder returns. In the current environment, we are always looking to deploy our
capital where it can earn the highest and best return, and at times that may mean repurchasing our shares. While
markets have declined, bitcoin has remained resilient, and our balance sheet gives us the flexibility to act on those
opportunities when they arise. We intend to use it deliberately, in the way that creates the most advantage for our

To put these milestones in context, it helps to start with the market in which we are building.
The Market
Investor sentiment toward AI infrastructure has become more cautious as the market assesses the pace of AI adoption,
community opposition, higher financing costs, and the credit structures behind some projects. These issues matter, but
they have not reduced the need for power.
Our customer conversations show that demand remains strong and increasingly favors sites that can deliver power on a
credible schedule. Customers are also asking about capacity in locations once considered peripheral, including our
international portfolio.
What has changed is that the market is applying a higher standard. Customers need power, land, permits, financing, and a
development partner who can meet a committed schedule. Lenders and investors are applying the same test.
That environment favors MARA.
MARA built our platform around these requirements. Bitcoin mining gave us deep expertise in power, infrastructure and
large-scale compute, and it is where we cut our teeth acquiring powered land. Today, we own and operate approximately
1 GW across a diversified portfolio of sites in the US and international markets. Subject to the closing of Long Ridge and
energization of Matagorda, we expect that portfolio to reach over 4 GW.
As AI and HPC demand grows, power-ready infrastructure has become the critical constraint. New generation and
transmission take years to build, and permitting and community opposition can stretch the timeline further. Customers are
paying a premium for sites that are already energized or have a credible path to near-term power. That makes what MARA
has built significantly more valuable.
That shift has shaped how we are evolving the business.
Where We Are Now
We are evolving into an energy-backed digital infrastructure enterprise spanning power, land, and compute.
It is important to remember that this is not a pivot away from bitcoin. Bitcoin remains our operating foundation and a
flexible way to monetize power while we develop higher-value infrastructure opportunities. Our strategic advantage is
optionality: we can deploy each megawatt toward the use that creates the greatest risk-adjusted return.

We see three transformational paths to value:
•Bitcoin mining remains a core business. It generates cash flow, keeps energized sites productive, and gives us
flexibility as other infrastructure is developed.
•AI and HPC infrastructure put our proposition of Speed, Certainty and Reliability into practice, converting
powered land into long-duration, contracted infrastructure for hyperscalers and other large compute customers.
•Exaion provides private cloud infrastructure, inference and GPU-as-a-service, enabling MARA to capture the full
value stack all the way up to the enterprise customer, maximizing the value of every electron while moving up the
value and margin curve.
Why MARA is Positioned to Win
Three advantages set MARA apart.
We have the power and the ability to scale. Once we complete the first phase of a campus, most of the hard work on
land, interconnection, permitting, and campus infrastructure is behind us. A customer that begins with 200 MW may
ultimately want 400 MW, 600 MW or more, and we increase the value of the site without starting over somewhere else.
Large campuses can also host a mix of customers (hyperscalers, AI cloud providers and direct enterprise workloads),
which improves utilization and economics.
We can serve the full range of customers. MARA plus Exaion gives us capabilities across the full compute stack, from
powered infrastructure and traditional colocation to private cloud and AI services. We can lease infrastructure to a
hyperscaler at a lower yield on cost or accept more risk for a higher return with frontier model providers and others. We are
open to new technology solutions that help our customers. And Exaion gives us a footprint and leverage in Europe and
Canada, where it can be harder for a U.S. company to go directly.
We watched as aggressive pricing, rising rates, and uncertain delivery schedules eroded project economics for others.
That experience reinforced our decision to prioritize contracts that can be financed and delivered at acceptable returns. In
our experience, waiting has improved the terms available to us.

Exaion
Our future business model already has traction, and it integrates the entire MARA enterprise stack. As we build out our
digital infrastructure platform, Exaion gives us the opportunity to capture a greater share of the value chain, from providing
power and infrastructure to providing the compute and cloud services that run on top of it.
Over time, that creates the potential for MARA to participate in higher-margin AI compute services, enhancing the margin
per megawatt while leveraging the same underlying power and infrastructure assets at the foundation of our strategy.
Exaion takes MARA above the infrastructure layer. It provides private cloud infrastructure, open-weight model
orchestration, and inference as a service for customers who need greater control over their data, infrastructure, and
jurisdiction. Those needs are especially important for governments, regulated industries and enterprises in Europe, and
other markets where sovereignty and security shape technology decisions.
Exaion also lets us share in the value created by the compute itself. Its operating model can utilize GPUs across their
lifecycle, extending their utility and cash flow well beyond initial contract durations. That improves utilization and returns
across the life of the asset.
This is no longer simply a future-state thesis. We now have an early proof point with Exaion recently signing a major GPU-
as-a-service contract with total contract value of $79 million over four years, on 100% take-or-pay terms. The customer
commits to pay for the capacity whether or not it uses it, which gives us predictable, contracted revenue for the life of the
agreement.
This agreement is also a signal of demand. Exaion’s customers and prospects have expressed demand for more than 30
MW of compute capacity for delivery by the end of 2027. We view this contract as an early proof point of the model we are
building: MARA provides the energy-backed infrastructure, and Exaion turns it into compute and cloud services.
This is how we intend to move up the value chain: not by trying to own every layer on our own, but through strategic
partnerships. That gives us a unique, differentiating right to win.
Bitcoin Mining
All of this rests on the business that built MARA, which remains core to our strategy.

The competencies it takes to run a large mining fleet (power procurement, modular deployment, infrastructure
optimization and real-time load management) are the same competencies that digital infrastructure tenants require.
Modular deployment is a good example. It lets us bring capacity online faster than a traditional build and at lower cost,
which matters to customers who need compute up and running quickly. We believe MARA is one of the largest operators
of modular Bitcoin mining digital infrastructure in the United States, and we intend to apply that experience as we
transition towards AI/HPC.
We continue to modernize the fleet where the gain in efficiency and the expected payback justify the investment. When
equipment pricing is weak, we have been selective, acquiring machines that raise output without increasing total mining
power consumption. We weigh each of these investments against the timing of the next halving and the payback over the
full cycle.
Our objective is to increase computing output within the existing electrical footprint, and to preserve the option to direct
power to the use that offers the best risk-adjusted return. At some sites, that will remain Bitcoin mining. At others,
contracted AI/HPC infrastructure may be the better long-term use. We do not have to choose one or the other.
Vertebrai
We recently launched Vertebrai, MARA's flexible computing platform built on nearly a decade of running power-intensive
computing across energy markets. Power and demand rarely arrive at the same time, and Vertebrai's software decides,
moment to moment, where power should go. It absorbs power that would otherwise sit idle or sell at a loss, then scales
back when the grid needs that power elsewhere.

We now see a similar opportunity with data center developers, who often secure power 18 to 24 months before their

Balance Sheet and Capital Allocation
Each of these priorities depends on a strong balance sheet.
Our balance sheet has to support the obligations we have today and the opportunities we are building for tomorrow. So
we manage cash, bitcoin, debt, and access to capital as parts of one balance sheet. Since the beginning of the year, we
have reduced our total debt by $1.4 billion, or 37%.
We view bitcoin as a strategic asset and a source of financial flexibility. Depending on market conditions and the expected
return on the use of proceeds, we may hold it, monetize a portion, or borrow against it. That capital can support operations,
reduce debt, fund acquisitions, or advance projects we believe will increase long-term shareholder value. We used this
flexibility earlier in the year to repurchase convertible notes at a discount, and we continue to monitor the put dates on our
remaining obligations.
We will keep balancing growth against leverage and dilution. The Starwood model reduces the corporate equity required
for qualifying data center projects, and our bitcoin holdings and other sources of liquidity add capacity. We expect our
direct spending to stay focused on site control, interconnection, development work, targeted infrastructure upgrades, and
selected project equity where the prospective return warrants it.
The capital markets remain open for well-structured developments. We price each project to reflect the customer’s credit,
the support available from a parent or technology provider, and the financing needed to build the facility.
Looking Ahead
The next stage will be measured by execution. Demand for compute capacity remains strong, but the market is becoming
more selective about how that demand is financed and delivered. We believe that selectivity is constructive. It should
favor companies with real power, credible sites, strong partners, and the patience to structure projects properly. MARA
has spent years building exactly that.
When we think about MARA’s future, the opportunity is bigger than monetizing the megawatt. It is about increasing the
value we can capture from that megawatt as we move from power to infrastructure to compute, and ultimately to fully
integrated cloud solutions.
We entered 2026 focused on expanding the platform. We are ending the year focused on converting that platform into
contracted leases and free cash flow. Bitcoin built the foundation and our power portfolio broadened the opportunity. Our
partnerships expand the ways we can capture value from each megawatt. Our responsibility now is to execute with
discipline and to show you the returns these assets can produce.
Thank you for your continued support.

(1) Non-GAAP Financial Measures. In order to provide a more
comprehensive understanding of the information used by our
management team in financial and operational decision-making, we
supplement our Condensed Consolidated Financial Statements that
have been prepared in accordance with generally accepted
accounting principles in the United States ("GAAP") with the non-
GAAP financial measure of Adjusted EBITDA.
The Company defines Adjusted EBITDA as GAAP net income (loss)
plus adjustments to add back the impacts of (1) interest, (2) income
taxes, (3) depreciation and amortization and (4) adjustments for non-
cash and/or non-recurring items, which currently include (i) stock-
based compensation expense, (ii) change in fair value of derivative
instrument, (iii) impairment of goodwill and other assets, (iv)
restructuring costs, (v) acquisition and integration costs, (vi) litigation
settlement, (vii) net gain from extinguishment of debt, (viii) net gain on
investments, and (ix) early termination expenses.
Management uses Adjusted EBITDA, together with the supplemental
information provided herein, to understand, manage and evaluate
business performance and to inform operating decision-making. The
Company relies primarily on its Condensed Consolidated Financial
Statements to evaluate financial performance and uses non-GAAP
financial measures only supplementally.
We believe that Adjusted EBITDA is useful to us and to our investors
because it excludes certain financial, capital structure and/or non-cash
items that we do not believe directly reflect our core operations or may
not be indicative of our recurring operations. These items may vary
significantly over time and across companies within our industry
independent of core operating performance. We believe that excluding
these items allows for more meaningful period-over-period
comparisons and improved comparability relative to other companies.
Adjusted EBITDA is not a recognized financial measure under GAAP.
Investors should consider Adjusted EBITDA in addition to, but not as a
substitute for, the most directly comparable financial results calculated
and presented in accordance with GAAP. Because our calculation of
Adjusted EBITDA may differ from that of other companies, our
presentation of Adjusted EBITDA may not be comparable to similarly
titled measures of other companies.
