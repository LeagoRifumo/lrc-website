London | 5 November 2026: National Grid plc today announces its Half Year results for the period ended 30 September 2026.
We bring energy to power possibilities
Zoë Yujnovich, Chief Executive said: “Our results for the first half show that capital investment is accelerating – increasing by one-third, our major projects are progressing, and the changes we are making within National Grid are sharpening performance in service of better outcomes for customers and stakeholders.
We’re working to get more from our existing networks, including through scaled use of technology and innovation, while investing to build capacity for the future – committing £70 billion over the next five years to modernise and expand energy networks in the UK and US Northeast. Our capital delivery will underpin economic growth, electrification, and energy security, supporting long term affordability and reliability. In the UK, our planned £40 billion capital investment will help reduce system constraint costs for the benefit of consumers, and support more than 60,000 jobs.
This first half progress further strengthens our confidence in delivering the five-year framework we set out in March.”

Highlights
Financial performance
– Record capital investment of £6.7 billion due to the step-up in onshore and offshore ASTI spend in UK Electricity Transmission as we seek to double our network capacity and reduce system constraint costs for the benefit of consumers, alongside wider network growth and reinforcement across our UK, New York and New England businesses.
– Higher underlying operating profit of £2.6 billion drove underlying EPS of 36.5p, up 22%, reflecting the first-year impact of the new RIIO-T3 price control, continued growth in our US rate base and one-off fair value gains in our NGP* portfolio.
– Statutory profit before tax of £1,730 million (HY 26 £826 million) includes favourable timing swings (£240 million year-on-year) and benefits from exceptional items including NGP* portfolio revaluation gains and the non-recurring impact of divestments (£333 million year-on-year). Statutory EPS was 26.9p.
– Interim dividend of 16.97p equates to 35% of the prior year full dividend, in line with our progressive inflation-linked policy.
Strategic progress
– Strong capital delivery progress in the first six months including a number of major project milestones completed, and supply chain and delivery mechanisms are now secured for c. 80% of our £70 billion five-year investment programme.
– Announced a $1.75 billion investment in Joulent to develop contracted power solutions for US large-load customers.
– Deploying technology and innovation to optimise assets across our networks, enhance customer experience and drive efficiency in central functions; which is supporting world-class UK network reliability and improved customer service in H1.
– Simplified our organisation to create clear accountability across our strategic framework and drive better quality decisions at pace.
– Filed a 1-year rate stabilisation proposal for our NY gas businesses (KEDNY/LI) and progressed our ED3* Business Plan.

Strategic overview
In the first half of 2026/27, we made strong early progress in executing our largest-ever investment programme of at least £70 billion over the five years to 2030/31, while maintaining our focus on delivering safe, reliable and resilient networks. We are strengthening the capabilities required to execute at scale, leveraging innovation to optimise our networks and improving how we serve customers. All whilst maintaining our strong safety performance and positioning National Grid to capture the significant growth opportunities created by a rapidly changing energy system.
Strategic framework
Earlier this year we introduced our strategic framework, which builds on our strengths and underpins delivery of our five-year financial framework. It focuses on delivering the Brilliant Basics, designed to drive better performance across the business through competitive benchmarking and continuous improvement. The Big Shifts focus our attention on the few transformations necessary to respond to the ever-changing external market. Delivering on both of these will position National Grid for the future by building a platform for Disciplined Growth, primarily through our core regulated networks, but also in adjacencies that will support longer-term growth, where there is a strong portfolio fit and the opportunity to earn attractive returns.
During the period, we continued to embed the framework across our businesses and functions, with clear accountabilities now established through our new operating model and a much stronger focus on performance outcomes.
Delivering the Brilliant Basics to lead on performance
The Brilliant Basics are the foundations of our business, where we focus on delivering world-class performance. These are the core activities that underpin safe, reliable and efficient operations. Our focus is on driving a step-up in performance across capital delivery, asset management, customer service and our functions.
Capital: best-in-class delivery of our largest-ever capital programme
We continued to scale delivery of our investment programme across our regulated businesses in the UK and US. Capital investment in the first half was £6.7 billion up 32% at constant currency from HY 26 (£5.1 billion) with increases across all regulated businesses, but with the largest step-up in UK Electricity Transmission.
In the UK, our Electricity Transmission business continued to progress its capital investment programme, with a number of major project milestones achieved in the first half. Examples in the period include the main tunnelling works on both the Grain to Tilbury and Snowdonia VIP Projects. These included a 2.2 kilometre tunnel under the River Thames and a 3.4 kilometre tunnel under the Dwyryd Estuary, which were completed in September and October 2026 respectively. Elsewhere we achieved Stage 1 commissioning of the super grid transformers at our Yorkshire Green project.
All six of our Wave 1 ASTI projects and the first two of our Wave 2 projects are now in construction. The remaining nine Wave 2 ASTI projects are progressing through the planning and consenting processes. For instance, we secured the Development Consent Order (DCO) for Sea Link at the end of October, enabling us to move the project into its next phase, and the outcome on Norwich to Tilbury is due in early 2027. We also made our DCO submissions for EGL 3 and EGL 4 in early November, and expect to submit all remaining ASTI project planning submissions by the end of March 2027.
During the period we enabled 2.5 GW of connections to be energised, bringing new generation and storage capacity onto the network including a 350 MW battery energy storage system at Uskmouth, Wales and the 1.4 GW Sofia offshore wind farm.
In August, Ofgem also issued their draft determination on the first of our reopener filings which we will file every six months under the RIIO-T3 framework. The draft determination supports approval of eligibility for 23 of 25 reopeners submitted, unlocking £373 million of Pre-Construction Funding and optioneering certainty for 12 projects, equating to c.£1.15 billion of investment. Ofgem’s ‘minded to’ position on Sea Link would approve £2.9 billion of project funding, more than 90% of the costs submitted for assessment.
UK Electricity Distribution saw a 36% increase in investment on connections, with works completed for over 380 MW of generation to connect to our primary network, and over 9,700 connections totalling 250 MW of new or upgraded connections to our secondary or low voltage network.
In the US, as part of our Upstate Upgrade in New York, we completed the Smart Path Connect transmission project, commissioning two 345 kV lines and moved our CLCPA Phase 1 and 2 projects from planning and development into construction. This included six new substations and four transmission projects, with capital investment for the Upstate Upgrade of around $300 million in the period. We also began construction of the electricity connection for Micron’s first semi-conductor fabrication plant in Upstate New York supporting 480 MW of demand. A second fabrication plant is already planned for an additional > 550 MW, bringing the two facilities to more than 1 GW of planned demand and supporting economic development in the region. At full build it is expected to account for around 2 GW of demand, equivalent to one-third of NIMO Electric’s current peak demand, and Micron will become our largest single electric customer in New York.
In New England, after completing the Vermont and New Hampshire portions of the century-old A1/B2 transmission line refurbishment project, New England Power received approval for, and started construction on the Massachusetts phase of the $470 million project. Across Massachusetts and New York gas distribution, 170 miles of leak prone pipe were replaced in the first half of 2026/27.
Asset: getting the most from our transmission and distribution assets
The safety of our people and contractors remains our highest priority. Our Lost Time Injury Frequency Rate was 0.10 on a rolling 12-month basis at half year, compared with 0.11 at full year. Our strong safety performance has therefore remained broadly stable. We continue to strengthen our safety culture through frontline engagement, critical risk management, control effectiveness, and the proactive sharing of safety learnings.
In the UK, reliability remained high across our UK Electricity Transmission network, operating without any ‘energy not supplied’ events during the period and with our strongest availability and reliability recorded over the last five years across our interconnector portfolio, including Viking Link reaching its full capacity of 1.4 GW. In UK Electricity Transmission, we also energised shunt reactors at Taunton, Wylfa and Willington substations. These shunt reactors support network stability through providing additional voltage management capability, supporting the integration of additional renewables, and reducing network management costs to benefit consumers.
In UK Electricity Distribution, network stability was maintained, but additional localised outages were experienced because of the significant increase in extreme heat during the UK summer period. We have continued to invest in asset health, and are on track to achieve our ED2 asset health commitments. Our Distribution System Operator (DSO) flexibility services have avoided 295 GWh curtailment to date, having completed 25% more outage studies compared to the equivalent point last year, demonstrating our increasing role in keeping customers connected to our network.
In the US, our asset optimisation programmes, including the roll-out of Fault Location Isolation and Service Restoration (FLISR) and Advanced Meter Infrastructure (AMI), continue to demonstrate their effectiveness and enhance efficiency. Across New York and Massachusetts, FLISR has restored power to more than 90,000 customers, and avoided almost 11 million customer outage minutes year to date, with a notable impact during the September Nor’easter storm in Massachusetts. FLISR restored power to 12,300 customers in less than 60 seconds, and AMI meters were able to confirm power remotely leading to a reduction of 185 required site checks.
In our New York Gas operations, we continue to expand our use of ‘Lighthouse’, our programme to drive productivity and efficiency by removing field obstacles, improving planning and scheduling, and enhancing performance management. In some of our early locations, this is helping us to get crews to their first jobs 15% faster, reduce travel time between jobs by 25%, complete up to a third more valve inspections each day, and it has cut unplanned overtime on Long Island by 75%. Also in New York, we advanced our $230 million Holtsville LNG Plant Modernization Project, completing the first of two phases of the project to extend the asset life by another 50 years. This supports ongoing reliability for Long Island, as the plant can supply 10% of gas during peak demand.
Customer: enhancing our customer experience
In UK Electricity Transmission, following the connections reforms introduced by NESO, we have issued the majority of our Phase 1 offers covering transmission connections, and are now working through queries with our customers. We have established new governance procedures for customer connections to strengthen delivery discipline, improve transparency and tackle delivery barriers. We recognise it has been a challenging process for some of our customers, and so we are doing all we can to support our customers through the process, including leveraging innovation to move faster where we can, in what is a complex wider ecosystem.
In UK Electricity Distribution, we are scaling up consistent, customer-led ways of working to improve our customer journeys, which has improved our Broad Measure Customer Satisfaction, currently 9/10. We are further enhancing proactive customer communications and winter resilience, issuing proactive communications to over 1.4 million affected customers and providing advice to those most vulnerable ahead of the periods of extreme heat in the summer. Our DSO achieved further improvements in their annual stakeholder satisfaction survey, and achieved a second year of the maximum DSO incentive reward.
The continued strong performance of our interconnectors in NGV has enabled payments of an additional £45 million to consumers in the period. This is part of almost £400 million in payments to consumers over the past three and a half years, with a further >£300 million forecast to be paid over the next two and a half years, subject to Ofgem approval.
In the US, AMI-enabled remote services are reducing truck rolls and operating costs, while digital self-service now supports over 50% of customer journeys, reducing call demand and making it easier for customers to interact with us. We have also deployed AI in our contact centres to strengthen performance management and provide agents with more targeted coaching, supporting customer service agent satisfaction of over 87%. Strong US “meter-to-cash right first time” billing success metric performance, measuring above 96% in New England, is helping deliver better customer outcomes. In addition, a focus on supporting vulnerable customers has led to the enrolment of an additional 28,000 customers onto low-income discount rates in Massachusetts, and over 38,000 in New York since September 2025.
At the same time, we are partnering with major commercial and industrial customers to support innovation, electrification and large-load growth across Massachusetts and New York. In New York we have partnered with 56 distributed generation developers on over 300 projects to deliver milestones on time or ahead of schedule during the half year, across 1.4 GW of future clean energy capacity. This is more than three times the project volume completed in 2025 and was enabled by operational improvements that are strengthening governance, increasing developer visibility and accelerating project delivery.
Functions: enhancing control and oversight while reducing friction in how we operate
Our functions play a critical role in supporting safe and efficient delivery. During the period, we continued to simplify governance, strengthen planning and controls, and identify opportunities for AI to support efficient processes while maintaining appropriate oversight.
For instance, in our Finance function we have been consolidating our data onto a single platform, to create a stronger and more consistent data foundation. We are also developing AI agents to support some of our processes, and transferring further global process activities to our Enterprise Business Services (our central shared services function) to leverage our scale as we grow. In August 2026, we successfully launched the first phase of our NextGen ERP programme in the US as we modernise our core financial systems. This release standardised the financial data model across our US businesses and automated complex regulatory and financial reporting, with approximately 400 end users across Finance transitioned to the new platform. In our legal function, we have enabled our businesses to get easier access to legal support through a digital front-end, and are using AI-enabled tools such as Harvey and Brief AI.
Driving the Big Shifts to define our future
The changing energy system requires us to transform how we lead, how we innovate, and how we shape our external environment. To achieve that transformation we are focussed on three Big Shifts:
Leadership, People & Performance: accountability, performance management and investment in leadership
We are focused on delivering clearer accountability, stronger performance management and we are investing in leadership across the Group. We launched a new simplified operating model in August, and reduced the Group Executive Committee (GEC), from 13 to 8 members. This strengthens accountability for performance and strategic delivery, and supports better-quality decisions at pace, more consistent execution and a stronger focus on stakeholder outcomes.
We are also investing in our capabilities and leadership, which includes the strengthening of our leadership and talent pipeline to build succession, depth and critical capabilities through a streamlined leadership framework for the Group’s top 140 leaders. Delivering our investment programme requires the expansion of our workforce including across specialist skills and delivery capacity. This includes skilled trade, engineering and network and capital delivery project management roles. We are on track to deliver our workforce plans, having recruited over 3,600 new employees during the first half of the year, including more than 400 graduates and apprentices.
We also recognise the need to support the next generation of talent. During the period, we committed £5 million to launch the Power within Employability fund to help 5,000 young people enter the workforce over the next five years, through practical training for the energy, construction, and infrastructure sector.
We also made progress in the period in our relations with trade unions, including pay and conditions agreements secured through our constructive, long-standing partnerships with UK trade unions supporting workforce stability and enabling delivery of our Great Grid Upgrade and wider transformation agenda. In the US, a number of multi-year union agreements were secured across our New York and New England jurisdictions, providing greater labour certainty while supporting productivity, operational flexibility and transformation.
Technology & Innovation: using technology more systematically to enable productivity and delivery
Technology and innovation are central to improving the performance of our networks and delivering more capacity at lower whole-system cost. During the period, we advanced digital, data and AI capabilities to strengthen our cyber defences, accelerate our capital delivery, unlock capacity on our existing networks, optimise our reliability and asset management, and enhance the customer experience. We recognise that cyber and AI-enabled threats are evolving rapidly, and we continue to build our understanding and adapt our approach in response.
Across capital delivery we are leveraging technology to help us deliver our capital investment programmes to schedule and budget. Our Capital Control Tower is helping us identify efficiencies from initiatives including direct sourcing, alternative haulage routes, and substation standardisation. Generative scheduling also allowed us to identify 100 days of schedule acceleration across our Eastern Green Link 1 joint venture project timeline. Using the Exodigo mapping tool to visualise infrastructure underground derisked our Walpole substation and Propel NY joint venture projects by providing accurate information on underground assets thereby reducing future change orders and supporting project scheduling. Elsewhere we are using Sensat to create digital twins of our project sites to allow multiple organisations involved in a project to view the same visual information. This allows testing of different options against real world constraints ahead of construction. We have also used Sensat successfully on our North London Reinforcement project to reduce site visits, enable earlier decisions, and reduce overhead costs.
New construction technology is also helping us simplify and speed up our delivery. For example we have moved innovative 3D-printed, low-carbon concrete foundations from trial stage into business-as-usual deployment, with the first supplier order now placed following successful testing. This technology can significantly reduce the volume of concrete required for substation foundations, supporting faster and lower-carbon network construction.
Across our assets we are using technology to unlock capacity on our existing networks, supporting network planning and resilience. For instance, in New England we are using Envelio’s mapping analytics to help identify where capacity exists on our network to assist developers with site selection and speed up customer connections. Having successfully deployed GridCare in our NY business to identify 600 MW of additional capacity earlier this year, we are now working with them in UK Electricity Transmission to unlock spare capacity and faster connections for large loads like data centres that can flex their load. Emerald AI provides one way for large data centres to flex their load without impacting their performance. After a successful pilot in the UK we are now exploring how we can leverage it more widely in our distribution network.
Technology also enables us to optimise reliability and enhance our asset management. In New England, we were awarded $49 million from the Department of Energy to support the Dynamic Rating Enhancement Advancements and Modernization (DREAM) project. This will establish dynamic line rating as a system-wide operational capability across New England Power’s approximately 2,600-mile overhead transmission network, maximising the utilisation of our transmission system. Sees.AI drone technology has also been successfully piloted to complete inspections, and its use is being extended across our business.
We are investing in our customer service platforms to support improved customer experience, enabled by better use of data. Our US businesses are introducing the Kraken platform to support end-to-end ownership of the customer experience, including issue resolution, accurate billing, and more proactive, timely communication. Kraken moved into the customer trial stage in the first half, and phased migration will start in the second half of the year, as we target deployment to our c.6.5 million US customers. In the UK, we modernised our Electricity Distribution contact centre with Amazon Connect strengthening resilience and service for our c.8 million customers and reducing call abandonment by around 10%. Further insights from AWS have improved customer experience and resolution, with a first contact resolution rate of 90%.
External Positioning: shaping outcomes that support affordability, resilience and growth
We have continued to engage constructively with governments, regulators and other stakeholders on the policy and regulatory choices required to support economic growth, energy security, affordability and decarbonisation. Our focus is on shaping practical reforms that enable timely investment and connections while protecting customers and maintaining system reliability.
We have continued to progress our price controls and rate cases in the period across a number of our jurisdictions. In the UK, priorities have included RIIO-T3 implementation and submitting our reopener filings, driving planning reform, delivering connections reform and supporting strategic spatial planning.
In the US, we filed a one-year rate stabilisation proposal for KEDNY and KEDLI, our downstate gas distribution businesses in New York. This proposal would extend the current rate plan by one year to 31 March 2028, and supports rate stability and affordability, while enabling continued investment with the ability to defer for future recovery the costs associated with necessary capital expenditures to maintain a safe and reliable service. In Massachusetts, our Massachusetts Gas rate case continued to progress with the completion of evidentiary hearings and briefings, ahead of an order expected in late November 2026.
Our external positioning work also includes helping to shape industry-wide policy direction, through working in coalitions on the future evolution of energy systems. In the UK we have played a leading role in developing the Electricity Networks Sector Growth Plan, bringing together industry, Government, the regulator and the supply chain to support a shared plan to translate the unprecedented expansion of Britain’s electricity networks into wider UK economic growth. The Plan sets out the scale of the opportunity through to 2035, including £181 billion of regulated network investment, £58 billion of direct value added to the UK economy and more than 210,000 jobs supported, alongside practical commitments on supply-chain investment, skills, demand visibility and UK manufacturing. National Grid is committed to supporting this and has recently announced a new long-term ambition for 70 per cent of the value of its future UK supply chain spend to be delivered in the UK, supporting domestic jobs in manufacturing and services.
In the US, we engaged with regulators and policy makers on large-load policy, affordability, resilience and transmission development. This included working with a bipartisan group to support the introduction of a reform bill in the US Senate to speed up federal permitting for infrastructure projects.
We recognise the role we play in delivering reliable, secure, affordable energy systems that support economic growth and enable decarbonisation. We continue to monitor developments in the external environment and will ensure that we adapt our Responsible Business Charter and Climate Transition Plan to reflect both our own progress and the policy and regulatory landscape we are now operating in, with publication planned ahead of the 2027 AGM.
Building optionality for Disciplined Growth
Delivering our Brilliant Basics and driving the Big Shifts will build a stronger platform for future long-term growth. Our priority remains execution of the high-quality, visible investment opportunities across our regulated networks, while selectively exploring new growth opportunities emerging from the transforming energy landscape. This approach allows us to sustain strong growth across our businesses, while creating additional growth options in parallel.
Regulated networks growth
Our regulated networks remain the principal driver of growth within our investment programme and we are focused on executing the significant organic growth opportunity it presents. We are also working with policy makers, regulators, customers and our supply chain to shape the policy and regulatory environments of the future, across both the UK and the US, not just for the next price control, but for the longer term.
For instance, having received the ED3 SSMD in May 2026, we are in the final stages of drafting our ED3 Business Plan, ahead of its submission to Ofgem in November 2026. This is expected to drive further growth in capital investment across the next five-year distribution price control. While in the US, across our upstate and downstate New York businesses, we are progressing the planning of our next multi-year rate cases.
Exploring new growth opportunities through National Grid Ventures
Alongside our regulated networks growth, National Grid Ventures continues to manage our portfolio of interconnectors and US generation assets and to assess selective opportunities. We are disciplined in our approach and will only consider opportunities where National Grid can leverage its capabilities whilst delivering long-duration cash flows and attractive returns with an appropriate risk profile.
During the half year, the Propel NY Energy transmission project, a project owned by our joint venture New York Transco, made significant progress in receiving approval of its Article VII permit from the New York Public Service Commission, a critical milestone to authorise advancing activities to complete all construction permits for an expected start by the end of FY2027.
In July 2026, we agreed to invest $1.75 billion to secure a 35% interest in Joulent LLC (“Joulent”), as part of a broader strategic partnership. Joulent will focus on developing contracted power generation and high voltage infrastructure for US large load customers, providing integrated solutions to help meet rapidly rising demand for reliable, scaled power at speed. The investment in Joulent is expected to deliver risk-adjusted returns in excess of our regulatory returns.
The majority of NGV’s investment will be used to enable the progression of Joulent’s foundational project, Project Kilby (“Kilby”), a 50/50 partnership with Chevron Corporation. Kilby is a 2.7 GW co-located power facility in West Texas that will provide dedicated electricity to a Microsoft-operated data centre campus under a 20-year power purchase agreement. Development of the project is at an advanced stage with all turbines and critical equipment secured, EPC capacity being arranged with leading contractors, and first power delivery targeted for 2028.
Our power-system expertise in energy management and high voltage substation development will be critical in delivering complex multi-gigawatt islanded mini-grid projects like Kilby. We also bring deep supply chain relationships for the necessary electrical equipment and we have longstanding experience of operating gas turbine power equipment at our US GenCo facilities on Long Island.
Beyond Project Kilby, Joulent has a multi-gigawatt pipeline, with three further projects being advanced in discussions with hyperscalers. We will progress that pipeline selectively and with discipline, through strict hurdle rate and risk criteria.
Elsewhere, we also continue to progress the next generation of hybrid interconnectors, with the LionLink offshore hybrid interconnector, being developed with TenneT in the Netherlands, achieving its multi-terminal direct current FEED award during the period. Our project pipeline includes two recent memoranda of understanding signed with joint venture partners.

Strong first half of delivery of our five-year financial framework

Five-year financial framework*
In March 2026, we set out our updated five-year financial framework for the period to 2030/31.
Capital investment and asset growth
We expect to invest at least £70 billion across our energy networks and adjacent businesses, in the UK and US, over the five-year period to March 2031, with Group assets trending towards £115 billion by March 2031. Of this investment, around 85% is considered to be aligned with the principles of the EU Taxonomy legislation as at the date of reporting, directly invested into the decarbonisation of energy networks.
In the UK, we expect around £31 billion of investment in UK Electricity Transmission increasing network capacity to support increased renewable generation, and reduce constraint costs, connecting new customers, and maintaining the resilience of the network. This includes investment across our 17 ASTI projects, as we invest in the critical infrastructure required to enable the energy transition and a decarbonised electricity network in the 2030s. We expect our UK Electricity Distribution network to invest around £9 billion in asset replacement, reinforcement and new connections, facilitating the infrastructure for electric vehicles, heat pumps and directly connected generation.
In our US regulated businesses, we expect to invest around £17 billion in New York, and £12 billion in New England. Of this investment, we expect over 60% to be into our electricity networks, as we see a step-up in investment for renewable connections, transmission network upgrades, and digital capabilities to enable the energy transition, and the remainder in our gas business on pipeline replacement, safety and resilience programmes.
National Grid Ventures (NGV) has committed capital investment of around £1 billion, including maintenance investment across the six operational interconnectors.
With the large step-up in investment, we expect to see asset growth of around 10% CAGR through to 2030/31.
Group earnings growth and dividend growth
We expect our CAGR in underlying EPS to be in the 8–10% range from our 2025/26 baseline of 78.0p. This includes our long-run average scrip uptake assumption of 25% per annum, which will support our sustainable, progressive dividend policy into the future.
We will maintain a progressive level of total dividend aiming to grow the dividend per share in line with UK CPIH in keeping with the current dividend policy (for details of our dividend policy please refer to page 19).
Balance sheet visibility
We remain committed to a strong, overall investment grade credit rating. We expect to maintain credit metrics above our thresholds for our current group credit ratings through to at least 2030/31, with current thresholds of 10% for S&P and Moody’s Funds from Operations (FFO)/adjusted net debt, and 7% for Moody’s Retained Cash Flow (RCF)/adjusted net debt. Regulatory gearing was 61% at March 2026, and is expected to increase to the high-60% range by March 2030/31. This balance sheet strength extends beyond 2030/31, complemented by significant hybrid capacity.

Macro resilience
Our investment case is underpinned by the visibility and resilience of our business model, our robust operational delivery, and supported by our regulatory frameworks. We are resilient to the impacts of the broader macro environment.
– Inflation: In our UK businesses, inflation protections across our regulated asset base, together with regulatory mechanisms that provide protection against the impact of inflation on our cost base, provide a strong natural hedge and underpin real equity returns. Around 10% of our debt book is inflation linked, aligned with UK regulatory mechanisms. In the US, our regulatory frameworks have mechanisms to manage the impacts of changes in inflation, including revenue indexation, the ability to pace activity to remain within agreed allowances, and mechanisms to recover efficiently incurred unremunerated spend over time.
– Commodity costs: Our businesses have limited exposure to volatility in wholesale energy prices. In the US, customer commodity costs are treated as pass through costs, and we use hedging programmes agreed with our regulators to manage the cost of energy procured for our customers.
– Interest rates: Our financing strategy is designed to ensure stability. Over the five-year framework around 75% of our debt is subject to operating company level recovery mechanisms enabling the efficient recovery of debt costs; with 25% of our debt having a relatively small interest rate exposure that we actively manage. Approximately 10% of our net debt is sensitive to interest rates, limiting the exposure to changes in rates on our embedded debt. Our five-year financial framework reflects a forward view of interest rates and our refinancing profile. From an earnings sensitivity perspective, a sustained increase in interest rates of 100 basis points across the five-year financial framework versus our forward rate assumptions would impact 2030/31 EPS by around 1 pence after accounting for capitalised interest.
– Foreign exchange: We are deliberate in managing our currency exposure. We hedge around 70% of our US gross assets with dollar-denominated debt. From an earnings perspective, that means a five cent move in the dollar–sterling exchange rate across a year translates to around a 1p impact on EPS, limiting volatility for shareholders.
Investment Case
National Grid offers a differentiated investment case, combining high-visibility growth with a resilient business model.
Our investment programme is supported by multiple structural drivers: network resilience and modernisation, connecting low-carbon generation, and the expansion of our networks to support electrification and rapidly growing demand, including from AI and data centres, and industrial reshoring. Importantly, that investment is going into critical infrastructure assets, generating long-duration cash flows with attractive, low-risk returns.
Alongside that growth, our business model is highly resilient. Our regulatory capabilities, delivery track record, disciplined balance sheet and resilience to macroeconomic volatility provide a unique stability, even as the energy system becomes more complex.
That combination supports our five-year framework, including 8–10% annual underlying EPS growth and a progressive inflation-protected dividend, designed to deliver attractive and consistent double-digit investor returns.
