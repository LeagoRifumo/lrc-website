To Our ShareholdersAt the beginning of this year, we established four principles to guide our work forward:•Customers want peace of mind, not more products.•Companies move fast or slow based on information flow.•The best way to integrate machine intelligence is to become it.•Intelligence depends on signal, and money is the most honest signal.We used these principles to refocus how we work, what we work on, and where we're going. And we saw this compound into another strong set of results in the third quarter. We exceeded our guidance with 22% gross profit growth, 92% Adjusted Operating Income growth, and 109% Adjusted Diluted EPS growth, and we raised our guidance across each of these metrics for the year.These principles also led us to refine the master plan we shared at Investor Day at the end of last year:•Make our #company an intelligence, and enable others to do the same.•Build the best intelligence to run your #business.•Build the best intelligence to run your #money.•Build open financial #infrastructure for all.•Connect #neighborhoods globally.Today we're delivering intelligence to our customers, connecting our ecosystems, and building open financial infrastructure to help achieve our financial results. Square Kiosk gives every restaurant the opportunity to have intuitive physical intelligence in every location, easing the ordering and line burden so they can focus more on their food and experience. Managerbot, which we just rebranded to Squaremates, now has an entirely new architecture and is available to Square sellers in the U.S., giving them access to intelligence and automation tools to help them save time and run their businesses better. We are planning to do the same with Moneybot, to predict and present the right interface to people with all the capabilities Cash App has to offer. We’re connecting our ecosystems in a way we never have before and are already seeing results: Neighborhoods was a key driver of Cash App scaling to 60 million monthly transacting actives in September, and there is much more we plan to do to connect local communities in the future. But what does it mean to "make our company an intelligence, and enable others to do the same"? We started down this path with goose, which helped us automate a lot of mundane engineering tasks earlier than most other companies, and which quickly evolved into a tool we could use for any role or task. It's now deeply embedded in our company's foundation, and enables us to help realize our vision to turn our company into a working intelligence, a mini AGI. We focused on separating our work into four layers: capabilities (the jobs we can do), interfaces (our distribution, from apps to hardware to payment wands), world models (the deep real-time understanding of both our customers and company), and proactive intelligence (a predictive engine to anticipate future needs, both for our customers and us).
Buzz is probably the greatest manifestation of the "Block as Intelligence" vision. It's an environment for building and collaborating that brings all of our ideas together into one surface. It's really great for solo work, and when someone is ready, even better for team work. It's designed to scale from a company of our size, to one much larger, and even to our smallest sellers (Squaremates is built on Buzz to offer our sellers best-in-class intelligence and team collaboration). It's model agnostic, entirely self-sovereign (meaning you can run it privately on your own network or system), and starts to reduce the dependency on tools like Slack, Discord, and GitHub.
We have so much confidence in Buzz and our related tools that we recently created a new ecosystem called Builderlab. Block was Builderlab’s first customer, but the problems we’re solving are not unique to us. Companies that want to transform how they work at scale need more than a model. They need simple, accessible interfaces, connections between existing tools, and secure ways to manage and evaluate the work agentic intelligence does. We built Builderlab to help transform how Block works, and our goal is to bring it to other companies to help them do the same.
In October we began piloting Buzz with prospective customers, and we expect to generate our first machine intelligence revenue in the fourth quarter. We have more to prove as we build a product offering and go-to-market strategy for an entirely new customer base, but we’re excited to extend what we’ve built for ourselves to others. We’ll approach this new ecosystem with the same principles that have guided us since the beginning: start small, give it soul, give time back, make it better. We're hosting an event in San Francisco on November 10 to properly introduce Builderlab.
Finally, we believe voice is the most natural way to interact with intelligence, so we created Dessa, which we view as the most authentic full-duplex speech model ever built. Talking with it feels like talking with a person…full of emotion, nuance, pauses, and a natural cadence. Dessa truly pushes the frontier of deep learning models and is a significant contribution to the field. I encourage you to listen to the samples we've shared, and expect more from us in the future on how we plan to apply this groundbreaking work to our capabilities.
We believe we've meaningfully crossed the chasm of transforming our company from one that creates traditional financial products into one that streams intelligence as people need it. Thank you for your support along the way, and thank you for investing in our future.

Business Highlights
Make our company an intelligence and enable others to do the same
The AI tools we’ve built are giving us time back and helping accelerate product velocity. In October, we began rolling out model routing to all employees at Block, giving everyone access to a simple, efficient way to match their work to the right model capability. In recent weeks, AI has been autonomously reviewing and merging an increasing number of our production code changes related to low-risk, routine work so our teams can focus more of their time on building differentiated products for customers. Our focus on velocity is delivering more value to our customers faster: through the first three quarters of the year, we've released over 180 new Square features, already more than in 2024 and 2025 combined.
We’re focusing more development effort on externalizing these capabilities so others can move faster too. Buzz makes AI multiplayer and serves as a communication and code repository tool accessible to everyone. We've shipped more than two dozen releases to Buzz since launching our alpha release in July. We’ve rebuilt onboarding so a new user opens the app directly into a live team of AI agents already at work, with their existing developer tools detected automatically rather than wired up by hand. We’ve also released the governance and identity infrastructure, including an admin console, encrypted identity backups, and centralized community moderation that enterprises need to deploy Buzz at scale.
Build the best intelligence to run your business
Square GPV growth accelerated to 14% in the third quarter, the strongest growth rate since the first quarter of 2023, driven by our continued distribution expansion and increased product velocity.6 Self-onboarding continued to drive strong new customer acquisition while the number of new sellers joining Square through Independent Sales Organization (ISO) partners grew 90% quarter over quarter. Our field sales team and expanded enterprise motion continued to drive new customer acquisition, both in the U.S. and internationally, including Tommy’s Tavern + Tap, a fast-scaling full-service restaurant chain that chose Square to run its 16-plus locations across four states, and Public House Group, the popular London hospitality group behind The Pelican and The Bull in Charlbury.
In October, we launched Squaremates, our rebranded AI product for Square sellers that builds on the heritage of Square AI and Managerbot. Squaremates is generally available in the U.S. and brings some of Block’s most powerful AI capabilities directly to Square sellers. We’ve redesigned the experience to offer what we think is a leading AI product for businesses, grounded in the unique commerce, customer, appointment, and inventory data of each seller. Squaremates accesses cloud compute to take on more complex, multi-step work, such as staff analyses, drafting customer win-back campaigns and holiday promotions, and preparing purchase orders for food and beverage sellers. We plan to keep expanding Squaremates’ capabilities, including an Apps platform that lets sellers build bespoke apps connected to their Square data and existing staff permissions, and procurement agents that help sellers manage inventory and order from suppliers. We also plan to bring Buzz into Squaremates, providing sellers with a team communication and task management platform built with AI agents as first-party participants.
We also continue to innovate on hardware with the October launch of the new Square Kiosk, our next-generation self-ordering device built from the ground up around voice-enabled AI. Customers can now order by voice or touch, ask questions about the menu, and get personalized recommendations, the same way they would at the counter. Kiosk was built with larger sellers in mind, and early response from multi-unit operators has been strong. We’ve seen franchise operators who started out skeptical of kiosks now wanting to place Square Kiosks throughout their national footprint. We set out to build hardware that saves sellers time and money while helping them serve more customers, and we believe we've delivered on that vision.

Connect neighborhoods globally

We plan to continue expanding the Neighborhoods network further while continuing to innovate on the product to make Neighborhoods more valuable to sellers and their customers. We’re also focused on increasing the connectivity between Neighborhoods and Square products like Loyalty and Square Online as well as expanding Neighborhoods functionality across more Square hardware, building on early rollouts on Square Stand and Reader.

Cash App

Key Operating Metrics And Non-GAAP
Financial Measures
To supplement our financial information presented in accordance with generally accepted accounting principles in the United States (GAAP), from period to period, we consider and present certain operating and financial measures that we consider key metrics or are not prepared in accordance with GAAP, including Gross Payment Volume (GPV), Adjusted EBITDA, Adjusted EBITDA margin, Adjusted Net Income (Loss), Adjusted Net Income (Loss) Per Share (Adjusted EPS), Adjusted Diluted Net Income (Loss) Per Share (Adjusted Diluted EPS), Adjusted Operating Income (Loss), Adjusted Operating Income (Loss) margin, Free Cash Flow, Non-GAAP Cash Flow, constant currency, and non-GAAP operating expenses. We believe these metrics and measures are useful to facilitate period-to-period comparisons of our business and to facilitate comparisons of our performance to that of other payments solution providers. GPV includes Square GPV and Cash App Business GPV. Square GPV is defined as the total dollar amount of all card and bank payments processed by sellers using Square, net of refunds. Cash App Business GPV comprises Cash App activity related to peer-to-peer transactions received by business accounts and peer-to-peer payments sent from a credit card. GPV does not include transactions from our BNPL products.
Adjusted Net Income (Loss), Adjusted Net Income (Loss) Per Share (Adjusted EPS), and Adjusted Diluted Net Income (Loss) Per Share (Adjusted Diluted EPS) are non-GAAP financial measures that represent our net income (loss) and net income (loss) per share, adjusted to eliminate the effect of restructuring share-based compensation expense, contingencies, restructuring, and other charges; goodwill and intangible asset impairment; amortization of intangible assets; amortization of debt discount and issuance costs; gain or loss on revaluation of equity investments; remeasurement gain or loss on revaluation of bitcoin investment; the gain or loss on the disposal of property and equipment; acquired deferred revenue and cost adjustments; the discrete benefits from the release of valuation allowances on our deferred tax assets; and the tax effect of non-GAAP net income adjustments, as applicable. Additionally, for purposes of calculating Adjusted Diluted EPS, we add back cash interest expense on convertible senior notes, as if converted at the beginning of the period, if the impact is dilutive. To calculate Adjusted Diluted EPS, we adjust the weighted-average number of shares of common stock outstanding for the dilutive effect of all potential shares of common stock. In periods when we recorded an Adjusted Net Loss, the diluted Adjusted EPS is the same as basic Adjusted EPS because the effects of potentially dilutive items were anti-dilutive given the Adjusted Net Loss position.

Adjusted EBITDA and Adjusted EBITDA margin are non-GAAP financial measures that represent our net income (loss), adjusted to exclude share-based compensation expense; restructuring share-based compensation expense; depreciation and amortization; contingencies, restructuring, and other charges; interest income and expense; remeasurement gain or loss on bitcoin investment; other income and expense; provision for (benefit from) income taxes; gain or loss on disposal of property and equipment; and acquired deferred revenue and cost adjustment, as applicable. Adjusted EBITDA margin is calculated as Adjusted EBITDA divided by gross profit.
Adjusted Operating Income (Loss) is a non-GAAP financial measure that represents our operating income (loss), adjusted to eliminate the effect of amortization of acquired technology assets; contingencies, restructuring, and other charges; restructuring share-based compensation expenses; goodwill and intangible asset impairment and amortization of customer and other acquired intangible assets. Adjusted Operating Income (Loss) margin is calculated as Adjusted Operating Income (Loss) divided by gross profit.

We exclude amortization of intangible assets arising from business combinations from Adjusted Net Income (Loss), Adjusted EPS, Adjusted Diluted EPS, Adjusted Operating Income (Loss), and Adjusted Operating Income (Loss) Margin because the amount of such expenses in any specific period may not directly correlate to the underlying performance of our ongoing business operations. Acquisition-related costs include amounts paid to redeem acquirees’ unvested stock-based compensation awards; charges associated with holdback liabilities; and legal, accounting, and due diligence costs. Integration costs include advisory and other professional services or consulting fees necessary to integrate acquired businesses. Other costs that are not reflective of our core business operating expenses may include contingencies, restructuring, and other charges; impairment charges; restructuring share-based compensation expense; and certain litigation and regulatory charges. For Adjusted Net Income (Loss), Adjusted EPS, and Adjusted Diluted EPS we also add back the impact of the acquired deferred revenue and deferred cost adjustment, which was written down to fair value in purchase accounting, and adjust for the tax effect of the non-GAAP net income adjustments.
