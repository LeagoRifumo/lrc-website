Financial Performance
BW LPG Limited (“BW LPG”, the “Company”, NYSE ticker code: “BWLP”, OSE ticker code: “BWLPG.OL”) reported a Q3 2026 Net Profit After Tax (NPAT) of US$359 million, yielding an annualised return on equity of 66%. The Q3 profit attributable to the equity holders of the Company was US$311 million, and earnings per share were US$2.05.
The Company reported ample liquidity of US$859 million, boosted by the convertible bond issued in September. The end-of-quarter net leverage ratio was 20.0%, compared to 23.5% as of 30 June 2026.

Trading results reported a gross profit of US$163 million and a profit after tax of US$118 million. Net asset value of Product Services was US$216 million at the end of the quarter.
Q3 Market Update
Entering the third quarter of 2026, the conflict in the Middle East shifted from open hostilities towards a prolonged standoff characterised by intermittent strikes and continued disruptions to LPG exports. The growing prospect of a protracted conflict contributed to higher LPG prices in the Far East, widening the US–Far East arbitrage.
This in turn increased demand for long-haul LPG shipments from the US, supporting higher VLGC spot rates and increasing vessel values throughout the quarter.
Meanwhile, continued congestion in the Panama Canal prompted VLGCs to reroute via the Cape of Good Hope, increasing voyage distances and reducing effective vessel supply. This provided further support to an already firm spot market.
Although major hostilities in the Middle East subsided during the quarter, vessels transiting the Strait of Hormuz continued to face significant security risks. Nevertheless, LPG exports from the Middle East increased in Q3 compared to Q2.
Cargo Movements
During the first nine months of 2026, US exports carried on VLGCs grew by 14%, supported by additional export capacity and demand from countries usually supplied by Middle Eastern volumes.
China remained the largest destination for US exports, with volumes growing 18% year-on-year during the first nine months of 2026. Japan remained the second-largest destination, while exports to India continued during the quarter and increased 2.5-fold in the first nine months of 2026, compared to the same period in 2025.
2
Middle Eastern exports carried on VLGCs declined by 48% during the first nine months of 2026, as the ongoing conflict continued to restrict LPG exports from the region. Despite the year-to-date decline, however, exports increased by 54% in Q3 2026 compared to the preceding quarter.
Far Eastern LPG imports declined by 16% during the first nine months of 2026, with China accounting for the largest share of the decline. Compared to Q2 2026, however, Far Eastern imports grew by 29% in the third quarter, nearly recovering to levels seen before the outbreak of war in the Middle East.
LPG imports into Southeast Asia carried on VLGCs grew by 2% compared to the same period in 2025. LPG is increasingly being sourced from the US, which accounted for 74% of the region’s imports during the third quarter.
Panama Canal
During the third quarter, water reserves for the Panama Canal deteriorated to the point where the Panama Canal Authority imposed restrictions on the number of transits through the original Panamax and newer Neopanamax locks. As water reserves stabilised towards the end of the quarter, restrictions on the Neopanamax locks were lifted. Still, future restrictions cannot be ruled out, particularly if El Niño adversely affects rainfall in Panama.
Overall, demand for canal transits remains high, with several shipping segments competing for a limited number of daily slots. This has prompted several VLGCs to avoid the canal, instead sailing around the Cape of Good Hope on their voyages to and from the Far East.
With US hydrocarbon exports expected to increase in the coming years, demand for Panama Canal transits is also expected to rise as additional LNG, ethane and LPG carriers compete for transit slots.
China PDH Plants
Average utilisation rates at Chinese PDH plants remained above 70% for almost all of Q3 2026, in line with levels seen before the outbreak of war in the Middle East earlier this year. Despite elevated PDH run rates, LPG inventories in China are healthy, as imports in Q3 rose compared to the preceding quarter.
One new PDH plant is scheduled to come online before the end of 2026, with an additional nine and four plants or expansions scheduled for 2027 and 2028, respectively.
Fleet Capacity
So far in 2026, 41 VLGCs have been delivered, with a further three vessels scheduled for delivery before year-end.
The current orderbook comprises 177 VLGCs, equivalent to 39% of the existing fleet, with deliveries extending into the beginning of 2031. Approximately 8% of the existing global VLGC fleet of 451 ships is 25 years old or older.
Market Outlook
Spot VLGC earnings are expected to remain highly sensitive to geopolitical developments and shifts in global trade patterns.
A full reopening of the Strait of Hormuz would almost certainly lead to a further increase in Middle Eastern LPG export volumes. However, increased LPG availability from the US could also narrow the US–Far East arbitrage and ultimately put downward pressure on VLGC spot rates.
Assuming a resolution of the conflict in Q4 2026, Middle Eastern exports are expected to recover gradually, although a full recovery is expected to take 12–36 months, depending on local conditions and the severity of infrastructure damage. North American LPG exports are expected to continue growing, supported by new export infrastructure and increasing gas-rich oil production from the Permian Basin.

About BW LPG
BW LPG is the world’s leading owner and operator of LPG vessels, with a fleet of about 50 Very Large Gas Carriers (VLGCs) and Large Gas Carriers (LGCs), including 20 vessels powered by LPG dual-fuel propulsion technology. Building on over five decades of LPG shipping experience, the company is also a significant player in the LPG commodity trading market. Combining its shipping expertise with in-house trading capabilities, BW LPG provides reliable services for sourcing and delivering LPG to customers worldwide.

APPENDIX - Non-IFRS financial measures
This interim financial report contains a number of non-IFRS financial measures that Management uses to monitor and analyse the performance of the Group’s business. Non-IFRS financial measures exclude amounts that are included in, or include amounts that are excluded from, the most directly comparable measure calculated and presented in accordance with IFRS, or are calculated using measures that are not calculated in accordance with IFRS. Non-IFRS financial measures may be considered in addition to, but not as a substitute for or superior to, information presented in accordance with IFRS.
The Group believes that these non-IFRS financial measures, in addition to IFRS measures, provide an enhanced understanding of the Group’s results and related trends, therefore increasing transparency and clarity of the Group’s results and business.
There are no generally accepted accounting principles governing the calculation of these measures and the criteria upon which these measures are based can vary from company to company. The non-IFRS financial measures presented in this interim financial report may not be comparable to other similarly titled measures used by other companies, have limitations as analytical tools and should not be considered in isolation or as a substitute for analysis of the Group’s operating results as reported under IFRS. The Group encourages investors and analysts not to rely on any single financial measure but to review the Group’s financial and non-financial information in its entirety.
The following non-IFRS measures are presented in this interim financial report.
TCE income – Shipping per calendar day (total)
The Group defines TCE income - Shipping per calendar day (total) as TCE income - Shipping divided by calendar days (total).
The Group defines calendar days (total) as the total number of days in a period during which vessels are owned or chartered-in is in its possession, including technical off-hire days and waiting days. Calendar days (total) are an indicator of the size of the fleet over a period and affect both the amount of revenue and the amount of expense that the Group records during that period.
The Group defines waiting days as the number of days its vessels are unemployed for market reasons, excluding technical off-hire days. Ballast voyages, positioning voyages prior to deliveries on time charters and time spent on cleaning of tanks when vessels are switching from one cargo type to another are not considered waiting time. Waiting days per vessel are calculated as total waiting days for owned and chartered-in vessels divided by the number of owned and chartered-in vessels (not weighted by ownership share in each vessel).
The Group defines technical off-hire as the time lost due to off-hire days associated with major repairs, dry dockings or special or intermediate surveys. Technical off-hire per vessel is calculated as an average for owned, bareboat and chartered-in vessels (not weighted by ownership share in each vessel).
The Group believes TCE income - Shipping per calendar day (total) is meaningful to investors because it is a measure of how well the Company manages the fleet technically and commercially.
