•New Phase 2 REC-4881 data presented at CGA-IGC in an expanded adult population aged 18 years and older show deep, durable polyp burden reductions in FAP, with activity throughout the GI tract and in larger, higher-risk polyps.

Internal Pipeline Highlights
Positive Phase 2 results from ongoing TUPELO trial of REC-4881 in FAP highlight activity across GI tract and on larger polyps
FAP is a genetically defined driver of GI cancer, affecting >50,000 patients in the US and EU5 who face major surgeries and life-long surveillance with no approved therapies, representing a potential $15 billion opportunity, based on Company estimates. Among the approximately 35,000 post-colectomy patients, upper GI disease represents the highest unmet need in FAP, due to risks of bleeding and perforation from polyp removal, high complication and morbidity rates from surgery, and higher cancer risk.
REC-4881, a potential first-in-class oral therapy granted FDA Orphan Drug and Fast Track designations, is designed to address both Wnt/β-catenin initiation and MAPK evolution pathways. The opportunity was identified through unbiased phenotypic screening using Recursion’s proprietary AI foundation models, which revealed MEK1/2 inhibition as a potential way to reverse cellular phenotypes associated with APC loss, a previously unappreciated synthetic rescue relationship.
On November 2, Recursion announced further positive data from its ongoing Phase 1b/2 TUPELO trial of REC-4881, an investigational, oral MEK1/2 inhibitor in development for FAP. These data were presented by Lisa Boardman, M.D., Professor of Medicine at Mayo Clinic, in a presidential plenary session at The Collaborative Group of the Americas on Inherited Gastrointestinal Cancer (CGA-IGC) Annual Meeting in Denver, Colorado.
•Deep, durable reductions in total polyp burden, strengthened in the expanded adult FAP population (18+): After 12 weeks of REC-4881 (4 mg QD), 87% of patients (13 of 15) had a
reduction in total polyp burden, with a 47% median reduction; approximately 1 in 3 achieved reductions of ≥65%. Twelve weeks after stopping treatment, 85% of evaluable patients (11 of 13) remained below baseline.
•Activity across the upper and lower GI tract, including near-clearance: After 12 weeks of treatment, median polyp burden reductions were 55% in the upper GI and 50% in the lower GI, supporting REC-4881’s potential as a single oral therapy to address FAP across the GI tract.
•Upper GI, the area of greatest unmet need in FAP (results first reported Oct. 15): 92% of patients (12 of 13) had reductions after 12 weeks of treatment, with two patients achieving ≥98% reductions; half (6 of 12) improved by at least one Spigelman stage; and 92% (11 of 12) remained below baseline 12 weeks after stopping treatment.
•New lower GI results highlight breadth of activity: 9 of 11 patients (82%) with measurable lower GI polyp burden at baseline had reductions after 12 weeks of treatment, with a 50% median reduction and 1 in 3 achieving ≥65%; 70% of evaluable patients remained below baseline 12 weeks after stopping treatment.
•New analysis shows substantial reductions in larger, higher-risk polyps: 6 of 8 patients with polyps >5 mm at baseline had fewer of these polyps after 12 weeks of treatment. Four achieved reductions of ≥80%, including two whose counts fell from more than 90 polyps to one. Six of eight remained below their baseline count 12 weeks after stopping treatment.
•No new safety signals observed: Favorable chronic dosing safety profile. The highest-grade treatment-related adverse event was Grade 1 or 2 in 21 of 26 patients (81%) and Grade 3 in four patients (15%); one patient had no TRAEs. No Grade 4 or 5 TRAEs, and no additional discontinuations since the previous data cutoff.
The Company anticipates initiating a registrational trial in the second half of 2027, pending ongoing FDA discussions, and will provide additional details later this year.
Early data from the Phase 1 dose-escalation portion of DAHLIA, our ongoing open-label Phase 1/2 study of REC-1245, show the following (data cutoff: August 21, 2026):
REC-1245 is a potential first-in-class oral molecular glue designed to exploit a synthetic-lethal vulnerability in cancers by degrading RBM39, a splicing factor that regulates genes involved in DNA repair and stress response. The Recursion platform contributed each of the three core elements of the program: the target, the molecule and the initial hypotheses on which patients are most likely to benefit. The target emerged from Recursion’s platform, which found that degrading RBM39 mimics the loss of CDK12, while sparing CDK13-associated liabilities. REC-1245 was identified within 18 months after screening only 204 compounds using Recursion’s design platform. The same platform-derived biology pointed to tumors intrinsically primed by replication stress and reliance on accurate splicing, including hard-to-treat cancers such as endometrial, hepatocellular and ovarian cancers and transcriptionally driven tumors.
•Three partial responses per RECIST 1.1 criteria (2 confirmed, 1 unconfirmed) in heavily pretreated patients with endometrial cancer and hepatocellular carcinoma, all at doses of 45 mg once daily (QD) or higher, with a disease control rate of 91.7% at those doses (n=12).
•Recursion has selected 45 mg QD for exploratory expansion cohorts, including endometrial cancer, where limited second-line treatments are approved; at the 45 mg QD dose (n=10), no dose-limiting toxicities or treatment-related serious adverse events were observed, and treatment-related adverse events were predominantly Grade 1 or 2. Most frequent TRAEs were Grade 1 or Grade 2 fatigue and anemia (30% each).
•Preliminary dose expansion data in endometrial cancer are expected in the second half of 2027. Dose escalation will also continue in other solid tumor indications.
Other Portfolio Updates: REC-7735 is an orally bioavailable, precision-designed small molecule inhibitor of PI3Kα H1047R generated using the Recursion OS. In the second half of 2026, Recursion initiated the Phase 1/2 ZINNIA clinical study for patients with select PIK3CA H1047R-mutant solid tumors.
As announced in October 2026, given the promising data in REC-4881 and REC-1245, Recursion plans to continue to focus resources on its high-impact partnerships and internal programs including REC-4881 (MEK1/2 - familial adenomatous polyposis), REC-1245 (RBM39 - endometrial cancer and other solid tumors), REC-4539 (LSD1 - solid tumors and hematology), REC-7735 (PI3Kα H1047R - solid tumors) and REC-102 (ENPP1 - hypophosphatasia). Recursion intends to pursue partnership opportunities to advance REC-617 (CDK7) and REC-3565 (MALT1).
Partnered Discovery Highlights Recursion’s 9th Milestone Achieved in Past Two Years
Sanofi options potential first-in-class immunology development candidate
Sanofi has optioned the first development candidate from the two companies’ multi-program research collaboration. The candidate has now been added to Sanofi’s development pipeline and is expected to enter Phase 1 clinical trials in 2027, with Sanofi funding and leading further development and commercialization to advance the program toward patients.
The option triggered a $10 million milestone payment to Recursion, bringing total upfront and milestone payments achieved from Sanofi under the collaboration to $144 million; Recursion is eligible for more than $300 million in additional milestones under the program, with double digit royalties on net product sales, if approved.
The potential first-in-class oral small molecule was designed to achieve a differentiated profile against a challenging dual target implicated in inflammatory disease. Optimizing for this target pair required navigating complex and competing chemistry constraints to achieve the desired balance of drug-like properties. The candidate was delivered by the combination of Recursion’s integrated AI-enabled drug design platform and small-molecule chemistry capabilities with Sanofi’s deep expertise in drug discovery and immunology. Recursion applied multiple computational design approaches across iterative cycles of prediction, chemical design, synthesis, and experimental validation to rapidly explore and optimize a complex chemical space.
With the first development candidate optioned, the collaboration has now achieved six progress-based milestones across five programs. Additional programs across the collaboration continue to advance. Over the next 12 months, other programs in the joint portfolio have the potential to reach development candidate and other progress-based milestones.
To learn more about this milestone, read the press release.
Genentech advanced first neuroscience target into early discovery program
In 3Q26, Genentech exercised the first Validated Target Option under the companies' neuroscience collaboration, advancing a previously unexplored neuroscience target.
The milestone provides additional early evidence that Recursion's AI-native platform can both discover and play a key role experimentally validating unexplored therapeutic targets in neuroscience, one of
medicine's most challenging therapeutic areas, where decades of research have largely focused on a limited number of well-studied targets. Predictions generated from the first whole-genome CRISPR knockout map, which was built by Recursion in partnership with Roche and Genentech from a subset of over 1 trillion internally manufactured iPSC-derived neuronal cells, were rigorously jointly validated with Genentech. Only targets that consistently demonstrated compelling evidence across pathway, functional, and disease validation progress into a validation package.
Next steps will include advancing the target through small molecule design, hit generation, and validation using Recursion's AI-native chemistry platform. More broadly, the neuronal and microglial maps of biology remain reusable assets capable of being collaboratively utilized with biological, genetics, and computational expertise to generate and experimentally validate additional therapeutic hypotheses. To date, Recursion has achieved $216 million in upfront and milestone payments from the Roche and Genentech collaboration. The collaboration includes up to 40 potential small molecule discovery programs, each carrying the potential for more than $300 million in development, commercialization, and net sales milestones as well as tiered royalties up to high single digits per small molecule program for Recursion.

Third Quarter 2026 Financial Results
•Cash Position: Cash, cash equivalents and restricted cash were $561.5 million as of September 30, 2026. Including net proceeds of approximately $187.2 million from the Company's underwritten offering which completed on October 16, 2026, pro forma cash, cash equivalents and restricted cash were approximately $748.7 million. Based on current operating plans with no additional financing, the Company expects its cash runway to extend into 2029.
•Revenue: Total revenue, consisting primarily of revenue from collaboration agreements, was $18.6 million for the third quarter of 2026, compared to $5.2 million for the third quarter of 2025. The increase was driven primarily by the Company's collaboration with Sanofi, under which the
Company achieved a $10.0 million milestone for an immunology small molecule program during the quarter.
•Research and Development Expenses: Research and development expenses were $99.1 million for the third quarter of 2026, compared to $121.1 million for the third quarter of 2025. The decrease was driven primarily by $22.8 million of in-process research and development costs related to the acquisition of full rights to REC-102, Recursion’s ENPP1 inhibitor for the treatment of hypophosphatasia (HPP), from its joint venture with Rallybio that were recognized in the third quarter of 2025 and did not recur in the current period.
•General and Administrative Expenses: General and administrative expenses were $32.6 million for the third quarter of 2026 compared to $41.6 million for the third quarter of 2025. The decrease in general and administrative expense compared to the prior period was primarily driven by a decrease in infrastructure technology expenses and professional service costs.
•Net Loss: Net loss was $119.0 million for the third quarter of 2026, compared to a net loss of $162.3 million for the third quarter of 2025.
•Operational cash flows: Net cash used in operating activities was $77.4 million for the three months ended September 30, 2026, compared to net cash used in operating activities of $117.4 million for the three months ended September 30, 2025. The decrease in cash used in operating activities was primarily driven by operating efficiencies across the company and working capital movements.
•Cash Operating Expense: Cash operating expense, excluding partnership inflows and transaction costs, for the nine months ended September 30, 2026 was $271.5 million compared to $323.2 million for the nine months ended September 30, 2025.

To supplement our financial statements prepared in accordance with U.S. GAAP, we monitor and consider operating cash expense, which is a non-GAAP financial measure. We define cash operating expense as the net cash used in operating activities, excluding non-ordinary course transaction costs and partnership cash inflows. This non-GAAP financial measure is not based on any standardized methodology prescribed by U.S. GAAP and is not necessarily comparable to similarly-titled measures presented by other companies. We believe operating cash expense to be a liquidity measure that provides useful information to management and investors about the amount of cash consumed by the operations of the business. A limitation of using this non-U.S. GAAP measure is that operating cash expense does not represent the total change in cash and cash equivalents for the period because it excludes cash provided by or used for other investing and financing activities. We account for this limitation by providing information about our capital expenditures and other investing and financing activities in the statements of cash flows in our financial statements. Additionally, we reconciled operating cash expense above to net cash used in operating activities, the most directly comparable U.S. GAAP financial measure. In addition,
it is important to note that other companies, including companies in our industry, may not use operating cash expense, may calculate operating cash expense in a different manner than we do or may use other financial measures to evaluate their performance, all of which could reduce the usefulness of operating cash expense as a comparative measure. Because of these limitations, operating cash expense should not be considered in isolation from, or as a substitute for, financial information prepared in accordance with U.S. GAAP.

Latest Data from Phase 2 Trial of REC-4881
Deep reductions in total polyp burden, sustained off treatment
The TUPELO study was designed to study polyp burden reduction after 12 weeks on REC-4881 (4 mg QD) and again after 12 weeks off-treatment. Results in an expanded adult population aged 18 years and older show a rapid and durable treatment effect across the GI tract. Trial results have strengthened since the November 2025 data cut, including expanded adult FAP population ages 18 years and older:
●87% of patients (13 of 15) had a reduction in total polyp burden after 12 weeks of treatment, with a 47% median reduction. One in three patients achieved reductions of 65% or more.
●All patients added since the November 2025 data cut (n=4) had a reduction in total polyp burden, including two patients with 89% and 98% reduction, respectively. At Week 25, after 12 weeks off-treatment, 85% of patients (11 of 13) remained below baseline.
●The persistence of polyp burden reduction below baseline in most patients may allow for dosing flexibility, which could matter in the long-term management of a chronic disease.
“The durability of a reduction in polyp burden is an important metric to understand for any type of chemopreventive agent in FAP. The dream chemopreventive agent for FAP is one that a patient doesn’t have to take lifelong and that potentially could be taken for a shorter amount of time or with an intermittent dosing pattern. Seeing durability over a twelve-week course is certainly encouraging. In longer trials we’ll see exactly how long that durability lasts, but I think it is a very encouraging first sign for this drug,” said Dr. Katona.
Activity across the upper and lower GI tract, including near-clearance
For post-colectomy patients, upper GI disease represents the highest unmet need in FAP, due to risks of bleeding and perforation from polyp removal, high complication rates from surgery, and high cancer risk. When endoscopic management is no longer sufficient to control duodenal disease, patients may require major surgery, including removal of the duodenum. Lower GI disease also remains a concern after colectomy, as polyps can continue to develop in the remaining rectum or ileal pouch, posing an ongoing cancer risk and potential need for additional surgery. REC-4881 has now shown polyp burden reductions in both regions.
Upper GI findings:
●92% of patients (12 of 13) had reductions in polyp burden after 12 weeks of treatment, with a 55% median reduction. Nearly 1 in 2 patients achieved reductions of ≥65%, and two patients achieved reductions of ≥98%.
●Half (6 of 12) improved by at least one Spigelman stage at Week 13, a widely used system clinicians use to grade duodenal polyposis and decide how often patients need endoscopy and when surgery is recommended.
●92% (11 of 12) remained below baseline at Week 25, after 12 weeks off-treatment, and 6 of 11 had equal or greater reductions during the off-treatment period than during treatment.
Lower GI findings:
●9 of 11 patients (82%) with measurable polyp burden at baseline had reductions after 12 weeks of treatment, with a 50% median reduction, and 1 in 3 achieved reductions of 65% or more.
●At Week 25, after 12 weeks off-treatment, 70% of evaluable patients remained below baseline.
Substantial reductions in larger, higher-risk polyps
Polyp size is one of the primary risk factors in FAP. The chance that a polyp contains high-grade dysplasia or cancer increases with polyp size, and clinical guidelines use size to decide which polyps to remove and how often patients need endoscopy. In a new analysis, REC-4881 reduced the number of polyps >5 mm in most patients who had them at baseline:
●Six of eight patients had fewer polyps >5 mm after 12 weeks of treatment, and in four patients the count fell by ≥80%.
●In two patients, more than 90 larger polyps were reduced to a single polyp. In both of those patients, all of the larger polyps were in the upper GI.
●The highest Week 13 responder went from 1,344 mm of total polyp burden to 32 mm, a 98% reduction, and from 96 upper GI polyps >5 mm to one. This is a single patient and is not representative of the overall population.
●Six of the eight patients assessed at Week 25 were below their baseline count after 12 weeks off-treatment. Most of the larger polyps at baseline were in the upper GI.
Note that the study collected polyp counts using >5 mm as the largest size category, which is inclusive of all polyps above this threshold; separate counts for polyps >10 mm were not collected.
No new safety signals observed
MEK1/2 inhibitors are an established class with a well-characterized side-effect profile. Across the 26 safety-evaluable patients dosed at 4 mg (five in Phase 1b and 21 in Phase 2), treatment was well tolerated, with a safety profile consistent with other MEK inhibitors and with previously reported results. No new safety signals were observed as of the data cutoff in the expanded patient population, which includes patients 18 and older.
●The majority of treatment-related adverse events (TRAE) were Grade 1 or 2 (21 of 26 patients, 80.8%). The highest-grade TRAE was Grade 3 in four patients (15.4%), and there were no Grade 4 or 5 TRAEs.
●The most common TRAEs were consistent with MEK1/2 inhibition, including dermatitis acneiform, rash, CPK increase, diarrhea, and asymptomatic LVEF decrease. Adverse events were manageable, and most resolved within 12 weeks off-treatment.
●Four patients discontinued for Grade 1 or 2 TRAEs, with no discontinuations for a Grade 3 event. No additional discontinuations were reported since the previous data cutoff.

About FAP
FAP is a rare, inherited disease that affects more than 50,000 patients in the US and EU5, with no approved pharmacologic therapies. It is caused by mutations in the APC gene, and without surgery to remove the colon, the lifetime risk of colorectal cancer approaches 100%. Colectomy does not stop the disease, as polyps keep forming in the remaining rectum or ileal pouch and in the upper GI tract, so patients face lifelong endoscopic surveillance, repeated polyp removal and, for some, further major surgery. Based on Company estimates, FAP represents a potential $15 billion opportunity.
Among the approximately 35,000 post-colectomy patients, upper GI disease is the area of greatest unmet need. Nearly all patients develop adenomas in the duodenum, and duodenal or ampullary cancer is a leading cause of cancer-related death after colectomy. The thin duodenal wall raises the risk of bleeding and perforation when these polyps are removed. When endoscopy can no longer control the disease, patients may need major surgery, such as removal of the duodenum or a Whipple procedure, which carries significant morbidity and a long recovery.
Natural History of FAP
To better understand the natural history of FAP and to put the single-arm results of TUPELO in context, the Company collaborated with Amsterdam University Medical Center to analyze a registry of approximately 200 patients with FAP. In 47 patients matched to the original TUPELO eligibility criteria, polyp burden increased over time in both the upper and lower GI. In the 35 patients with upper GI involvement, mean annualized polyp burden increased by 33%. In the 28 patients with lower GI involvement, mean annualized polyp burden increased by 88%. These results help to contextualize the polyp burden reduction observed after 12 weeks of REC-4881 treatment.
REC-4881 Background and Dual Mechanism
REC-4881, a potential first-in-class oral therapy granted FDA Orphan Drug and Fast Track designations, is an allosteric inhibitor of MEK1 and MEK2 designed to address both Wnt/β-catenin initiation and MAPK evolution pathways. The opportunity was identified through unbiased phenotypic screening using Recursion’s AI foundation models, which showed that MEK1/2 inhibition could potentially reverse the cellular effects of APC loss. This dual mechanism differentiates REC-4881 from other single-agent therapies studied in FAP, which have each been directed at a single pathway, and provides the potential to suppress both pathways and reduce polyp burden across the GI tract.
About the TUPELO Trial of REC-4881
REC-4881 is being evaluated in the Phase 1b/2 TUPELO open label study (NCT05552755) assessing its safety, tolerability, pharmacokinetics (PK), and efficacy as a monotherapy in post-colectomy patients aged 18+ years with familial adenomatous polyposis (FAP).
