Research and development expenses were $23.5 million and $79.1 million for the three and nine months ended September 30, 2026, respectively, compared with $27.3 million and $88.5 million during the same periods in 2025, respectively. The decrease was primarily driven by lower development expenses, reflecting reduced clinical trial costs following completion of our Phase 3 program for COMP360 psilocybin treatment in TRD, as well as reduced discovery program expenses following the termination of certain programs in connection with the
reorganization that took place in the fourth quarter of 2024 and the related contract terminations in 2025. This decrease was partially offset by an increase in facilities and other expenses primarily due to an increase in external consulting fees and contractors.
Selling, general and administrative expenses were $33.0 million and $72.6 million for the three and nine months ended September 30, 2026, respectively, compared with $13.2 million and $44.6 million during the same periods in 2025, respectively. The increase was primarily due to increased costs to support our commercial preparedness activities in advance of potential FDA approval and commercialization of COMP360 for TRD, including investments in commercial infrastructure, market access and reimbursement capabilities, supply chain readiness and other launch preparation activities.
Net loss was $9.8 million, or $0.07 net loss per share (basic) and $0.31 net loss per share (diluted), and $172.4 million, or $1.35 net loss per share (basic and diluted), for the three and nine months ended September 30, 2026, respectively, compared with $137.7 million, or $1.44 net loss per share (basic and diluted), and $194.0 million, or $2.09 net loss per share (basic and diluted), during the same periods in 2025, respectively. The decrease in net loss was primarily driven by non-cash fair value adjustments related to our warrants. The change in fair value was a $40.8 million gain and $33.9 million loss, for the three and nine months ended September 30, 2026, respectively, compared with $101.3 million loss and $84.4 million loss for the same periods in 2025, respectively. As the fair value of the warrants fluctuates with our share price, this adjustment can result in significant variability in our reported net income or net loss.

About treatment resistant depression (TRD)
TRD is a form of depression, distinct from major depressive disorder (MDD), that is defined as an inadequate response to two or more courses of antidepressants of adequate dose and duration. An estimated 4 million people in the U.S. live with TRD, of whom fewer than 5% are currently on a TRD-indicated treatment.2,3,4 TRD has a significantly greater impact on individuals compared to MDD, leading to residual symptoms, poorer quality of life, increased comorbidities, higher mortality, and an increased risk of suicide.5 Research suggests this may be attributed to
the role of altered brain network functioning in TRD, with biological changes becoming more entrenched with recurrent or prolonged depressive episodes.5,6,7,8,9
Many people living with TRD experience years of cycling through treatments that aren't providing meaningful relief, with an impact extending across work, school, relationships and daily life. A national survey of people with TRD and their caregivers revealed the cumulative burden of the treatment cycle, with 95% of surveyed patients describing themselves as either functioning but struggling, surviving day-to-day or barely getting by. Furthermore, the impact extends well beyond the individual, with approximately 70% of caregivers reporting that TRD negatively affects the whole family's social, physical and/or mental health, reinforcing the urgent need for new treatment approaches that can meaningfully improve the lives of those affected by TRD.
About Post-Traumatic Stress Disorder
Post-Traumatic Stress Disorder (PTSD) is a serious mental health condition that can develop after exposure to traumatic events such as personal assault, combat, natural disasters, or serious accidents. Characterized by symptoms such as intrusive memories, avoidance behaviors, negative shifts in mood and cognition, and heightened arousal, PTSD affects approximately five percent of adults in the U.S. annually. Symptoms may appear within months of the trauma or be delayed, and they must persist for over a month and interfere with daily functioning to meet diagnostic criteria.
PTSD can impact anyone, though certain populations—including veterans, first responders, and survivors of abuse—are at elevated risk. Individuals living with PTSD frequently experience comorbid mental health conditions, most commonly, depression, anxiety disorders, substance use disorders, as well as a significantly increased risk of suicide. These overlapping conditions can intensify distress and complicate treatment.
Affecting 13 million people in the U.S. each year, PTSD remains an underserved condition. There are currently only two FDA-approved medications for PTSD. This limited pharmacological landscape underscores the urgent need to advance care for patients experiencing this debilitating condition.
About Compass Pathways
We believe mental health patients deserve the possibility of a better future. Compass Pathways plc (Nasdaq: CMPS) is a biotechnology company dedicated to unlocking urgently needed new treatment options in mental health care. Our initial focus is developing COMP360 psilocybin, a proprietary, investigational, synthetic psilocybin treatment under evaluation for treatment-resistant depression (TRD) and post-traumatic stress disorder (PTSD). COMP360 is a potentially first-in-class treatment and has Breakthrough Therapy designation from the U.S. Food and Drug Administration (FDA), as well as Innovative Licensing and Access Pathway (ILAP) designation in the UK for TRD. We are leading the world’s largest classic psychedelic clinical program in TRD, and building upon that robust foundation, we are executing a late-stage trial evaluating COMP360 for PTSD, another condition with high unmet need. Our goal is to
advance treatments that move the field of psychiatry towards treatment options that offer rapid onset and sustained durability with infrequent dosing.
Compass is headquartered in London, UK, with a U.S. office in New Jersey.
