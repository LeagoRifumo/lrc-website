CMB.TECH FLEET DEVELOPMENTS
On 9 October 2026, CMB.TECH announced that Mineral Europa (2026, 210,000 dwt), the world's first ammonia-powered dry bulk vessel, had embarked on its maiden voyage to Australia.
Mineral Europa marks the beginning of what can be the fifth propulsion revolution in the history of shipping following the oar, the sail, the steam engine and the diesel engine. For the first time in more than 200 years, ships can sail without CO₂ emissions.
Scaling up ammonia-powered shipping
Mineral Europa is the first in a new generation of ammonia-powered vessels entering the CMB.TECH fleet. In total, twenty-one ammonia-powered ships are scheduled for delivery between now and 2029, including eighteen dry bulk vessels, one container ship and two chemical tankers. Most of these vessels are expected to be delivered within the next nine months.

Seaborne iron ore trade softened somewhat in recent months following a very strong first half of the year. While iron ore remained a key pillar of dry bulk demand during the third quarter of 2026, overall global seaborne iron ore exports decreased by 2.1% year-on-year in Q3 2026 and by 0.5% between Q2 and Q3 2026. The moderation was mainly driven by weaker Brazilian exports, which fell 8.8% year-on-year in Q3 2026 (-8.7 MMT (million metric tonnes)), as some higher-cost iron ore miners reportedly cut production in response to higher mining costs (cfr. mining equipment diesel costs) and shipping freight costs. Chinese importers increasingly turned to Australian suppliers, with exports increasing by 1.8% year-on-year in Q3 2026 (+4.1 MMT), where higher transportation costs had less of an impact.
Chinese crude steel production declined by 2.8% year-to-date in 2026, while steel exports remained at elevated levels, reflecting softer domestic steel demand. Steel margins remain under pressure in both spot and forward markets. At the same time, China imported 329.7 MMT of iron ore in Q3 2026, up 0.5% year-on-year and 4.3% between Q2 and Q3 2026. Continued strong iron ore imports are supported by declining Chinese domestic iron ore production (JAN-SEP: -18 MMT per month or -22.2%). China is also expanding port stockpile capacity, which could provide additional flexibility to accommodate higher seaborne iron ore volumes.
Looking ahead, continued strength in seaborne iron ore trade is supported by the production and shipment guidance of the major iron ore miners in Australia and Brazil and the ongoing ramp-up of the Guinean Simandou project. With exports on track to reach ~26 MMT by the end of the first full year of operations, the pace of shipments could accelerate further in the coming months as the SimFer port nears completion. Moreover, there is upside risk to the official target, as the design capacity of the mines and infrastructure is well above 120 MMT/y. Overall, iron ore tonne-mile trade is still forecast to grow by 4.3% in 2027 and 4.3% in 2028.
Seaborne coal exports reached 270 MMT in Q3 2026, declining by 2.1% year-on-year and by 1.8% between Q2 and Q3 2026. Thermal coal tonne-mile demand rose by a solid 7.0% year-on-year in September, resulting in growth of 8.1% for Q3. Longer sailing distances were the main driver, as volumes increased by a more moderate 2.6% over the quarter. As noted in previous reports, trade has been restricted by limited supplies this year, primarily due to a sharp drop in Indonesian exports (cfr. logistics have been severely hampered by low water levels in the Kalimantan rivers since early August). Sailing distances, meanwhile, have been boosted by increased U.S. exports and shifting trade patterns following the Iran war and hostilities in the Black Sea.
While coal demand remains closely linked to weather patterns and energy markets, current market fundamentals suggest continued support for dry bulk demand through Q4. Demand for thermal coal remains solid, and industry experts have also raised their longer-term outlook for coal amid a tighter global energy balance and increased energy-security concerns. Overall, seaborne coal tonne-mile trade is forecast to grow by 1.6% in 2027 and 0.6% in 2028.
Bauxite continues to be one of the strongest growth commodities in the dry bulk market. Volumes have remained robust and largely uninterrupted, despite the summer period typically representing the seasonally weakest part of the year. Global seaborne bauxite transportation reached 60.4 MMT in Q3 2026, increasing by 18.3% year-on-year and decreasing by only 1.9% between Q2 and Q3 2026. Improving weather conditions are expected to support a recovery in volumes during Q4, with seasonal momentum potentially extending into Q1. As a result, bauxite is expected to remain an important source of tonne-mile demand during the second half of the year and to keep on playing an important role in global dry bulk trade growth, with growth of 3.3% forecast for 2027 and steady volumes for 2028/30.
Grain trade softened during the quarter. Global seaborne grain exports reached 58.6 MMT in Q3 2026, declining by 5.3% year-on-year and by 16.2% between Q2 and Q3 2026. While trade has been supported by solid crops from key exporters this year, higher freight costs and hostilities in the Black Sea have weighed on volumes in recent months. Meanwhile, preliminary data show that Brazilian coarse grain exports fell 24% year-on-year in September amid a seasonal lull, while soybean exports held steady at 7.4mt (flat y-o-y). Looking ahead, grain trade in billion tonne-miles is forecast to grow by 4.2% in 2027 and 2.5% in 2028.

Euronav – Tanker Markets4
Crude tanker rates climbed further in Q3 2026 from already elevated levels. By early Q4, rates had reached record highs, with VLCCs achieving some of the highest rates ever observed across all shipping markets. Average VLCC TCE rates peaked at USD 804,601 per day in October. While rates were strongest on Middle East routes, particularly TD3 (inside Hormuz), reflecting the elevated local risk, Atlantic routes still exceeded USD 500,000 per day. Strength in the VLCC market also cascaded down to smaller crude tanker segments, with cargo-splitting pushing Suezmax rates to all-time highs and average TCEs reaching USD 735,440 per day in October.
Surging freight rates triggered also a sharp increase in asset values, with several transactions in September and October concluded at new record levels as National Oil Companies (NOCs) and traders scrambled to secure vessels. Crude tanker values rose severely. The second-hand price of a 15-year-old VLCC reached USD 165 million, far above the 10-year historical average of USD 46.0 million (x 3.6 or +259.7%) and well above newbuild prices of USD 132 million.
The extraordinary freight environment has primarily been driven by the continued recovery in Middle Eastern crude oil flows, combined with severe inefficiencies across the transportation chain. Hormuz flows have increased significantly. By the end of October, AXS Marine data showed flows of 10.9 mbd from the MEG (Saudi Arabia, Iraq, UAE, Kuwait and Qatar), up 61.4% from 6.7 mbd in April. Overall seaborne crude oil imports stood at 27.3 mbd in October, 4.5 mbd below pre-war levels, but 4.6 mbd above April levels. VLCC tonne-miles remain 14.8% below 2025 levels, whilst Suezmax tonne-miles are 10.2% above 2025 levels. Overall crude tonne-miles are down 3.6%, however, crude tonne-days are up 6.0%, highlighting the disconnect between exceptionally high freight rates and underlying transportation demand, and increasing inefficiencies.
Part of the disconnect between tonne-miles and tonne-days is because of the increased inefficiency of the logistics of moving these barrels. Ship-to-ship activity in and around the Gulf of Oman remained exceptionally elevated, materially extending voyage durations and reducing effective vessel availability. A total of 141 oil tankers are now assessed to be deployed purely for shuttling crude and products. VLCCs are by far the tanker of choice for these operations, accounting for 72% of all shuttlers. However, the number of vessels deployed for shuttling only partly explains why tanker tonnage has become so tight. A key additional factor is waiting time, with average round voyages extending to 23 days compared with an optimal 9 to 10 days. Additional Saudi Arabian exports through the Red Sea, with part of these volumes sailing east via the Cape of Good Hope amid continued security risks, further increased transportation tightness.
As a cascading effect, strong Middle East demand and these logistical inefficiencies have attracted vessels away from the Atlantic basin. Combined with stable Atlantic exports, particularly from South America, this tightening of available tonnage has also driven a sharp increase in Atlantic freight rates. Chinese crude buying has recovered further since Q2 2026, although import volumes remain below pre-conflict levels (-18.9% year-on-year in October). More recently, exceptionally high freight costs have started to affect the economics of long-haul crude movements, providing some downward pressure on crude tanker rates. However, as long as the current inefficiencies surrounding Middle East exports and ship-to-ship operations persist, effective vessel supply, rather than underlying crude oil demand, is expected to remain the dominant driver of crude tanker earnings.
While conventional demand metrics, such as barrels transported and tonne-mile growth, are currently overshadowed by the exceptional inefficiencies surrounding Middle Eastern crude flows and ship-to-ship operations, these conditions are unlikely to persist indefinitely. As trade patterns gradually normalise, effective vessel supply should increase, and underlying fleet fundamentals will again become a more important driver of earnings. After very limited fleet growth in 2024 and 2025, crude tanker supply is expected to accelerate, reaching 7.1% in 2027, 16.1% in 2028, and 12.3% in 2029 for VLCCs (Suezmax: 8.7% in 2027, 11.9% 2028, and 8.5% in 2029). The orderbooks for both VLCCs and Suezmaxes now exceed 30% of the existing fleet, with the heaviest delivery schedule concentrated in 2028 and 2029. Delphis – Container Markets5
Throughout Q3 2026, the SCFI steadily increased, reaching 3,686 by the end of September. By October, the SCFI remained steady at over 3,678, its highest level since mid-2022. While part of this increase was bunker-price related, much of it reflected genuinely healthy demand and continued supply-side inefficiencies. In the charter market, time charter rates remained strong and continued to increase steadily, with the November 2026 Clarksons average containership TC earnings standing at USD 53,954 per day, compared with the 10-year November TC average of USD 31,917 per day (+69.0%).
Vessel demand has been supported by various inefficiencies requiring greater flexibility across liner networks. Elevated port congestion and waiting times have been notable drivers, triggered by knock-on disruptions from the Middle East conflict, recent weather-related disruption caused by typhoons in Asia, and port bottlenecks in parts of the developing world where trade volumes have been growing rapidly. Against this backdrop, further momentum towards a return to Red Sea routing, with Gulf of Aden transits now back to >50% of normal levels from ~30% two months ago, has so far had no material impact on the market, as liner companies have used released tonnage to help manage disruptions elsewhere in their networks. In addition, high bunker prices have constrained sailing speeds, further absorbing available vessel capacity.
The ultimate risk remains the orderbook, currently equivalent to 44.4% of the existing fleet, which makes high fleet growth inevitable, particularly in 2028-29, when deliveries are expected to reach record levels, on top of already strong fleet growth in recent years. Supply-side risks are therefore likely to build, with fleet growth set to increase to 9.5% in 2027 as deliveries ramp up through the year. At the same time, there is potential for the continued unwinding of Red Sea rerouting to erode more of the ~12% TEU-mile demand uplift observed at the peak of the diversions.
A further risk is a broader macroeconomic slowdown weighing on consumer goods demand. The world economy has so far weathered the high energy prices driven by the war, but the impact of continued or further inflation remains a major threat. Overall, supply-demand fundamentals therefore have clear potential to weaken through 2027. Container trade in billion TEU-miles is forecast to decline by -0.7% in 2027 before moderately increasing by 1.0% in 2028.
Delphis has 4 x 6,000 TEU (average age 1.8y) on the water and 1 NB 1,400 TEU container vessel. All vessels are employed under 10 to 15-year time charter contracts.
Bochem – Chemical Markets6
The chemical tanker market remained volatile during the third quarter. Following the decline in Q2, spot freight rates largely moved sideways during Q3 2026 before gaining momentum towards the end of the period. The one-year TC rate for a 19,999 dwt stainless steel chemical tanker increased slightly from USD 17,667 per day in Q2 to USD 17,958 per day in Q3 2026, 13.1% above the 10-year average one-year TC rate of USD 15,625 per day.
The exceptionally strong crude and product tanker markets are increasingly providing support to chemical tanker earnings, as higher returns in these segments reduce the availability of swing tonnage and push additional CPP volumes towards chemical tankers. In addition, 2-4% of the global chemical tanker fleet remains stuck in the Persian Gulf.

Windcat – Offshore Energy Markets7
The European CSOV market remained very strong during the third quarter, with fleet utilisation close to 100% and very limited availability of Tier 1 vessels, resulting in TCE rates of USD 55,000-78,000 per day. Rates at the higher end of this range were primarily achieved for short-term or unplanned work and oil and gas-related charters. Tight supply also increased demand for Tier 3 tonnage equipped with rental gangway systems to meet short-notice requirements.
Activity remains high entering the fourth quarter, resulting in one of the tightest autumn and winter markets seen in the CSOV segment, although rates are expected to soften seasonally from peak levels. Fleet growth continues, with 16 CSOVs delivered so far in 2026 and a further 11 vessels scheduled for delivery before year-end, subject to potential delays. Despite this expanding supply, most of the existing fleet remains contracted through 2026 and into 2027, supporting a healthy market balance.
Demand is also becoming increasingly diversified, with more CSOV and Walk-to-Work capacity being deployed in oil and gas alongside the core offshore wind market. Meanwhile, new ordering remains well below the 2023-24 peak, although six speculative newbuild orders during the third quarter indicate that owner appetite has not disappeared entirely. The current fleet consists of 78 active CSOVs, with a further 31 CSOVs on order.
The CTV market remained strong during the third quarter, with very limited vessel availability for spot requirements as most vessels remained on charter into the beginning of the fourth quarter. Charter rates remained at high levels, particularly for 24-passenger CTVs, while 12-passenger vessels also continued to achieve attractive rates in the spot market. Rates are expected to soften seasonally during the winter months from November onwards.
Looking ahead to 2027, the first contracts for the next peak season have already been secured, with further awards expected in the coming weeks. This points to continued tight vessel availability, particularly for 24-passenger CTVs, during the 2027 peak season.
Windcat has 4 (+4NB) CSOVs (average age <1y), and 60 (+3NB) CTVs (average age 10.4y).
