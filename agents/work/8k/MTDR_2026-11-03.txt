MATADOR RESOURCES COMPANY
REPORTS THIRD QUARTER 2026 RESULTS INCLUDING RECORD OIL PRODUCTION AND CONTINUED DEBT REPAYMENT
DALLAS, Texas, November 3, 2026 -- Matador Resources Company (NYSE: MTDR) (“Matador” or the “Company”) today reported financial and operating results for the third quarter of 2026. A slide presentation summarizing the details of this release is included on the Company’s website at www.matadorresources.com on the Events and Presentations page under the Investor Relations tab.
Third Quarter 2026 Financial and Operational Highlights
•Debt Repayment: $150 million repaid in October 2026.
•Current Production: 230,470 barrels of oil equivalent (“BOE”) per day, a 10% increase compared to the third quarter 2025 production volumes of 209,184 BOE per day. Production for the third quarter was 3% above guidance midpoint of 224,000 BOE per day.
•Capital Expenditures (“CapEx”): $381 million, a 19% reduction or $91 million less than the third quarter of 2025 and 10% below guidance midpoint of $425 million.
•Oil and Natural Gas Reserves: increased 16% to 771 million BOE from December 31, 2025 reserves of 667 million BOE.1
•Natural Gas Revenue: $162 million in the third quarter of 2026 compared to second quarter of 2026 revenue of negative $39 million. This sequential improvement was driven by increased natural gas realizations and the commissioning of the Hugh Brinson pipeline on September 1, 2026.
Management Summary Comments
Joseph Wm. Foran, Matador’s Founder, Chairman and CEO, commented, “The Company's third quarter results beat our expectations handily. The outperformance was built on strong production numbers and momentum provided by acquisitions across both the upstream and midstream businesses. Additionally, we have continued to execute our strategic priorities of integrating this year’s acquisitions into Matador and San Mateo operations. In doing so, we have grown both our oil and natural gas production and our reserve base. We continued to add high-quality inventory and new discoveries, such as the Woodford. This asset growth was achieved while also scheduling regular debt repayments funded by free cash flow generation. Matador previously announced our intention to repay between $350 and $400 million in borrowings by the end of 2026 and we are pleased to report that we have already repaid over $150 million in borrowings so far in the fourth quarter of 2026, which was $50 million more than previously announced. We now expect our leverage ratio to approach 1.5x by year-end 2026.
“As a result of the Company’s operations, production, progress on debt repayments and year-to-date free cash flow generation, we recently announced the eighth dividend increase in five years. Matador’s annual dividend increased to $1.75 per share per year—a 17% increase from our prior annual dividend of $1.50 per share.

San Mateo Growth
“This was a landmark year of growth for our midstream business. San Mateo recently announced increased commitments and dedications for natural gas gathering, treating and processing with a large, financially strong producer in the Delaware Basin. This expansion, together with the integration of the recently acquired Cardinal Midstream assets, are expected to provide sustainable long-term throughput volume growth and increased utilization of San Mateo’s integrated natural gas processing and midstream infrastructure.
“In addition to these agreements, San Mateo delivered solid execution across its existing midstream business, resulting in net income for the first nine months of 2026 of $135 million, compared to net income for the first nine months of 2025 of $160 million. Third Quarter 2026 Operating Expenses
For the third quarter of 2026, operating expenses of $33.67 per BOE were above the midpoint of Matador’s expected 2026 guidance range of $32.00 to $34.00 per BOE, primarily due to higher midstream operating expense of $3.89 per BOE, as compared to full-year expectations of $3.30 per BOE, primarily due to the Cardinal Midstream acquisition and certain non-recurring costs during the quarter. Notably, however, lease operating expenses (“LOE”) of $5.66 per BOE were better than expectations of $5.80 per BOE, primarily due to lower-than-expected repair and maintenance costs.

Through September 30, 2026, Matador has achieved approximately $15 million in operating expense savings through improved efficiencies relative to its guidance expectations.

Acquisitions Update
As of November 1, 2026, Matador has closed and initiated the integration of all previously announced acquisitions, including the May 2026 Federal Lease sale, Paloma and Ridge Runner. Looking ahead through year-end 2027, we expect to commence operations on 35 to 40 gross wells associated with the Federal lease sale and Paloma acreage, alongside up to 15 gross Woodford wells in New Mexico and Texas. Complementing these major deals, Matador’s “brick-by-brick” strategy continues to be effective, adding over 25,000 net acres in the Delaware Basin across more than 1,100 selective transactions throughout 2025 and 2026.
Shareholder Returns Update
During the third quarter of 2026, Matador repurchased 253,603 shares of its common stock at a weighted average price of approximately $47.64 per share for a total of $12 million, as compared to Matador’s closing price on October 30, 2026 of $60.88 per share. Matador’s Board of Directors, management and staff also continue to be regular purchasers of Matador’s shares in the open market, further aligning the Company with its shareholders. Matador’s directors and executive officers purchased approximately 23,600 shares of Matador stock during the third quarter of 2026. In addition, over 95% of Matador employees continued to participate in Matador’s Employee Share Purchase Plan, or ESPP.

About Matador Resources Company
Matador is an independent energy company engaged in the exploration, development, production and acquisition of oil and natural gas resources in the United States, with an emphasis on oil and natural gas shale and other unconventional plays. Its current operations are focused primarily on the oil and liquids-rich portion of the Wolfcamp and Bone Spring plays in the Delaware Basin in Southeast New Mexico and West Texas. Matador also operates in the Haynesville shale and Cotton Valley plays in Northwest Louisiana. Additionally, Matador conducts midstream operations in support of its exploration, development and production operations and provides natural gas processing, oil transportation services, natural gas, oil and produced water gathering services and produced water

This press release includes the non-GAAP financial measure of Adjusted EBITDA. Adjusted EBITDA is a supplemental non-GAAP financial measure that is used by management and external users of the Company’s consolidated financial statements, such as securities analysts, investors, lenders and rating agencies. “GAAP” means Generally Accepted Accounting Principles in the United States of America. The Company believes Adjusted EBITDA helps it evaluate its operating performance and compare its results of operations from period to period without regard to its financing methods or capital structure. The Company defines, on a consolidated basis and for San Mateo, Adjusted EBITDA as earnings before interest expense, income taxes, depletion, depreciation and amortization, accretion of asset retirement obligations, property impairments, unrealized derivative gains and losses, non-recurring transaction costs for certain acquisitions, certain other non-cash items and non-cash stock-based compensation expense and net gain or loss on asset sales and impairment. Adjusted EBITDA is not a measure of net income (loss) or net cash provided by operating activities as determined by GAAP. All references to Matador’s Adjusted EBITDA are those values attributable to Matador Resources Company shareholders after giving effect to Adjusted EBITDA attributable to third-party non-controlling interests, including in San Mateo.
Adjusted EBITDA should not be considered an alternative to, or more meaningful than, net income (loss) or net cash provided by operating activities as determined in accordance with GAAP or as an indicator of the Company’s operating performance or liquidity. Certain items excluded from Adjusted EBITDA are significant components of understanding and assessing a company’s financial performance, such as a company’s cost of capital and tax structure. Adjusted EBITDA may not be comparable to similarly titled measures of another company because all companies may not calculate Adjusted EBITDA in the same manner.
