Imperial reported estimated net income in the third quarter of $2,454 million, up from net income of $2,190 million in the second quarter of 2026, primarily driven by higher commodity prices and downstream volumes.
Quarterly cash flows from operating activities were $3,228 million, up from $2,704 million generated in the second quarter of 2026. Excluding the impact of working capital1, cash flows from operating activities were $2,957 million, up from $2,522 million in the second quarter of 2026.
"Imperial's advantaged integrated business model continues to deliver strong financial results across all operating segments," said John Whelan, chairman, president and chief executive officer. "Downstream performance was enhanced by our investment in the Renewable Diesel facility at the Strathcona refinery which delivered high-value, low-carbon, locally-produced renewable diesel fuel to Canadian markets."
Upstream production in the quarter averaged 418,000 gross oil-equivalent barrels per day. At Kearl, quarterly total gross production averaged 282,000 barrels per day (200,000 barrels Imperial's share) including the impacts of a third-party pipeline outage which resulted in a full production shut down for multiple days. Given the impacts of third-party events to Kearl's year-to-date production, the company is now targeting total gross production of 280,000 barrels per day (199,000 barrels Imperial's share) in 2026. Cold Lake averaged 139,000 barrels per day inclusive of the planned turnaround. The company's share of Syncrude production in the quarter averaged 70,000 gross barrels per day inclusive of the planned coker turnaround.

Refinery throughput in the quarter averaged 381,000 barrels per day, resulting in refinery capacity utilization of 88 percent, including the impacts of a third-party pipeline outage and planned turnaround work at the Sarnia refinery. The Renewable Diesel facility at the Strathcona refinery achieved its highest canola throughput and delivered strong financial performance in the quarter. Work associated with debottlenecking rail logistics at Strathcona, to enable higher crude runs while supporting continued growth in canola throughput, is progressing and is anticipated to enhance refinery capacity utilization by year end. Petroleum product sales averaged 474,000 barrels per day.
During the quarter, Imperial returned $2,506 million to shareholders through dividend payments and share repurchases under the accelerated normal course issuer bid (NCIB) program, which is anticipated to be completed prior to year end. The company also declared a fourth quarter dividend of 87 cents per share.
"In the quarter, we increased our cash balance to $2.9 billion while funding our capital expenditures, dividend payments, and accelerated share repurchases," said John Whelan. "We're on pace to complete our NCIB by year end and are well positioned to return additional surplus cash to shareholders in the new year."

Third quarter highlights
•Net income of $2,454 million or $5.11 per share on a diluted basis, up from $539 million or $1.07 per share in the third quarter of 2025. Results in the third quarter of 2025 include unfavourable identified items1 of $555 million after tax.
•Cash flows from operating activities of $3,228 million, up from cash flows from operating activities of $1,798 million in the third quarter of 2025. Cash flows from operating activities excluding working capital1 of $2,957 million, up from $1,600 million in the third quarter of 2025.
•Capital and exploration expenditures totaled $668 million, up from $505 million in the third quarter of 2025.
•The company returned $2,506 million to shareholders in the third quarter of 2026, including $420 million in dividends paid and $2,086 million in share repurchases under the NCIB. Imperial anticipates repurchasing all remaining allowable shares prior to year end.
•Upstream production averaged 418,000 gross oil-equivalent barrels per day, compared to 462,000 gross oil-equivalent barrels per day in the third quarter of 2025, primarily due to lower production at Kearl and planned turnaround impacts at Cold Lake and Syncrude.
•Total gross bitumen production at Kearl averaged 282,000 barrels per day (200,000 barrels Imperial's share), compared to 316,000 barrels per day (224,000 barrels Imperial's share) in the third quarter of 2025, primarily driven by the absence of exceptional high-quality ore grade versus the third quarter of 2025 and the impacts of a third-party pipeline outage.
•Gross bitumen production at Cold Lake averaged 139,000 barrels per day, compared to 150,000 barrels per day in the third quarter of 2025, primarily driven by planned turnaround impacts.
•The company's share of gross production from Syncrude averaged 70,000 barrels per day, compared to 78,000 barrels per day in the third quarter of 2025.
•Refinery throughput averaged 381,000 barrels per day, compared to 425,000 barrels per day in the third quarter of 2025. Capacity utilization was 88 percent, compared to 98 percent in the third quarter of 2025. Lower refinery throughput and capacity utilization were primarily due to unplanned downtime, a third-party pipeline outage, and planned turnaround impacts.
•Petroleum product sales were 474,000 barrels per day, up from 464,000 barrels per day in the third quarter of 2025, due to higher export sales.
•Chemical net income of $52 million in the quarter, up from $21 million in the third quarter of 2025.

Recent business environment
During the third quarter of 2026, the price of crude oil decreased, the Canadian WTI/WCS spread narrowed slightly, and industry refining margins improved relative to the second quarter of 2026. Global supply disruptions continued to drive volatility in crude oil prices, heavy crude differentials, and industry refining margins.
Starting in 2025, the United States implemented and adjusted a variety of trade-related measures, including tariffs on certain imports from Canada and several other countries. In response, Canada announced its own retaliatory tariffs. Based on Imperial's assessment of these actions and their effects to date, the company does not expect them to have a material impact on its consolidated financial position, results of operations, or cash flows.

Net income (loss) excluding identified items
Net income (loss) excluding identified items is a non-GAAP financial measure that is total net income (loss) excluding individually significant non-operational events with an absolute corporate total earnings impact of at least $100 million in a given quarter. Net income (loss) excluding identified items per common share is a non-GAAP ratio which is calculated by dividing Net income (loss) excluding identified items by the weighted-average number of common shares outstanding, assuming dilution. The net income (loss) impact of an identified item for an individual segment in a given quarter may be less than $100 million when the item impacts several periods or several segments. Net income (loss) excluding identified items does include non-operational earnings events or impacts that are generally below the $100 million threshold utilized for identified items. The most directly comparable financial measure that is disclosed in the financial statements is "Net income (loss)" within the company’s Consolidated statement of income. Management uses these figures to improve comparability of the underlying business across multiple periods by isolating and removing significant non-operational events from business results. The company believes this view provides investors increased transparency into business results and trends, and provides investors with a view of the business as seen through the eyes of management. Net income (loss) excluding identified items is not meant to be viewed in isolation or as a substitute for net income (loss) as prepared in accordance with U.S. GAAP. All identified items are presented on an after-tax basis.
