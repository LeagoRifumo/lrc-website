Cenovus announces third-quarter 2026 results
Calgary, Alberta (November 4, 2026) – Cenovus Energy Inc. (TSX: CVE) (NYSE: CVE) today announced its third-quarter 2026 financial and operating results. In the quarter, the company generated approximately $5.0 billion of adjusted funds flow and $3.6 billion of free funds flow. Operating results in the quarter included Upstream production of 1,024 thousand barrels of oil equivalent per day (MBOE/d)1 and Downstream crude throughput of 437 thousand barrels per day (Mbbls/d), representing an overall crude unit utilization rate of 92%.
Highlights
•Achieved highest-ever quarterly Upstream production of 1,024 MBOE/d, an increase of over 50 MBOE/d from Q2 2026.
•Record quarterly Oil Sands production of 833 MBOE/d, including quarterly production records at Foster Creek, Christina Lake and Sunrise.
•Delivered Oil Sands operating costs of $10.23/bbl, a decrease of over $0.50/bbl from Q2 2026.
•Exceptional Downstream financial and operational performance while executing the Lima Refinery turnaround, including operating margin of $1.8 billion2, crude unit utilization of 92% and U.S. Refining adjusted market capture of 88%3.
•Returned $1.9 billion to shareholders in the third quarter, including $1.5 billion through common share repurchases and $403 million through common share dividends.
•Entered into a definitive arrangement agreement to acquire Athabasca Oil Corporation (“Athabasca”) in October, which will add approximately 45 MBOE/d4 of current production and 1.3 billion BOE of proved plus probable reserves.5 The acquisition is expected to close in December 2026.
“We exceeded one million BOE per day of production in the quarter for the first time ever. This milestone was achieved in only our seventeenth year operating as an independent company. Our asset performance continues to unlock significant value across our portfolio,” said Jon McKenzie, Cenovus President & Chief Executive Officer. “As we close the year, our focus remains on consistent execution, safe operations and leveraging the competitive advantages created by our people, asset base and integrated business model.”

Third-quarter results
Operating1
Cenovus’s total revenues were $16.0 billion in the third quarter, down from $17.4 billion in the second quarter of 2026. Upstream revenues were $10.6 billion, compared with $12.6 billion in the previous quarter, while Downstream revenues were $8.6 billion, up from $8.2 billion in the second quarter.
Operating margin6 was $6.3 billion compared with $5.9 billion in the prior quarter. Upstream operating margin2 was $4.5 billion, a slight decrease from $4.9 billion in the second quarter, as a result of lower benchmark oil prices. Downstream operating margin was $1.8 billion, an increase from $1.0 billion in the prior quarter, reflecting strong operations and improved market crack spreads. Operating margin in the U.S. Refining segment was $1.4 billion, which included the receipt of small refinery exemption (SRE) waivers related to the Superior Refinery and a $30 million inventory holding loss.
Total Upstream production was 1,024 MBOE/d, up from 970 MBOE/d in the second quarter. Christina Lake achieved record quarterly production of 381 Mbbls/d, up from 372 Mbbls/d in the prior quarter. Strong well pad performance at Narrows Lake and continued progress on the sustaining pad and redevelopment well program at Christina Lake North more than offset the impact of the Christina Lake turnaround that was completed ahead of schedule in the quarter. Foster Creek production reached a quarterly record of 248 Mbbls/d, up from 215 Mbbls/d in the prior quarter reflecting resumed operations after an unplanned disruption in late May and continued ramp up of the Foster Creek optimization project. Sunrise production was a quarterly record of 70 Mbbls/d, up from 66 Mbbls/d in the prior quarter.
Production from the Lloydminster upstream assets was 131 Mbbls/d, compared with 132 Mbbls/d in the second quarter.
Production in the Conventional segment was 120 MBOE/d, up from 118 MBOE/d in the previous quarter.
In the Offshore segment, production was 70 MBOE/d, up from 66 MBOE/d in the second quarter. In Asia Pacific, production was 57 MBOE/d, up from 51 MBOE/d in the prior quarter following maintenance in China and Indonesia completed during the second quarter. In the Atlantic region, production was 14 Mbbls/d, compared with 15 Mbbls/d in the prior quarter.
Total Downstream crude throughput in the third quarter was 437 Mbbls/d. Canadian Refining crude throughput increased to 121 Mbbls/d from 102 Mbbls/d in the prior quarter, reflecting strong operational performance and a record quarterly utilization rate of 112%.

In U.S. Refining, crude throughput was 317 Mbbls/d compared with 350 Mbbls/d in the second quarter as a result of a planned turnaround at the Lima Refinery, which was completed in October. Financial
Cash from operating activities in the third quarter was $5.6 billion, consistent with the second quarter. Adjusted funds flow was $5.0 billion, consistent with the prior quarter, and free funds flow was $3.6 billion, down slightly from $3.8 billion in the second quarter as a result of increased capital investment in the third quarter. Net earnings rose to $3.4 billion from $2.9 billion in the prior quarter. Total royalties and income taxes recognized in the quarter were $3.0 billion.
Long-term debt, including the current portion, was $8.5 billion as at September 30, 2026. Net debt was $3.0 billion as at September 30, 2026, a decrease of $2.4 billion from the prior quarter, as a result of strong financial results and a $624 million decrease in non-cash working capital.
Growth projects
At Christina Lake North, a fifth steam generator was successfully brought online ahead of schedule. The redevelopment well program continues to progress as planned, and the facility expansion project remains on track for completion in 2028. At Sunrise, the optimization project successfully reached the production growth target of 70 Mbbls/d one year ahead of schedule.
At West White Rose, drilling of the first well is complete and completion and tie-ins are underway.
Athabasca transaction

Subject to the approval of Athabasca shareholders and the Court of King’s Bench, and the satisfaction or waiver of other customary closing conditions, Cenovus expects the transaction to close in December.
Sale of Canadian commercial fuels business

Oil and Gas Information
Natural gas volumes have been converted to BOE on the basis of six thousand cubic feet (Mcf) to one barrel (bbl). BOE may be misleading, particularly if used in isolation. A conversion ratio of one bbl to six Mcf is based on an energy equivalency conversion method primarily applicable at the burner tip and does not represent value equivalency at the wellhead. Given that the value ratio based on the current price of crude oil compared with natural gas is significantly different from the energy equivalency conversion ratio of 6:1, utilizing a conversion on a 6:1 basis is not an accurate reflection of value.

Adjusted Market Capture
Adjusted market capture contains a non-GAAP financial measure and is used in the company’s U.S. Refining segment to provide an indication of margin captured relative to what was available in the market based on widely used benchmarks. Cenovus defines adjusted market capture as refining margin, net of holding gains and losses, divided by the weighted average 3-2-1 market benchmark crack, net of RINs, expressed as a percentage. The weighted average crack spread, net of RINs, is calculated on Cenovus’s operable capacity-weighted average of the Chicago and Group 3 3-2-1 benchmark market crack spreads, net of RINs.

OVERVIEW OF CENOVUS
We are a Canadian-based integrated energy company headquartered in Calgary, Alberta. We are one of the largest Canadian-based crude oil and natural gas producers, with upstream operations in Canada and the Asia Pacific region, and one of the largest Canadian-based refiners and upgraders, with downstream operations in Canada and the United States (“U.S.”).
Our upstream operations include oil sands projects in northern Alberta; thermal and conventional crude oil, natural gas and natural gas liquids (“NGLs”) projects across Western Canada; crude oil production offshore Newfoundland and Labrador; and natural gas and NGLs production offshore China and Indonesia. Our downstream operations include upgrading and refining operations in Canada and the U.S.
Our operations involve activities across the full value chain to develop, produce, refine, transport and market crude oil, natural gas and refined petroleum products in North America and internationally. Our physically and economically integrated upstream and downstream operations help us mitigate the impact of volatility in light-heavy crude oil price differentials and contribute to our net earnings by capturing value from crude oil, natural gas and NGLs production through to the sale of finished products such as transportation fuels.
QUARTERLY RESULTS OVERVIEW
In the third quarter of 2026, we safely delivered strong and reliable operating performance across our business. Total upstream production exceeded 1.0 million BOE per day, marking a significant milestone for the Company. Our financial results reflect the impact of reliable operations across our business and a strong commodity price environment through the third quarter.
•Ongoing commitment to safety. Strengthening our safety record and maintaining reliable operations throughout our portfolio continues to be our focus. We safely completed a turnaround at Christina Lake and commenced the Lima Refinery turnaround, which is now complete. Safety continues to be our top value.
•Achieved upstream production milestone. Total upstream production was 1,023.5 thousand BOE per day, increasing from 970.4 thousand BOE per day in the second quarter of 2026. Production reflected solid base performance across our Oil Sands assets, incremental volumes from the Foster Creek optimization project, strong results from the redevelopment programs at Christina Lake and the continued ramp-up of production at Narrows Lake. The impact of the Christina Lake turnaround was less than expected due to completion ahead of schedule and an accelerated post-turnaround ramp-up enabled by facility optimization initiatives.
•Continued Oil Sands growth. We continued to progress the Christina Lake facility expansion project and advanced optimization activities. In October, we brought a new steam generator online at Christina Lake North. At Sunrise, we achieved production of approximately 70.0 thousand BOE per day as we continued to ramp up production and bring new well pads online in the east development area.
•Delivered Oil Sands cost improvements. Per-unit operating expenses at our Oil Sands assets were $10.23 per barrel, a five percent decrease from $10.80 per barrel in the second quarter of 2026.

Crude Oil and Condensate Benchmarks
In the nine months ended September 30, 2026, global crude oil benchmark prices, Brent and WTI, increased compared with the same period in 2025. Global crude oil prices entered 2026 at lower levels than the previous year, as global supply exceeded demand, leading to a continued building of inventory globally. However, the onset of the U.S.-Iran conflict drove a sharp increase in crude oil prices. The effective closure of the Strait of Hormuz, a critical maritime route for global crude and refined product trade, stranded shipments and created a significant supply shortfall, with the resulting inventory draws sustaining elevated prices. The Dated Brent-WTI spread widened as international buyers competed for available physical barrels and higher transportation costs increased the premium for seaborne supply. Brent prices are tied to our East Coast Canada production as well as select Asia Pacific gas sales agreements.
The WTI-WCS differential at both Hardisty and Nederland widened in the nine months ended September 30, 2026, compared with 2025. Heavy crude weakened relative to WTI, driven by several factors. Rising Canadian and Venezuelan heavy crude supply increased competition among producers for access to U.S. Gulf Coast refinery capacity, while higher freight rates, refinery outages and seasonal maintenance added further pressure toward the end of the third quarter of 2026.
In Canada, we upgrade heavy crude oil and bitumen into a sweet synthetic crude oil, the Husky Synthetic Blend (“HSB”), at the Lloydminster Upgrader (“Upgrader”). The price realized for HSB is primarily driven by the price of WTI, and by the supply and demand of sweet synthetic crude oil from Western Canada, which influences the WTI-Synthetic differential.
In the nine months ended September 30, 2026, synthetic crude oil at Edmonton strengthened relative to WTI compared with the same period in 2025. The strength in pricing was driven in part by strong diesel pricing, as synthetic crude yields a higher proportion of diesel than other crude grades. Upgrader turnarounds also supported synthetic pricing.
In the nine months ended September 30, 2026, the average Edmonton condensate benchmark traded at less of a discount to WTI, compared with the same period in 2025, due to tight Canadian supply, strong synthetic pricing and global naphtha shortages, which supported premiums earlier in the year. Softer seasonal blending requirements weakened condensate pricing in the third quarter, bringing the nine-month average to a slight discount to WTI.

Refining Benchmarks
RUL and ULSD benchmark prices are representative of inland refined product prices and are used to derive the Chicago 3-2-1 market crack spread. The 3-2-1 market crack spread is an indicator of the Adjusted Refining Margin generated by converting three barrels of crude oil into two barrels of regular unleaded gasoline and one barrel of ultra-low sulphur diesel, using current-month WTI-based crude oil feedstock prices.
Cenovus Energy Inc. – Q3 2026 Management's Discussion and Analysis | 10

In the nine months ended September 30, 2026, refined product crack spreads in Chicago and Group 3 increased compared with the same period in 2025, primarily due to a sharp spike in gasoline and diesel pricing following the U.S.-Iran conflict, which has limited global supply of both refined products and crude oil. Drone attacks on Russian refinery infrastructure have further reduced global refined products supply, exacerbating the shortfall and supporting prices. Pricing was further supported by low refined product inventories and unplanned refinery outages in the U.S. Midwest. The average cost of RINs was higher in the nine months ended September 30, 2026, compared with 2025, due to increasing volumetric requirements raising demand, and weak U.S. production and imports of renewable diesel and biodiesel causing a shortfall in RINs generation.
North American refining crack spreads are expressed on a WTI basis, while refined products are generally set by global prices. The strength of refining market crack spreads in the U.S. Midwest and Midcontinent generally reflects the differential between Brent and WTI benchmark prices. In the third quarter of 2026, light crude grades sourced by U.S. refiners, including Bakken and Midland, continued to trade at a premium to the WTI monthly average, although these premiums were substantially lower than in the second quarter of 2026. Backwardation in the forward curve continued to influence physical pricing and feedstock acquisition costs. These premiums reduced refining margins relative to benchmark market crack spreads, but to a lesser extent than in the second quarter of 2026.
The benchmark market crack spreads do not precisely mirror the configuration and product output of our refineries, or the location we sell product; however, they are used as a general market indicator. Natural Gas Benchmarks
In the nine months ended September 30, 2026, AECO and NYMEX prices increased compared with the same period in 2025, supported by strong winter heating demand in the first quarter. Prices moderated in the second and third quarters as heating demand subsided, while strong production and comfortable storage levels limited upward pressure from summer cooling demand and liquefied natural gas (“LNG”) exports. Ample Western Canadian supply and constrained takeaway capacity continued to weigh on AECO pricing. The price received for our Asia Pacific natural gas production is largely based on long-term contracts.
Foreign Exchange and Interest Rate Benchmarks
Our revenues are subject to foreign exchange exposure as the sales prices of our crude oil, NGLs, natural gas and refined products are determined by reference to U.S. dollar benchmark prices. In the nine months ended September 30, 2026, on average, the Canadian dollar strengthened relative to the U.S. dollar compared with 2025, negatively impacting our reported revenues and positively impacting our U.S. Refining operating expenses.
