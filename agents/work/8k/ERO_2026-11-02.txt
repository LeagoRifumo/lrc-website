BUSINESS OVERVIEW
Ero is a Brazil-focused, growth-oriented mining company with a diversified portfolio of copper and gold assets, headquartered in Vancouver, B.C. The Company operates two copper mines – the Caraíba Operations in Bahia State and the Tucumã Operation in Pará State – as well as the Xavantina Operations, a producing gold mine in Mato Grosso State. In addition to its operating assets, Ero is advancing the Furnas Copper-Gold Project, located in the mineral-rich Carajás Province in Pará State, through a definitive earn-in agreement with Vale Base Metals to acquire a 60% interest in the project.
Ero’s operating philosophy is grounded in a commitment to safety, operational excellence, and the responsible production of minerals essential for a better tomorrow. The Company’s shares are publicly traded on the Toronto Stock Exchange and the New York Stock Exchange under the symbol “ERO.” Additional information, including technical reports on the Company’s operations and projects, is available on the Company’s website (www.ero.com), SEDAR+ (www.sedarplus.ca), and on EDGAR (www.sec.gov).

Q3 2026 Highlights
Record financial results and accelerated deleveraging driven by solid operating performance and value unlocked through increased sales of gold recovered from historic concentrate stockpiles
•Consolidated copper production increased 6% quarter-on-quarter to 18,345 tonnes in concentrate, at C1 cash costs(1) of $2.51 per pound produced.

◦Gold recovered from historic gold concentrate stockpiles increased by 47% quarter-on-quarter to a record 17,414 ounces, at C1 cash costs(1) and AISC(1) of $621 and $710 per ounce, respectively. The increase reflected drier seasonal conditions and the benefits of the mobile filter press and industrial dryer installed in Q2 2026.

The Company continued to advance the Furnas Copper-Gold Project, completing the Phase 3 drill program in Q3 2026, more than two years ahead of schedule
•During Q3 2026, the Company completed the 45,000-meter Phase 3 drill program, satisfying the exploration component of the Furnas Project earn-in agreement with Vale Base Metals ("VBM"). To date, the Company has completed more than 100,000 meters of exploration drilling at Furnas with drilling requirements completed more than two years ahead of schedule. Assay results from Phase 2 and the first 7,000 meters of the Phase 3 program, published in Q2(1), continued to demonstrate high-grade continuity within the SE and NW zones and extension of mineralization both to depth and along strike adjacent to planned underground infrastructure as described in the Preliminary Economic Assessment ("PEA")(2).
•The Company continues to advance technical work streams including permitting, hydrogeological studies, ongoing environmental studies and confirmatory metallurgical testwork in support of a Pre-Feasibility Study ("PFS") expected to be published in 2027.
•The Furnas PEA(2), which was filed on SEDAR+ on March 30, 2026, outlines a large-scale, long-life operation with compelling economics, including an after-tax net present value (8%) of $2.0 billion and a 27.0% after-tax internal rate of return based on long-term copper, gold and silver prices of $4.60 per pound, $3,300 per ounce, and $40.00 per ounce, respectively.
The PEA(2) is preliminary in nature and includes inferred mineral resources, which are considered too speculative geologically to have the economic considerations applied to them that would enable them to be categorized as mineral reserves, and there is no certainty that the PEA(2) will be realized. Mineral resources that are not mineral reserves do not have demonstrated economic viability.

The Caraíba Operations produced 9,387 tonnes of copper in concentrate during the period, representing a quarter-on-quarter increase of 12%, driven by higher processed volumes and grades. Caraíba's C1 cash cost(1) was $2.89 per pound of copper produced for the quarter. Higher C1 cash costs(1) primarily reflected accelerated waste stripping at Surubim, which delayed access to the high-grade pit bottom, as well as higher diesel and maintenance costs. The continued strength of the BRL also increased the US-dollar equivalent of unit operating costs.

Full-year 2026 copper C1 cash cost(1) guidance has been updated to $2.55 to $2.75 (from $2.30 to $2.50) per pound produced to reflect the previously communicated impacts of a stronger Brazilian real on the Company's reported C1 cash costs(1) as well as inflationary pressures associated with the ongoing U.S.-Iran conflict.
The cash impact of the stronger BRL has been offset by year-to-date realized gains of $30.3 million from the Company's foreign exchange hedge program. Allocating the year-to-date realized foreign exchange hedge gains across the Company's operations on a pro rata basis would translate to an estimated reduction of approximately $0.20 per pound on the Caraíba's full-year C1 cash costs(1) equating to $2.35 to $2.55 per pound produced, generally in line with the Company's original 2026 guidance.

In 2022, one of the Company's customers in Brazil, Paranapanema S/A ("PMA"), filed for bankruptcy protection. As a preferred supplier to PMA, the Company had a note receivable arrangement with PMA, which was excluded from the judicial recovery process and provides the Company with certain judicial guarantees. According to the note receivable arrangement, repayment was structured over 24 monthly installments beginning in March 2024, with an annual interest rate equivalent to Brazil's CDI rate of approximately 11.65%.
At September 30, 2026, PMA continued to be in default of the agreement and the gross amount of accounts and note receivable from PMA was $27.0 million (December 31, 2025 - $24.4 million). Accordingly, the note receivable is considered credit impaired, and the Company recorded a credit loss provision and present value discount of $17.3 million (December 31, 2025 - $14.9 million). The carrying value of the PMA note receivable at September 30, 2026 was $9.7 million (December 31, 2025 - $9.5 million), entirely included in deposits and other non-current assets. During the three and nine months ended September 30, 2026, the Company has recognized nil and $1.5 million of credit loss provision (nil and $0.2 million for the three and nine months ended September 30, 2025).
Liquidity risk
Liquidity risk is the risk associated with the difficulties that the Company may have meeting the obligations associated with financial liabilities that are settled with cash payments or with another financial asset. The Company's approach to liquidity management is to ensure as much as possible that sufficient liquidity exists to meet its maturity obligations on the expiration dates, under normal and stressful conditions, without causing unacceptable losses or with risk of undermining the normal operation of the Company.
