Third Quarter Highlights
•Net Sales of $22.7 billion, up 1% from prior year
•IFRS Adjusted EBITDA of $1,181 million, down 36% from prior year
•USGAAP¹ Adjusted EBITDA of $1,001 million, down 38% from prior year
•IFRS Adjusted operating income of $521 million, down 58% from prior year
•USGAAP¹ Adjusted operating income of $597 million, down 53% from prior year
•EPS of $0.15, vs. $0.52 from prior year
•Adjusted EPS of $0.16, vs. $0.54 from prior year
“In 3Q26, JBS faced a more challenging operating environment across several of its business units. The weaker operating performance resulted in an increase in leverage to 3.54x. JBS Beef North America returned to positive adjusted EBITDA under US GAAP, supported by better market conditions and continued improvements in operational integration, commercial execution and industrial efficiency.
JBS Brazil’s third-quarter performance was impacted by lower exports to China following the completion of the 2026 quota. At the beginning of the fourth quarter Chinese demand strengthened as the market began positioning for the 2027 quota. The Brazilian domestic market also enters a seasonally stronger consumption
1
period. Together, JBS Beef North America and JBS Brazil are showing more constructive market dynamics at the beginning of the fourth quarter." said Gilberto Tomazoni — Global CEO.
JBS Beef North America continued to see an improving operating environment throughout the quarter. Industry processing capacity adjustments, together with the reopening of key U.S.-Mexico cattle crossings, with heavier cattle reaching processors more quickly, and robust beef demand, supported the recovery in profitability.
Internally, the integration of fed beef, regional beef and case-ready operations under a single management structure improved coordination across procurement, production and sales. The Company continued its focus on improving yields, increasing productivity, upgrading maintenance equipment and enhancing operational reliability, while expanding the sharing of best practices across its global operations.
Combined, these factors supported a sequential improvement in margins throughout the quarter, from negative 2.9% in July to breakeven in August and positive 3.9% in September.
Pilgrim's Pride: Within the U.S., demand for chicken continued to grow across retail and foodservice channels. U.S. production peaked with a 4.7% growth in July and subsequently reduced its pace throughout the quarter.
Prepared Foods inside PPC continues to drive profitable growth as both sales and margins increased versus last year. Just Bare® remains the fastest growing brand in the frozen fully cooked category as market share in volume rose over 300 basis points year over year. In Europe, poultry and meal offerings continued to resonate with consumers seeking affordable protein options, while profitability remained pressured by challenging pork market conditions and higher input costs. In Mexico, profitability improved sequentially as live commodity fundamentals strengthened, while branded fresh and prepared offerings continued to grow.
JBS USA Pork reported lower net sales compared to the prior year, reflecting an unusually challenging market environment for the business. Domestic demand for pork softened as inflation continued to pressure the American consumer, while increased global pork supply weighed on export demand across key markets. Lower volumes and average selling prices impacted profitability during the quarter. The Company continues to expand its value-added and branded product portfolio as part of its strategy to mitigate commodity market volatility and further strengthen the resilience and consistency of what has historically been one of its most stable business units.
JBS Australia profitability was pressured during the quarter by a more challenging environment in the beef business. Export performance was impacted by the completion of quotas in key markets, including China and South Korea, as well as lower shipments to the U.S. Higher cattle and lamb costs also weighed on results, reflecting continued rainfall across key livestock-producing regions, with lamb prices reaching record levels during the quarter. While these conditions pressured profitability in the short term, improved pasture conditions are supportive of livestock availability over the longer term. Huon delivered a strong performance, supported by increased harvest and higher fish weights.
JBS Brazil reported lower net sales compared to the prior year, mainly reflecting lower export volumes, particularly to China, following the completion of the 2026 Chinese quota.
In the domestic market, higher sales reflected strong commercial execution, supported by the continued expansion of Friboi+, the Company’s retailer relationship program, and a broader value-added product portfolio.
Additionally, profitability was also impacted by higher cattle prices during the quarter.
At the beginning of the fourth quarter, Chinese demand strengthened as customers began securing volumes for the 2027 quota, supporting a recovery in export volumes and a more favorable commercial environment. This recovery coincides with the seasonally stronger period for beef consumption in Brazil, driven by year-end demand.
Seara continued to deliver strong profitability during the quarter. In the export market, fresh poultry performance was supported by higher prices and volumes, with September reaching record export volumes for the Company. Pork exports, however, remained pressured by ample global pork supply, also affecting market dynamics in Brazil.
2
In Brazil, Seara continued to strengthen its operations by expanding its value-added product portfolio, building its brand and maintaining solid commercial and operational execution. The business also gained market share and achieved its highest-ever levels of brand consideration and preference.
Free Cash Flow & Leverage
Free cash flow was negative US$167 million in 3Q26, with a US$551 million decrease year-over-year, explained by (i) a US$653 million decline in Adjusted EBITDA and (ii) higher working capital consumption in Brazil, particularly inventories. The increase mainly reflected higher grain inventories at Seara, as well as higher chicken inventories at Seara in Europe. The Company also faced higher inventories in JBS Brazil beef products to be sent to China in 4Q26 to fulfill the 2027 quota.
The average debt term reached 14.4 years, with an average cost of 5.7%, of which more than 95% of this debt is at fixed rates. Net leverage ended the quarter at 3.54x. However, the Company maintains a well-balanced debt maturity profile, with no material maturities over the next five years. In 3Q26, JBS increased its revolving credit facility from US$3.5 billion to US$4.2 billion, while reducing the all-in cost. Considering this increase, JBS grew its total liquidity to US$8.2 billion.
