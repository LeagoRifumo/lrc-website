Message From the Management
“The third quarter of 2026 was another quarter of strong growth for PicPay. These results reflect the consistent execution of our strategy to expand the credit portfolio, combined with operating leverage gains driven by a continued focus on efficiency.
Our scale is one of our main competitive advantages. We ended the quarter with 72 million accounts and more than 46 million quarterly active clients, an expansion of 10% year over year. This large and engaged base gives us a significant opportunity to accelerate monetization by increasing the penetration of our credit offering. As more clients adopt credit, we expect higher levels of cross-selling and principality, which in turn deepen engagement and monetization.
In August, we completed the acquisition of Kev and began consolidating its results from that month onward. To preserve comparability, the highlights and guidance discussed in this release exclude Kev. On that basis, we met or exceeded all nine guidance metrics, with total revenue and financial income of R$4.7 billion, Adjusted EBT of R$388 million, more than double the level of a year ago, and Adjusted Net Income of R$271 million.
Credit remains the main engine of this expansion, and we continue to grow it with discipline. Our total credit portfolio reached R$35.0 billion, up 88% year over year and R$3 billion in the quarter, led by private payroll loans and mature credit cards. In cards, we remain focused on clients with at least 12 months of tenure, whose behavior on our platform gives us a clear information advantage and allows us to increase limits gradually. Our principle is unchanged: prioritize risk-adjusted returns, with pricing and exposure calibrated to the risk of each product.
Our SMB business is continuing to gain traction. Monthly average new accounts reached 126 thousand in the quarter, 48% above the first-half average, and supply chain finance origination reached R$1.4 billion, up 37% from the previous quarter. We also launched a free online store for businesses, strengthening a front we see as an important driver of future growth.
Kev, our insurance business, is a new source of growth for PicPay. It is a large-scale, rapidly expanding platform that brings underwriting, product capability, and a solid pipeline of other distribution partners, allowing us to broaden our offering to our customer base and diversify our business. In August and September, its first two months as part of PicPay, Kev delivered R$56.3 million in gross profit, R$6.4 million in EBT, and R$4.5 million in net income. These are the first Kev results we report under IFRS, and they still reflect accounting adjustments from converting its financial statements to this methodology. In addition, consolidating Kev carries a timing effect from the elimination of intercompany commissions, which reduces our consolidated result with no cash impact. We expect this effect to normalize over the coming quarters.
On asset quality, portfolio maturation remains the main driver of the quarter-over-quarter increase in NPL 15-90 and NPL 90+. This is a natural effect of a credit book growing at our pace, as recent vintages reach the stage of the lifecycle at which delinquency typically emerges. In this context, we will change our write-off policy exclusively for our unsecured personal loans portfolio in the fourth quarter, reducing the write-off period from 360 to 120 days past due. With a more mature portfolio and robust historical data, we now have greater visibility into repayment and recovery dynamics. All other products remain unchanged. This one-time, non-recurring adjustment will impact our asset quality indicators, particularly NPL 90+, without changing our underwriting standards, risk appetite, or growth outlook.
Finally, growth with discipline is what we set out to build. This quarter is what it looks like in practice: a larger ecosystem, a more efficient company, and a credit business growing in a way we can forecast and explain. Looking ahead, our priorities are clear: sustain profitable growth, deepen the relationship with our customers, and expand the range of financial solutions available through PicPay. With a solid capital position, a growing funding base and a technology-driven distribution platform, we are confident in our ability to create long-term value. We will continue to move with urgency, but never at the expense of quality, discipline or the trust of our customers. I thank our customers, employees and shareholders for their support.
This quarter shows that PicPay’s model scales, is diversified, and predictable. Revenue per client grew 58% while costs to serve rose 20%, and the efficiency ratio fell 11 points in a year, with AI increasingly a lever. 73% of our revenue comes from sources with lower or no credit risk, allowing us to grow credit with discipline. We beat all nine guidance metrics and are confident enough to give a full-year outlook under two accounting policies at once. This is growth with discipline: a larger ecosystem, a more efficient company, and a credit business growing in a way we can forecast and explain.
Eduardo Chedid

Performance in the Period
3Q26 Guidance1 (ex-Kev)
PicPay’s third-quarter 2026 results, excluding any contribution from Kev, met or exceeded all nine guidance metrics we gave in August, and we did it while becoming more efficient, not less.

Gross Profit reached R$1,303 million, 2.6% above the ~R$1,270 million guidance.

IFRS Net Income totaled R$258 million (+1.2% vs. ~R$255 million guidance), and Adjusted Net Income reached R$271 million (+2.3% vs. ~R$265 million guidance).

NPL 15 to 90 days reached 9.2% in 3Q26, up 1.1 percentage points from 3Q25 and 1.6 percentage points sequentially. Two things explain this movement: (i) portfolio aging, which added 127 basis points, reflecting the natural aging of our portfolio, particularly in private payroll loans; and (ii) the Desenrola program, which added 118 basis points. The Desenrola effect comes from clients who renegotiated their debt under the program in the second quarter but did not keep up with their new installments. Because the renegotiated contracts moved out of the 90-plus bucket, these clients now show up again in early NPL. Excluding the Desenrola effect, early NPL would have been 8.0% in the quarter, and we expect it to come back to 7.9% by the end of the year. This shows the stability of this metric over the last 18 months, which has remained in a range between 7.5% and 8.4% since the third quarter of 2025.
NPL over 90 days reached 10.7% in 3Q26, up from 9.8% in 2Q26. The increase in NPL over 90 days is primarily a function of portfolio aging, which added 307 basis points. As our vintages mature, a larger share of the book naturally migrates into later stages of delinquency. This is a mechanical, expected dynamic for a portfolio that has grown as fast as ours, not a sign of deterioration.

The two waterfall charts above reinforce that the NPL dynamics we are observing are mainly driven by vintage maturation and our deliberate strategy of intentional risk-taking in private payroll loans, rather than by a deterioration in the underlying quality of our portfolio.
Stage 3 formation reached 4.6% in 3Q26, up 0.8 percentage points year over year and 1.0 percentage point from 3.6% in 2Q26. As previously disclosed, the second-quarter figure benefited from the Desenrola renegotiation program; excluding this effect, Stage 3 formation would have been approximately 4%. The third-quarter level reflects the normalization of that one-off benefit, combined with the natural aging of the portfolio and our deliberate risk-taking strategy in private payroll loans.
The increase in Stage 3 balances was also partly driven by a higher share of Desenrola participants whose exposures had already passed our 360-day write-off threshold. When these customers entered the program, their previously written-off exposures were reinstated on our balance sheet, contributing to both Stage 3 formation and outstanding Stage 3 balances. This increase reflects the reactivation of previously written-off exposures through renegotiations, rather than a deterioration in the underlying credit quality of our performing portfolio.
Quarterly Cost of Risk stands at 3.8% in 3Q26, at the same time, credit loss allowance reached R$ 1.3 billion, up 8% sequentially and more than doubled year over year, reflecting the scale-up of the credit book. Provisions are scaling with the portfolio, while cost of risk remains under control. The level of provisioning is consistent with the growth and maturation of the book, and the sequential decline in cost of risk shows that there has been no loss of discipline in underwriting or risk management.

Total Coverage ratio reached 14.9% in 3Q26, as provisions build ahead of portfolio maturation. This represents an increase of 100 basis points from 13.9% in the previous quarter, and the highest level since the beginning of our credit activities.
Stage 3 coverage closed the quarter at 71.9%. As explained in the previous quarter, this level still reflects the effect of the Desenrola program. Loans renegotiated under Desenrola benefit from a guarantee from the FGO, the Operations Guarantee Fund, covering 50% of the outstanding balance. This guarantee increases the expected recovery on these loans and therefore reduces the LGD (Loss Given Default) applied to them, which mechanically lowers the overall stage 3 coverage ratio. Excluding this effect, stage 3 coverage would be 76.4% in 3Q26. Therefore, this movement does not reflect a change in our provisioning standards or in our risk appetite.

On a standalone basis, PicPay recognizes its credit insurance commissions upfront, at the moment the loan is originated, while Kev recognizes the credit insurance revenue and commission cost on a deferred basis, over the life of the contract. On consolidation, we eliminate PicPay’s full upfront revenue, but only the portion of Kev’s cost that has already been amortized, which creates a negative effect in the period.

The amount eliminated today will be recognized in our consolidated results over the life of the contracts. As the book of contracts matures, the amortization of earlier policies will increasingly offset the elimination of new upfront commissions, and this effect will normalize over time. In addition, now that we manage our insurance operation as a single business, from distribution at PicPay to underwriting at Kev, we are evaluating adjustments to the commission structure between the two companies, which could further reduce this effect.

6. Capital Ratio

7. Upcoming Write-Off Policy Change
As part of the continuous evolution of our credit risk management framework, starting in the fourth quarter of 2026, we will change our write-off policy exclusively for our unsecured personal loans portfolio, reducing the write-off period from 360 to 120 days past due. This change is strictly limited to this portfolio, with all other credit products maintaining their existing 360-day write-off policy.
The primary rationale behind this change is to ensure that our reported asset quality metrics, particularly NPL over 90 days, better reflect the underlying performance of our credit portfolio by mitigating the impact of portfolio aging on reported indicators. This is particularly relevant for a fast-growing credit platform like PicPay, where portfolio growth and aging dynamics can significantly influence reported asset quality trends. This refinement also brings our write-off policy closer to industry practices.
18
It is important to emphasize that this is not a response to credit deterioration or changes in market conditions. Our credit portfolio remains healthy, with underlying asset quality trends performing in line with our expectations. The change does not affect our underwriting standards, risk appetite, collection strategy or recovery efforts. We will continue pursuing recoveries on written-off loans through our existing collection processes.
We expect the implementation of the new policy in the fourth quarter of 2026 to result in a one-time net income impact of approximately R$ 325 million on our quarterly earnings, with no cash impact.
Looking ahead, even after absorbing the impact of the write-off policy change in the fourth quarter, we expect to close the year with a total capital ratio between 13% and 13.5%, and a common equity tier 1 ratio between 11% and 11.5%. These levels are comfortably above the regulatory minimum of 8.5%, which already includes the conservation buffer, and give us the flexibility to continue our growth trajectory in 2027.
