Global Ship Lease Reports Results for the Third Quarter of 2026
Annualized dividend to increase to $2.75 per Class A Common Share
Further de-risked our newbuilding program with $1.0 billion credit facility
100% contract cover for 2026, 98% for 2027, and 74% for 2028
ATHENS, GREECE – November 9, 2026 - Global Ship Lease, Inc. (NYSE: GSL) (the “Company”, “Global Ship Lease” or “GSL”), an owner of containerships, announced today its unaudited results for the three and nine-month periods ended September 30, 2026.
Third Quarter of 2026 and Year to Date Highlights and Other Recent Developments
- 3Q 2026 operating revenue of $201.4 million. 9M 2026 operating revenue of $598.1 million.
- 3Q 2026 net income available to common shareholders of $99.2 million, or $2.75 Earnings per Share (“EPS”). 9M 2026 net income available to common shareholders of $279.9 million, or $7.77 EPS.
- 3Q 2026 normalized net income (a non-U.S. GAAP financial measure, described below)3 of $99.3 million, or $2.75 normalized EPS³. 9M 2026 normalized net income of $280.6 million, or $7.79 normalized EPS.
- 3Q 2026 Adjusted EBITDA (a non-U.S. GAAP financial measure, described below)3 of $138.8 million. 9M 2026 Adjusted EBITDA of $403.4 million.
- In October 2026, we agreed a $1.0 billion syndicated loan facility (the “New Facility”) bearing an interest rate of SOFR + 1.5% and a duration of seven years, to finance 15 of our newbuildings. We intend to draw on the New Facility in tranches corresponding to the respective deliveries of the 15 newbuildings. The eight-member syndicate is comprised of Bank of America as Bookrunner, Mandated Lead Arranger, Facility Agent and Security Agent, with ABN-AMRO, Bank of China (Hong Kong) and KFW IPEX as Mandated Lead Arrangers, CaixaBank and CTBC served as Lead Arrangers, with BOCOM (Hong Kong) and First Citizens Bank as Arrangers.
- In October 2026, we announced that, subject to certain conditions precedent being met, we had exercised options to build two mid-size, ultra-high-reefer, wide-beam, latest generation containerships for an aggregate contract price of $163.0 million, scheduled to be delivered in the fourth quarter of 2029. We are in discussions with prospective charterers and lenders to secure employment and financing of the vessels upon delivery from the shipyard.
- In June 2026, we announced that we agreed individual newbuilding contracts for 15 mid-size, ultra-high-reefer, wide-beam, latest-generation containerships for an aggregate purchase price of approximately $1.3 billion. These highly flexible ships have been designed and specified to ensure a superior fit for existing and future market needs, with deliveries scheduled to take place between the fourth quarter of 2028 and the first quarter of 2030. Upon delivery from the respective shipyards, the newbuildings are contracted to commence employment on multi-year charters, with an average TEU-weighted firm charter term of 7.1 years and at rates expected to generate more than $1.0 billion of aggregate Adjusted EBITDA. These 15 newbuildings are expected to be financed with a combination of cash from our balance sheet and the New Facility.

- Added $1.82 billion of contracted revenue during 9M 2026 from new charters and extensions on our existing fleet and initial firm charters for 15 of our newbuildings, bringing total contracted revenue as of September 30, 2026 to $3.4 billion, over a TEU-weighted average remaining duration (assuming median firm charter periods) of 3.5 years.
- Declared a dividend of $0.6875 per Class A common share for the third quarter of 2026, to be paid on December 3, 2026 to Class A common shareholders of record as of November 20, 2026 (the “Third Quarter Dividend”). The Board of Directors determined that sustained market demand for GSL’s fleet and our success with securing forward fixtures at attractive levels supports a 25.0% ($0.0625 per share) increase in our quarterly supplemental dividend, bringing our total annualized dividends per share to $2.75 ($0.6875 per quarter), commencing with, and reflected in, the Third Quarter Dividend. Paid a dividend of $0.625 per Class A common share for the second quarter of 2026 on September 3, 2026.
- On June 16, 2026, announced updates by two leading credit rating agencies. Moody’s Investor Service maintained our Ba2 Corporate Family Rating and upgraded to a positive outlook from a stable outlook. Kroll Bond Rating Agency maintained our corporate credit rating at BB+, with a stable outlook, while also affirming the BBB/stable investment grade rating and stable outlook for our 5.69% Senior Secured Notes due July 15, 2027 (the “2027 Secured Notes”). In addition, on July 7, 2026, S&P Global issued a press release maintaining our Issuer Credit Rating for GSL of BB+, with a stable outlook.
- During April and May of 2026, we entered into agreements for the forward sales of four non-core ships, built 2000 – 2002, for an aggregate price of $65.5 million and an anticipated gain on sale of approximately $33.0 million. Three out of the four ships are scheduled to be delivered to the buyers upon expiry of the vessels’ respective charters: Kumasi and Julie (2,200 TEU, 2002-built) in the first quarter of 2027 and the third quarter of 2027, respectively, and Ian H (5,900 TEU, 2000-built) in the fourth quarter of 2027. On November 4, 2026, Manet (2,200 TEU, 2001-built) was delivered to the buyers. The vessel’s sale price was $13.5 million and the net gain from the sale of vessel was approximately $5.7 million.
- On December 1, 2025, we announced the purchase of three 8,586 TEU Korean-built containerships with ECO upgrades (the “Three Newly Acquired Vessels”) for an aggregate purchase price of $90.0 million. Two of the vessels were delivered to us in December 2025 and the third was delivered to us in January 2026. In June 2026, we entered into a loan agreement with Bank of America for $55.5 million to finance these acquisitions. The loan bears interest at SOFR + 1.40% and has a maturity of five years.
George Youroukos, our Executive Chairman, stated: “Amid continued market and geopolitical volatility throughout the third quarter of 2026, GSL once again benefitted from our focus on maximizing optionality and providing our customers with highly flexible tonnage. With the continuing fragmentation and decentralization of container supply chains liner service networks are being constantly reshaped, meaning that flexibility and agility are increasingly valuable qualities. At the same time, a combination of port congestion, extreme weather events, and freight flow imbalances weighted towards the head-haul trades, has meant that demand for containership capacity has grown even faster than underlying container volume growth. By remaining focused on being responsive, disciplined, and well-positioned to provide our liner customers with the highly specified, mission-critical, mid-sized and smaller containerships that they need to adapt in a dynamic and unpredictable trade environment, we have built a contracted revenue backlog of $3.4 billion over an average of 3.5 years, with 100% coverage for 2026, 98% coverage for 2027 and 74% for 2028. As a result, we are very pleased to further upsize our supplemental dividend by 25%, bringing our overall annualized dividend to $2.75 per common share.
With the disciplined, selective renewal of our fleet as a longstanding priority, and aided by our fortress balance sheet, we have expanded our orderbook of mid-size, ultra-high-reefer, wide beam, latest generation containerships to 17 vessels delivering between late 2028 and early 2030. We have already secured long-term employment for 15 of these ships, together with highly attractive financing. We are confident that these state-of-the-art containerships, which are core assets for the future of the Company, are now materially de-risked and will serve as long-term industry workhorses, providing significant future value and cashflow to GSL.”
Thomas Lister, our Chief Executive Officer, stated: “Optionality, prudence, and discipline continue to be foundational to how we operate. Our capacity to secure an oversubscribed, competitively priced, $1 billion syndicated loan facility comprising eight top-tier banks from around the world comes on the back of our fortress balance sheet and strong relationships throughout the global banking sector. From this enhanced financial footing, and having added over $1.8 billion of contracted revenue during the first nine months of this year, we are pleased to once again increase our quarterly dividend, starting with that to be paid for the third quarter of 2026, to $0.6875 per common share – which amounts to $2.75 on an annualized basis. Through a combination of chartering, fleet renewal, balance sheet optimization, and our upsized quarterly dividend, GSL is positioned to continue to deliver significant value to our shareholders while managing the risks and capitalizing on the opportunities of this cyclical industry and unpredictable macro environment.”

Operating Revenues and Utilization
Operating revenues derived from fixed-rate, mainly long-term, time-charters were $201.4 million in the third quarter of 2026, up $8.7 million (or 4.5%) on operating revenues of $192.7 million in the prior year period. The period-on-period increase in operating revenues was principally due to (i) the net effect of higher rates on charter renewals, (ii) the addition of the Three Newly Acquired Vessels offset by the sale of Dimitris Y in the fourth quarter of 2025 and (iii) a non-cash $0.1 million positive effect from straight lining time charter modifications and amortization of intangible liabilities arising on below-market charters. There were 221 days of off-hire in the third quarter of 2026, of which 206 were for scheduled drydockings for seven vessels, compared to 263 days of off-hire in the prior year period, of which 137 were for scheduled drydockings for five vessels. Utilization for the third quarter of 2026 was 96.6% compared to utilization of 95.9% in the prior year period.
For the nine months ended September 30, 2026, operating revenues were $598.1 million, up $22.6 million (or 3.9%) on operating revenues of $575.5 million in the comparative period, mainly due to (i) the net effect of higher rates on charter renewals, (ii) the addition of the Three Newly Acquired Vessels offset by the sales of four vessels in 2025 (Tasman, Keta, Akiteta and Dimitris Y) and (iii) a non-cash $4.6 million positive effect from straight lining time charter modifications and amortization of intangible liabilities arising on below-market charters. There were 549 days of off-hire in the nine-month period ended September 30, 2026, of which 471 were for scheduled drydockings for 11 vessels, compared to 851 days of off-hire and idle time in the prior year period, of which 612 were for scheduled drydockings for 12 vessels. Utilization for the nine-month period ended September 30, 2026, was 97.2% compared to utilization of 95.5% in the prior year period.

Vessel Operating Expenses
Vessel operating expenses, which are primarily the costs of crew, lubricating oil, repairs, maintenance, insurance and technical management fees, were down 1.5% to $51.3 million for the third quarter of 2026 or an average of $7,851 per day, compared to $52.1 million in the prior year period, or an average of $8,199 per day, a decrease of $348 per day, or 4.2%. The decrease of $0.8 million was mainly due to (i) a decrease in stores, spares and maintenance expenses for planned main engine maintenance and overhaul of diesel generators as well as main engine annual spares delivery due to timing of planned schedule offset by (i) an increase in crew expenses following the continued strength of the market that led to crew shortage, resulting in an increase in crew wages by approximately 6.3% and (ii) the impact of inflation on fees and expenses, including management fees.
For the nine-month period ended September 30, 2026, vessel operating expenses were $161.0 million, or an average of $8,309 per day, compared to $152.6 million in the comparative period, or an average of $8,017 per day, an increase of $292 per day, or 3.6%. The increase of $8.4 million was mainly due to (i) the addition of the Three Newly Acquired Vessels, offset by the sale of four vessels in 2025, (ii) an increase in crew expenses following our decision to increase the number of seafarers on board to improve the vessels’ conditions, (iii) an increase in annual premiums for all P&I Clubs and (iv) the impact of inflation on fees and expenses, including management fees, offset by a decrease in stores, spares and maintenance expenses for planned main engine maintenance and overhaul of diesel generators as well as main engine annual spares delivery due to timing of planned schedule.

Time Charter and Voyage Expenses
Time charter and voyage expenses comprise mainly commissions paid to ship brokers, the cost of bunker fuel for owner’s account when a ship is off-hire or idle, and miscellaneous owner’s costs associated with a ship’s voyage. Time charter and voyage expenses were $6.9 million for the third quarter of 2026, compared to $7.0 million in the prior year period due to a decrease in voyage administration costs and operational requests from charterers offset by an increase in brokerage commissions on charter renewals at higher rates.
For the nine-month period ended September 30, 2026, time charter and voyage expenses were $19.0 million, or an average of $979 per day, compared to $18.6 million in the comparative period, or $975 per day, an increase of $4 per ownership day, or 0.4% mainly due to (i) increased commissions on charter renewals at higher rates, (ii) an increase in voyage administration costs and operational requests from charterers offset by a decrease in bunkering expenses due to less off-hire days.
Depreciation and Amortization
Depreciation and amortization for the third quarter of 2026 was $33.7 million, compared to $30.7 million in the prior year period. The increase was mainly due to the ten drydockings completed after September 30, 2025, and the addition of the Three Newly Acquired Vessels offset by the sale of Dimitris Y in the fourth quarter of 2025.
Depreciation and amortization for the nine-month period ended September 30, 2026 was $101.3 million, compared to $90.8 million in the comparative period, mainly due to the factors noted above, offset by the sale of four vessels in 2025.
General and Administrative Expenses
General and administrative expenses were $6.2 million in the third quarter of 2026, compared to $3.7 million in the comparative period. The increase was mainly due to the non-cash charge for stock-based compensation expense recognized in relation to the awards of Class A common shares under our Equity Incentive Plan.
General and administrative expenses were $22.2 million for the nine-month period ended September 30, 2026, compared to $12.4 million in the comparative period mainly due to the factors noted above.
Gain on sale of vessels
Tasman (5,900 TEU, built 2000), Akiteta (2,200 TEU, built 2002), and Keta (2,200 TEU, built 2003) were sold for an aggregate gain of $28.3 million in the first quarter of 2025. None of our vessels were sold during the nine-month period ended September 30, 2026.

As of September 30, 2025, 17 of our vessels were unencumbered.
Interest and other finance expenses for the third quarter of 2026 were $7.5 million, down from $9.5 million for the prior year period. The decrease was mainly due to $2.4 million of interest expense that was capitalized in our vessels under construction.
Interest and other finance expenses for the nine-month period ended September 30, 2026 were $26.3 million, down from $30.0 million for the prior year period. The decrease was mainly due to (i) $2.4 million of interest expense that was capitalized in our vessels under construction and (ii) interest expense of 2025 included (i) a prepayment fee of $0.2 million following the full repayment of the Macquarie Credit Facility, and (ii) the non-cash write off of deferred financing costs of $0.7 million on the full repayments of the Macquarie Credit Facility, the HCOB-CACIB Credit Facility, and the ESUN Credit Facility.
Interest income for the third quarter of 2026 was $5.8 million, up from $5.4 million for the prior year period, mainly due to higher invested amounts.
Interest income for the nine-month period ended September 30, 2026 was $17.1 million, up from $13.3 million for the prior year period, mainly due to higher invested amounts.

Fair value adjustment on derivatives and other financial instruments
In December 2021, we entered into a USD 1-month LIBOR interest rate cap of 0.75% through the fourth quarter of 2026 on $484.1 million of floating rate debt, which reduces over time in-line with anticipated debt amortization and represented approximately half of the outstanding floating rate debt. In February 2022, we entered into two additional USD 1-month LIBOR interest rate caps of 0.75% through the fourth quarter of 2026 on the remaining balance of $507.9 million of floating rate debt. As a result of the discontinuation of LIBOR, on July 1, 2023, our interest rate caps automatically transited to one-month Compounded SOFR at a net rate of 0.64%. A negative fair value adjustment of $1.5 million for the nine-month period ended September 30, 2026, was recorded through the statement of income.
In January 2026, we entered into a series of FX Reverse Convertible transactions with UBS AG to hedge our exposure to foreign exchange risk while also achieving improved interest income on deposits. These instruments are USD-denominated structured notes with returns linked to the EUR/USD exchange rate. We elected the Fair Value Option to measure these instruments.

About Global Ship Lease
Global Ship Lease is a leading independent owner of containerships with a diversified fleet of mid-sized and smaller containerships. Incorporated in the Marshall Islands, Global Ship Lease commenced operations in December 2007 with a business of owning and chartering out containerships under fixed-rate charters to top tier container liner companies. It was listed on the New York Stock Exchange in August 2008.
Our operating fleet of 71 containerships as of September 30, 2026, had an average age weighted by TEU capacity of 18.7 years. 41 ships are wide-beam Post-Panamax. As of September 30, 2026, our fleet also included 17 newbuilding containerships under construction with scheduled deliveries between the fourth quarter of 2028 and the first quarter of 2030.
As of September 30, 2026, the average remaining term of the Company’s charters, to the mid-point of redelivery, including options under the Company’s control and other than if a redelivery notice has been received, including our newbuildings, was 3.5 years on a TEU-weighted basis. Contracted revenue, including our newbuildings, on the same basis was $3.4 billion. Contracted revenue was $4.3 billion, including options under charterers’ control and with latest redelivery date, representing a weighted average remaining term of 4.6 years.

A. Adjusted EBITDA
Adjusted EBITDA represents net income available to common shareholders before interest income and expense, earnings allocated to preferred shares, depreciation and amortization, gains or losses on the sale of vessels, amortization of intangible liabilities, charges for stock-based compensation, fair value adjustment on derivative assets and other financial instruments, income tax, and the effect of the straight lining of time charter modifications. Adjusted EBITDA is a non-U.S. GAAP quantitative measure used to assist in the assessment of our ability to generate cash from our operations. We believe that the presentation of Adjusted EBITDA is useful to investors because it is frequently used by securities analysts, investors and other interested parties in the evaluation of companies in our industry. Adjusted EBITDA is not defined in U.S. GAAP and should not be considered to be an alternative to net income or any other financial metric required by such accounting principles. Our use of Adjusted EBITDA may vary from the use of similarly titled measures by others in our industry.
Adjusted EBITDA is presented herein both on a historic basis and on a forward-looking basis in certain instances.
