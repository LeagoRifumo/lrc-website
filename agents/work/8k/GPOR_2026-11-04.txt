Gulfport Energy Reports Third Quarter 2026 Financial and Operating Results
OKLAHOMA CITY (November 4, 2026) Gulfport Energy Corporation (NYSE: GPOR) (“Gulfport” or the “Company”) today reported financial and operating results for the three months ended September 30, 2026.
Third Quarter 2026 Results and Recent Highlights
● | Reported $96.4 million of net income and $226.5 million of adjusted EBITDA(1)
● | Generated $183.6 million of net cash provided by operating activities and $89.0 million of adjusted free cash flow(1)
● | Produced approximately 1,057.2 MMcfe per day
● | Delivered net liquids production of 23.5 MBbl per day, an increase of approximately 67% over second quarter 2026
● | Incurred $111.7 million of capital expenditures, which includes $103.5 million of operated D&C capital expenditures and $8.2 million of maintenance land and seismic investment
● | Added opportunistic discretionary acreage acquisitions totaling $46.9 million
● | Completed previously announced Ohio state land lease acquisitions totaling $83.0 million
● | Repurchased approximately 218.7 thousand shares of common stock for approximately $36.2 million during the three months ended September 30, 2026
● | Expanded stock repurchase authorization by 33% to $2.0 billion
● | Completed fall borrowing base redetermination of revolving credit facility with reaffirmed borrowing base and elected commitments of $1.1 billion
● | Signed supply agreement for 50,000 MMBtu/d supporting growing Midwest demand, bringing premium priced supply contracts to approximately 10% of forecasted net production
● | Released annual Corporate Sustainability Report, reflecting Gulfport’s continued commitment to environmental stewardship, responsible operations and sound corporate governance

Nick Dell’Osso, Gulfport’s President and CEO, commented, “Our 2026 development program gained significant momentum in the third quarter. As planned, our back-half-weighted turn-in-line schedule drove production up approximately 10% from the second quarter, led by new liquids-rich wet gas Utica pads, with liquids now representing approximately 13% of total volumes. That higher-value production mix, combined with strong realized liquids pricing and our disciplined cost structure, translated directly into stronger margins and earnings, with adjusted EBITDA and adjusted free cash flow both up meaningfully from the second quarter. We expect to continue building on that strength in the fourth quarter, keeping us on track to deliver full-year production and capital expenditures in line with our guidance.”
Dell’Osso continued, “We also continued to enhance the depth of our inventory, completing the Ohio state land acquisitions, and expect to complete our $140 million 2026 discretionary acreage acquisition program by year-end through disciplined, targeted leasing in the core of our position. As we look to 2027, we are evaluating a more continuous two-rig Appalachia development program, which we believe will drive meaningful operational efficiencies through steadier rig and crew utilization, lower our development costs, concentrate capital in our highest-return asset area and position the business for production growth beginning in 2028. This shift in capital allocation would allow Gulfport to balance reinvestment with returns to shareholders while preserving our strong balance sheet, and we look forward to sharing our full 2027 outlook in February.”
“With a high-quality, deep and concentrated asset base in the heart of Appalachia, adjacent to growing power demand in Ohio, a strong financial foundation and an expanded stock repurchase authorization, we believe we are well positioned to generate durable adjusted free cash flow and deliver meaningful value to our shareholders,” Dell’Osso concluded.

Capital Investment
Capital investment was $111.7 million (on an incurred basis) for the third quarter of 2026, of which $103.5 million related to operated drilling and completion activity and $8.2 million related to maintenance land and seismic investment. Gulfport also invested approximately $46.9 million in discretionary acreage acquisitions, $83.0 million related to the previously announced Ohio state land lease acquisitions and incurred approximately $0.9 million related to non-operated drilling and completion activities.
For the nine-month period ended September 30, 2026, capital investment was $382.0 million (on an incurred basis), of which $363.0 million related to operated drilling and completion activity and $19.0 million related to maintenance land and seismic investment. Gulfport also invested approximately $126.6 million in discretionary acreage acquisitions, $83.0 million in the Ohio state land lease acquisitions and incurred approximately $1.6 million related to non-operated drilling and completion activities. Discretionary acreage acquisition expenditures included $39.5 million associated with the completion of the prior year program and $87.1 million associated with the 2026 discretionary acreage acquisition program, which is targeting $140 million of acreage acquisitions through the end of the year.
Expanded Common Stock Repurchase Program
Gulfport’s board of directors recently expanded the Company’s stock repurchase program, authorizing the Company to repurchase up to $2.0 billion of its outstanding stock.
Gulfport repurchased approximately 218.7 thousand shares of common stock during the third quarter of 2026, totaling approximately $36.2 million. As of September 30, 2026, the Company had repurchased approximately 8.8 million shares of common stock (including the underlying shares of common stock into which the preferred stock was convertible) at a weighted-average share price of $135.84 since the program initiated in March 2022, totaling approximately $1.2 billion in aggregate. As of September 30, 2026, the Company had approximately $800 million of remaining capacity under the expanded stock repurchase program.
Financial Position and Liquidity
As of September 30, 2026, Gulfport had approximately $2.6 million of cash and cash equivalents, $419.0 million of borrowings under its revolving credit facility, $37.9 million of letters of credit outstanding and $650.0 million of outstanding 2029 senior notes.
Gulfport’s liquidity at September 30, 2026, totaled approximately $645.6 million, comprised of the $2.6 million of cash and cash equivalents and approximately $643.1 million of available borrowing capacity under its credit facility.
Credit Facility Borrowing Base Redetermination
On October 30, 2026, Gulfport completed its semi-annual borrowing base redetermination, which reaffirmed both the borrowing base and elected commitments at $1.1 billion.
Firm Sales Agreements
During the fourth quarter, Gulfport signed a firm sales agreement for 50,000 MMBtu per day beginning in 2028, supporting growing Midwest demand. Combined with a previously executed 10-year agreement for 65,000 MMBtu per day beginning in mid-2027, Gulfport has contracted approximately 10% of forecasted net production at a premium to historical realizations. Both agreements will be supplied using the Company’s existing firm transportation capacity and are priced off sales indices positioned to benefit from growing regional power demand.

About Gulfport
Gulfport is an independent natural gas-weighted exploration and production company focused on the exploration, acquisition and production of natural gas, crude oil and NGL in the United States with primary focus in the Appalachia and Anadarko basins. Our principal properties are located in eastern Ohio targeting the Utica and Marcellus formations and in central Oklahoma targeting the SCOOP Woodford and Springer formations.

Non-GAAP Reconciliations
Gulfport’s management uses certain non-GAAP financial measures for planning, forecasting and evaluating business and financial performance, and believes that they are useful tools to assess Gulfport’s operating results. Although these are not measures of performance calculated in accordance with generally accepted accounting principles (GAAP), management believes that these financial measures are useful to an investor in evaluating Gulfport because (i) analysts utilize these metrics when evaluating company performance and have requested this information as of a recent practicable date, (ii) these metrics are widely used to evaluate a company’s operating performance, and (iii) we want to provide updated information to investors. Investors should not view these metrics as a substitute for measures of performance that are calculated in accordance with GAAP. In addition, because all companies do not calculate these measures identically, these measures may not be comparable to similarly titled measures of other companies.
These non-GAAP financial measures include adjusted net income, adjusted EBITDA, adjusted free cash flow, and recurring general and administrative expense. A reconciliation of each financial measure to its most directly comparable GAAP financial measure is included in the tables below. These non-GAAP measures should be considered in addition to, but not instead of, the financial statements prepared in accordance with GAAP.

Definitions
Adjusted net income is a non-GAAP financial measure equal to net income less non-cash derivative (gain) loss, non-recurring general and administrative expenses comprised of expenses related to the continued administration of our prior Chapter 11 filing, costs associated with management transitions, stock-based compensation expenses, other non-material expenses and the tax effect of the adjustments to net income.
Adjusted EBITDA is a non-GAAP financial measure equal to net income (loss), the most directly comparable GAAP financial measure, plus interest expense, income tax expense (benefit), depreciation, depletion, amortization and accretion, non-cash derivative loss (gain), non-recurring general and administrative expenses comprised of expenses related to the continued administration of our prior Chapter 11 filing, costs associated with management transitions, stock-based compensation and other non-material expenses.
Adjusted free cash flow is a non-GAAP measure defined as adjusted EBITDA plus certain non-cash items that are included in net cash provided by operating activities but excluded from adjusted EBITDA less interest expense, current income tax expense (benefit), capitalized expenses incurred and capital expenditures incurred. Gulfport includes an adjusted free cash flow estimate for 2026. We are unable, however, to provide a quantitative reconciliation of the forward-looking non-GAAP measure to its most directly comparable forward-looking GAAP measure because management cannot reliably quantify certain of the necessary components of such forward-looking GAAP measure. Accordingly, Gulfport is relying on the exception provided by Item 10(e)(1)(i)(B) of Regulation S-K to exclude such reconciliation. Items excluded in net cash provided by (used in) operating activities to arrive at adjusted free cash flow include interest expense, income taxes, capitalized expenses as well as one-time items or items whose timing or amount cannot be reasonably estimated.
Recurring general and administrative expense is a non-GAAP financial measure equal to general and administrative expense (GAAP) plus capitalized general and administrative expense, less non-recurring general and administrative expenses comprised of expenses related to the continued administration of our prior Chapter 11 filing and costs associated with management transitions. Gulfport includes a recurring general and administrative expense estimate for 2026. We are unable, however, to provide a quantitative reconciliation of the forward-looking non-GAAP measure to its most directly comparable forward-looking GAAP measure because management cannot reliably quantify certain of the necessary components of such forward-looking GAAP measure. Accordingly, Gulfport is relying on the exception provided by Item 10(e)(1)(i)(B) of Regulation S-K to exclude such reconciliation. Items excluded in general and administrative expense to arrive at recurring general and administrative expense include capitalized expenses as well as one-time items or items whose timing or amount cannot be reasonably estimated. The non-GAAP measure recurring general and administrative expenses allows investors to compare Gulfport’s total general and administrative expenses, including capitalization, to peer companies that account for their oil and gas operations using the successful efforts method.
