ENERFLEX LTD. ANNOUNCES THIRD QUARTER 2026 FINANCIAL AND OPERATIONAL RESULTS AND INCREASED DIVIDEND
ADJUSTED EBITDA OF $129 MILLION, FREE CASH FLOW OF $102 MILLION AND RETURN ON CAPITAL EMPLOYED OF 15.8%
RECORD ES BOOKINGS AND BACKLOG OF $1.0 BILLION AND $2.1 BILLION, RESPECTIVELY
QUARTERLY DIVIDEND INCREASED BY ~17% TO US$0.035 PER SHARE; SUPPORTS DIRECT SHAREHOLDER RETURNS
NEWS RELEASE
CALGARY, Alberta, October 29, 2026 – Enerflex Ltd. (TSX: EFX) (NYSE: EFXT) (“Enerflex” or the “Company”) today reported its financial and operational results for the three and nine months ended September 30, 2026.
All amounts presented are in U.S. Dollars unless otherwise stated.
MANAGEMENT COMMENTARY
Paul Mahoney, Enerflex's President and Chief Executive Officer stated: “Enerflex delivered a solid third quarter, highlighted by healthy cash generation, record bookings and backlog, notable commercial wins for behind-the-meter natural gas-fired power generation, and continued progress towards the strategic objectives highlighted during our May investor update. We are very pleased with the progress to date and adoption of operational and efficiency initiatives across our integrated company.
Enerflex’s focus is on competing intentionally in the markets where we can win and improving relentlessly through operational excellence. We remain confident in our ability to improve profitability, cash conversion, and returns through operational excellence, grow revenue ahead of our underlying markets, and deliver disciplined value adding growth for our shareholders. The momentum we are seeing in behind-the-meter natural gas-fired power generation, combined with investments to expand our Engineered Systems capacity and capabilities, reinforces our confidence in achieving our value creation objectives.
Behind-the-meter natural gas-fired power generation is becoming an increasingly important growth platform for Enerflex and complements the strength we continue to see in Enerflex’s core natural gas markets. Including awards secured in early Q4, our backlog of behind-the-meter natural gas-fired power generation now exceeds 1.75 gigawatts. We are uniquely positioned to support customers throughout the lifecycle of these assets, from design, engineering, manufacturing and assembly through commissioning, after-market services, operations, maintenance, and digital optimization enabled by ReliaCore™.”
Preet Dhindsa, Enerflex’s Senior Vice President and Chief Financial Officer, added: “Strong cash generation during the third quarter strengthened Enerflex’s financial position and provides significant flexibility as we continue to progress towards our strategic objectives. During the quarter, we completed the divestiture of the majority of our Asia Pacific operations, further sharpening our focus on core operating regions. Reflecting confidence in our business and outlook, the Board has approved an increase of approximately 17% to Enerflex’s quarterly dividend, aligning with our objective of providing meaningful direct shareholder returns. We remain focused on maintaining balance sheet strength and disciplined capital allocation, including investing in organic growth and evaluating selective value-enhancing tuck-in acquisition opportunities.”

Capital Allocation
Enerflex is refining its organic capital expenditure guidance for 2026. The Company continues to target spending of $185 million to $195 million during 2026, which includes: (1) organic growth capital expenditures of approximately $100 million (unchanged from prior guidance); (2) maintenance capital expenditures of approximately $55 million (prior guidance of $70 million to $80 million); and (3) PP&E and infrastructure investments of $30 million to $40 million to support the Company's ES business line and activity in adjacent markets, including electric power generation (prior guidance of approximately $15 million).
Organic growth capital spending will continue to focus on customer-supported opportunities and is primarily allocated to expand the Company's contract compression fleet in the U.S. We continue to target customer-supported fleet growth of 10% to 15% during 2026 and are also securing long-lead time components to support fleet growth into 2030.
The reduction in forecasted maintenance capital reflects efficiencies as well as a shift of some spending into 2027.
As previously announced, Enerflex has authorized investments in its ES facilities and capabilities, including $30 million to $40 million included in Enerflex’s 2026 capital expenditure guidance and approximately $85 million expected to be incurred during 2027. These investments, combined with on-going initiatives to enhance collaboration, leverage scale, improve operational efficiency, and solidify capabilities, are expected to significantly increase revenue capacity of Enerflex’s ES business line.
Enerflex continues to evaluate selective, disciplined tuck-in acquisition opportunities. Inorganic growth will remain focused on enhancing capabilities and accelerating scale in the Company's core North American markets. All opportunities will be balanced with Enerflex's focus on maintaining a strong financial position and providing direct shareholder returns.

FREE CASH FLOW
The Company defines free cash flow as cash provided by (used in) operating activities, less total capital expenditures (growth and maintenance) for EI assets - operating leases and PP&E, mandatory debt repayments, and lease payments, while proceeds on disposals of PP&E and EI assets - operating leases are added back. Free cash flow may not be comparable to similar measures presented by other companies as it does not have a standardized meaning under IFRS. Management uses this non-IFRS measure to assess the level of free cash generated to fund other non-operating activities. These activities could include dividend payments, share repurchases, and non-mandatory debt repayments. Free cash flow is also used in calculating the dividend payout ratio.

(a) IFRS 18 Presentation and Disclosure in Financial Statements ("IFRS 18")
On April 9, 2024, the IASB issued IFRS 18, the new standards on presentation and disclosure in financial statements. IFRS 18 will require defined subtotals in the consolidated statement of earnings, require disclosure of management-defined performance measures ("MPM"), provide principles for the aggregation and disaggregation of information, and improve comparability across entities and reporting periods. IFRS 18 will replace IAS 1, Presentation of financial statements, and retains many of the existing principles in IAS 1. IFRS 18 will be effective for years beginning on or after January 1, 2027. Retrospective application is required.
The Company will adopt IFRS 18 and the related amendments on January 1, 2027. Management's assessment concludes that the standard will primarily impact the presentation, classification, and disclosure of financial information, with no significant impact on the Company's underlying financial performance or net income. Based on management's preliminary assessment, the most significant changes to the statement of earnings include the introduction of defined operating, investing, and financing categories and related presentation changes, including the classification of interest income (not related to finance lease receivable) within the investing category, gains and losses on redemption options within the financing category, and foreign exchange gains and losses based on the nature of the underlying activity.
IFRS 18 also introduces enhanced disclosure requirements for operating expenses, including additional disclosures by nature for certain expenses presented by function in a single note. Based on management's preliminary assessment, management expects EBITDA, Adjusted EBITDA, Gross Margin Before D&A, and Recurring Gross Margin Before D&A to qualify as MPMs and, accordingly, these measures will require disclosures, explanations, and reconciliations within the audited financial statements. These measures are currently presented in the Company's Management's Discussion and Analysis ("MD&A").
The consequential amendments to IAS 7 will also result in presentation changes to the consolidated statement of cash flows, including changing the starting point for determining cash flows from operating activities under the indirect method from net earnings to operating profit or loss and removing the optionality around the classification of cash flows from dividends and interest, with no impact on total cash flows.

In June 2026, the IASB issued amendments to the fair value option for investments in associates and joint ventures. The amendments clarify which entities are eligible to elect the fair value option for investments in associates and joint ventures instead of applying the equity method of accounting. Specifically, the amendments clarify that entities whose main business activity is investing in particular types of assets may qualify as “similar entities” and therefore be eligible to measure such investments at fair value through profit or loss. The amendment will be effective for years beginning on or after January 1, 2027. This amendment is not expected to have any impact on the Company's Financial Statements.

Note 4. Energy Infrastructure Assets
The Company’s EI assets are comprised of Build-Own-Operate-Maintain (“BOOM”) assets and contract compression assets which are leased to client partners. At the inception of a lease contract, all leases are classified as either an operating lease or a finance lease in accordance with IFRS Accounting Standards.
(a) EI Assets - Finance Leases Receivable
Lease arrangements for certain EI assets are considered finance leases when the risks and rewards of ownership are transferred to the lessee, which generally occurs in the following circumstances; ownership of the lease is transferred to the lessee by the end of the lease term; the lessee has the option to purchase the leased asset at a price that is sufficiently lower than the fair value at the date the option becomes exercisable for it to be reasonably certain, at the inception date, that option will be exercised; the term of the lease is for the major part of the economic life of the asset; or the present value of the lease payments amounts to substantially all of the fair value of the asset.
