1 | NATURE OF OPERATIONS

OceanaGold Corporation (the “Company” or “OceanaGold”) is domiciled in British Columbia, Canada and the registered address of the Company is Suite 1020, 400 Burrard Street, Vancouver, British Columbia, V6C 3A6, Canada. The Company’s common shares trade under the symbol 'OGC' on the Toronto Stock Exchange (“TSX”) in Canada and, as of April 7, 2026, on the New York Stock Exchange (“NYSE”) in the United States.
The Company is engaged in the exploration, development and operation of gold and gold/copper mines. OceanaGold operates four operating mines: the wholly-owned Haile Gold Mine in the United States of America; the wholly-owned Macraes and Waihi operations in New Zealand; and the 80%-owned Didipio Mine in the Philippines.
These unaudited condensed interim consolidated financial statements were approved by the Board of Directors on November 4, 2026.

IFRS 18: Presentation and Disclosure in Financial Statements
In April 2024, the International Accounting Standards Board issued IFRS 18, Presentation and Disclosure in Financial Statements (“IFRS 18”), which will replace IAS 1, Presentation of Financial Statements. Although IFRS 18 does not change the recognition or measurement of items in the financial statements, it introduces new presentation and disclosure requirements. IFRS 18 is effective for annual reporting periods beginning on or after January 1, 2027, including the related interim reporting periods, and is required to be applied retrospectively.
The Company is assessing the detailed implications of applying IFRS 18. Based on its preliminary assessment, the Company has identified the following potential impacts on its financial statements.
IFRS 18 introduces a defined structure for the statement of profit or loss and requires income and expenses to be classified into operating, investing and financing categories, together with income tax and discontinued operations, where applicable. The requirements will result in the presentation of new required subtotals, including operating profit, and may change the composition of certain existing subtotals and line items. Interest income arising from cash and cash equivalents is expected to be classified in the investing category. Foreign exchange differences will generally be classified in the same category as the income and expenses arising from the underlying items, subject to the specific requirements of IFRS 18.
IFRS 18 also introduces enhanced requirements for the aggregation and disaggregation of information presented in the primary financial statements and disclosed in the notes. The standard does not change which items are recognized outside profit or loss or the applicable classification requirements for items of other comprehensive income.
IFRS 18 also requires disclosures relating to management-defined performance measures (“MPM”). An MPM is a subtotal of income and expenses that is used in public communications outside the financial statements to communicate management’s view of an aspect of the financial performance of the Company as a whole. Based on its preliminary assessment of the performance measures currently used in external communications, the Company has identified Adjusted net profit, EBITDA and Adjusted EBITDA as measures that may meet the definition of an MPM. This assessment remains subject to further review.
In the statement of cash flows, interest received is expected to be classified as an investing cash flow, rather than an operating cash flow under the Company’s current presentation. In addition, because the Company applies the indirect method, operating profit will be used as the starting point for presenting cash flows from operating activities.

There are two components to each performance share right: a performance condition based on the Company's share price performance relative to peers ("TSR") and a service condition. (a) | OGPI is party to an addendum agreement with a syndicate of original claim owners, led by the late Mr. J. The Gonzales Group and OGPI are involved in an arbitration proceeding with respect to the Addendum Agreement (the “Arbitration”). The Arbitration commenced in 2000 but is presently suspended due to the Liggayu dispute (discussed below) and the irrevocable resignation of the arbitrator.

In a complaint dated July 4, 2008 before the Philippines Regional Trial Court ("RTC"), a third-party, Mr. Liggayu, disputed the terms of the Addendum Agreement and the rights of the Gonzales Group to claim an interest in the Didipio Mine. Mr. Liggayu alleged that he is the true and beneficial owner and real-party-in-interest of the Didipio mining claims, and sought to enjoin the Company from making any payments to, or in dealing with, the Gonzales Group, and instead to recognize his rights.
In a decision dated March 11, 2025, the RTC declared that Mr. Liggayu and the heirs of Mr. Gonzales Sr. are partners on a 50-50 basis, to all the rights, participation and interests, as claim owners of the Didipio mining claims in the name of Mr. Gonzales Sr., beginning January 2007 onwards. It further declared that the rights and entitlements of Mr. Liggayu cannot be directly enforced by him against OceanaGold in the existing agreements, specifically the FTAA, which Mr. Liggayu can internally claim and enforce only against the heirs of Mr. Gonzales Sr, and vice versa, (the “March 2025 Decision”).
On April 2, 2025, Mr. Liggayu moved for partial reconsideration of this decision, claiming that, among others, he is the true and lawful owner of the Didipio mining claims; if a partnership exists, it should be from 1985 and should cover all the subject mining claims and not just for Mr. Gonzales Sr’s portion of the claim; and his rights and entitlements should be directly enforceable by him against OceanaGold. Both the Gonzales Group and OceanaGold filed an opposition to Mr. Liggayu’s partial motion for reconsideration. In a decision dated October 21, 2025, the RTC denied Mr. Liggayu’s motion for partial reconsideration (the “October 2025 Resolution”). Mr. Liggayu has now filed a Notice of Appeal to each of the March 2025 Decision and October 2025 Resolution. This case is now elevated to the Court of Appeals.
On September 1, 2026, OGPI received Mr. Liggayu’s brief. On October 16, 2026, OGPI and the Gonzales Group filed their respective brief in response.
The Company believes there is no near-term impact on its business or operations as the decisions do not require payment of money by OceanaGold and the Arbitration proceeding is yet to be resolved.
As of September 30, 2026, the Company has accrued $93.9 million ($71.9 million of royalties and $22.0 million related to free-carried interest) pertaining to the Addendum Agreement (December 31, 2025: $83.6 million).

Production
The Company produced 133,400 ounces of gold and 3,400 tonnes of copper in the third quarter of 2026. Gold production for the quarter was 4% lower than the prior quarter, with 8% lower production at Haile and 10% lower production at Macraes being partially offset by 15% and 6% higher production at Waihi and Didipio, respectively. At Haile, the lower production was due to a decrease in underground ore tonnes mined from the Horseshoe Underground at lower grades, resulting in lower grades processed. At Macraes, the lower production was primarily due to lower open pit ore tonnes mined at lower grades due to the completion of the current ore phase in Innes Mills Phase 8, as planned. The higher production at Waihi reflects an increase in ore tonnes mined at higher grades.
Third quarter gold production was 29% higher than the prior corresponding quarter, primarily driven by an 82% increase in gold production at Haile as a result of open pit mine sequencing in Ledbetter Phase 3 and increased tonnes and grade mined from the Horseshoe Underground. Macraes gold production was 13% higher due to an increase in underground ore tonnes mined and the processing of higher grade stockpiles, as compared to the prior corresponding quarter. At Didipio, production was 4% higher than the prior corresponding quarter as a result of a higher proportion of underground material being processed. Production at Waihi was comparable to the prior corresponding quarter.
Year to date, the Company has produced 402,300 ounces of gold, an 18% increase compared to the prior corresponding year to date period, driven by 43% higher production at Macraes and 20% higher production at Haile. Production at Macraes was driven by higher grade ore mined and processed from Innes Mills Phase 8, while increased production at Haile reflected higher mill throughput as a result of process plant improvement initiatives. Production at both Waihi and Didipio was comparable to the prior corresponding year to date period.
AISC†
The Company recorded third quarter AISC† of $1,874 per ounce, 13% lower than the prior quarter. The reduction was primarily driven by lower sustaining capital at all operations, higher by-product credits at Didipio and the benefit of higher gold sales volumes, partially offset by higher labour costs and increased maintenance and consumables costs from higher usage and inflation.
Third quarter AISC† was 20% lower than the prior corresponding quarter, primarily due to a 21% increase in gold ounces sold. This was partially offset by labour cost inflation inclusive of higher stock-based compensation settled in the first quarter of 2026 (as AISC† includes the cash settled element of stock-based compensation over the year of vesting) and ongoing cost inflation.
The Company recorded year to date AISC† of $2,038 per ounce on gold sales of 412,000 ounces. AISC† was consistent with the prior corresponding year to date period, as the benefits of a significant increase in gold ounces sold, primarily at Macraes and Haile, together with higher by-product credits at Didipio, were largely offset by higher labour costs, including increased stock-based compensation, investment in maintenance and reliability improvements, and inflationary pressures on operating consumables and maintenance parts.
