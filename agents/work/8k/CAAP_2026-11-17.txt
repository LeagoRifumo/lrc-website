NOTE 1 – COMPANY ACTIVITIES
Aeropuertos Argentina 2000 S.A. was incorporated in the Autonomous City of Buenos Aires in 1998, after the consortium of companies won the national and international bid for the concession rights for the use, management and operation of the “A” Group of the Argentine National Airport System. “A” Group includes 33 airports that operate in Argentina (the “Concession”).
Currently, with the incorporation into Group A of the NSA of the airports of El Palomar (by Decree No. 1107/17) and Rio Hondo (by Resolution ORSNA No. 27/21), the Company has the concession rights for the operation, administration and operation of 35 airports.
Decree No. 163/98, published in the Official Gazette on February 13, 1998, approved the Concession Agreement entered into between the National State and the Company on February 9, 1998 (hereinafter, the "Concession Agreement"). The concession agreement was amended and supplemented by the Concession Agreement Adjustment Act, signed between the Argentine National State and the Company on April 3, 2007, and approved by Decree No. 1799/07 (hereinafter the "Adjustment Act"), as well as by Decree No. 1009/20, dated December 16, 2020, which approved a 10-year extension to the initial expiration date of the Concession (originally set for February 13, 2028), while maintaining exclusivity under the terms established in the Technical Conditions for the Extension (hereinafter the "Technical Conditions for the Extension").
By virtue of the provisions of the Technical Conditions for the Extension, the concession completion period is February 13, 2038 and the exclusivity provided in clauses 3.11 and 4.1 of the Concession Agreement will be maintained with the following exceptions: (i) The zones of influence in the interior of the country are canceled, but not in the area of the Metropolitan Region of Buenos Aires (RMBA) made up of the Ezeiza, Aeroparque, San Fernando and Palomar airports (ii) the exclusivity in the areas of influence will be maintained throughout the national territory for the activity of fiscal warehouses (iii) the exclusivity and from the area of influence for the realization of new airport infrastructure projects in the Rio de la Plata promoted by the National Public Sector, when due to its characteristics it cannot be financed and operated by the Company.
The macroeconomic and operational conditions resulting from the post-pandemic context affected the balance of the economic-financial equation of the Concession Agreement, leading to a reduction in the Internal Rate of Return relative to the parameters established in the Adjustment Agreement and the Technical Conditions for the Extension.
Given this scenario, the ORSNA and the Company initiated discussions aimed at a contractual renegotiation to restore the financial sustainability of the concession.
Consequently, on September 1, 2026, a Contractual Readjustment Agreement (hereinafter, the "Agreement") was signed between the Regulatory Body and the Company. This Agreement, along with its respective annexes, was approved by Administrative Decision No. 27/26 of the Office of the Chief of the Cabinet of Ministers, which was published in the Official Gazette on September 28, 2026.
Hereinafter, references to the concession agreement shall encompass the agreement as modified and supplemented by Decree No. 163/98, the Adjustment Agreement, the Technical Conditions for the Extension, and the aforementioned Agreement.

The Agreement establishes a comprehensive solution for restoring the economic-financial balance of the Concession and includes a new PFIE extending until the year 2056.
Likewise, the ORSNA shall implement the necessary measures to align airport charges with the values contemplated in the PFIE, which will be modified as necessary as a result of the Ordinary Reviews procedure -carried out every three years- or Extraordinary Reviews of the economic and financial equation of the Concession, and of the periodic adjustments provided for in the PFIE.
For the purposes of readjusting the contract and in order to restore the balance of the economic and financial equation, a new equilibrium TIR of 14.30% is estimated for the contractual economic and financial projection.
The Agreement provides for an extension of the Concession term until February 13, 2056, with a portion of this extension being conditional upon the Company demonstrating—by June 30, 2027—the availability of funds, credit facilities, or financial commitments necessary to cover an additional direct investment of US$600,000,000 (VAT included). Furthermore, it requires that 75% of the works stipulated therein be completed by December 31, 2031. Failure to meet these conditions will result in the Concession term ending on February 13, 2049.
In accordance with the terms of the Agreement, a filing was submitted requesting judicial ratification of the Agreement and its corresponding Annexes. Consequently, upon obtaining the relevant court ruling to that effect, the Company waived its claims in the legal proceeding “Aeropuertos Argentina 2000 S.A. v. ORSNA – Res. 56/23 (Case File No. CAF 32610/2023)”—a case in which the Company had sought a review of the Investment and Infrastructure Plan (PFIE) for the Group “A” Concession within the National Airport System, as well as the adoption of appropriate and necessary measures to restore its economic-financial balance.
The Company also withdrew all claims, appeals, and legal actions related to damages caused by the COVID-19 pandemic. Furthermore, it is withdrawing other prior administrative challenges, with the exception of reviews concerning investments, which remain ongoing in accordance with the express terms of the Concession Agreement .
Additionally, the following were established: (i) a triennial ordinary review mechanism to be conducted by ORSNA; (ii) a requirement for the Company to continue allocating 15% of the total Concession revenues on a monthly basis to the contractually stipulated dedicated asset pools (trusts); and (iii) the creation of a three-member Technical Panel for the resolution of disputes related to the Concession, which must be engaged prior to any intervention by the competent federal administrative-litigation court.
As of the date hereof, the Company has fulfilled the commitments undertaken under the aforementioned agreements.
On October 8, 2026, the ORSNA issued ORSNA Resolution No. 69/2026, which approves the new tariff schedule applicable to the airports comprising Group “A” of the NSA, in accordance with the maximum values, scope and conditions set forth in Annex I of Administrative Decision 27/26. This tariff schedule establishes that the fees payable by passengers are denominated in U.S. dollars.

NOTE 3 – ACCOUNTING POLICIES (Contd.)
3) Segment Information
The Company operates as a single unit and does not assess the management of its airports on an individual basis, but rather on a consolidated basis, considering all airports as a whole. The Company’s Board of Directors is the body responsible for evaluating the Company’s performance as a whole and for making decisions regarding the allocation of resources, and therefore constitutes, for these purposes, the chief operating decision maker. In evaluating the Company’s performance, the Board of Directors considers, among other financial and operating indicators, adjusted EBITDA. Accordingly, for the purposes of segment reporting, there is a single operating segment.
The Argentine National Government granted the Company the concession of the “A” Group airports of the NSA under the basis of “cross-subsidies”: i.e., the income and funds generated by some of the airports should subsidize the liabilities and investments of the remaining airports, in order for all airports to be compliant with international standards as explained below.
All airports must comply with measures of operative efficiency that are independent from the revenues and funds they generate. All works performed must follow international standards established by the respective agencies (IATA, OACI, etc.).
Revenues of the Company comprise non-aeronautical revenues and aeronautical revenues; the latter being the tariffs determined by the ORSNA and regulated on the basis of the review of the PFIE of The Company in order to verify and preserve the "equilibrium" of the variables on which it was originally based.
The investment decisions are assessed and made with the ORSNA based on the master plans of the airports considering the needs of each airport based on expected passenger flow and air traffic, in the framework of the standards previously mentioned. The Company’s Board of Directors is responsible for ensuring that the decisions adopted are carried out.

7) Foreign currency conversion and financial information in hyperinflationary economies
Functional and presentation currency
The figures included in these financial statements were measured using their functional currency, that is, the currency of the primary economic environment in which the Company operates. The functional currency of the Company is the Argentine peso, which is the same as the presentation currency of the financial statements.
IAS 29 "Financial information in hyperinflationary economies" requires that the financial statements of an entity whose functional currency is that of a hyperinflationary economy be expressed in terms of the current unit of measurement at the reporting date of the reporting period, regardless of whether they are based on the historical cost method or the current cost method. For this, in general terms, inflation produced from the date of acquisition or from the revaluation date, as applicable, must be computed in the non-monetary items.
These requirements also correspond to the comparative information of these Consolidated Condensed Interim financial statements.
In order to conclude on whether an economy is categorized as hyperinflationary under the terms of IAS 29, the standard details a series of factors to be considered, including the existence of a cumulative inflation rate in three years that approximates or exceed 100%. Taking into account that the accumulated inflation rate of the last three years exceeds 100% and the rest of the indicators do not contradict the conclusion that Argentina should be considered as a hyperinflationary economy for accounting purposes, the Company Management understands that there is sufficient evidence to conclude that Argentina is a hyperinflationary economy under the terms of IAS 29, as of July 1, 2018. It is for this reason that, in accordance with the IAS 29, these Consolidated Financial Statements are restated reflecting the effects of inflation in accordance with the provisions of the standard.

Inflation adjustment
In an inflationary period, any entity that maintains an excess of monetary assets over monetary liabilities will lose purchasing power, and any entity that maintains an excess of monetary liabilities over monetary assets will gain purchasing power, provided that such items are not subject to a mechanism of adjustment.
Briefly, the re-expression mechanism of IAS 29 establishes that monetary assets and liabilities will not be restated since they are already expressed in the current unit of measurement at the end of the reporting period. Assets and liabilities subject to adjustments based on specific agreements will be adjusted in accordance with such agreements.
The non-monetary items measured at their current values at the end of the reporting period, such as the net realization value or others, do not need to be re-expressed. The remaining non-monetary assets and liabilities will be re-expressed by a general price index. The loss or gain from the net monetary position will be included in the comprehensive net result of the reporting period, revealing this information in a separate line item.
