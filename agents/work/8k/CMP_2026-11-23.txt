SALT BUSINESS RECAP
Salt segment delivered improved results in both the fourth quarter and the full fiscal year. Fourth-quarter revenue increased 13% to $204.6 million, driven by a 7% increase in volumes and a 5% increase in aggregate pricing. Fourth-quarter sales volumes year over year saw 8% higher volumes in highway product and a 5% increase in consumer and industrial (C&I) salt product. Aggregate pricing for the segment was up as a result of increased weighting of highway volume in the current quarter, which saw a 7% and 4% increase in price per ton for highway and C&I products, respectively. Operating income increased 27% to $27.4 million, while adjusted EBITDA increased 16% to $46.5 million in each case from the prior-year period. These increases reflect higher pricing and lower per-unit product costs, partially offset by higher per-unit shipping and handling costs.

For the fiscal year 2026, Salt segment revenue increased 7% to $1,092.6 million and adjusted EBITDA increased 8% to $235.8 million. Adjusted EBITDA per ton improved approximately 7% to $21.72. Full-year highway volumes declined approximately 1% because inventory availability constrained sales despite favorable winter demand in key markets. Higher pricing, improved mine efficiency and lower per-unit production costs provide a stronger operating foundation entering fiscal 2027, partially offset by higher fuel-related shipping and handling costs.
PLANT NUTRITION BUSINESS RECAP
Plant Nutrition delivered a significant improvement in full-year profitability. Revenue increased 1% to $207.8 million despite an 8% decline in reported volumes, primarily associated with the March 2026 disposition of the Wynyard SOP business. Operating income increased to $23.3 million from $6.5 million, adjusted EBITDA increased 57% to $54.8 million, and adjusted EBITDA margin expanded to 26.4% from 16.9%. Fourth-quarter adjusted EBITDA was $10.1 million compared with $13.5 million in the prior-year period, reflecting the absence of the prior-year Wynyard contribution and the timing of sales. Completion of the Ogden dryer and compaction project in fiscal 2027 is expected to create an additional opportunity to improve capacity, product quality and long-term operating performance.
CASH FLOW AND FINANCIAL POSITION
Net cash provided by operating activities was $108.1 million in fiscal 2026, compared to $197.7 million in fiscal 2025. Prior-year operating cash flow benefited from an $87.3 million reduction in inventories. During fiscal 2026 the company realized a working capital release of approximately $14 million out of finished goods inventory and increases in accounts payable and accrued expenses, offset by increases in receivables and the settlement of the previously disclosed tax dispute in Ontario during the first quarter of 2026.
Net cash used in investing activities was $69.9 million in fiscal 2026 compared to $50.0 million in fiscal 2025. Capital expenditures totaled $91.5 million compared to $69.7 million in fiscal 2025, with fiscal 2026 spending primarily directed toward maintenance-related projects. The company also received $23.3 million of net cash proceeds from the sale of the Wynyard SOP business, after amounts held in escrow and customary closing adjustments.
Net cash used in financing activities was $79.9 million in fiscal 2026, primarily reflecting the redemption of the remaining $150.0 million principal amount of the company’s 6.75% Senior Notes due 2027 (the 2027 Notes), partially offset by $78.0 million of net borrowings under the revolving credit facility. As a result, total debt declined by $65.0 million during fiscal 2026 to $767.2 million at year end. In fiscal 2025, net cash used in financing activities was $108.3 million and included the issuance of $650.0 million of the company’s 8.00% Senior Notes due 2030 (the 2030 Notes). Proceeds from that issuance were used to repurchase $350.0 million of the 2027 Notes and repay $43.5 million of borrowings under the company’s revolving credit facility and repay $191.3 million under its term loan facility.

The company entered the 2026-2027 North American highway bid season with the objective of expanding margin through both price and customer mix. Compared with fiscal 2026, North American highway pricing in the fiscal 2027 plan is expected to be up approximately 12%. Our U.S. markets saw increases in the mid-teens, with some regions well above that level, while Canada, where more of the business is under multi-year contracts, is expected to be up in the mid-to-high single digits. We also improved the quality of our commercial contracts. Guaranteed terms increased to 78% of the committed U.S. portfolio from 65% a year ago, providing a strong level of sales demand confidence heading into the winter.
The strong 2025-2026 winter drew down inventories across the industry, and market tender sizes this season are up roughly 5% year over year. With its mines running at full production, the company sized its commitments to the salt it expects to have available. As a result, highway sales volumes are forecast at 7.5 million to 7.9 million tons, a decline of approximately 13% at the midpoint. Ultimately, sales will be driven by winter weather and how that drives demand in served markets.
Salt guidance reflects lower per-ton production costs at the Goderich and Cote Blanche assets from higher utilization and labor efficiency, partially offset by higher logistics costs driven by fuel. The company uses Brent crude as a proxy for its fuel exposure. Guidance assumes Brent at $105 per barrel in the first half of the fiscal year and $90 per barrel in the second half.
Compass Minerals expects that approximately 70% of its highway sales will be achieved in the first half of the fiscal year.

About Compass Minerals
Compass Minerals (NYSE: CMP) is a leading global provider of essential minerals focused on safely delivering where and when it matters to help solve nature’s challenges for customers and communities. The company’s salt products help keep roadways safe during winter weather and are used in numerous other consumer, industrial, chemical and agricultural applications. Its plant nutrition products help improve the quality and yield of crops, while supporting sustainable agriculture. Compass Minerals operates 11 production and packaging facilities with more than 1,800 employees throughout the U.S., Canada and the U.K. Visit compassminerals.com for more information about the company and its products.
