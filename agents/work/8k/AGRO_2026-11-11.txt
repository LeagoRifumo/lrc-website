Sugar, Ethanol & Energy segment (SE&E)
Performance Highlights
◦Adjusted EBITDA amounted to $101.1 million in 3Q26 and $195.0 million in 9M26, 16.0% and 10.7% lower year-over-year, respectively. Effective September 1, 2026, operational and financial metrics for the SE&E business include Caarapó mill.
▪(+/-) Year-to-date crushing reached 10.4 million tons, up 5.9% year-over-year, although 3Q26 saw a 5.1% decline due to above-average rainfall in September. TRS per hectare increased 24.0% year-over-year in 9M26 and 31.0% in 3Q26.
▪(+) Quarterly mix of 47% sugar - 53% ethanol; year-to-date 35% - 65%.
▪(-/+) Lower net sales on lower sugar prices and volumes, partially offset by higher ethanol volumes as we accelerated sales to capture a favorable pricing window (all-in avg net selling price of $503/m³).
▪(-) Year-over-year losses in biological assets on lower Consecana prices.
▪(-) Cost of production stood at 9.2 cts/lb (versus 8.3 cts/lb in 9M25) despite higher crushing volume, driven by the appreciation of the Brazilian real. Excluding FX, costs in local currency remained broadly flat year-over-year.
Outlook
◦(+) We expect record crushing in 2026, with mid-double-digit growth versus 2025 on greater cane productivity (including Caarapó from September 1 and assuming normal weather through year-end). Our continuous harvest model ensures any cane left unharvested at year-end will be crushed from January onwards.
◦(+) As of this date, 100% of our 2026 sugar production is hedged at an average of 16.5 cts/lb, and 41% of 2027 production at 18.3 cts/lb. Unhedged volumes provide upside exposure in 1H27.
◦(+) We are accelerating ethanol sales to capture stronger prices, supported by a higher demand due to the low parity at the pump.

Food & Agriculture segment (F&A)
Performance Highlights
◦Adjusted EBITDA reached $12.6 million in 3Q26, compared to $1.5 million in 3Q25. On a year-to-date basis, Adjusted EBITDA reached $18.9 million, in line with 9M25.
▪(+) Higher quarterly sales driven by a strong performance of our consumer brands in the domestic market, mainly in UHT milk, coupled with higher white rice volumes as prices gradually recovered.
▪(+) Strong operational performance: higher grain and rice yields during 2025/26 harvest season, record quarterly cow productivity and higher rice and dairy processing volumes.
▪(+) Year-over-year gains in biological assets on better corn and peanut yields during the 2025/26 harvest.
▪(-) Higher costs in U.S. dollar terms.
Outlook
◦(+) 2026/27 planting activities are underway, with a forecasted planting plan of 245.4 thousand hectares, 2.9% higher than the previous campaign. We forecast yields in line to slightly above historical average, supported by above-average rainfall expected due to "El Niño" weather event. We expect continued strong dairy performance, supported by higher raw milk production, with cow productivity reaching an all-time high in October (41 liters/cow/day), along with higher processing volumes and prices.
Remarks
Caarapó Acquisition and Integration into our SE&E Cluster
◦On September 1, 2026, we completed the acquisition of Caarapó Mill in Mato Grosso do Sul from Raízen Group for R$705 million (∼US$136 million), paid in cash at closing. Based on Caarapó’s 3.5 million tons crushed in the 2025/26 season, the acquisition implies a purchase price of approximately US$39 per ton of cane crushed.
◦Similar in scale to our existing mills in Mato Grosso do Sul, Caarapó has an installed capacity of approximately 6 to 7 million tons of cane per year. By redirecting excess cane from our Cluster to Caarapó, we will increase crushing volumes from the outset, estimating reaching 4.5 million tons in
3
2027, initially by extending the harvest season and ultimately migrating toward a continuous harvest model.
◦We see significant room to improve Caarapó’s performance by applying the same operating model and know-how that have made our Cluster one of the lowest-cost producers of sugar, ethanol and energy in the world. In 2027, our Angelica, Ivinhema and Caarapó mills will conform a new Cluster which is expected to crush approximately 17 million tons of sugarcane, making it one of the largest in Brazil.
Strengthening Our Balance Sheet for the Long Term
◦As part of our commitment to maintaining a healthy balance sheet and a well-balanced capital structure, we continue to proactively manage our liabilities, with a focus on extending our debt maturity profile, optimizing funding costs and preserving financial flexibility. Our diversified access to funding sources, including attractive local financing opportunities available through our operating subsidiaries, supports these efforts.
◦In line with this strategy, on October 28, 2026, we fully redeemed our outstanding 6.000% Senior Notes due 2027, for an aggregate principal amount of US$234.9 million, at par plus accrued and unpaid interest.
◦Following these actions, long-term debt accounts for 78% of total pro forma debt, up from 64%, extending our average debt life to 4.2 years and further strengthening our maturity profile.

Significantly higher rainfall in September (141% above the 10-year average in Angelica/Ivinhema region and 179% above average in Caarapó) reduced effective milling days during the quarter, resulting in a 5.1% year-over-year decline in 3Q26 crushing volumes to 4.6 million tons. Nevertheless, sugarcane productivity continued to improve, with TRS content per hectare up 31.0% year-over-year. On a year-to-date basis, crushing increased 5.9% year-over-year to 10.4 million tons, supported by strong yields of 82 tons per hectare which offset a smaller harvested area. Higher rainfall, however, weighed on TRS content, which declined 3.3% year-over-year despite improving versus previous quarters.
During the quarter, we dynamically adjusted our production mix to consistently maximize the product offering the most attractive margins, resulting in a sugar-ethanol mix of 47% - 53%. Within our ethanol production, we continued to favor the production of hydrous ethanol.
Despite the lower quarterly crushing, exported energy was in line with 3Q25. On a year-to-date basis, energy exported was 14.5% higher than 9M25, driven by the aforementioned 5.9% year-over-year increase in crushing and the use of our stored bagasse to produce energy to comply with our contracts.
Year-to-date, our production costs excluding depreciation and amortization totaled 9.2 cts/lb, 9.9% higher year-over-year. This was primarily driven by the appreciation of the Brazilian real versus 9M25, coupled with higher harvesting costs due to higher diesel prices. Excluding the impact of FX appreciation, our production costs in Brazilian reals remained broadly in line with the prior year.

Net sales reached $128.2 million in 3Q26 and $367.2 million in 9M26, down 6.4% and 16.3% year-over-year, respectively. Despite the increase in ethanol selling volumes, particularly during 3Q26, the decrease in revenues was mainly explained by lower sugar sales, reflecting both lower prices and volumes sold.
Sugar sales declined both on a quarterly and year-to-date basis, fully explained by lower volumes sold and lower global prices. We maximized ethanol production until mid-August 2026 given its better margin (versus a higher sugar mix during 1H25), before shifting towards sugar as global prices rebounded by the end of 3Q26 on a tighter global S&D balance driven by lower-than-expected production across key exporting countries.
Higher ethanol sales during both periods was mainly explained by higher volumes sold. By mid-3Q26, we accelerated ethanol sales to capture higher prices, driven by tighter domestic supply following weather-related disruptions in Brazil’s Center-South region, as well as government measures supporting ethanol’s competitiveness versus gasoline (recorded under Other Operating Income). Thereby, our all-in average net selling price reached $503/m³ during 3Q26. On a year-to-date basis, our top-line also benefited from the sale of our carry-over inventories and daily production during 1Q26, when we capitalized on peak prices ahead of the harvest season, as well as from a stronger Brazilian real.
Quarterly energy sales were broadly in line with 3Q25, as the appreciation of the Brazilian real offset lower volumes sold. On an accumulated basis, the increase in sales was driven by higher volumes due to higher crushing and the use of stored bagasse, coupled with higher prices reflecting attractive spot market opportunities during 1H26 and a stronger Brazilian real.
Adjusted EBITDA reached $101.1 million in 3Q26 and $195.0 million in 9M26, 16.0% and 10.7% lower than in 3Q25 and 9M25, respectively. This was mainly driven by the aforementioned decline in sales; coupled with year-over-year losses in the mark-to-market of our biological assets on lower Consecana prices.

Following the scheduled turnaround maintenance completed at the end of 2025 (prior to the acquisition), and leveraging more than 25 years of operating experience, we set a new monthly urea production record in August, while ammonia production reached new monthly highs in both August and September. As a result, urea production reached 342 thousand tons in 3Q26, up 4.1% year-over-year, and 960 thousand tons in 9M26, 11.4% higher than the same period of last year, mainly driven by a higher plant utilization rate.
Sales reached $263.2 million in 3Q26 and $559.0 million in 9M26, up 16.0% and 25.0% year-over-year, respectively. After the hike in prices in 1H26 amid the escalation of the conflict in the Middle East, a region that accounts for approximately 30% of global urea trade, international urea prices declined sharply in June, supporting a recovery in demand. As the South American summer crop planting season approached, demand continued to strengthen, setting a floor for prices which gradually rose in the following weeks. Consequently, urea selling volumes amounted to 470 thousand tons during 3Q26, up 30.8% year-over-year and more than double 2Q26 volumes - mainly reflecting the typical seasonality of the business. Our average selling price stood at $511/ton in 3Q26, while on a year-to-date basis it reached $561/ton, 16.9% higher year-over-year, reflecting our ability to maximize sales during the peak prices.
As a result, Adjusted EBITDA totaled $109.3 million during 3Q26, 7.4% higher than the same period of last year, fully explained by the year-over-year increase in sales. On an accumulated basis, Adjusted EBITDA amounted to $283.1 million in 9M26, marking a 64.9% year-over-year increase. Along with higher sales, results also benefited from greater cost dilution due to the aforementioned increase in production coupled with other costs efficiencies achieved, driving margin expansion.

Adjusted EBITDA & Adjusted EBIT
Adjusted Consolidated EBITDA equals the sum of our Adjusted Segment EBITDA for each of our operating segments.
We define “Adjusted Consolidated EBITDA” as (i) consolidated net profit (loss) for the year, as applicable, before interest expense, income taxes, depreciation of property, plant and equipment and amortization of intangible assets, net gain or loss from fair value adjustments of investment property land, foreign exchange gains or losses, other net financial results and bargain purchase gain on acquisition and any charges related to impairments (ii) adjusted by those items, that do not impact profit and loss, but are recorded directly in shareholders’ equity, including (a) the gains or losses from disposals of noncontrolling interests in subsidiaries whose main underlying asset is farmland, reflected under the line item: "Reserve from the sale of noncontrolling interests in subsidiaries” and (b) the net increase in value of sold farmland, which has been recognized in either revaluation surplus or retained earnings; and (iii) net of the combined effect of the application of IAS 29 and IAS 21 from the Argentine operations included in profit from operations.
We believe that Adjusted Consolidated EBITDA and Adjusted Segment EBITDA are important measures of operating performance for our company and each operating segment, respectively, because they allow investors to evaluate and compare our consolidated operating results and to evaluate and compare the operating performance of our segments, respectively, including our return on capital and operating efficiencies, from period to period by removing the impact of our capital structure (interest expense from our outstanding debt), asset base (depreciation and amortization), tax consequences (income taxes), bargain purchase gain, any charges related to impairments, foreign exchange gains or losses and other financial results. In addition, by including the gains or losses from disposals of noncontrolling interests in subsidiaries whose main underlying asset is farmland, investors can also evaluate and compare the full value and returns generated by our land transformation activities. Other companies may calculate Adjusted Consolidated EBITDA and Adjusted Segment EBITDA differently, and therefore our Adjusted Consolidated EBITDA and Adjusted Segment EBITDA may not be comparable to similar measures used by other companies. Adjusted Consolidated EBITDA and Adjusted Segment EBITDA are not measures of financial performance under IFRS, and should not be considered in isolation or as an alternative to consolidated net profit (loss), cash flows from operating activities, segment profit from operations and other measures determined in accordance with IFRS. Items excluded from Adjusted Consolidated EBITDA and Adjusted Segment EBITDA are significant and necessary components to the operations of our business, and, therefore, Adjusted Consolidated EBITDA and Adjusted Segment EBITDA should only be used as a supplemental measure of our company’s operating performance, and of each of our operating segments, respectively. We also believe Adjusted Consolidated EBITDA and Adjusted Segment EBITDA are useful for securities analysts, investors and others to evaluate and compare the financial performance of our company and other companies in the agricultural industry.
These non-IFRS measures should be considered in addition to, but not as a substitute for or superior to, the information contained in either our statements of income or segment information.

Net Debt & Net Debt to Adjusted EBITDA
Net debt is defined as the sum of non-current and current borrowings less cash and cash equivalents and short-term investments. This measure is widely used by management. Management is consistently tracking our leverage position and our ability to repay and service our debt obligations over time. We have therefore set a leverage ratio target that is measured by net debt divided by Adjusted Consolidated EBITDA.
We believe that the ratio net debt to Adjusted Consolidated EBITDA provides useful information to investors because management uses it to manage our debt-equity ratio in order to promote access to capital markets and our ability to meet scheduled debt service obligations.
Adjusted Net Income
We define Adjusted Net Income as (i) profit/(loss) of the period/year before net gain/(losses) from fair value adjustments of investment property land, bargain purchase gain on acquisition and any impairment; plus (ii) any non-cash finance costs resulting from foreign exchange gain/losses for such period, which are composed by both exchange differences and cash flow hedge transfer from equity, included in Financial Results, net, in our statement of income; net of the related income tax effects, plus (iii) gains or losses from disposals of non-controlling interests in subsidiaries whose main underlying asset is farmland, which are reflected in our shareholders’ equity under the line item “Reserve from the sale of non-controlling interests in subsidiaries” if any, plus (iv) the reversal of the aforementioned income tax effect, plus (v) inflation accounting effect; plus (vi) the net increase in value of sold farmland, which has been recognized in either revaluation surplus or retained earnings, if any.
We believe that Adjusted Net Income is an important measure of performance for our company allowing investors to properly assess the impact of the results of our operations in our equity. In fact, results arising from the revaluation effect of our net monetary position held in foreign currency in the countries where our functional currency is the local currency do not affect the equity of the Company, when measured in foreign / reporting currency. Conversely, the tax effect resulting from the aforementioned revaluation effect does impact the equity of the Company, since it reduces/increases the income tax to be paid in each country. Accordingly we have added back the income tax effect to Adjusted Net Income.
In addition, by including the gains or losses from disposals of non-controlling interests in subsidiaries whose main underlying asset is farmland, investors can also include the full value and returns generated by our land transformation activities.
Other companies may calculate Adjusted Net Income differently, and therefore our Adjusted Net Income may not be comparable to similar measures used by other companies. Adjusted Net Income is not a measure of financial performance under IFRS, and should not be considered in isolation or as an alternative to consolidated net profit (loss). This non-IFRS measure should be considered in addition to, but not as a substitute for or superior to, the information contained in our financial statements.
