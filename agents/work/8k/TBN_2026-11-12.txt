4 Operations Overview Shenandoah South Pilot Project Tamboran 44.375 per cent working interest in 20,239 acres and operator of the Northern Pilot Area(1) In July 2026, Tamboran and DWE (together the Beetaloo Joint Venture (BJV)) completed stimulation, cleanout and completion activities on the SS2-3H, -4H and -5H wells on the SS2 pad. The program was the first three-well zipper stimulation and the largest campaign conducted in the Beetaloo Basin to date, with 178 stages placed across ~30,000 lateral feet within the Mid Velkerri B Shale using the Liberty Energy frac fleet. The campaign averaged 6.7 stages per day and set Beetaloo Basin records, with pumping operations exceeding 20 hours per day and 12 stages completed in a single day. The BJV also placed locally produced Beetaloo Red Sand (BRS) in 10 stages along the lateral of the SS2-5H well, the first deployment of BRS in the basin. Tracers were deployed to compare the performance of these stages against stages using imported sand. Long-duration flow testing of wells on the SS2 pad commenced in September 2026. Initial rates confirm the positive deliverability demonstrated in prior testing of wells in the basin. The SS2-2H ST1 well delivered an average rate of 10.2 MMcf/d over 720 hours of interrupted production across ~5,500 feet of lateral, materially better than the initial 90-day flow period. The well normalized to 18.4 MMcf/d over a 10,000-foot horizontal, in-line with the original SS-1H well, which was flow tested in 2024. The SS2-2H ST1 well demonstrates deliverability potential of a cleaned-up well. The SS2-3H and SS2-5H wells delivered average IP30 rates of 8.8 and 10.0 MMcf/d, respectively, and both continue to produce 300 to 500 barrels of water per day, as the wells were tied into the SPCF immediately following the stimulation campaign earlier this year. Both wells are sustaining relatively high calculated bottomhole pressures, which suggests further deliverability to come as the wells clean up. Observations from subsurface work indicate that well delivery materially increases as water used in the stimulation program is recovered or imbibes into the formation over time. Based on these initial results, Netherland, Sewell & Associates, or NSAI, has estimated a P50 EUR of 17 Bcf per 10,000-foot lateral. The SS2-5H well included 10 stages stimulated with BRS. Early performance shows no degradation in deliverability from using local sand, providing confidence in its deployment in future wells. The BJV plans to further test BRS on at least one well during the SS1 pad stimulation program.
