FedEx Freight Reports Solid Financial Results for the
Four-Month Transition Period Ended September 30, 2026
Delivered Solid Financial Performance During Inaugural Period as an Independent Company
Achieved Second Consecutive Quarter of Record-Low Claims Ratio, Reflecting Improved Customer Experience
Introduces Positive Outlook for Three Months Ending December 31, 2026
MEMPHIS, Tenn. – October 29, 2026 – FedEx Freight (NYSE: FDXF) today reported financial results for the
four-month transition period ended September 30, 2026.
“Our first four months as a standalone company reflect solid execution across the organization and meaningful
progress against the clear strategic priorities we outlined earlier this year,” said John Smith, president and chief
executive officer, FedEx Freight. “While we are in the early stages of our journey as an independent company,
we are encouraged by the momentum we are seeing across our operational, commercial, and technology
initiatives. Guided by a clear and consistent strategy, we remain confident in the opportunities ahead and our
ability to continue strengthening the business for long-term success.”
Transition to Calendar Year Reporting
This reporting period represents a one-time four-month transition period as the company moves toward a
calendar-year reporting cycle. Results include the month of June and the three months ended September 30,
2026. To support year-over-year comparability, FedEx Freight has published calendarized historical results in an
accompanying Form 8-K and Stat Book that can be found at ir.fedexfreight.com, providing a consistent quarterly
view for year-to-date 2026.

Four Months Ended September 30, 2026 Financial Summary
FedEx Freight revenue increased 9.2% year-over-year for the four months ended September 30, 2026. The
increase was primarily driven by the favorable impact of fuel surcharge revenue, higher weight per shipment, an
additional operating day, and an increase in base revenue per hundredweight. These factors were partially offset
by softer volumes.
FedEx Freight adjusted operating income increased 16.4% year-over-year for the four months ended September
30, 2026. The increase was primarily driven by higher revenue, partially offset by performance-based
2
compensation and merit-related expenses, Transition Service Agreement (TSA) costs as a result of the spin-off,
and planned higher costs to operate the standalone business, primarily related to information technology.
“The operational, commercial, and technology progress we achieved during the period is beginning to translate
into tangible financial results,” said Marshall Witt, chief financial officer, FedEx Freight. “Underlying
performance was largely in line with our expectations as our initiatives are strengthening the foundation for
improved growth and profitability. We have updated our transition period outlook to reflect fuel tailwinds and
slightly higher volume assumptions, and we remain confident in the base business as we approach 2027.”
Capital Allocation Priorities
FedEx Freight maintains a disciplined approach to capital allocation, prioritizing an investment-grade balance
sheet, steady investment in the business, and returning capital to shareholders. As of September 30, 2026, FedEx
Freight had $388 million of cash and cash equivalents and total liquidity of $1.6 billion, supporting financial
flexibility to execute its strategic priorities. During the period, the company repaid $100 million of outstanding debt
ahead of schedule with cash generated from operations.
Capital expenditures for the four months ended September 30, 2026 were $151 million, and the company expects
aggregate capital expenditures for the Transition Period (June 1 through December 31, 2026) to total
approximately $365 million. Capital expenditures will be focused on investments in equipment, technology, and
facilities that support safety, service, and operational efficiency.
The company remains committed to returning capital to shareholders and is on track to announce its dividend and
share repurchase plan for calendar year 2027, subject to board approval.
Outlook
FedEx Freight is unable to forecast the mark-to-market (MTM) retirement plan accounting adjustments for the
Transition Period. As a result, FedEx Freight is unable to provide a Transition Period earnings per share (EPS) or
effective tax rate (ETR) outlook on a GAAP basis and is relying on the exemption provided by the Securities and
Exchange Commission (SEC). It is reasonably possible that the Transition Period MTM retirement plan
accounting adjustments could have a material effect on the Transition Period consolidated financial results and
ETR.

Corporate Overview
FedEx Freight is North America’s largest less-than-truckload (LTL) carrier, delivering industry-leading published
transit times, service levels, and reliability. FedEx Freight’s service offerings — including Priority, Economy, and
Direct — allow customers to balance speed and cost to meet their unique needs. FedEx Custom Critical, a
subsidiary, provides expedited, time- and temperature-specific freight solutions, including Surface Expedite and
White Glove Services, available 24/7/365. With nearly 30,000 vehicles, of which nearly 17,000 are tractors, and
40,000 dedicated team members to support our network of over 365 locations, we ensure freight arrives safely,
securely, and on time across all 50 U.S. states, Canada, Mexico, Puerto Rico, and the U.S. Virgin Islands. FedEx
Freight leverages operational efficiency, data-driven technology, and a focused sales organization to provide
outstanding service. To learn more, visit www.fedexfreight.com.

Non-GAAP Financial Measures
FedEx Freight reports its results in accordance with accounting principles generally accepted in the United States
(GAAP). FedEx Freight has supplemented these GAAP results and its outlook with certain non-GAAP (adjusted)
financial measures, including adjusted operating income, adjusted operating margin, adjusted diluted earnings per
share, revenue per shipment excluding fuel surcharges, and revenue per hundredweight excluding fuel
surcharges.
Management uses these non-GAAP measures in making financial, operating, and planning decisions and
evaluating the company’s ongoing performance. These non-GAAP financial measures are intended to
supplement, and should be read together with, FedEx Freight’s GAAP results. These supplemental non-GAAP
financial measures should not be considered a substitute for, or superior to, the most directly comparable GAAP
measures. Accordingly, users of our financial statements should not place undue reliance on these non-GAAP
financial measures. Because non-GAAP measures are not standardized, they may not be comparable to similarly
titled measures presented by other companies. As required by SEC rules, reconciliations of the supplemental non-
GAAP financial measures to the most directly comparable GAAP measures are presented below.

3
Non-GAAP Financial Measures
FedEx Freight reports its financial results in accordance with accounting principles generally accepted in the United States
(“GAAP”). In addition to the financial information presented herein in accordance with GAAP, FedEx Freight presents
certain non-GAAP financial measures, including revenue per shipment excluding fuel surcharges and revenue per
hundredweight excluding fuel surcharges.
These measures exclude fuel surcharge revenue which is largely driven by fluctuations in fuel prices and may obscure
underlying pricing trends. Management believes these supplemental measures provide investors with useful information
regarding underlying yield performance and facilitate meaningful comparisons of pricing trends between periods by
removing the impact of fluctuations in fuel surcharge revenue. Management uses these non-GAAP financial measures in
making financial, operating, and planning decisions and evaluating the Company’s ongoing performance.
These non-GAAP financial measures are intended to supplement, and should be read together with, FedEx Freight’s
GAAP results. These supplemental non-GAAP financial measures should not be considered a substitute for, or superior
to, the most directly comparable GAAP measures. Because non-GAAP measures are not standardized, they may not be
comparable to similarly titled measures presented by other companies. As required by Securities and Exchange
Commission rules, reconciliations of the supplemental non-GAAP financial measures to the most directly comparable
GAAP measures are presented below.
