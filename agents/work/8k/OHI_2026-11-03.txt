PRESS RELEASE – FOR IMMEDIATE RELEASE
OMEGA REPORTS THIRD QUARTER 2026 RESULTS
AND RECENT DEVELOPMENTS
Completed $1.5 Billion in New Investments Year-to-Date, Including $946 Million Since Quarter-End
Raises Full Year 2026 Guidance
HUNT VALLEY, MARYLAND – November 3, 2026 – Omega Healthcare Investors, Inc. (NYSE: OHI) (the “Company” or “Omega”) announced today its results for the quarter ended September 30, 2026.
THIRD QUARTER 2026 AND RECENT HIGHLIGHTS
● | Net income for the quarter of $87 million, or $0.27 per diluted share.

● | Adjusted Funds From Operations (“Adjusted FFO” or “AFFO”) for the quarter of $262 million, or $0.82 per diluted share, on 321 million weighted-average common shares outstanding, compared to $243 million, or $0.79 per diluted share, on 308 million weighted-average common shares outstanding, for the third quarter of 2025.

● | Funds Available for Distribution (“FAD”) for the quarter of $250 million, or $0.78 per diluted share, compared to FAD of $231 million, or $0.75 per diluted share, for the third quarter of 2025.

● | Net Operating Income (“NOI”) for the quarter of $332 million, compared to NOI of $310 million for the third quarter of 2025.

● | Completed $182 million of investments during the third quarter and $946 million since quarter-end, bringing total 2026 investment activity to approximately $1.5 billion.

● | Converted two U.K. triple-net operator relationships covering 30 care homes to RIDEA structures for a payment of $48 million.

● | Issued 4.7 million common shares in the quarter for gross proceeds of $234 million.

● | In October 2026, transitioned 32 Florida skilled nursing facilities (“SNFs”) to PACS Group, Inc. (NYSE: PACS) (“PACS”) for payment of a $90 million termination fee to the prior operator, increasing annual contractual rent on the facilities by $26.1 million in the first year.

● | In October 2026, the Genesis Healthcare, Inc. (“Genesis”) bankruptcy sale was finalized, with Omega’s lease assumed and assigned to the buyer and the DIP and term loans repaid.

Nareit Funds From Operations (“Nareit FFO”), AFFO, FAD and NOI are supplemental non-GAAP financial measures the Company believes are useful in evaluating the performance of real estate investment trusts (“REITs”). Reconciliations and further information regarding these non-GAAP measures are provided at the end of this press release.
CEO COMMENTS
Matthew Gourmand, Omega’s Chief Executive Officer, stated, “We are pleased to report strong third quarter results, as we continued to grow FAD per share year over year, while reducing leverage to the lowest level in the Company’s history. As a result, we were again able to increase our AFFO guidance, moving the midpoint up by five cents to $3.29.”
​
Mr. Gourmand continued, “The investments made in the third and early fourth quarter highlight the breadth of opportunities available to us to continue to grow shareholder value. The pipeline remains strong, and we expect to make further accretive investments through the remainder of this year and into early 2027.”
​
Mr. Gourmand concluded, “Importantly, the backdrop that has allowed us to deliver strong shareholder returns over the past few years remains in place. Operating metrics continue to be robust, with operator coverage further improving in the quarter, and we have a strong balance sheet and cost of capital that should allow us to continue to accretively invest.”
THIRD QUARTER 2026 PORTFOLIO AND RECENT ACTIVITY
Operator Updates:
PACS – On October 1, 2026, the Company transitioned 32 SNFs in Florida from Airamid Health Services, LLC (“Airamid”) to PACS and added the facilities to the Company’s existing PACS master lease. Initial annual contractual rent attributable to the facilities is $58.0 million, an increase of $26.1 million from the prior annual contractual rent on the portfolio. Annual contractual rent increases by an additional $2.5 million in year two, with 2% annual escalators thereafter through the lease’s January 31, 2038 maturity. The Company also paid Airamid a $90.0 million lease termination fee in connection with the transition, which was capitalized and will be amortized over the remaining term of the PACS lease.
Genesis – In October 2026, the Genesis Chapter 11 bankruptcy sale was finalized, resulting in the assumption and assignment of Omega’s master lease to affiliates of 101 W State Street Holdings, LLC (“NewGen”). In connection with the bankruptcy finalization, Omega received $149.5 million during October 2026 related to repayments of amounts outstanding under its DIP and term loans. During the third quarter of 2026, Omega recognized $13.3 million of rental income and $4.8 million of interest income related to Genesis.
Maplewood – In the third quarter of 2026, Maplewood Senior Living (along with affiliates “Maplewood”) paid $20.1 million in rent (compared to $19.6 million in the second quarter of 2026).

$167 Million in Real Estate Acquisitions – In the third quarter of 2026, the Company acquired 17 facilities for $167.3 million, comprised of:
● | $73 Million Triple-Net U.S. Real Estate Acquisition – In July 2026, the Company acquired six SNFs in Texas for $73.4 million and leased them to a new operator.

● | $78 Million Operating Portfolio U.S. Real Estate Acquisition – In September 2026, the Company acquired five senior housing communities for $77.7 million, including $57.2 million of cash consideration and $20.5 million of existing loans from the Company to the sellers that were satisfied through their application toward the purchase price rather than repayment. The Company will operate the communities through an existing third-party property manager under a RIDEA structure with expected stabilized yields in the low double digits.

● | $16 Million Triple-Net U.K. Real Estate Acquisition – In September 2026, the Company acquired six care homes in the U.K. for $16.2 million and leased them to an existing operator.

$14 Million in Real Estate Loans – In the third quarter of 2026, the Company funded $14.2 million in real estate loans, including $12.8 million (CAD 18.0 million) of additional funding under a previously disclosed Canadian development loan.
$946 Million in Q4 2026 Investment Activity – From October 1, 2026 through the date of this release, the Company completed $945.5 million of new investments, comprised of:
● | $846 Million U.K. Mortgage Loan – In November 2026, the Company funded a £631.7 million ($845.7 million) GBP-denominated mortgage loan to an affiliate of Foundation Partners Asset Management Limited (“Foundation”), an existing operator, secured by 48 U.K. care homes and leasehold interests in 7 additional care homes (collectively, the “Runwood Portfolio”). The loan bears interest at 8.72%, increasing to 8.94% in year two and matures in November 2031. The mortgage loan was funded using balance sheet cash, capital recycled from loan repayments and borrowings under the Company’s unsecured revolving credit facility. The Company has a purchase option on the underlying real estate and leasehold interests that becomes exercisable following the completion of customary regulatory conditions. The Company expects to exercise the option when available with no incremental consideration required and, upon acquisition, enter into a long-term lease with Foundation covering the acquired real estate and leasehold interests. The lease is expected to provide an initial cash yield consistent with the mortgage loan interest rate, with opportunities for additional upside over the term of the lease.

● | $49 Million Operating Portfolio U.S. Real Estate Acquisitions – In two October 2026 acquisitions, the Company acquired five senior housing communities in Texas, Florida and South Carolina for $49.3 million. The communities will be operated by two third-party managers under RIDEA structures, including one existing and one new manager relationship, with expected stabilized yields in the low double digits.

● | $51 Million Triple-Net U.S. Real Estate Acquisition – In November 2026, the Company acquired a continuing care retirement community in Georgia for a contractual purchase price of $50.5 million that will be added to an existing operator’s master lease.

Other Portfolio Activity:
$48 Million in U.K. RIDEA Conversions – During the third quarter of 2026, the Company acquired the operators of 30 U.K. care homes for £35.8 million ($48.0 million) in cash and converted the facilities from triple-net leases to RIDEA structures, which will be managed by three established U.K. operators with whom the Company has existing relationships. Annual contractual rent associated with the 30 U.K. care homes prior to the conversion was £20.1 million ($26.7 million) with an EBITDAR coverage ratio of 1.47x for the trailing twelve months ended June 30, 2026. Substantially all of the purchase consideration represented payments to terminate the Company’s preexisting leases with the operators. These amounts, together with related transaction expenses, were recorded as acquisition, merger and transition related costs.
Saber PropCo JV – $88 Million in Real Estate Investments – In July 2026, SHH Holdings, LLC (“Saber PropCo JV”), a property holding company in which Omega owns a 49% equity interest, completed the acquisition of five SNFs for $87.7 million as part of the Ciena Laurels portfolio transactions previously disclosed in the second quarter of 2026. As of September 30, 2026, Saber PropCo JV owned 71 facilities leased to Saber Healthcare Holdings, LLC under triple-net leases generating annual contractual rent of $83.1 million and had $584.4 million of mortgage debt outstanding with a weighted-average interest rate of 5.2%.
Held for Sale and Impairments:
Held for Sale – At September 30, 2026, one care home with a net book value of $1.3 million was classified as held for sale.
Impairments – In the third quarter of 2026, the Company recorded an $11.4 million net impairment charge related to one facility.

BALANCE SHEET AND LIQUIDITY
As of September 30, 2026, the Company had $4.1 billion in outstanding indebtedness with a weighted average annual interest rate of 4.2%. The Company’s indebtedness consisted of an aggregate principal amount of $3.8 billion of senior unsecured notes and a $300.0 million term loan. As of September 30, 2026, total cash and cash equivalents were $195.9 million, and the Company had $2.0 billion in undrawn capacity under its unsecured revolving credit facility.
DIVIDENDS
On October 26, 2026, the Board of Directors declared a quarterly cash dividend of $0.68 per share, to be paid November 16, 2026, to common stockholders of record as of the close of business on November 5, 2026.
2026 GUIDANCE INCREASED
The Company’s expected 2026 Adjusted FFO range is $3.28 to $3.30 per diluted share compared to the previous range of $3.22 to $3.26 per diluted share.
The guidance assumes:
● | all portfolio activity, including new investments outlined above in the press release;

● | no additional operators are placed on a cash-basis for revenue recognition;

● | NewGen pays its full contractual rental obligations of $13.3 million per quarter following its assumption of Omega’s lease which will equate to approximately $14.0 million in straight-line revenue per quarter;

● | Maplewood continues to pay monthly rent of $6.9 million, consistent with September 2026;

● | quarterly G&A expense of approximately $14 million to $15.5 million;

● | no material changes in market interest rates or changes in foreign currency exchange rates, including those due to derivative instruments entered into to minimize the fluctuation in the GBP spot rates;

● | quarterly dividends of $0.68 per share;

● | $306 million of loans outstanding at September 30, 2026 will be repaid in the fourth quarter (including $150 million of loans to Genesis that were repaid in October 2026); and

● | asset sales of approximately $15 million to $25 million in the fourth quarter.

The Company’s guidance is based on several assumptions including those noted above, which are subject to change and many of which are outside the Company’s control. However, it excludes any additional:
● | acquisitions or other portfolio activities and related costs;

● | capital markets activity;

● | interest refinancing expenses;

● | provisions for credit losses, if any; and

● | certain revenue and expense items.

If actual results vary from these assumptions, the Company’s expectations may change. Without limiting the generality of the foregoing, the timing of collection of rental obligations from operators on a cash basis and the timing and completion of acquisitions, divestitures, restructurings and capital and financing transactions may cause actual results to vary materially from the Company’s current expectations. There can be no assurance that the Company will achieve its projected results. The Company may, from time to time, update its publicly announced AFFO guidance, but it is not obligated to do so.
The Company does not provide a reconciliation for its AFFO guidance to GAAP net income because it is unable to determine meaningful or accurate estimates of reconciling items without unreasonable effort. This is due to the inherent difficulty of forecasting the timing and/or amounts of various items that would impact future net income. This includes, but is not limited to, changes in the provision for credit losses, real estate impairments, acquisition, merger and transition related costs, straight-line write-offs, gain/loss on assets sold, etc. In particular, the Company is unable to predict with reasonable certainty the amount of change in the provision for credit losses in future periods, which is often a significant reconciling adjustment.

NON-GAAP FINANCIAL MEASURES
Nareit Funds From Operations (“Nareit FFO”), Adjusted FFO, Funds Available for Distribution (“FAD”) and Net Operating Income (“NOI”) are non-GAAP financial measures. As used in this press release, GAAP refers to generally accepted accounting principles in the United States of America. The Company has provided reconciliations of the non-GAAP financial measures to the most directly comparable GAAP financial measures in the tables titled “Nareit FFO, Adjusted FFO and FAD Reconciliation” and “NOI Reconciliation.”
NAREIT FFO – The Company calculates and reports Nareit FFO in accordance with the definition and interpretive guidelines issued by the National Association of Real Estate Investment Trusts (“Nareit”), and consequently, Nareit FFO is defined as net income (computed in accordance with GAAP), adjusted for the effects of asset dispositions and certain non-cash items, primarily depreciation and amortization and impairments on real estate assets, and after adjustments for unconsolidated partnerships and joint ventures and changes in the fair value of warrants. Adjustments for unconsolidated partnerships and joint ventures will be calculated to reflect funds from operations on the same basis. Revenue recognized based on the application of security deposits and letters of credit or based on the ability to offset against other financial instruments is included within Nareit FFO. The Company believes that Nareit FFO, Adjusted FFO and FAD are important supplemental measures of its operating performance. Because the historical cost accounting convention used for real estate assets requires depreciation (except on land), such accounting presentation implies that the value of real estate assets diminishes predictably over time, while real estate values instead have historically risen or fallen with market conditions. The term funds from operations was designed by the real estate industry to address this issue. Funds from operations described herein is not necessarily comparable to funds from operations of other real estate investment trusts, or REITs, that do not use the same definition or implementation guidelines or interpret the standards differently from the Company.
Adjusted FFO and FAD – Adjusted FFO is calculated as Nareit FFO excluding the impact of non-cash stock-based compensation and certain revenue and expense items (e.g., acquisition, merger and transition related costs, straight-line rent and other write-offs, recoveries and provisions for credit losses (excluding certain cash recoveries on impaired loans), severance expense, loss on extinguishment of debt and other normalizing items). FAD is calculated as Adjusted FFO less non-cash expense, such as the amortization of deferred financing costs, and non-cash revenue, such as straight-line rent. FAD includes the non-cash amortization of premiums associated with the fair value of debt assumed in acquisitions. The Company believes these measures provide an enhanced measure of the operating performance of the Company’s core portfolio as a REIT. The Company’s computation of Adjusted FFO and FAD may not be comparable to the Nareit definition of funds from operations or to similar measures reported by other REITs, but the Company believes that they are appropriate measures for this Company.
NOI – NOI is calculated as total revenues less property-level expenses. The Company believes NOI provides investors relevant and useful information because it measures the operating performance of the Company’s properties at the property level on an unleveraged basis. NOI also includes the Company’s pro rata share of NOI from its unconsolidated entities. We utilize our share of NOI in assessing our performance as we have various unconsolidated entities that contribute to our performance. Our share of NOI should not be considered a substitute for, and should only be considered together with and as a supplement to, our financial information presented in accordance with GAAP. Our pro rata share information is prepared on a basis consistent with the comparable consolidated amounts, is intended to reflect our proportionate economic interest in the operating results of properties in our portfolio and is calculated by applying our actual ownership percentage for the period. We do not control the unconsolidated entities, and the pro rata presentations of reconciling items included in NOI do not represent our legal claim to such items. The unconsolidated entities’ members or partners are entitled to profit or loss allocations and distributions of cash flows according to the entity agreements, which provide for such allocations generally according to their invested capital.
Use and Limitations – The Company uses these non-GAAP measures among the criteria to measure the operating performance of its business. The Company also uses FAD among the performance metrics for performance-based compensation of officers. The Company further believes that by excluding the effect of depreciation, amortization, impairments on real estate assets and gains or losses from sales of real estate, all of which are based on historical costs, and which may be of limited relevance in evaluating current performance, funds from operations can facilitate comparisons of operating performance between periods. The Company offers these measures to assist the users of its financial statements in analyzing its operating performance. These non-GAAP measures are not measures of financial performance under GAAP and should not be considered as measures of liquidity or cash flow, alternatives to net income or indicators of any other performance measure determined in accordance with GAAP. Investors and potential investors in the Company’s securities should not rely on these non-GAAP measures as substitutes for any GAAP measure, including net income.
