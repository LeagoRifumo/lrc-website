HEI REPORTS THIRD QUARTER 2026 RESULTS
•Strong and Effective Response to Elevated Storm Activity During the Quarter
◦Power Restored to Customers Quickly and Safely Following Storms
◦Grid Resilience investments Continuing to Reduce Wildfire Risk While Delivering Value for Customers In Face of Severe Weather
•Consolidated GAAP Net Income for the Quarter of $35 Million Compared to $31 Million in 2025
◦Core1 Net Income for the Quarter Was $23 Million Compared to $33 Million in 2025
◦Consolidated GAAP Net Income Includes Gains from American Savings Bank’s Initial Public Offering, Partially Offset by Non-Cash Impacts Related to HEI’s Strategic Business Simplification and Non-Cash Impacts Related to Maui Wildfire Settlement Liability Accounting Adjustments
◦GAAP and Core Results Also Reflect $14 Million of Incremental Pre-Tax Storm-Related Costs; Utility Will Be Requesting Recovery of Storm Costs Once Final Incremental Costs Are Known.
•$80 Million of Second Settlement Payment Prepaid To Date, Reducing the $479 Million Amount Due by $82 Million (After Early-Payment Discount). Company Will Continue to Evaluate Early Payments of Wildfire Settlement Liability
HONOLULU - Hawaiian Electric Industries, Inc. (NYSE - HE) (HEI) today reported net income for the third quarter of 2026 of $35 million, or $0.20 per share, compared to net income of $31 million, or $0.18 per share in the third quarter of 2025. The quarter’s results include $64

“Our company continued to execute well on strategic priorities during the third quarter, working to deliver safe, affordable, reliable and resilient power to our customers. Our resilience investments under the Wildfire Mitigation Plan proved beneficial during the severe weather in August and September, which caused damage statewide. Our crews restored power quickly and safely to affected customers,” said Scott Seu, HEI CEO.
“Reducing wildfire risk to our communities remains a top priority and is central to our grid investments. Our Wildfire Mitigation Plan is on schedule, and has significantly reduced wildfire risk across our service territories since 2023. We’ve implemented industry-wide best practices, adopting proven measures used by wildfire-affected utilities on the mainland, and tailored to our very different risk profile.
“Maintaining the financial capacity to deliver a safe, reliable and resilient grid is essential. We also recognize customers’ significant cost pressures, especially for low-income customers, and are focused on financing and phasing investments to manage bill impacts. Our pending rate rebasing request proposes a 5.3% base rate increase, phased in over two years and aligned with inflation, to support continued grid investment. We also recently sought approval for up to $500 million in wildfire safety and resilience investments through a structure passed by our legislature last year to lower customer costs while advancing public safety.”
HAWAIIAN ELECTRIC COMPANY (HAWAIIAN ELECTRIC) EARNINGS
Hawaiian Electric’s net income for the third quarter of 2026 was $13 million compared to net income of $37 million in the third quarter of 2025, with the decrease primarily driven by the following pre-tax variances (among others):
•$20 million in higher O&M expense, driven by $14 million of incremental storm costs in August and September and approximately $6 million of expenses transferred from the

holding company to the utility following integration (but not included in customer rates)2. These were partially offset by lower transmission and distribution costs and lower wildfire mitigation program expenses (as certain Wildfire Mitigation Program expenses were deferred starting in the fourth quarter of 2025); and
•$23 million in higher interest expense, which includes $18 million of non-cash accretion expense, reflecting the increase in the recorded value of the wildfire settlement liability as scheduled payment dates approach.
These items were partially offset by (among others):
•$8 million in higher revenues (net of fuel and purchased power), primarily from the annual revenue adjustment mechanism; and
•$3 million in higher allowance for funds used during construction (AFUDC).
Hawaiian Electric’s Core net income, which excludes wildfire-related expenses (primarily the non-cash accretion expense), was $28 million for the quarter compared to $40 million in the third quarter of 2025, with the decrease primarily driven by higher O&M expense due to the aforementioned storm costs and HEI integration costs.
HOLDING AND OTHER COMPANIES
The holding and other companies’ net income was $22 million in the third quarter of 2026 compared to a net loss of $6 million in the third quarter of 2025. The holding company’s net income for the quarter primarily reflects the pre-tax $18 million gain on sale of ASB shares, and the pre-tax $46 million unrealized gains on the remaining ASB shares owned. As previously disclosed, HEI sold 35% of its 9.9% stake in ASB’s initial public offering. HEI’s remaining stake in ASB is approximately 6.4% of ASB’s total shares outstanding, and this remaining stake was marked-to-market based on ASB’s closing share price at the end of the third quarter. Benefits from HEI’s stake in ASB were partially offset by a pre-tax $51 million asset impairment recorded at Pacific Current related to exiting Pacific Current’s last remaining asset, the Mahipapa biomass plant on Kauai. HEI is in the final stages of its strategic exit from the Pacific Current business.
