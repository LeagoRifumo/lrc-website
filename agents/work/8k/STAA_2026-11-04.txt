Advancing the Future of Vision with Product Innovation

STAAR’s lens-based vision correction leadership is built on the differentiated capabilities of EVO ICL and our proprietary Collamer® technology. As the global leader in phakic IOLs and the manufacturer of the most clinically studied phakic IOL, with more than 30 years of clinical evidence, over 100,000 eyes studied, and 4+ million lenses distributed worldwide, STAAR has set the standard in cornea-preserving vision correction. As our label harmonization efforts continue to broaden access, our commercial priority is to convert expanded eligibility into adoption.

The FoRS report confirmed what we have seen in our own results: the refractive market is shifting, and EVO ICL is driving that shift. Our job now is to accelerate the shift. We are making changes to how we operate commercially, sharpening our global model, strengthening local execution, and removing the barriers that slow surgeons and patients from choosing EVO.

Delivering with Excellence with Operational Innovation
We are focused on achieving advancements in manufacturing and supply chain management to support continued profit expansion.
In the third quarter, we completed the operational transition to allow for all China-bound ICLs to be manufactured in Switzerland, an initiative designed to improve gross margin by reducing U.S.-China tariff-exposure while strengthening supply chain resilience. Our ability to scale Swiss production was further enhanced by recently receiving DEKRA certification of a second clean room at our Nidau facility.
Additional efforts underway include targeted initiatives to improve manufacturing yields and expand capacity while maintaining the rigorous quality standards required to produce Collamer. We are expanding machining capacity at our Monrovia facility and evaluating automation opportunities throughout our operations. We estimate that each percentage point improvement in manufacturing yield could generate approximately $750,000 to $1 million in annual incremental gross profit.
This year, we implemented a new enterprise resource planning (ERP) system, and we ramped up production to support the launch of EVO+ in China, where demand was stronger than anticipated. In addition, we are strengthening our supply chain planning and inventory management capabilities.
Over the past four quarters, we made steady progress in reducing distributor inventory in China, which ended the third quarter below our six-month target. Inventory decreased

China: Continued Growth in Our Largest Market
What we see in China, our largest market, reinforces what we see globally. EVO ICL is gaining share, and the shift from laser to lens-based correction is accelerating.
Through the first nine months of 2026, unit volume shipments by our distributors in China grew 12.4%, a meaningful step up from the mid-single-digit growth in 2025. Year-over-year growth so far in 2026 has come from share gains, with in-market volume again outpacing the broader Chinese refractive market. Because of the mix shift to EVO+ and a higher percentage of toric lenses, the average selling price by our distributors of our lenses in China has risen substantially.
China sales grew 31% year over year excluding $25.9 million of previously deferred sales recognized in the third quarter of 2025. The deferred China sales related to an order placed and shipped in 2024. It was recognized in the third quarter of 2025 with no corresponding cost of sales, increasing sales and gross profit margin in the prior-year period and affecting year-over-year comparability.
Growth in China sales was led by performance in public hospitals. We are encouraged that hundreds of mid-tier hospitals have begun to close the gap with our largest and longest-established centers, which continue to set the benchmark for ICL productivity. In fact, most of our incremental volume increases came from rising procedures per hospital in the mid-tier category. These hospitals still perform a fraction of the procedures our leading centers do, so we are early in this effort with considerable opportunities to increase volumes. We also saw lower-diopter procedures grow faster than higher-diopter procedures, an encouraging signal for our strategy to move EVO ICL down the diopter curve, where the addressable patient population is substantially larger.
The increasing adoption of EVO+ and toric relative to EVO and spherical and the outperformance of public hospital channels are still in their early phases but are creating tailwinds that we believe will allow us to realize materially higher average selling prices with further opportunity to increase the mix of EVO+ in China. Because we were not yet fully able to meet lens demand during the third quarter, all of our sales increase in China during the quarter was attributable to increased average selling prices.

During his earlier tenure with STAAR, Pascal spearheaded our expansion across Asia and the Middle East, and his expertise will be vital as we capture the significant opportunity across APAC.
EMEA net sales increased 31% year-over-year, or 51% excluding the Middle East. We saw strong double-digit growth in Spain, Germany, the UK, and distributor operations. EMEA’s strong growth benefited from improved conditions and a much smaller decline in revenue in the Middle East compared with the first half. Our commercial efforts have supported this growth by expanding surgeon engagement and strengthening customer relationships. User meetings in France and Poland have broadened our reach among surgeons and provided opportunities to share our clinical evidence, while agreements with key partners in Spain and Germany have deepened important customer relationships.
In the Americas, net sales increased 14% year-over-year, led by $6.5 million in U.S. sales, our third consecutive quarter exceeding $6 million. Data provided by the Refractive Surgery Council suggests that in 2026, laser vision correction procedures have declined 5.8% vs. 2025. Our consumer research reinforces that many patients want freedom from glasses and contacts, and that they value choice, reversibility, confidence, and understanding their treatment options.
This opportunity is reflected in our business performance and treatment patterns in the U.S. Alongside continued adoption among patients with high myopia, we have seen three consecutive quarters of significant growth among patients with less severe myopia, broadening EVO adoption across a wider range of refractive errors. Third Quarter 2026 Financial Results
STAAR delivered third-quarter net sales of $83.4 million, a 12% decrease from $94.7 million in the same period last year. The prior-year quarter included $25.9 million of previously deferred China sales related to an order placed and shipped in 2024 for which revenue recognition was delayed until the third quarter of 2025 in accordance with GAAP. Excluding those sales, prior-year net sales were $68.8 million, and third-quarter 2026 net sales increased 21% against that base.
“Our record nine-month performance keeps us firmly on track to deliver strong full-year GAAP net income with free cash flow and a debt-free balance sheet,” said Deborah Andrews, Executive Vice President and Chief Financial Officer. “Temporary gross margin

pressures are expected to abate beginning in the fourth quarter, while China seasonality should moderate revenue and earnings, but not free cash flow.”
China net sales in the third quarter were $39.3 million, compared with $55.8 million in the prior-year quarter, which included $25.9 million of deferred sales. Excluding the deferred sales, net sales in China grew 31% in the third quarter compared with $29.9 million in the prior-year quarter.
Excluding China, net sales were $44.1 million, an increase of 13% as compared to $38.9 million in the prior-year quarter. Growth outside China reflected increased sales in India, Taiwan, and other APAC markets, along with double-digit percentage growth in the Americas and EMEA. Within APAC, those increases were partially offset by year-over-year declines in Japan, primarily due to currency, and South Korea, due to overall market weakness. In the EMEA region, net sales increased 31% compared with the prior-year quarter, despite ongoing turmoil in the Middle East. Excluding the Middle East, EMEA achieved a 51% increase in net sales during the quarter.
Gross profit margin for the third quarter of 2026 was 70.9% of total net sales, compared to 82.2% in the prior-year quarter. The prior-year quarter included $25.9 million of deferred China revenue recognized with no corresponding cost of sales, resulting in a 100% gross margin on that revenue. This increased third-quarter 2025 gross profit margin by 6.7 percentage points. Excluding that revenue and its associated gross profit, prior-year gross profit margin would have been 75.5%.
Gross profit margin in the third quarter of 2026 also was negatively impacted by several temporary factors, including higher manufacturing costs due to low production volumes in the second quarter of 2026 resulting from the implementation of the Company’s new ERP system, higher freight costs, tariffs on U.S. goods produced for China, and an increase in inventory provisions from expiring inventory.
By the end of the third quarter, manufacturing of all ICLs destined for China had been transitioned to our Swiss facility, which is expected to reduce exposure to tariffs imposed on the U.S.-origin products entering China going forward. U.S. product in inventory and sold to China will continue to be subject to tariffs.
Unit costs will likely stay elevated for the rest of the year as STAAR sells through inventory produced in the second quarter of 2026. However, lower tariffs and smaller inventory provisions are also expected to lift fourth-quarter 2026 gross margins above third-quarter levels. With both production facilities now running at full capacity, unit costs are expected to improve further in 2027.

Excluding restructuring and merger-related costs of $6.0 million in the prior-year quarter, operating expenses were flat compared to the prior-year quarter. STAAR remains on track to hit the $225 million operating expense target set at the start of the year.
General and administrative expenses were $18.9 million, compared with $20.8 million in the prior-year quarter. The decrease reflects a decrease in variable compensation, partially offset by an increase in depreciation expense related to the Company’s new ERP system.
Selling and marketing expenses were $24.3 million, up from $23.5 million in the prior-year quarter. The increase in selling and marketing expenses was due to an increase in consulting and depreciation expenses related to the new ERP system and an increase in advertising and promotional activities, partially offset by a decrease in variable compensation.
Research and development expenses were $10.5 million, up from $9.2 million in the prior-year quarter, primarily reflecting increases in salary-related expenses, expenses related to the new ERP system, and increased professional fees.
Operating income for the third quarter of 2026 was $5.5 million compared to $18.5 million in the prior-year quarter. Prior-year operating income included $25.9 million of gross profit from the recognition of deferred China revenue.
Net income for the third quarter of 2026 was $5.6 million, or $0.11 per diluted share, down from $8.9 million, or $0.18 per diluted share, for the prior-year quarter. Recognition of the deferred China revenue increased prior-year net income by $15.2 million after tax. Excluding that after-tax impact, the prior-year net loss would have been $6.3 million, or $0.13 per diluted share.

Prior-year Adjusted EBITDA1 included the impact of recognizing $25.9 million of deferred China revenue. Excluding that impact, prior-year Adjusted EBITDA1 would have been $8.9 million, or $0.18 per diluted share.

Use of Non-GAAP Financial Measures
To supplement the Company’s financial measures prepared in accordance with U.S. generally accepted accounting principles (GAAP), this press release and the accompanying tables include certain non-GAAP financial measures, including Adjusted EBITDA, Adjusted EBITDA per diluted share and constant currency measures, free cash flow and net income (loss) and related per-share amounts excluding the impact of the China deferred revenue. Management uses these non-GAAP financial measures in its evaluation of Company operating performance and believes investors will find them useful in evaluating the Company’s operating performance, including cash flow generation, and in analyzing period-to-period financial performance of core business operations and underlying business trends. Non-GAAP financial measures are in addition to, not a substitute for, or superior to, measures of financial performance prepared in accordance with GAAP.
EBITDA is a non-GAAP financial measure, which is calculated by adding interest income and expense, net; provision for income taxes; and depreciation and amortization to net income. In calculating Adjusted EBITDA and Adjusted EBITDA per diluted share, the Company further adjusts for stock-based compensation expense, restructuring, impairment and related charges, and commencing with the first quarter ended March 28, 2025, merger transaction and related costs. As stock-based compensation is a non-cash expense that can vary significantly based on the timing, size, and nature of awards granted, the Company believes that the exclusion of stock-based compensation expense can assist investors in comparisons of Company operating results with other peer companies because (i) the amount of such expense in any specific period may not directly correlate to the underlying performance of our business operations and (ii) such expense can vary significantly between periods as a result of the timing of grants of new stock-based awards, including inducement grants in connection with hiring. Additionally, the Company

believes that excluding stock-based compensation from Adjusted EBITDA and Adjusted EBITDA per diluted share assists management and investors in making meaningful comparisons between the Company’s operating performance and the operating performance of other companies that may use different forms of employee compensation or different valuation methodologies for their stock-based compensation. Investors should note that stock-based compensation is a key incentive offered to employees whose efforts contributed to the operating results in the periods presented and are expected to contribute to operating results in future periods. Investors should also note that such expenses will recur in the future. The Company believes that restructuring, impairment and related charges are not indicative of the underlying operating expense profile for the Company. These charges, which include costs related to severance, reduction in force and consulting expenses, impairment expenses on leasehold improvements and machinery and equipment, impairment on real property right-of-use assets, and impairment of internally developed software, are anticipated to be completed within a finite period of time and can vary significantly in any specific period. The Company believes that excluding restructuring, impairment and related charges from Adjusted EBITDA allows investors to analyze period-to-period financial performance of its core business operations more consistently and better assess the Company’s current and future continuing operations. Similarly, the Company believes that merger transaction and related costs are not indicative of the underlying operating expense profile for the Company and that excluding such costs from Adjusted EBITDA allows investors to more consistently analyze period-to-period financial performance of its core business.
To supplement our consolidated financial statements presented in accordance with U.S. generally accepted accounting principles (GAAP), we evaluate our liquidity using Free Cash Flow, which is a non-GAAP financial measure. We define Free Cash Flow as net cash provided by operating activities, as reported on our consolidated statements of cash flows, less purchases of property, plant, and equipment (capital expenditures).
Management believes that Free Cash Flow provides useful information to investors and analysts because it reflects the cash generated by our ongoing core operations that is available to pursue strategic business opportunities, invest in research and development, make acquisitions, repay outstanding debt obligations, pay dividends, and repurchase common stock. In addition, Free Cash Flow is one of the primary financial metrics utilized by our management team and Board of Directors to evaluate liquidity, assess operating performance, and allocate capital resources across our operating segments.
Free Cash Flow has inherent limitations and should be considered in addition to, and not as an alternative or substitute for, net cash provided by operating activities or any other measure of liquidity or operating performance determined in accordance with GAAP.

About STAAR Surgical
STAAR Surgical (NASDAQ: STAA) is the global leader in implantable phakic intraocular lenses, a vision correction solution that reduces or eliminates the need for glasses or contact lenses. Since 1982, STAAR has been dedicated solely to ophthalmic surgery, and for over 30 years, STAAR has been designing, developing, manufacturing, and marketing advanced Implantable Collamer® Lenses (ICLs), using its proprietary biocompatible Collamer material. STAAR ICLs are clinically proven to deliver safe long-term vision correction without removing corneal tissue or the eye's natural crystalline lens. Its EVO ICL™ product line provides visual freedom through a quick, minimally invasive procedure.

Headquartered in Lake Forest, California, the Company operates research, development, manufacturing, and packaging facilities in California and Switzerland.
