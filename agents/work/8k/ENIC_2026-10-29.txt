EXECUTIVE SUMMARY
· | Net income attributable to Enel Chile S.A.’s shareholders reached US$ 390 million as of September 2026, a 10.7% higher than September 2025, mainly due to improved operating performance during the period. On a quarterly basis, net income totaled US$ 118 million in Q3 2026, an US$ 11 million increase compared to Q3 2025, partly due to lower expenses in the financial results.

· | Operating revenues totaled US$ 3,480 million as of September 2026, similar to the level recorded as of September 2025. In Q3 2026, operating revenues grew 0.9% to US$ 1,212 million, mainly due to higher energy sales in the Distribution and Grids Segment.

· | Procurement and services costs totaled US$ 2,146 million as of September 2026, down 0.2% from the same period a year earlier. This was largely due to lower fuel consumption costs in the Generation Segment and lower costs for other variable procurement and services in both the Generation and the Distribution and Grids Segments. In Q3 2026, procurement and services costs rose 3.4% to US$ 768 million, mainly due to higher energy purchase costs across both Segments.

· | As a result of the factors described above, the Company’s EBITDA increased by 1.7% to US$ 1,021 million as of September 2026. In Q3 2026, EBITDA totaled US$ 336 million, a 2.6% decrease compared to Q3 2025.

· | Financial results moved from a US$ 157 million expense as of September 2025 to a US$ 179 million expense as of September 2026, mainly due to a larger loss from foreign exchange rate differences and lower financial income. In Q3 2026, financial results were US$ 15 million lower than in Q3 2025, at US$ 58 million, primarily due to a gain on foreign exchange rate differences.

· | On August 17, 2026, Enel Chile, through its subsidiary Enel Green Power Chile, began construction of the Finis Terrae BESS battery energy storage system in the Antofagasta Region, a project that is part of the Finis Terrae photovoltaic plant currently in operation. The system will have a capacity of 100 MW and a storage capacity of 168 MWh.

INFORMATION RELEVANT TO THE ANALYSIS OF THESE FINANCIAL STATEMENTS
Regulatory Changes:
> | As part of the government’s social agenda, the Ministry of Energy published Law No. 21,185 in the Official Gazette on November 2, 2019, establishing a Transitional Electricity Price Stabilization Mechanism for Customers Subject to Tariff Regulation (hereinafter, the “Tariff Stabilization Law”). Under this law, from July 1, 2019, to December 31, 2020, prices charged to regulated customers were set at the levels established for the first half of 2019 (Decree 20T/2018), known as the “Stabilized Price for Regulated Customer” (Spanish acronym, PEC). Between January 1, 2021, and the end of the stabilization mechanism, prices will be those set through the semiannual adjustments referred to in Article 158 of the Electric Law; however, they may not exceed the adjusted PEC, calculated based on the Consumer Price Index beginning January 1, 2021 (the “adjusted PEC”). The resulting billing differences will give rise to accounts receivable in favor of the generators, subject to a limit of US$ 1,350 million through 2023. This limit was reached in January 2022. The outstanding balance must be recovered no later than December 31, 2027.

On December 14, 2020, the National Energy Commission (Spanish acronym CNE) published Exempt Resolution No. 340, amending the technical provisions for implementing the Tariff Stabilization Law. The resolution clarified that payments to each supplier “must be applied to the balances in chronological order, paying off the oldest balances first,” rather than being allocated in a weighted manner across the total outstanding balances, as the industry had interpreted it until then.
Furthermore, this Resolution established that balances would be paid at the observed dollar exchange rate on the sixth business day following publication of the Coordinator's Balance Payment Schedule, replacing the previously established average dollar exchange rate for the billing month.
> | On August 2, 2022, Law No. 21,472 created a Tariff Stabilization Fund and established a New Temporary Price Stabilization Mechanism for electricity for customers subject to price regulation. The law also establishes a customer protection mechanism (Spanish acronym, MPC) to cover the difference between the prices of regulated supply contracts and the stabilized tariff. The goal is to prevent increases in electricity bills in 2022 and to allow gradual increases over the next decade. The differences will be covered by a transitional fund of US$ 1.8 billion, disbursed through a new instrument called the Payment Document, issued monthly by the General Treasury of the Republic to electricity generation companies. The Payment Document is in US dollars, adjustable and transferable, with a maximum maturity date of December 2032, and is backed by a State guarantee.

This fund will be financed through an additional charge to end customers, with rates segmented by consumption levels. Customers with monthly consumption below 350 kWh will be exempt from the charge, as will micro and small businesses with monthly consumption of up to 1,000 kWh.
The fund, managed by the General Treasury of the Republic, will receive an annual fiscal contribution of US$20 million from 2023 until its expiration on December 31, 2032, supplementing the US$15 million contributed in 2022. All balances exceeding the US$ 1,350 million fund specified in Law 21.185 are recognized as part of the mechanism established by Law No. 21.472.
On March 14, 2023, the CNE published Exempt Resolution No. 86, which sets out the technical provisions for implementing Law No. 21,472. Subsequently, on August 9, 2023, the CNE issued Exempt Resolution No. 334, which amends Exempt Resolution No. 86 and, among other matters, establishes provisions, procedures, deadlines, and conditions for the proper implementation of that law.

- | It will prevent supply companies from accumulating further debt, as tariffs for customers subject to price regulation will gradually reflect their actual energy and power costs.

Meanwhile, customers with consumption less than or equal to 350 kWh per month will pay the actual price of energy and power, starting with the publication of the decree for the second half of 2024. From the decree for the first half of 2025, the MPC charge will be added to their bill.
On October 14, 2025, the National Energy Commission (Spanish acronym CNE) issued Exempt Resolution No. 633, approving the “Preliminary Technical Report for the Setting of the Average Node Prices of the National Electric System for the first half of 2026,” hereinafter referred to as “ITP” (by its Spanish acronym). This document, among other matters, corrected a methodological inconsistency in the CNE’s treatment of inflation when applying the CPI variation and, simultaneously, the use of the current interest rate for non-adjustable operations in national currency. All of this was implemented with the enactment of Supreme Decree No. 7T of 2024 by the Ministry of Energy and followed by the determination of Average Node Prices for electricity supplies, as outlined in Article 158 of the General Electric Services Law.
This inconsistency was corrected in the Final Technical Report on the Average Node Price for November 2025 (FTN for November 2025), which covered the first half of 2026 and established payment of the total difference in 6 equal instalments during this tariff period. The FTN for November 2025 was published in Supreme Decree No. 24T of January 2026. For the Enel Chile Group, the total amount of the difference was approximately US$42 million, with the provision to be recognized at the end of 2025.
Change of functional currency and reporting currency:
Effective January 1, 2025, Enel Chile changed its functional currency from Chilean pesos to U.S. dollars, as the U.S. dollar became the currency that significantly influences the economic environment in which the Company operates.

This change in functional currency was primarily driven by the fact that, also effective January 1, 2025, its subsidiary Enel Generación Chile changed its functional currency from Chilean pesos to U.S. dollars. This was primarily because, starting in 2025, the subsidiary’s main source of revenue will come from contracts with non-regulated customers, which—given the billing and collection cycles—result in substantially lower exposure to exchange rate fluctuations compared to contracts with regulated customers, which require significantly more time to complete the collection process. The regulated customer contract portfolio represented the Company’s primary source of revenue through fiscal year 2024.
Thus, given the importance of the generation segment to the Group—Enel Chile’s primary source of revenue—the dividends received from its subsidiaries will be determined on a U.S. dollar basis.
The change in the presentation currency at Enel Chile was accounted for as a change in accounting policy and applied retrospectively, as if the new presentation currency had always been the presentation currency of the consolidated financial statements. For further information, please refer to Note No. 3 of Enel Chile’s Consolidated Financial Statements as of September 30, 2026.
