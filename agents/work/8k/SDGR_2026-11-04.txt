Schrödinger Reports Third Quarter 2026 Financial Results
Third Quarter ACV of $45 Million, Representing 17% Growth
Increases Full-Year 2026 ACV and Drug Discovery Revenue Guidance
New York, November 4, 2026 – Schrödinger, Inc. (Nasdaq: SDGR) today announced financial results for the quarter ended September 30, 2026.
“We are very pleased with our third quarter performance, delivering 17% ACV growth, $24 million of drug discovery revenue, and raising our full-year ACV and drug discovery revenue outlook. Our strong performance highlights the power of our industry-leading platform and also our business model in which our software and drug discovery businesses reinforce each other,” said Ramy Farid, Ph.D., chief executive officer of Schrödinger. “Applying our platform to complex drug discovery and materials science challenges gives us direct insight that continuously enhances our computational platform. At the same time, our sustained track record of delivering on high-impact programs gives our partners the confidence to expand their software commitments. The continuous feedback loop is building durable value across our business.”
Third Quarter 2026 Operating and Financial Highlights (comparisons are to third quarter 2025, unless otherwise noted)
•ACV was $44.7 million, a 17% increase, and $214 million on a trailing four-quarter basis.
•ACV excluding contribution increased 24%.
•Software revenue was $38.2 million, a 6% increase.
•Hosted software revenue was $17.2 million, a 49% increase.
•Hosted software revenue was 45% of total software revenue, and 32% on a trailing four-quarter basis.
•Drug discovery revenue was $24.2 million compared to $13.0 million, primarily due to the recognition of a milestone payment from Structure Therapeutics.
•Total revenue was $64.5 million, a 19% increase.
•Software gross margin was 75%, a 2% decrease reflecting the company’s ongoing transition to hosted licenses.
•Operating expenses were $64.4 million, a 13% decrease.
•Other expense, which includes changes in fair value of equity investments and interest income/expense, was $11.4 million, primarily due to changes in the market value of the company’s equity positions.
•Net loss was $37.7 million, compared to a net loss of $32.8 million.
•Cash, cash equivalents, restricted cash and marketable securities were $373 million.
Schrödinger presents contribution revenue and cost of revenue separately from software and drug discovery revenue and cost of revenues. Prior periods have been reclassified to conform to this presentation to facilitate year-over-year comparability.
2026 Financial and Operational Outlook
As of November 4, 2026, Schrödinger provided the following financial guidance for the fiscal year ending December 31, 2026:
•ACV is expected to range from $222 million to $232 million, representing 12-17% growth over 2025, compared to the prior expectation of $218 million to $228 million or 10-15% growth over 2025.
•Drug discovery revenue is expected to range from $75 million to $85 million, compared to the prior expectation of $65 million to $75 million.
•Operating expenses are expected to be less than 2025.
Recent Highlights
Platform
•In October, Schrödinger scientists published research in Nature using machine learning force fields to reveal key atomic-scale insights into the structure of lithium-ion battery electrolytes. This new computational approach to understanding battery electrolytes has the potential to transform battery development from empirical trial-and-error toward predictive design to high-throughput, rational, computationally driven design for higher-capacity, faster-charging, and longer-lasting batteries.
•Schrödinger researchers published research in JACS Au using Absolute Binding Free Energy Perturbation (AB-FEP) to predict how small molecules interact with integrin proteins, which are targets for a broad range of diseases, including autoimmune disorders. This method outperformed traditional techniques by precisely classifying whether a ligand stabilizes integrins in an active or inactive state, a key factor in developing safer, more effective therapeutics.
•Schrödinger scientists published research in The Journal of Physical Chemistry C, describing a computational method to accurately predict the glass transition temperature of organic electronic materials, which is crucial for ensuring the stability and performance of devices like OLED displays. The approach achieved accuracy comparable to experimental methods, enabling rapid screening of new materials early in the design process, which can significantly accelerate the development of next-generation organic electronics.
Therapeutics Portfolio
•In September, Schrödinger announced a licensing and collaboration agreement with Tectora Therapeutics, a new biotechnology company it co-founded with New Enterprise Associates and RA Capital Management, focused on developing innovative therapies for immunology and inflammation. In connection with Tectora’s $55 million Series A financing, Schrödinger contributed two high-value, early-stage small molecule programs (SDGR-4594 and SDGR-8139) in exchange for an equity stake, as well as eligibility for future milestones and royalties. Tectora will also partner with Schrödinger’s drug discovery experts and leverage Schrödinger’s computational platform at scale to further advance these programs to clinical candidates.
•In September, Takeda announced the Food and Drug Administration accepted its New Drug Application under Priority Review for zasocitinib (TAK-279), the company’s oral tyrosine kinase 2 (TYK2) inhibitor for the treatment of moderate-to-severe plaque psoriasis. Zasocitinib was co-invented by Schrödinger and Nimbus and subsequently acquired by Takeda.
•In September, Schrödinger received a milestone payment from Structure Therapeutics, a company it co-founded, associated with Structure’s Phase 1/2a study of ACCG-2671, its oral small molecule amylin candidate for the treatment of obesity and metabolic diseases.
•In November, Schrödinger announced that updated Phase 1 data for SGR-1505 will be presented in an oral session at the American Society of Hematology (ASH) Annual Meeting taking place December 12-15, 2026, in New Orleans.

Non-GAAP Information
Included in this press release is certain financial information that has not been prepared in accordance with generally accepted accounting principles in the United States (GAAP). The company presents adjusted EBITDA, which is a non-GAAP financial measure. Adjusted EBITDA is defined as net income (loss) before interest, taxes, depreciation, amortization, and stock-based compensation expense, and further adjusted to exclude gains and losses on equity investments, changes in fair value of equity investments, restructuring costs, litigation and settlement expenses, and, when applicable, other non-recurring items that management does not consider indicative of ongoing operating performance.
Management believes adjusted EBITDA is a useful measure for investors, taken in conjunction with the company’s GAAP financial statements because they provide greater period-over-period comparability with respect to the company’s operating performance, by excluding the effects of capital structure, tax impacts, non-cash depreciation and amortization, non-cash equity compensation expense, non-cash mark-to-market and other valuation adjustments for the company’s equity investments, non-recurring cash distributions from the company’s equity investments, and other non-recurring items that are not reflective of the ongoing performance of the business. However, adjusted EBITDA as a non-GAAP financial measure should be considered only in addition to, not as a substitute for or as superior to, net income (loss) or other financial measures prepared in accordance with GAAP.
Other companies in Schrödinger’s industry may calculate adjusted EBITDA differently than Schrödinger does, limiting its usefulness as a comparative measure. For a reconciliation of adjusted EBITDA to GAAP net income (loss), please refer to the tables at the end of this press release.

Operating Metrics
To supplement the financial measures presented in this press release and related conference call or webcast in accordance with generally accepted accounting principles in the United States (GAAP), Schrödinger also presents certain other performance metrics, such as annual contract value, or ACV, and ACV by certain industries and customer cohorts.
Annual Contract Value (ACV). Schrödinger tracks the ACV for each customer. With respect to contracts that have a duration of one year or less, or contracts of more than one year in duration that are billed annually, ACV is defined as the contract value billed during the applicable period. For contracts with a duration of more than one year that are billed upfront, ACV in each period represents the total billed contract value divided by the term. We present ACV as a supplemental operating metric because it provides a consistent measure of the underlying performance of our software business that is not affected by differences in revenue recognition timing across contract types, delivery models, or billing structures. ACV should be viewed independently of revenue and does not represent revenue calculated in accordance with GAAP on an annualized basis, as it is an operating metric that can be impacted by contract execution start and end dates and renewal rates. ACV is not intended to be a replacement for, or forecast of, revenue.
ACV by Cohorts. Schrödinger tracks ACV by certain industries and customer cohorts. These cohorts include contribution, which consists of customers from which we derive contribution revenue. We present this ACV separately because it relates to grant agreements accounted for as non-exchange contributions, rather than commercial software contracts. The operating metrics for the cohorts are not prepared in accordance with GAAP and do not correspond to the company’s reportable segments or the allocation of costs for GAAP purposes. These metrics allow management to better understand differences in sales cycles, contract duration, deployment models, renewal behavior, and expansion opportunities among customer and industry groups, supplementing but not replacing Schrödinger’s GAAP results.
