Copa Holdings Reports Third-Quarter Financial Results
Panama City, Panama --- November 18, 2026. Copa Holdings1, S.A. (NYSE: CPA), today announced financial results for the third quarter (3Q26), which reflect the resilience of the Company's business model and disciplined execution amid a significantly higher jet fuel price environment. Key highlights include:
•Net profit of US$175.8 million or US$4.29 per share, a 2.2% year‑over‑year increase in earnings per share.
•Operating margin of 18.6% and net margin of 14.6%, a decrease of 4.6 and 4.4 percentage points, respectively, compared to 3Q25.
•Capacity, measured in ASMs, increased by 16.3% year over year, while passenger traffic in RPMs increased by 17.1%. As a result, load factor increased by 0.7 percentage points to 88.7%.
•Operating revenue increased 31.9% year over year, and revenue per available seat mile (RASM) increased 13.5% compared to 3Q25.
•Operating cost per available seat mile excluding fuel (Ex-Fuel CASM) increased 1.1% year over year to 5.7 cents.
•The Company ended the quarter with approximately US$1.6 billion in cash, short-term and long-term investments, which represent 38% of the last twelve months’ revenues.
•The Company closed 3Q26 with a Net Debt to EBITDA ratio of 0.8 times.
•In September, Copa Airlines was recognized by Skytrax for the eleventh consecutive year as the "Best Airline in Central America and the Caribbean.”
•Copa Airlines had an on-time performance for the quarter of 87.1% and a flight completion factor of 99.7%, once again positioning itself among the best in the industry.
Subsequent events
•On November 18, 2026, the Board of Directors of Copa Holdings ratified its fourth dividend payment for the year of US$1.71 per share. Dividends will be paid on December 15, 2026, to shareholders on record as of November 30, 2026.
•Between October 9 and October 10, 2026, the Company cancelled approximately 295 flights as a result of an earthquake that affected Panama and temporarily disrupted operations at Tocumen International Airport due to repeated runway inspections. The Company estimates the net impact of this event, which is included in its outlook, will be between US$12 and US$14 million on fourth-quarter operating profit.

Management’s comments on 3Q26 results
Copa Holdings’ third quarter results demonstrate the resilience of its business model and the strength of demand across the region, despite a significantly higher jet fuel price environment. During the quarter, the Company delivered operating income of US$224.4 million and an operating margin of 18.6%, while growing earnings per share by 2.2% year over year to US$4.29. These results reflect Copa’s ability to leverage its unique competitive advantages to generate strong profitability amid elevated fuel costs.
Demand across the Company’s network remained robust in the quarter. Operating revenues increased 31.9% year over year to US$1.2 billion, driven by 16.3% capacity growth and a 13.5% increase in unit revenues (RASM) to 12.6 cents. Passenger yields increased 13.8% year over year to 13.5 cents, while load factor improved 0.7 percentage points to 88.7%, highlighting the strength and resilience of air travel demand throughout Latin America.
Total CASM increased 20.3% to 10.2 cents, primarily as a result of significantly higher fuel prices. Cost per available seat mile excluding fuel (CASM Ex-Fuel) increased 1.1% year over year to 5.7 cents. Year-over-year comparisons were impacted by the benefit recorded in 3Q25 related to the extension of one leased aircraft, as well as a US$8.3 million benefit recorded during 3Q26 related to an increase in the discount rate used to calculate the present value of expected end-of-lease costs. Excluding both items, CASM Ex-Fuel would have increased approximately 1.7% year over year.
This increase in CASM Ex-Fuel was mainly attributable to the year-over-year appreciation of certain Latin American currencies.
Despite a 61.5% year-over-year increase in the Company’s all-in average jet fuel prices, strong demand and higher yields enabled the Company to recover 86% of the increase in fuel-related expenses through higher revenues. This once again demonstrates the strength of Copa’s business model and the robust demand environment across the region.
Copa Holdings continues to maintain a strong liquidity and balance sheet position, ending the quarter with approximately US$1.6 billion in cash, short-term and long-term investments, representing 38% of the last twelve months’ revenues, and a Net Debt-to-EBITDA ratio of 0.8 times.
Copa Airlines once again delivered industry-leading operational performance, recording an on-time performance of 87.1% and a completion factor of 99.7% during the quarter.
In recognition of its consistent service quality and reliability, Copa Airlines was named the “Best Airline in Central America and the Caribbean” by Skytrax for the eleventh consecutive year.
The Company remains focused on leveraging its Hub of the Americas® to offer the most comprehensive and convenient intra-Americas network. In November, Copa Airlines will launch service to Porlamar, Venezuela, its 88th destination, further strengthening network connectivity throughout the region.
The transition from six to eight connecting banks, beginning in March 2027, will improve connectivity, aircraft utilization, and travel options for passengers. In addition, the Company continues to make progress on the rollout of Starlink connectivity across its fleet, with approximately a quarter of its fleet currently equipped and full implementation expected by mid-2027. These initiatives further strengthen the Company’s competitive advantages and customer value proposition.
Copa’s proven business model, built on Panama’s strategic geographic position, industry-leading operational reliability, structurally low unit costs, a passenger-friendly product, and a strong balance sheet, positions the Company well to navigate periods of fuel price volatility while continuing to generate profitable growth and long-term shareholder value. Strong demand trends across the region continue to support the Company’s outlook and reinforce its competitive advantages.

Outlook for 2026
While demand trends across the network continue to be strong, fuel prices have increased relative to the assumptions incorporated in the Company’s prior outlook. Based on current demand trends and fuel cost projections, the Company is updating its full-year 2026 outlook and now expects an operating margin in the range of 16% to 17% and a capacity increase in ASMs of approximately 14.5% compared to 2025.
Financial Outlook | 2026Guidance | 2025
Capacity – YOY ASM growth | ~14.5% | 7.8%
Operating Margin | 16-17% | 22.6%

Factored into this outlook are a load factor of approximately 87.5%, unit revenues (RASM) in the range of 12.3 to 12.4 cents, unit costs excluding fuel (Ex-Fuel CASM) of approximately 5.8 cents, and an all-in fuel price of approximately US$3.90 per gallon.
Preliminarily, for the full year 2027, the Company currently anticipates increasing its capacity measured in ASMs by approximately 8% to 9% compared to 2026, based on the current order book projection and consistent with Copa’s long-term growth plan. Copa’s unit cost outlook excluding fuel (Ex-Fuel CASM) is expected to be in the range of 5.7 to 5.8 cents.

Operating revenue
Consolidated revenue for 3Q26 totaled US$1.2 billion, a 31.9% increase compared to 3Q25 on 16.3% capacity growth, measured in ASMs.
Passenger revenue totaled US$1.1 billion, an increase of 33.3% year-over-year, driven by a 17.1% increase in RPMs and a 13.8% increase in passenger yield.
Cargo and mail revenue totaled US$36.3 million, up 22.2%, due to higher cargo volumes and yields, including the year-over-year effect of adding a second B737-800 freighter to the fleet.
Other operating revenue totaled US$20.1 million, a 9.3% decrease compared to 3Q25, primarily due to the year-over-year effect of the renewal of a co-branded credit card agreement in 2025.
Operating expenses
Consolidated operating costs for 3Q26 totaled US$980.0 million, a 39.8% increase year-over-year, primarily due to higher fuel prices and capacity growth.
Fuel totaled US$434.7 million, an increase of 83.6%, driven by a 61.5% higher all-in average fuel price and a 14.7% increase in gallons consumed.
Wages, salaries, benefits, and other employee expenses totaled US$134.8 million, an increase of 8.5%, mostly reflecting additional operational staff to support capacity growth, partially offset by lower overhead and administrative expenses.
Passenger servicing totaled US$32.2 million, an increase of 17.2%, driven by a 17.1% increase in onboard passengers.
Airport facilities and handling charges totaled US$84.7 million, a 19.8% year-over-year increase, mainly driven by increased departures, higher fees in certain airports, and the year-over-year appreciation of certain Latin American currencies.
Sales and distribution totaled US$62.1 million, a 17.3% increase, mostly due to higher sales, partially offset by lower effective commission and incentive rates.
Maintenance, materials, and repairs totaled US$39.7 million, a 13.2% increase compared to 3Q25. Year-over-year comparisons were impacted by the benefit recorded in 3Q25 related to the extension of one leased aircraft, as well as a US$8.3 million benefit recorded during 3Q26 related to an increase in the discount rate used to calculate the present value of expected end-of-lease costs.
Excluding these items, Maintenance, materials, and repairs would have increased by 21.5% year over year, mainly driven by increased flight hours and higher material consumption.
Depreciation and amortization totaled US$110.4 million, an 18.6% year-over-year increase, due to higher amortization of engine restoration events and higher depreciation of aircraft.
Flight operations totaled US$45.9 million, a 26.3% year-over-year increase, mainly driven by a 15.7% increase in block hours, higher overflight rates in certain countries and the year-over-year appreciation of certain Latin American currencies.
Other operating and administrative expenses totaled US$35.4 million, a 47.1% increase compared to the same period in 2025, mainly due to the year-over-year effect of realized gains from engine exchange transactions during 3Q25. Excluding this item, other operating and administrative expenses would have increased by 7.9%.

Non-operating Income (Expense)
Consolidated non-operating income (expense) totaled US$(19.9) million in 3Q26.
Finance cost totaled US$(29.5) million, comprised of US$22.0 million related to loan interest expenses, US$4.0 million in interest charges related to operating leases and US$3.5 million related to the discount rate utilized for the calculation of leased aircraft charges.
Finance income totaled US$18.0 million, related to proceeds from investments.
Gain (loss) on foreign currency fluctuations totaled US$(5.7) million, related to the currency exchange rate impact on net assets and liabilities positions, mainly in Colombia and Brazil.
Net change in fair value of derivatives totaled US$(0.8) million, due to mark-to-market losses on hedge positions related to the Brazilian real.
Other non-operating income (expense) totaled US$(1.8) million in 3Q26.
