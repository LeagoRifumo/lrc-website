SSR MINING REPORTS THIRD QUARTER 2026 RESULTS
DENVER - SSR Mining Inc. (Nasdaq/TSX: SSRM) ("SSR Mining" or the “Company") reports consolidated financial results for the third quarter ended September 30, 2026. In addition, the Board of Directors declared a quarterly cash dividend of $0.03 per common share payable on December 11, 2026, to holders of record at the close of business on November 13, 2026. This dividend qualifies as an 'eligible dividend' for Canadian tax purposes.
•Consolidated operating results from continuing operations: Third quarter 2026 production was 103,585 gold equivalent ounces at consolidated cost of sales of $1,755 per payable ounce and all-in sustaining costs (“AISC”) of $2,527 per payable ounce.(1,2) In the first nine months of 2026, the Company produced 315,459 gold equivalent ounces at consolidated cost of sales of $1,751 per payable ounce and AISC of $2,523 per payable ounce. Year-to-date results remain aligned with full-year 2026 production guidance of 450,000 to 535,000 gold equivalent ounces and the Company’s expectations of strong fourth quarter results. At Puna, the Company continues to identify additional ore that supports potential mine life extension, resulting in resequencing that defers lower-cost production from stockpiled ore that was planned to be processed in 2026. This decision, combined with higher royalty costs, persistent diesel price pressures and broader labor and input cost inflation, is expected to result in full-year AISC trending approximately 10% above the upper end of the Company’s 2026 guidance.
•Financial results from continuing operations: In the third quarter of 2026, SSR Mining reported net income attributable to SSR Mining shareholders of $94.8 million, or $0.46 per diluted share, and adjusted net income attributable to SSR Mining shareholders of $130.7 million, or $0.64 per diluted share. In the third quarter of 2026, operating cash flow was $106.0 million and free cash flow was $5.8 million. Third quarter operating cash flow before changes in working capital was $211.0 million, and free cash flow before changes in working capital was $110.8 million. Over the first three quarters of 2026, operating cash flow and operating cash flow before working capital changes totaled $526.5 million and $707.9 million, respectively, while free cash flow and free cash flow before changes in working capital were $304.9 million and $486.4 million, respectively.
•Cash and liquidity position: As of September 30, 2026, SSR Mining had a cash and cash equivalent balance of $1.6 billion and no outstanding long-term debt. Total liquidity, inclusive of the Company’s undrawn $600 million revolving credit facility, was $2.2 billion.
•Capital Returns: In the third quarter of 2026, SSR Mining completed $193.6 million in share buybacks through the repurchase of 6.2 million shares. In the first nine months of 2026 share buybacks totaled approximately 16.6 million shares or more than $530 million. Additionally, on September 11, 2026, the Company paid its first quarterly dividend of 2026 to shareholders of record on August 14, 2026. Total capital returns, inclusive of share buybacks over the first nine months of the year and the third quarter dividend, represent an effective yield of approximately 10% for 2026.
•Completed strategic repositioning to the Americas: On July 17, 2026, the Company closed the sale of its 20% ownership stake in the Hod Maden development project (the "Hod Maden Project") for an uncapped 4.0% net smelter return royalty ("NSR") on 100% of the Hod Maden Project. This transaction, combined with the Company’s divestment of its ownership in the Çöpler mine in the second quarter of 2026, completed SSR Mining’s strategic repositioning to a free-cash-flow-focused Americas gold and silver producer anchored by its long-lived Marigold and CC&V operations in the United States.
•Updated Marigold Technical Report Summary: On October 13, 2026, the Company announced an updated Technical Report Summary ("TRS") for the Marigold Complex ("2026 Marigold TRS") in Nevada, USA with a $3.3 billion NPV5% and a more than 50% increase in total gold production over the prior life of mine plan. The 2026 Marigold TRS includes 4.6 million ounces of gold Mineral Reserves supporting a 20-year mine life and significant potential for future growth.

•Accretive Acquisition of Roxmore Resources: On October 13, 2026, SSR Mining announced an agreement for the all-cash acquisition of Roxmore Resources and its multi-million-ounce Converse Gold Project adjacent to the Company’s Marigold Complex. Converse has the potential to add 150,000 to 170,000 ounces of annual gold production to SSR Mining’s U.S. platform, with first production targeted for 2033. The acquisition is subject to the satisfaction of customary closing conditions, including court approval, and is expected to be completed in the fourth quarter of 2026.
•Development & exploration in the Americas: SSR Mining continues to advance key brownfield organic growth projects across the portfolio, including Buffalo Valley at Marigold, Cortaderas at Puna, and Porky at Seabee. These projects represent low capital intensity, high-return development opportunities and have the potential to meaningfully extend the mine lives at each asset. Earlier-stage exploration activity is also continuing across the Company’s more than 13,000-hectare land position in Nevada and at regional targets in Saskatchewan.
Rod Antal, Executive Chairman of SSR Mining, said, “Over the last few months, we have delivered a number of positive milestones designed to create long-term value for shareholders and affirm SSR Mining as a free-cash-flow-focused gold and silver producer in the Americas. This includes completing our exit from Türkiye with the Çöpler and Hod Maden divestments, delivering an improved, multi-decade mine plan for Marigold, and announcing an agreement to acquire the Converse Project in Nevada to further bolster our U.S. platform.
Operationally, we remain on track for full-year production guidance with steady third quarter results ahead of a strong fourth quarter. Although costs are trending approximately 10% above the top end of our original guidance ranges, we expect robust free cash flow into year-end.
Overall, we continue to see significant latent value in our share price. Our consistent and ongoing activity on the share buyback program should demonstrate our confidence in this upside.”

About SSR Mining
SSR Mining Inc. is a free-cash-flow-focused gold and silver mining company and the third-largest gold producer in the United States. SSR Mining has a diversified portfolio of operating, development and exploration assets across the Americas, including four operating mines in the USA, Canada, and Argentina. Non-GAAP Measure - Cash Costs and AISC
Cash Costs and All-In Sustaining Costs (“AISC”) per payable ounce of gold and respective unit cost measures are non-U.S. GAAP metrics developed by the World Gold Council to provide transparency into the costs associated with producing gold and provide a standard for comparison across the industry. The World Gold Council is a market development organization for the gold industry.
The Company uses cash costs per ounce of precious metals sold and AISC per ounce of precious metals to monitor its operating performance internally. The most directly comparable measure prepared in accordance with GAAP is cost of sales. The Company believes this measure provides investors and analysts with useful information about its underlying cash costs of operations and the impact of by-product credits on its cost structure. The Company also believes these are relevant metrics used to understand its operating profitability. When deriving the cost of sales associated with an ounce of precious metal, the Company includes by-product credits, which allows management and other stakeholders to assess the net costs of gold and silver production.
AISC includes total cost of sales incurred at the Company's mining operations, which forms the basis of cash costs. Additionally, the Company includes sustaining capital expenditures, sustaining mine-site exploration and evaluation costs, reclamation cost accretion and amortization, and general and administrative expenses. This measure seeks to reflect the ongoing cost of gold and silver production from current operations; therefore, growth capital is excluded. The Company determines sustaining capital to be capital expenditures that are necessary to maintain current production and execute the current mine plan. The Company determines growth capital to be those payments used to develop new operations or related to projects at existing operations where those projects will materially benefit the operation.
The Company believes that AISC provides additional information to management and stakeholders that provides visibility to better define the total costs associated with production and better understanding of the economics of the Company's operations and performance compared to other producers. In deriving the number of ounces of precious metal sold, the Company considers the physical ounces available for sale after the treatment and refining process, commonly referred to as payable metal, as this is what is sold to third parties.
