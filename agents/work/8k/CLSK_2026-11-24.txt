EXHIBIT 99.1
CleanSpark Reports Fiscal Fourth Quarter and Full Year 2026 Financial and Operating Results
Executed $6.6B high-investment grade lease in Sandersville; project is on time, on budget, and fully funded
585 MW in Texas assigned Batch Zero baseload status, setting the stage for continued commercial growth
LAS VEGAS, November 24, 2026 -- CleanSpark, Inc. (Nasdaq: CLSK) (“CleanSpark” or the “Company”), a market leading data center developer, today reported financial and operating results for the fourth quarter and fiscal year ended September 30, 2026.
Fourth Quarter and Fiscal Year Highlights
•
Expanded energy and infrastructure portfolio through strategic acquisitions of up to 885 MW in Texas, establishing a foundation for future AI and HPC development
•
Secured 20-year, $6.6 billion triple-net lease at Sandersville with high investment-grade tenant
•
Fully funded Sandersville development through issuance of 7.875% $2.276 billion senior secured notes due 2031
•
Maintains more than $1 billion in liquidity to support disciplined growth and infrastructure development
Management Commentary
“Fiscal 2026 was a defining year for CleanSpark as we made significant progress in executing our strategic transformation,” said Matt Schultz, Chairman and Chief Executive Officer. “We have spent years building the energy expertise, operational capabilities, and infrastructure portfolio that differentiate our platform today. This year, we demonstrated our ability to translate those strengths into long-term contracted opportunities, while securing the capital necessary to execute. As we look ahead, our focus remains on disciplined execution, strengthening our relationships with the communities and stakeholders critical to our success and creating long-term value for our shareholders.”
President and CFO Gary Vecchiarelli commented, “Our financial strategy remains centered on disciplined capital stewardship, maintaining flexibility, and allocating capital to opportunities that deliver the strongest risk-adjusted returns. The completion of our Sandersville financing provides dedicated capital and fully funds the project. Combined with more than $1 billion in liquidity, we believe our financial position provides the flexibility to execute on current projects and selectively pursue future opportunities without compromising our commitment to prudent capital stewardship.”
Financial Results:
•
Total revenue of $120.9 million and $576.5 million for the fourth quarter and full year, respectively, representing year-over-year decreases of 46% and 25%
•
Net income of $9.8 million or $0.04 per basic share and net loss of ($987.1) million or ($3.74) per basic share for the fourth quarter and full year, respectively
•
Adjusted EBITDA, a non-GAAP measure reconciled below, of $345.4 million in the fourth quarter and an adjusted EBITDA loss of ($304.2) million for the full year
•
Year-end liquidity of approximately $1.3 billion, consisting of approximately $204.8 million in cash and cash equivalents and approximately $1,130.5 million in bitcoin holdings, including current and non-current holdings and bitcoin pledged as collateral

Business and Operational Highlights
AI and HPC Infrastructure Development
Sandersville, GA
•
Secured 20-year, $6.6 billion triple-net lease with high investment grade tenant
•
Fully funded project development through $2.276 billion issuance of 7.875% senior secured notes due 2031
•
Mitigated exposure to cost overruns by achieving basis of design alignment on 100% of anticipated project, agreeing to a turnkey, lump sum development contract with our Engineering, Procurement and Construction Management general contractor and pre-paying to secure long-lead items

Obtained all material building and development permits (zoning, building, drainage, etc.)
•
Construction progress remains on time and on track to meet first planned data hall in November 2027
Texas Portfolio
•
Strategic acquisitions of Sealy (285 MW) and Brazoria (up to 600 MW) assets
•
Secured Conditional Batch Zero Baseload designation on 585 MW (285 MW in Sealy and 300 MW in Brazoria Phase 1)
•
Received Conditional Batch Zero Studied Load designation for Brazoria Phase 2 (up to 300 MW)
•
Exclusivity period at Sealy and Brazoria expired during a period of temporary regulatory uncertainty, commercialization efforts are proceeding with existing Sandersville tenant and others
•
Received TCEQ permits for Brazoria project
Washington, GA
•
86 MW of currently energized, grid-connected power
•
Submitted high-density line study to increase power to 500 MW. Preliminary results indicate ability to support up to 500 MW transmission capacity over next several years.

Acquired 500 acres of adjacent land to support future AI data center campus
Bitcoin Mining Operations
•
Held 13,530 bitcoin, including collateral, at fiscal year-end, reflecting a total value of $1.1 billion based on bitcoin prices as of September 30, 2026
•
Mined 7,253 bitcoin in fiscal year 2026, a decrease of 8% year-over-year despite a 21% decrease in average hashrate to 36.0 EH/s between those fiscal years
•
Improved fleet efficiency as measured by a fiscal year-end miner efficiency of 14.8 J/Th compared to 16.7 J/Th at fiscal year-end 2025
•
Generated $30.6 million of derivative-related proceeds in the fiscal year as a part of the Company’s Digital Asset Management program

About CleanSpark
CleanSpark (Nasdaq: CLSK), is a market-leading data center developer with a proven track record of success. We control a portfolio of up to 2.1 GW of power, land, and data centers across the United States powered by globally competitive energy prices. Sitting at the intersection of Bitcoin, energy, operational excellence, and capital stewardship, we optimize our infrastructure to deliver superior returns to our shareholders. Monetizing low-cost, high reliability energy by producing a global emerging critical resource – compute – positions us to prosper in an ever-changing world.

Non-GAAP Measure
We present Adjusted EBITDA, which is not a measurement of financial performance under GAAP. Our non-GAAP “Adjusted EBITDA” excludes (i) impacts of interest, taxes, and depreciation; (ii) our share-based compensation expense, unrealized gains/losses on securities, and changes in the fair value of contingent consideration with respect to previously completed acquisitions, all of which are non-cash items that we believe are not reflective of our general business performance, and for which the accounting requires management judgment, and the resulting expenses could vary significantly in comparison to other companies; (iii) non-cash impairment losses related to long-lived assets; (iv) realized gains and losses on sales of equity securities, the amounts of which are directly related to the unrealized gains and losses that are also excluded; (v) legal fees related to litigation and various transactions, which fees management does not believe are reflective of our ongoing operating activities; (vi) gains and losses on disposal of assets, the majority of which are related to obsolete or unrepairable machines that are no longer deployed; (vii) gains and losses related to discontinued operations that would not be applicable to our future business activities; and (viii) severance expenses.
Management believes that providing this non-GAAP financial measure that excludes these items allows for meaningful comparisons between the Company’s core business operating results and those of other companies and provides the Company with an important tool for financial and operational decision making and for evaluating its own core business operating results over different periods of time. In addition to management’s internal use of non-GAAP Adjusted EBITDA, management believes that Adjusted EBITDA is also useful to investors and analysts in comparing our performance across reporting periods on a consistent basis. Management believes the foregoing to be the case even though some of the excluded items involve cash outlays and some of them recur on a regular basis (although management does not believe any of such items are normal operating expenses necessary to generate our bitcoin-related revenues). For example, we expect that share-based compensation expense, which is excluded from Adjusted EBITDA, will continue to be a significant recurring expense over the coming years and is an important part of the compensation provided to certain employees, officers and directors.
The Company’s Adjusted EBITDA measure may not be directly comparable to similar measures provided by other companies in our industry, as other companies in our industry may calculate non-GAAP financial results differently. The Company’s Adjusted EBITDA is not a measurement of financial performance under GAAP and should not be considered as an alternative to operating (loss) income or any other measure of performance derived in accordance with GAAP. Although management utilizes internally and presents Adjusted EBITDA, we only utilize that measure supplementally and do not consider it to be a substitute for, or superior to, the information provided by GAAP financial results.
Accordingly, Adjusted EBITDA is not meant to be considered in isolation of, and should be read in conjunction with, the information contained in our Condensed Consolidated Financial Statements, which have been prepared in accordance with GAAP.
