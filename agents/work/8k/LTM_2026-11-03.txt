Santiago, Chile, November 3, 2026 – LATAM Airlines Group S.A. (NYSE: LTM; SSE: LTM) announced today its consolidated financial results for the third quarter ending September 30, 2026. References to "LATAM", "LATAM Airlines Group", the “Company” or the "parent company" pertain to LATAM Airlines Group S.A., and references to “LATAM group,” “we,” “us,” “our,” or the “group” refer to LATAM Airlines Group S.A. and its consolidated affiliates, both passenger and cargo affiliates. MANAGEMENT COMMENTS - THIRD QUARTER 2026
The third quarter of 2026 marked another period of solid performance for LATAM group. Revenues grew 18.4% year-over-year and the adjusted operating margin reached 12.5%, while facing an approximately US$520 million jet fuel price impact compared to 3Q25. Following the exceptionally challenging second quarter, LATAM group maintained its commercial momentum, supported by the dedication of its 44 thousand employees.
LATAM group's differentiated value proposition continued to drive customer preference and support strong commercial execution. On the passenger front, main cabin revenues grew year-over-year while continuing to grow capacity. The premium segment delivered an even better performance, with premium revenues growing 23% year-over-year. This customer preference, supported by the group’s extensive network and continued investment in customer experience, allowed LATAM group to capture higher fares and cover higher fuel costs. As a result, passenger unit revenues (PRASK) increased 9.4% year-over-year on a consolidated basis, led by the international segment with 10.2% growth, followed by LATAM Airlines Brazil's domestic market and the Spanish-speaking affiliates domestic markets, with increases of 9.2% and 8.7%, respectively.
Beyond the passenger business, LATAM group's diversified ecosystem continued to contribute to commercial growth. Cargo unit revenues increased 16% year-over-year, benefiting from shorter sales cycles that enabled a faster pass-through of higher fuel costs, while LATAM Pass continued to expand its membership base, reaching 57 million members, up 9% year-over-year, further deepening customer engagement and supporting recurring demand. These businesses form an integral part of LATAM group's unique ecosystem, bringing together strategic assets that strengthen one another and underpin a differentiated and sustainable business model. The value of this integrated approach was particularly evident during the quarter, enabling LATAM group to pass-through approximately 80%1 of the year-over-year jet fuel price impact. More broadly, LATAM group’s robust business model gained further external recognition amid challenging market conditions, with both S&P Global Ratings and Fitch Ratings upgrading LATAM's credit rating to BB+.
Operational efficiency and cost discipline also contributed to the group's solid performance, allowing LATAM to maintain control over unit costs while advancing its strategic priorities. With this, the group has been prioritizing investments that strengthen its differentiated value proposition, particularly product enhancements and premium offerings, alongside digital transformation projects aimed at driving further efficiencies and sustaining cost competitiveness over time.
Looking ahead, LATAM group remains confident in its ability to navigate evolving market conditions. Although continued fuel price volatility calls for a prudent approach, the group's extensive network, differentiated product offering, diversified revenue streams, fleet flexibility and a strong balance sheet provide a solid foundation to navigate this environment. LATAM's performance throughout 2026 has reinforced its ability to sustain profitability while pursuing its long-term strategy.

MANAGEMENT DISCUSSION AND ANALYSIS OF THIRD QUARTER 2026 RESULTS
Total operating revenues amounted to US$4,567 million in the third quarter, an increase of 18.4% compared to the same period of 2025, explained by a 17.8% increase in passenger revenues and a 19.5% increase in cargo revenues. For the third quarter of 2026, passenger and cargo revenues represented 87.5% and 10.6% of total operating revenues, respectively.
•Passenger revenues reached US$3,998 million in the third quarter, increasing 17.8% versus the same period of 2025. This growth was supported by sustained yield strength across the network, with yields increasing 11.7% year-over-year and passenger unit revenue (PRASK) growing 9.4%, complemented by a 7.7% expansion in capacity. The premium segment remained an important contributor to passenger revenue.
•Cargo revenues amounted to US$483 million in the third quarter, representing a 19.5% increase compared to the same period of 2025. The main driver of the increase was higher cargo yields, up 13.6% year-on-year, complemented by a 5.2% increase in cargo traffic (RTKs). In total, LATAM group transported 256 thousand tons during the quarter.
•Other income amounted to US$86 million in the third quarter, driven mostly by an increase in the LATAM Travel business and higher revenues from non-airline products within the LATAM Pass business.
Total adjusted operating expenses reached US$3,995 million in the third quarter of 2026, increasing 26.5% versus 3Q25, largely reflecting the impact of higher jet fuel prices during the quarter and the appreciation of local currencies, particularly the Brazilian real which strengthened by approximately 5.7% year-over-year. Adjusted passenger CASK ex-fuel rose 3.5% to US$4.6 cents in the quarter, with local currency appreciation contributing approximately US$0.1 cents to the increase. Excluding the impact of foreign exchange movements, unit costs would have remained broadly stable year-over-year.
The changes in adjusted operating expenses during the quarter were mainly explained by:
•Wages and benefits rose 17.7% year-over-year, primarily due to an 8.7% increase in the average workforce, in support of the group's expansion of operations and the appreciation of the Brazilian real and Chilean peso against the U.S. dollar.
•Aircraft fuel costs increased 60.9% versus the same period of 2025, amid continued pressure from elevated jet fuel prices. The average all-in jet fuel price (including hedges) increased 52.1% per barrel, while fuel consumption increased 5.8% year over year.
•Commissions to agents increased 26.5% compared to 3Q25, driven by a 17.8% increase in passenger revenues, supported by higher fares, increased operations and a greater contribution from the premium and corporate segments to the revenue mix.
•Depreciation and amortization increased 15.6% versus 3Q25, mainly driven by a higher average number of aircraft in the fleet. During the third quarter of 2026, LATAM group operated an average of 389 aircraft, up from 362 aircraft in the same period last year. The increase is also driven by a shift in fleet composition, as the group continues incorporating newer and higher-value aircraft as part of its fleet renewal strategy.
•Other rental and landing fees increased 14.9% year-over-year, primarily driven by an 8.7% increase in international operations, as well as the appreciation of local currencies.
•Passenger services expenses rose 11.4% versus the same period of 2025, driven by a growing premium traveler segment.
•Maintenance expenses totaled US$229 million, related to maintenance activities associated with fleet expansion.
•Other operating expenses remained stable compared to 3Q25, decreasing by 0.6%.

MARKET RISKS MANAGEMENT
Fuel price risk
LATAM’s fuel hedging policy has the main objective of protecting against medium-term liquidity risk from fuel price increases, while benefiting from fuel price reductions. It seeks to provide stability amid normalized volatility of jet fuel prices. Accordingly, LATAM hedges a portion of its estimated fuel consumption with a strategy based on the projected booking curve and pass-through capacity, managing this exposure over the next 12 months. LATAM hedges on jet fuel directly, via US Gulf Coast 54, generally utilizing asymmetric hedge instruments (for example, capped collars) that provide protection within a limited range against higher prices, while allowing participation from declining fuel prices. For the third quarter, LATAM had contracted call options for additional protection given the heightened volatility. For the period, LATAM had 37% of its estimated fuel consumption covered through collar structures and 8% through call options. During the third quarter of 2026, LATAM recognized gains of US$26.7 million for fuel hedging net of premiums (vs losses of US$2.0 million in the third quarter of 2025). LATAM does not hold call options under its fuel hedging contracts for the coming quarters.
Given the persistent volatility and prevailing market conditions, LATAM has maintained its fuel hedging positions unchanged for the coming quarters since its last disclosure.

About LATAM Airlines Group S.A.:
LATAM Airlines Group S.A. and its affiliates are the principal group of airlines in Latin America present in five domestic markets in the region: Brazil, Chile, Colombia, Ecuador and Peru, in addition to international operations within Latin America and to / from Europe, the United States, Oceania, Africa and the Caribbean.
The group has a fleet of Boeing 767, 777, 787, Airbus A321, A321Neo, A320, A320Neo, and A319 aircraft. Additionally, the Airbus A330, operated under ACMI leases, is also part of the current operations.
LATAM Cargo Chile, LATAM Cargo Colombia and LATAM Cargo Brazil are the LATAM group cargo subsidiaries. In addition to having access to the bellies of the passenger affiliates' aircraft, they have a fleet of 20 freighters. They operate on the LATAM group network as well as international routes that are exclusively operated by freighters. They offer modern infrastructure, a wide variety of services and protection options to meet all customer needs.
