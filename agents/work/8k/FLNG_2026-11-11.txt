“We are Flexcited to present our strongest third quarter revenues ever. Our fleet sailed in revenues of $97.4 million, or $95.2 million excluding EUAs. The reopening of the Strait of Hormuz and normalization of Qatari LNG supply remain key near-term swing factors, together with the potential effects of the El Niño climate pattern and the upcoming ban on Russian LNG imports to the EU from next year. Results for the three months ended September 30, 2026
The Company recorded vessel operating revenues of $97.4 million1 for the third quarter 2026, compared to $106.8 million in the second quarter 2026. The decrease in revenue is primarily due to lower spot market revenues from Flex Volunteer compared to the previous quarter. This was partially offset by an increase in the number of onhire days following the completion of the drydocking of Flex Vigilant, in the second quarter 2026. The TCE rate2 was $78,875 per day for the third quarter 2026, compared to $86,119 per day for the second quarter 2026.
Voyage expenses were $3.0 million in the third quarter 2026, compared to $6.4 million in the second quarter 2026. The decrease was driven by lower bunker and repositioning costs, as well as lower costs for EUAs under the EU ETS. An equivalent reduction in EUA-related revenue was recorded within vessel operating revenues.
Vessel operating expenses were $19.5 million in the third quarter 2026, compared to $19.2 million in the second quarter 2026. Operating expenses ("Opex") per day2 was $16,267 for the third quarter 2026, compared to $16,260 for the second quarter 2026.
The Company recorded a gain on derivatives of $10.2 million in the third quarter 2026, which includes an unrealized gain of $7.7 million and a realized gain of $2.4 million from interest rate swap settlements during the period.
Net income for the third quarter 2026 was $42.8 million, with basic earnings of $0.79 per share, compared with net income of $44.9 million and basic earnings of $0.83 per share for the second quarter 2026. Adjusted net income2 was $35.0 million, with adjusted basic earnings of $0.65 per share2 for the third quarter 2026, compared to $42.5 million for the second quarter 2026 and adjusted basic earnings of $0.79 per share.
As of September 30, 2026, the book value of vessels was $2,057.9 million, compared to $2,077.1 million as of June 30, 2026. The movement primarily reflects depreciation of $20.2 million, partially offset by $1.0 million of capitalized drydocking expenditures.
As of September 30, 2026, total long-term debt was $1,766.2 million, compared to $1,793.7 million as of June 30, 2026, with the decrease attributable to scheduled debt repayments made during the third quarter 2026.
Cash and cash equivalents were $370.8 million, which includes fully drawn revolving tranches under the $270 Million Facility, $290 Million Facility and the Flex Enterprise $150 Million Facility.
Net cash provided by operating activities in the third quarter 2026 was $41.8 million, compared to $76.8 million in the second quarter 2026. This decrease primarily reflects the timing of advance charter hire receipts and the lower spot revenues mentioned above.
Total equity was $704.7 million, compared to $702.5 million as at June 30, 2026. This increase in equity primarily reflects net income of $42.8 million for the period, partially offset by distributions of $40.6 million.

LNG Market Update
The third quarter of 2026 saw a sharp reversal in LNG freight rates, despite rising gas prices and continued geopolitical uncertainty. Modern two-stroke vessels earned approximately $100,000 per day in the Atlantic in early July, supported by limited prompt availability and charterers retaining vessels for trading flexibility. Conditions weakened in August as European gas prices outpaced Asian prices, reducing the incentive to ship Atlantic cargoes to Asia. The resulting decline in sailing distances coincided with increasing vessel availability. Shipbrokers reported Atlantic and Pacific rates bottoming out at approximately $10,000-$20,000 per day in early September. Current spot rates for modern tonnage are quoted by shipbrokers at approximately $30,000-$50,000 per day.
The term market softened at the shorter end of the curve. Shipbrokers reported that one-year rates declined from the high $70,000s per day at the start of the quarter to the low $50,000s by quarter-end, while longer-term rates were more resilient. Five-year rates remained around $70,000–$75,000 per day. With many charterers having secured winter cover early, demand for additional short-term employment weakened. Interest in index-linked charters increased as charterers sought flexibility amid uncertain spot rates and tonnage expected to become available in 2027.
The LNG carrier orderbook today stands at approximately 290 vessels, equivalent to around 35% of the existing fleet. Newbuilding activity slowed during the third quarter following stronger ordering in the first half of the year. Around 75 vessels were delivered during the first ten months of the year, with a further 25 deliveries expected before year-end, followed by 93 deliveries in 2027 and 74 deliveries in 2028. Newbuilding prices softened somewhat, with Korean yards quoting approximately $240-245 million for a single vessel. Vessel retirements remained limited, with seven reported demolition sales during the first nine months of the year.
For the first ten months, global LNG exports are estimated at approximately 342 million tonnes, down around 2% year-on-year, with U.S. exports rising by 20% to approximately 105 million tonnes. The strong U.S. growth was supported by increased volumes from Australia and Russia, but also Canada and Nigeria. Beginning in 2027, additional U.S. export volumes from Golden Pass, Rio Grande, Port Arthur Phase 1 and Venture Global CP2 Phase 1 are expected to enter the market.
Qatari exports declined by nearly 44 million tonnes year-on-year, more than offsetting the growth elsewhere. The disruption to Qatari exports remains a key constraint on supply. Although LNG carrier transits through the Strait of Hormuz increased during the quarter, activity remained well below pre-conflict levels and many vessels transited without transmitting their positions.
On the demand side, Egypt continued to rely on LNG to offset lower domestic production and disrupted pipeline supply. Egypt is now one of the largest importers of U.S. LNG. European demand decreased by 7% to 95 million tonnes during the first ten months of the year. European gas storage stood at approximately 73% as of the date of this report, approximately 10 percentage points below the previous year and around 15 percentage points below the five-year average. Regional differences are substantial, with Germany at around 59% and Italy at 89%. Gas prices

In Asia, high gas prices constrained demand from several major importers. These projects support longer-term LNG supply growth, although newly sanctioned capacity will not materially improve the near-term supply balance.
Finance update
In October 2026, we amended the terms of the $330 Million Sale and Leaseback for Flex Artemis and Flex Amber. The amended terms include a reduction in the margin from 215 basis points to 140 basis points, an extended repayment profile with lower periodic installments and higher purchase option amounts at the end of the lease.
In November 2026, we signed a $145.5 million sale and leaseback agreement with an Asian-based lease provider for the vessel, Flex Enterprise. Under the terms of the agreement, the vessel will be sold for a consideration of $145.5 million, with a bareboat charter back of approximately 6 years. The lease is repaid on an age-adjusted repayment profile3 of approximately 21 years. The Company has the option to terminate the lease and repurchase the vessel at a fixed price after approximately 6 years. The bareboat lease is comprised of a fixed interest element and a floating interest element based on Term SOFR, plus a margin. The Company will repay the full amount outstanding under the Flex Enterprise $150 Million Facility. The new financing is expected to be completed in the fourth quarter of 2026, subject to final documentation and customary closing conditions. As a result of this re-financing, the Company is expected to generate net cash proceeds of approximately $34 million after repayment of the amount outstanding under the Flex Enterprise $150 Million Facility.
To mitigate risks associated with fluctuations in interest rates, the Company has entered into interest rate swap transactions, whereby floating interest rates have been swapped for fixed interest rates. As of September 30, 2026, the Company has fixed interest rates on an aggregate notional principal amount of $775.0 million. The interest rate swaps have a fixed rate of interest based on the Secured Overnight Financing Rate ("SOFR"), with a weighted average fixed interest rate of 2.46% and a weighted average duration of 2.0 years.
3 The age-adjusted repayment profile is a metric that is used to calculate the repayment period of our loans and leases to zero, in years and adjusted for the age of the financed vessel. The metric is used to compare repayment profiles between facilities on vessels of different ages.

(C) Reconciliation of Total Operating Revenues to Time Charter Equivalent Income and Time Charter Equivalent Rate
Time charter equivalent rate ("TCE rate") represents the weighted average daily TCE income of our entire operating fleet. TCE rate is a measure of the average daily income performance. Our method of calculating TCE rate is determined by dividing TCE income by onhire days during a reporting period. We define TCE income as vessel operating revenues less voyage expenses. Onhire days are calculated on a vessel by vessel basis and represent the net of available days and offhire days for each vessel (owned or chartered in) in our possession during a reporting period. Available days for a vessel during a reporting period is the number of days the vessel (owned or chartered in) is in our possession during the period. Offhire days for a vessel during a reporting period is the number of days the vessel is in our possession during the period but is not operational as a result of unscheduled repairs, scheduled drydockings or special or intermediate surveys and lay-ups, if any.
