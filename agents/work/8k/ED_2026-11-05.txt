CON EDISON REPORTS 2026 THIRD QUARTER EARNINGS
NEW YORK - Consolidated Edison, Inc. (Con Edison) (NYSE: ED) today reported 2026 third quarter net income of $766 million or $2.07 a share compared with $688 million or $1.91 a share in the 2025 third quarter. Adjusted earnings (non-GAAP) were $766 million or $2.07 a share in the 2026 period compared with $686 million or $1.90 a share in the 2025 period. Adjusted earnings and adjusted earnings per share in the 2025 period exclude accretion of the basis difference of Con Edison’s equity interest in Mountain Valley Pipeline, LLC (MVP).
For the first nine months of 2026, net income was $1,998 million or $5.44 a share compared with $1,726 million or $4.84 a share in the first nine months of 2025. Adjusted earnings were $1,864 million or $5.08 a share in the 2026 period compared with $1,718 million or $4.82 a share in the 2025 period. Adjusted earnings and adjusted earnings per share in the 2026 period exclude transaction costs associated with the strategic alternatives review of Con Edison’s equity interests in MVP and Honeoye Storage Corporation (Honeoye), the gain on the sale of Con Edison’s equity interest in MVP and the effects of hypothetical liquidation at book value (HLBV) accounting for tax equity investments. Adjusted earnings and adjusted earnings per share in the 2026 and 2025 periods exclude accretion of the basis difference of Con Edison’s equity interest in MVP. Adjusted earnings and adjusted earnings per share in the 2025 period exclude adjustments to the gain and other impacts related to the sale of all of the stock of Con Edison’s former subsidiary, Con Edison Clean Energy Businesses, Inc. (the Clean Energy Businesses), in 2023.
“Con Edison is a durable, long-term investment, supported by consistent performance, financial strength, and our ability to navigate an evolving landscape,” said Tim Cawley, Chairman and CEO of Con Edison. “We’re at the center of one of the nation’s largest electrification efforts, serving a region with unique needs, complex infrastructure, and vital national assets. We remain focused on delivering world-class reliability to our customers while building the capacity and resilience New York needs to grow.
“We have a responsibility to protect our equipment and customers from increasingly extreme weather and plan to invest approximately $797 million in climate resilience from 2026 through 2030,” Cawley added. “Our robust capital investments modernize our system for customers’ changing energy needs while balancing customer costs and supporting stable returns for investors.”
“We see a bright future as we expand our earnings base with strategic upgrades to our energy delivery systems that help us maintain our industry-leading reliability and provide attractive, steady earnings opportunities,” said Kirk Andrews, Senior Vice President and CFO. “We’ve reached agreement with our regulator and other stakeholders on a new investment plan for our Steam business that is awaiting approval, demonstrating our strong ability to work with diverse parties to find consensus and make our region stronger.”

(CECONY), a regulated utility providing electric service in New York City and New York’s Westchester County, gas service in Manhattan, the Bronx, parts of Queens and parts of Westchester, and steam service in Manhattan; Orange and Rockland Utilities, Inc. State Utility Regulation • In May 2026, as part of New York State’s Fiscal Year 2026-2027 Enacted Budget, the state enacted Chapter 58 of the Laws of 2026, which, among other things, amends and adds provisions to the Public Service Law governing utility rate cases and addressing affordability. Effective for base rate filings submitted by the Utilities after January 1, 2027, Chapter 58: (i) requires the Utilities to submit, in addition to their recommended proposal, a budget-constrained rate plan that limits aggregate revenue increases to the average Consumer Price Index growth over the prior three years; (ii) directs the Utilities to return revenues above the authorized return on equity (ROE), with retention of up to 0.25% over the authorized ROE if certain criteria are met; (iii) further defines non-recoverable lobbying expenses and formalizes non-recoverability of public relations and goodwill advertising expenses; (iv) extends the NYSPSC’s authority to suspend a rate case to 14 months; and (v) authorizes approval of multi-year litigated rate plans. Chapter 58 further: (i) directs the NYSPSC to develop performance-based employee compensation targets and authorizes consideration of adjustments to ROE based on affordability performance; (ii) directs the NYSPSC to establish rules limiting recovery of rate case expenses, including legal, consultant, expert and employee costs; (iii) requires affordability analyses in rate cases; and (iv) directs the NYSPSC to adopt rules for calculating a newly created Energy Affordability Index and to issue annual affordability reports. Chapter 58 also creates a temporary and independent commission to study rising utility rates, affordability and generation, including utility-owned renewable generation. The Utilities are continuing to assess the impact of the new law and the timing of their upcoming rate case filings. • In September 2026, the NYSPSC established a proceeding to develop a methodology for calculating an energy affordability index for residential customers. The NYSPSC order (i) requires the NYSPSC to issue an annual energy affordability report, the first of which is due July 1, 2027; (ii) requires any utility filing a major rate case to include the energy affordability index showing the energy burden for their customers at current rates and at the utility's proposed rates; and (iii) authorizes the NYSPSC to install an independent utility affordability monitor if a change in rates results in a customer energy burden greater than 3% for electric or gas individually or 6% for electric and gas collectively.
