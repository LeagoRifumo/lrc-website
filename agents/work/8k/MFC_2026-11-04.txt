Manulife Financial Corporation – Third Quarter 2026 | 1

Manulife Financial Corporation (“Manulife” or the “Company”) reported its third quarter results for the
period ended September 30, 2026, delivering record core earnings1 and continued strong momentum in
Asia.
Key highlights for the third quarter of 2026 (“3Q26”) include:
•Core earnings of $2.1 billion, up 4% on a CER basis2 compared with the third quarter of 2025 (“3Q25”)
•Net income attributed to shareholders of $1.4 billion, down $0.4 billion from 3Q25
•Core EPS3 of $1.24, up 6%2 from 3Q25. EPS of $0.82, down 21%2 from 3Q25
•Core ROE3 of 17.8% and ROE of 11.8%
•LICAT ratio4 of 136%
•APE sales5, new business CSM2 and new business value (“NBV”)5 were broadly in line with 3Q25
•Global Wealth and Asset Management (“Global WAM”) net outflows5 of $3.6 billion, compared with $6.2 billion of net
outflows in 3Q25
“We delivered strong third quarter results, achieving record core EPS and 17.8% core ROE, driven by double-digit core
earnings growth in Asia and meaningful contributions from our North American insurance businesses. These results reflect the
disciplined execution of the strategy we launched a year ago and our ability to deliver sustained growth in an uncertain
environment.”
“During the quarter, we expanded customer choice through enhanced investment and protection offerings, completed Manulife

partnerships. Together, these actions are strengthening our competitive position, deepening customer relationships, and
creating long-term value for shareholders.”

we completed our annual review of actuarial methods and assumptions, resulting in a net favourable impact and reinforcing

Strategic Highlights
We are expanding customer access to wealth solutions through product enhancements and expansion
In Canada, we expanded our segregated fund lineup by adding eight new funds and broadened access to eleven existing
funds. The enhanced suite increases investment choice and flexibility for customers and advisors, helping Canadians diversify
their portfolios, build long-term wealth, and pursue their evolving financial goals.
In Global WAM, we raised US$5.4 billion of capital through the close of Comvest Credit Partners VII. This marked Manulife |
Comvest’s largest fundraise in the history of Comvest’s private credit platform1, attracting commitments from both existing and
new institutional investors and expanding the platform’s global investor relationships.
In the U.S., we broadened our ability to meet a wider range of customer protection and long-term planning needs through
enhancements to our indexed universal life and survivorship indexed universal life solutions, and the introduction of three
differentiated term life insurance solutions. Together, these enhancements and new offerings expand our protection portfolio
and provide financial professionals with greater choice and flexibility to address evolving customer needs.
We are scaling and advancing solutions as an AI-powered organization while further enhancing AI governance and
responsible use
We expanded our strategic partnership with Microsoft through a new five-year agreement. The agreement includes extending
Microsoft 365 Copilot to more than 30,000 employees to further enable productivity and innovation, as well as deploying
Microsoft Agent 365 to strengthen enterprise AI governance and drive AI solutions at enterprise scale.
In Asia, we continued to extend proven AI-enabled advisory capabilities into new markets, bringing these capabilities to the
Chinese Mainland and Malaysia to help agents analyze customer and family coverage, identify protection gaps across life,
health, and investment goals, recognize life-event triggers, and prepare for customer meetings. In Malaysia, the tool also
generates personalized talking points, including needs-based analysis questions, and recommends next steps linked to
customers' financial planning goals.
In Global WAM, we completed the rollout of an AI-enabled advisor engagement platform to all external wholesalers and
investment specialists across North America Retail, identifying four times more product opportunities and capturing richer
advisor insights. The platform also improved productivity through automated documentation, reducing meeting administration
and follow-up activities, saving approximately 30 minutes per session.
In the U.S., we scaled our Quick Quote underwriting support tool to automate more than 70% of preliminary assessments, up
from nearly 50% in the first quarter of 2026, rapidly expanding our proven AI-enabled underwriting capability to reduce average
turnaround time and optimize our utilization of underwriting expertise.
We are advancing our health and longevity leadership through innovative strategic partnerships, expanded wellness
offerings and simpler access to healthcare
In Asia, we signed a Memorandum of Understanding (“MOU”) with Ant Health2, setting out a collaboration framework covering
health management services, cross-boundary healthcare support, digital health capabilities and insurance technology. In
October, we launched the first initiative under the MOU, offering cashless direct billing through Alipay for eligible customers at
participating healthcare providers in the Chinese Mainland, making healthcare access easier.
In Canada, we began offering AI-enabled virtual physiotherapy in partnership with Sword®, a global AI health company, to
eligible Group Benefits members on disability with musculoskeletal conditions. This initiative helps address barriers to care
through convenient, personalized support from licensed Canadian clinicians while supporting faster recovery and return-to-
work outcomes.
In the U.S., we strengthened our leadership in longevity and differentiated behavioural insurance by expanding the health and
wellness offerings available through John Hancock Vitality, including Prenuvo’s advanced heart health scan and ŌURA’s latest
smart ring, providing customers access to additional tools and insights to help them live longer, healthier, better lives.

Core earnings of $2.1 billion in 3Q26, up 4% from 3Q25
The increase in core earnings reflected strong business growth in Asia and the U.S., higher revenues in Global WAM,
improved insurance experience, and the net positive impact of 3Q26 updates to actuarial methods and assumptions, partially
offset by increased expected credit loss (“ECL”) provisions and the impact of the eMPF transition in Hong Kong.

•Global WAM core earnings decreased 5%, as higher net fee income from favourable market impacts over the past 12
months and contributions from the Manulife | Comvest business were more than offset by the impact of the eMPF
transition in Hong Kong, higher workforce-related expenses and investment in business growth, favourable tax true-ups in
3Q25, and lower performance fees.

Annual review of actuarial methods and assumptions
We completed our annual review of actuarial methods and assumptions, which resulted in a net favourable impact of a
$128 million1 decrease in pre-tax fulfilment cash flows. Under International Financial Reporting Standards (as issued by the
International Accounting Standards Board) (“IFRS”) 17, the impact of the annual review of actuarial methods and assumptions
is reported in several places. The $128 million decrease in pre-tax fulfilment cash flows was comprised of a decrease in pre-
tax net income attributed to shareholders of $217 million ($175 million post-tax), a decrease in pre-tax net income attributed to
participating policyholders of $4 million ($3 million post-tax), an increase in CSM of $47 million, an increase in pre-tax other
comprehensive income attributed to shareholders of $306 million ($240 million post-tax), and a decrease in pre-tax other
comprehensive income attributed to participating policyholders of $4 million ($2 million post-tax).
The actuarial review this year included valuation model enhancements and assumption updates for U.S. life insurance riders,
morbidity and mortality assumptions in Vietnam, valuation model updates for participating products in Asia, lapse assumptions
for investment-linked products in Singapore, mortality assumptions in our U.S. life insurance business, and other methodology
refinements.

Quarterly profitability
Manulife’s net income attributed to shareholders was $1,424 million in the third quarter of 2026 (“3Q26”) compared with $1,799
million in the third quarter of 2025 (“3Q25”). Net income attributed to shareholders is comprised of core earnings (consisting of
items we believe reflect the underlying earnings capacity of the business), which amounted to $2,125 million in 3Q26
compared with $2,035 million in 3Q25, and items excluded from core earnings, which amounted to a net charge of $701 million
in 3Q26 compared with a net charge of $236 million in 3Q25. The effective tax rate on net income (loss) attributed to
shareholders was 15% in 3Q26 compared with 14% in 3Q25 primarily due to differences in the jurisdictional mix of earnings
and the impact of tax true-ups.
Net income attributed to shareholders in 3Q26 decreased $375 million compared with 3Q25, reflecting a larger net charge
from market experience and a net charge from our Vietnam operations1, partially offset by growth in core earnings. Total
market experience was a net charge of $388 million in 3Q26, primarily from lower-than-expected returns on alternative long-
duration assets (“ALDA”), mainly related to private equity, real estate and farmland investments, lower-than-expected returns
on public equities and a net charge from derivatives and hedge accounting ineffectiveness. The increase in the net charge
from market experience in 3Q26 compared with 3Q25 largely consisted of a net charge from lower-than-expected returns on
public equities in 3Q26 compared to a net gain in 3Q25 and a higher net charge from derivatives and hedge accounting
ineffectiveness, partially offset by a lower net charge from lower-than-expected returns on ALDA.
Core earnings increased $90 million or 4% on a constant exchange rate (“CER”) basis2 compared with 3Q25. The increase in
our insurance business was driven by business growth, primarily in our Asia and U.S. segments, improved net insurance
experience, and the net positive impact of 3Q26 updates to actuarial methods and assumptions, partially offset by an increase
in the provision for expected credit losses (“ECL”) in 3Q26 compared with a release in 3Q253. The improved net insurance
experience reflects improved U.S. life insurance claims experience and more favourable Canada Individual Insurance claims
experience, partially offset by unfavourable U.S. lapse experience and Canada Group Insurance claims experience in 3Q26
compared with favourable experience in 3Q25. Higher Manulife Bank earnings also contributed to the increase in core
earnings. Global Wealth and Asset Management (“Global WAM”) core earnings decreased primarily due to the impact of the
electronic Mandatory Provident Fund (“eMPF”) transition in Hong Kong, higher expenses and lower performance fees, partially
offset by higher net fee income4 and contributions from the Manulife | Comvest business5. In addition, a number of tax-related
items resulted in a net decrease in core earnings. In 3Q26, the items included favourable tax-related true-ups in Corporate and
Other and in the U.S. In 3Q25, the items included a favourable adjustment to the withholding tax accrual following the
announcement of the Comvest acquisition5, and favourable tax true-ups in Global WAM.
The annual review of actuarial methods and assumptions that flow directly through income is discussed in section D2
“Actuarial Methods and Assumptions” below.

Expense efficiency ratio
We use the expense efficiency ratio to measure progress on our expense management initiatives. It reflects core expenses
which are equal to total expenses1 less those expenses reported in items excluded from core earnings. Total expenses consist
of general expenses, directly attributable maintenance expenses and directly attributable acquisition expenses for products
measured using the premium allocation approach (“PAA”) and for other products without a CSM.
Quarterly expense efficiency ratio
The expense efficiency ratio was 42.7% in 3Q26, compared with 43.1% in 3Q25. The 0.4 percentage point improvement in
the ratio in 3Q26 compared with 3Q25 is attributed to pre-tax core earnings1 growing at a faster rate than core expenses. The
impact of the increase in core expenses mainly reflects higher workforce-related costs, primarily driven by business growth,
and continued strategic investments in transformational efforts, including AI-focused initiatives, partially offset by operational
efficiencies.
Total general expenses increased in 3Q26 compared with 3Q25, driven by the items noted above related to the overall
increase in core expenses, partially offset by a smaller increase in 3Q26 expenses reported in items excluded from core
earnings compared with 3Q25. The expenses reported in items excluded from core earnings in 3Q26 were primarily from the
amortization of acquisition-related intangible assets in Global WAM, and in 3Q25, were mainly related to the acquisition of
Comvest and the amortization of acquisition-related intangible assets.

We are expanding customer access to wealth solutions through product enhancements and expansion
In Canada, we expanded our segregated fund lineup by adding eight new funds and broadened access to eleven existing
funds. The enhanced suite increases investment choice and flexibility for customers and advisors, helping Canadians diversify
their portfolios, build long-term wealth, and pursue their evolving financial goals.

enhancements to our indexed universal life and survivorship indexed universal life solutions, and the introduction of three
differentiated term life insurance solutions. Together, these enhancements and new offerings expand our protection portfolio
and provide financial professionals with greater choice and flexibility to address evolving customer needs.
We are scaling and advancing solutions as an AI-powered organization while further enhancing AI governance and
responsible use
We expanded our strategic partnership with Microsoft through a new five-year agreement. The agreement includes extending
Microsoft 365 Copilot to more than 30,000 employees to further enable productivity and innovation, as well as deploying
Microsoft Agent 365 to strengthen enterprise AI governance and drive AI solutions at enterprise scale.
In Asia, we continued to extend proven AI-enabled advisory capabilities into new markets, bringing these capabilities to the
Chinese Mainland and Malaysia to help agents analyze customer and family coverage, identify protection gaps across life,
health, and investment goals, recognize life-event triggers, and prepare for customer meetings. In Malaysia, the tool also
generates personalized talking points, including needs-based analysis questions, and recommends next steps linked to
customers' financial planning goals.
In Global WAM, we completed the rollout of an AI-enabled advisor engagement platform to all external wholesalers and
investment specialists across North America Retail, identifying four times more product opportunities and capturing richer
advisor insights. The platform also improved productivity through automated documentation, reducing meeting administration
and follow-up activities, saving approximately 30 minutes per session.
In the U.S., we scaled our Quick Quote underwriting support tool to automate more than 70% of preliminary assessments, up
from nearly 50% in the first quarter of 2026, rapidly expanding our proven AI-enabled underwriting capability to reduce average
turnaround time and optimize our utilization of underwriting expertise.
We are advancing our health and longevity leadership through innovative strategic partnerships, expanded wellness
offerings and simpler access to healthcare
In Asia, we signed a Memorandum of Understanding (“MOU”) with Ant Health2, setting out a collaboration framework covering
health management services, cross-boundary healthcare support, digital health capabilities and insurance technology. In
October, we launched the first initiative under the MOU, offering cashless direct billing through Alipay for eligible customers at
participating healthcare providers in the Chinese Mainland, making healthcare access easier.
In Canada, we began offering AI-enabled virtual physiotherapy in partnership with Sword®, a global AI health company, to
eligible Group Benefits members on disability with musculoskeletal conditions. This initiative helps address barriers to care
through convenient, personalized support from licensed Canadian clinicians while supporting faster recovery and return-to-
work outcomes.
In the U.S., we strengthened our leadership in longevity and differentiated behavioural insurance by expanding the health and
wellness offerings available through John Hancock Vitality, including Prenuvo’s advanced heart health scan and ŌURA’s latest
smart ring, providing customers access to additional tools and insights to help them live longer, healthier, better lives.
