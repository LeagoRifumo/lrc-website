HYATT REPORTS THIRD QUARTER 2026 RESULTS
CHICAGO (October 29, 2026) - Hyatt Hotels Corporation ("Hyatt," "the Company," "we," "us," or "our") (NYSE: H) today reported third quarter 2026 results. Highlights include:
•Comparable system-wide hotels RevPAR increased 7.2%, compared to the third quarter of 2025
•Comparable system-wide all-inclusive resorts Net Package RevPAR decreased 2.5%, compared to the third quarter of 2025
•Net rooms growth for the trailing twelve months was 3.8%, or 3.9% excluding rooms from the Playa Hotels Acquisition that were removed from Hyatt's room count in the fourth quarter of 2025
•Pipeline of executed management or franchise contracts was approximately 158,000 rooms, an increase of 12.1%, compared to the third quarter of 2025
•Diluted EPS was $0.60 and Adjusted Diluted EPS was $1.27
•Net income attributable to Hyatt Hotels Corporation was $58 million and Adjusted Net Income was $122 million
•Gross fees were $327 million, an increase of 15.2%, compared to the third quarter of 2025
•Adjusted EBITDA was $295 million, an increase of 6.2%, compared to the third quarter of 2025, or an increase of 19% after adjusting for assets sold in 2025
•Repurchased 867,109 shares of Class A common stock during the third quarter for an aggregate purchase price of $147 million. Year-to-date through September 30, 2026, the Company returned $336 million to shareholders through dividends and share repurchases
•Full Year 2026 Outlook:
◦Comparable system-wide hotels RevPAR growth is projected to be between 5.0% and 5.5%, compared to the full year 2025
◦Net rooms growth is projected to be approximately 6%, compared to the full year 2025
◦Net income attributable to Hyatt Hotels Corporation is projected to be between $240 million and $310 million
◦Adjusted EBITDA is projected to be between $1,170 million and $1,200 million, an increase of 14% to 17%, compared to the full year 2025, after adjusting for the period of ownership of hotels acquired as part of the Playa Hotels Acquisition and assets sold in 2025
◦Capital returns to shareholders have been increased and are projected to be between $375 million and $425 million through dividends and share repurchases
Mark S. Hoplamazian, Chairman, President and Chief Executive Officer, said, "Our strong third quarter results, including 15% growth in Gross Fees, reinforce the strength of Hyatt's differentiated brands, growing commercial platform, and asset-light business model. Continued momentum across our business, a record development pipeline, and growing engagement among World of Hyatt members reflect increasing preference for our brands among guests, owners, and developers. We believe these competitive advantages position us to drive quality growth, durable fee earnings, increasing free cash flow, and long-term shareholder value."

Third Quarter Operational Commentary
•Luxury led RevPAR growth in the quarter. Leisure transient and group RevPAR each delivered strong growth, while business transient RevPAR increased in the low single digits. RevPAR in the United States grew 8.2%, reflecting the strength of the U.S. consumer and benefitting in part from the FIFA World Cup and the timing of the Jewish holidays.
•Net Package RevPAR decreased 2.5%, compared to the third quarter of 2025, primarily reflecting lower demand for travel to certain destinations in Mexico, partially offset by growth in the Caribbean and Europe.
•Gross fees increased 15.2%, compared to the third quarter of 2025, reflecting strong core business performance.
◦Base management fees increased 10.0%, driven by managed hotel RevPAR growth, strong performance in the United States, fees from the new management agreements from the Playa Hotels Acquisition, and new hotel openings.
◦Incentive management fees increased 21.9%, driven by fees from the new management agreements from the Playa Hotels Acquisition and strong performance in Asia Pacific.
◦Franchise and other fees increased 16.8%, driven by non-RevPAR fee contributions, RevPAR growth in the United States, and new hotel openings.
•Owned and leased segment Adjusted EBITDA declined 1%, compared to the third quarter of 2025, after adjusting for 2025 asset sales.
•Distribution segment Adjusted EBITDA declined, compared to the third quarter of 2025, primarily due to temporary factors, including hotel closures in Jamaica related to Hurricane Melissa and lower demand in Mexico.
Openings and Development
During the third quarter, the Company:
•Opened 3,808 rooms. Notable openings included Hyatt Vivid Punta Cana, marking the brand's debut in the Dominican Republic; Beldersoy Resort and SPA, the first Destination by Hyatt property in Uzbekistan and the country's first premium mountain resort; and Thompson Seville, a new addition to Hyatt's Lifestyle portfolio.
•Delivered strong development activity, resulting in 12% pipeline growth compared to the third quarter of 2025. During the quarter, more than half of signings within the Essentials portfolio in the United States were in new markets for Hyatt.
Balance Sheet and Liquidity
As of September 30, 2026, the Company reported the following:
•Total debt of $4.4 billion.
•Total liquidity of $1.8 billion, inclusive of:
◦$495 million of cash and cash equivalents and short-term investments; and
◦$1,347 million of available borrowing capacity under Hyatt's revolving credit facility, net of letters of credit outstanding.
•The Company repurchased $147 million of Class A common stock during the third quarter. Remaining share repurchase authorization as of September 30, 2026 totaled approximately $1.4 billion.
•The Company's board of directors has declared a cash dividend of $0.15 per share for the fourth quarter of 2026. The dividend is payable on December 7, 2026 to Class A and Class B stockholders of record as of November 23, 2026.

About Hyatt Hotels Corporation
Hyatt Hotels Corporation, headquartered in Chicago, is a leading global hospitality company guided by its purpose – to care for people so they can be their best. As of September 30, 2026, the Company's portfolio included more than 1,500 hotels and all-inclusive properties in 84 countries across six continents. The Company's offering includes brands in the Luxury Portfolio, including Park Hyatt®, Alila®, Miraval®, Impression by Secrets, and The Unbound Collection by Hyatt®; the Lifestyle Portfolio, including Andaz®, Thompson Hotels®, The Standard®, Dream® Hotels, The StandardX®, Breathless Resorts & Spas®, JdV by Hyatt®, Bunkhouse® Hotels, and Me and All Hotels; the Inclusive Collection, including Zoëtry® Wellness & Spa Resorts, Hyatt Ziva®, Hyatt Zilara®, Secrets® Resorts & Spas, Dreams® Resorts & Spas, Hyatt Vivid® Hotels & Resorts, Bahia Principe Hotels & Resorts, Alua Hotels & Resorts®, and Sunscape® Resorts & Spas; the Classics Portfolio, including Grand Hyatt®, Hyatt Regency®, Destination by Hyatt®, Hyatt Centric®, Hyatt Vacation Club®, and Hyatt®; and the Essentials Portfolio, including Caption by Hyatt®, Unscripted by Hyatt®, Hyatt Place®, Hyatt House®, Hyatt Studios®, Hyatt Select®, and UrCove. Subsidiaries of the Company operate the World of Hyatt® loyalty program, ALG Vacations®, Mr & Mrs Smith®, Unlimited Vacation Club®, Amstar® DMC destination management services, and Trisept Solutions® technology services. For more information, please visit www.hyatt.com.

Definitions
Adjusted Earnings Before Interest Expense, Taxes, Depreciation, and Amortization ("Adjusted EBITDA")
Adjusted EBITDA, which is a non-GAAP measure, is used throughout this earnings release. We define Adjusted EBITDA as net income (loss) attributable to Hyatt Hotels Corporation plus net income (loss) attributable to noncontrolling interests, adjusted to exclude the following items:
•payments to customers ("contra revenue"), including performance cure payments and amortization of management and hotel services agreement and franchise agreement assets ("key money assets");
•revenues for reimbursed costs;
•reimbursed costs that we intend to recover over the long term;
•stock-based compensation expense;
•transaction and integration costs;
•depreciation and amortization;
•equity earnings (losses) from unconsolidated hospitality ventures;
•interest expense;
•gains (losses) on sales of real estate and other;
•asset impairments;
•other income (loss), net; and
•benefit (provision) for income taxes.
We calculate consolidated Adjusted EBITDA by adding the Adjusted EBITDA of each of our reportable segments and eliminations to unallocated overhead expenses.
Our board of directors and executive management team focus on Adjusted EBITDA as one of the key performance and compensation measures both on a segment and on a consolidated basis. Adjusted EBITDA assists us in comparing our performance over various reporting periods on a consistent basis because it removes from our operating results the impact of items that do not reflect our core operations both on a segment and on a consolidated basis. Our Chairman, President and Chief Executive Officer, who is our chief operating decision maker, also evaluates the performance of each of our reportable segments and determines how to allocate resources to those segments, in part, by assessing the Adjusted EBITDA of each segment. In addition, the talent and compensation committee of our board of directors determines the annual variable compensation and long-term incentive compensation for certain members of our management based in part on financial measures including and/or derived from consolidated Adjusted EBITDA, segment Adjusted EBITDA, or some combination of both.
We believe Adjusted EBITDA is useful to investors because it provides investors with the same information that we use internally for purposes of assessing our operating performance and making compensation decisions and facilitates our comparison of results with our prior-period and forecasted results as well as our industry and competitors.
Adjusted EBITDA excludes certain items that can vary widely across different industries and among companies within the same industry, including interest expense and benefit or provision for income taxes, which are dependent on company specifics, including capital structure, credit ratings, tax policies, and jurisdictions in which they operate; depreciation and amortization, which are dependent on company policies including how the assets are utilized as well as the lives assigned to the assets; contra revenue, which is dependent on company policies and strategic decisions regarding payments to hotel owners; and stock-based compensation expense, which varies among companies as a result of different compensation plans companies have adopted.
We exclude revenues for reimbursed costs and reimbursed costs which relate to the reimbursement of payroll costs and system-wide services and programs that we operate for the benefit of our hotel owners as contractually we do not provide services or operate the related programs to generate a profit or bear a loss over the long term. If we collect amounts in excess of amounts spent, we have a commitment to our hotel owners to spend these amounts on the related system-wide services and programs. Additionally, if we spend in excess of amounts collected, we have a contractual right to adjust future collections or expenditures to recover prior-period costs. These timing differences are due to our discretion to spend in excess of revenues earned or less than revenues earned in a single period to ensure that the system-wide services and programs are operated in the best long-term interests of our hotel owners. Finally, we exclude other items that are not core to our operations and may vary in frequency or magnitude, such as transaction and integration costs, asset impairments, unrealized and realized gains and losses on marketable securities, and gains and losses on sales of real estate and other.
Adjusted EBITDA is not a substitute for net income (loss) attributable to Hyatt Hotels Corporation, net income (loss), or any other measure prescribed by GAAP. There are limitations to using non-GAAP measures such as Adjusted EBITDA. Although we believe that Adjusted EBITDA can make an evaluation of our operating performance more consistent because it removes items that do not reflect our core operations, other companies in our industry may define Adjusted EBITDA differently than we do.
