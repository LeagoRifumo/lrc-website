This is my first quarterly letter as CEO of Hafnia.
I have been part of building this company since its beginnings in 2010, and I took over a business with a clear strategy, a strong balance sheet, and an experienced team at sea and ashore. My role now is to build on these strong foundations and take the company further. I want to thank our former CEO Mikael Skov for 16 years of leadership, and our seafarers and onshore colleagues for their ongoing efforts, particularly through a demanding geopolitical period.
More than six months after the conflict in the Persian Gulf began, the market is still far from normal.
Crude tankers have taken most of the market uplift. Their exceptional earnings pulled more LR2 vessels into dirty trades, which reduces the effective capacity of the clean fleet. Diesel markets are tight, inventories are low, and refinery runs are set to rise from their seasonal low in October. Heading into the winter market, the product tanker market is very strong.
Against this backdrop, Hafnia delivered a net profit of USD 170.8 million in Q3 2026, bringing our year-to-date profit to USD 628.3 million. Our fee-based business generated USD 6.3 million. Scheduled drydockings resulted in approximately 259 off-hire days in the quarter, and we expect approximately 99 off-hire days in Q4.
Our average fleet TCE1 for Q3 was USD 32,708 per day. As of 13 November 2026, 74% of our Q4 earning days are covered at an average of USD 55,148 per day. That supports a strong end to the year.
At the end of the third quarter, our net asset value (NAV2) rose to approximately USD 5.2 billion, up USD 0.8 billion from Q2 2026. This is equivalent to USD 9.80 (~NOK 94.16) per share. Higher vessel values across all segments amid a strong market and a higher value for our investment in TORM have driven the increase. Our net Loan-to-Value (LTV) ratio fell further to 11.9% at the end of the quarter, primarily reflecting strong operational cash flow generation and proceeds from our recent equity raise.
We remain strong believers in industry consolidation. However, our approaches to TORM have not been reciprocated, and we believe continued resistance and the passage of time would erode the benefits we had hoped to deliver for shareholders. We will therefore pursue other growth and consolidation opportunities to deliver value to our shareholders. In the coming quarters, we plan to exercise eight purchase options totaling approximately USD 235 million, adding the vessels to our owned fleet at prices well below their current market value. The investment in TORM has been financially successful, and after careful consideration of our options, the Board has determined that it would be best to exit our investment in TORM, preferably through a distribution to our shareholders of all our TORM shares as a special dividend.
The value of such a distribution would be well in excess of the maximum dividend distribution contemplated by our dividend policy and would also satisfy our dividend obligations for both Q3 and Q4 2026. If successfully completed, this distribution would pass the value of our TORM shares directly to shareholders and let each shareholder decide whether to hold or sell.
Based on TORM's closing share price of USD 33.73 on November 17, 2026, the special dividend would have a total value of approximately USD 686.6 million, or USD 1.2826 per Hafnia share. Hafnia would distribute 1 TORM share for every 26.3 Hafnia shares held, equivalent to 0.0380 TORM shares per Hafnia share. Entitlements that do not add up to a whole TORM share would be paid equivalent in cash, currently at USD 1.2826 for each Hafnia share they relate to. The special dividend would replace our cash dividends for Q3 and Q4 2026. The ratio would be fixed in shares, not dollars, and there would be no adjustment if the TORM share price moves before payment. Upon completion, the distribution would reduce Hafnia’s NAV by approximately USD 1.28 per share. For more information, please see our separate announcement regarding the Company’s plans for the potential special dividend.

Market
Market Fundamentals | The third quarter saw a steady but uneven recovery in Middle East oil flows after the severe disruption of the second quarter. According to the International Energy Agency (IEA), global oil supply rose by 2.3 million barrels per day (mb/d) month-on-month to 105.4 mb/d in October, driven by Gulf producers. Crude exports from the region increased sharply, and flows through the Strait of Hormuz grew by 0.4 mb/d to 11.8 mb/d. Ship-to-ship transfers in the Gulf of Oman helped lift these flows. In those transfers, cargoes shuttled through the Strait are moved onto other tankers for onward delivery. These inefficiencies have contributed to exceptional crude tanker rates. The dislocation has also tightened product markets, particularly diesel. Although refined product exports from the Middle East recovered to 2.6 mb/d in October, highest level since the conflict began, they remain 44% below February levels. Diesel inventories fell at a time when they would normally build, ahead of autumn refinery maintenance and peak winter demand. Middle East net diesel exports in October were 0.8 mb/d lower year-on-year and Russian exports were down by 0.4 mb/d, only partly offset by higher supply from other sources. For product tankers, the effect is mixed but increasingly supportive. In our view, lower exports from Russia and the Middle East have shifted sourcing to alternative suppliers, lengthening some trade routes and adding tonne-miles. Inventories are also low, with global oil stocks being observed at 462 mb below end-of February levels at the end of October.
Forward View | The outlook remains highly uncertain and depends on security in the Strait of Hormuz and the pace of recovery in Middle Eastern product exports. The recent escalation in attacks on regional energy infrastructure and tanker traffic threatens further gains, and damage to refineries continues to hold back product exports. High prices and a weaker economic outlook are also weighing on demand, particularly for diesel. The IEA now forecasts global oil demand to fall by 2.7 mb/d in 2026 and recover by 2.0 mb/d in 2027, leaving consumption 0.6% below 2025 levels. Inventories, however, remain supportive of seaborne trade. Global refinery runs are forecast to rise from a seasonal low of 81.3 mb/d in October to 84.7 mb/d by year-end. IEA member countries have also backed faster emergency stock releases, with priority on diesel. In our view, rebuilding depleted strategic and commercial stocks should add further cargo demand, although the transition may be volatile. For 2027, the IEA expects global oil supply to rise by 6.6 mb/d, well ahead of demand, as OPEC+ production recovers and non-OPEC+ output keeps growing. This would allow inventories to be rebuilt, but only if Gulf flows recover sustainably and the security situation stabilizes. Tanker demand depends heavily on geopolitics, but the supply side is more predictable, and our view is unchanged. With crude tanker earnings at exceptional levels, more LR2s are moving into the Aframax trade. This reduces effective supply in the product tanker market and could also limit the net growth in clean-trading capacity from the existing orderbook, as we believe this migration pattern will continue. While any easing of OFAC sanctions could bring some tonnage back into the mainstream market, we expect the impact to be limited. A significant share of OFAC-sanctioned tankers remains subject to UK or EU sanctions, limiting their access to certain trades. Furthermore, the relatively advanced age of these vessels could delay their return to mainstream trading, given potential maintenance, inspection, insurance, and commercial requirements.
