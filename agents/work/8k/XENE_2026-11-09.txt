VANCOUVER, BC and BOSTON, MA, November 9, 2026 (GLOBE NEWSWIRE) – Xenon Pharmaceuticals Inc. (Nasdaq: XENE), a neuroscience-focused biopharmaceutical company dedicated to drug discovery, clinical development, and commercialization of life-changing therapeutics for patients in need, today reported financial results for the third quarter ended September 30, 2026 and provided a business update.
“We've made significant progress advancing azetukalner with our recent NDA submission to the FDA for focal seizures and through ongoing scientific exchange opportunities. We continue to have great confidence in azetukalner’s product profile in epilepsy based on strong efficacy and consistent safety across more than 1,500 patient-years of data, which has been reiterated in conversations with both epileptologists and neurologists,” said Ian Mortimer, President and Chief Executive Officer of Xenon. “Following the completion of enrollment in X-NOVA2, we are working toward our first Phase 3 data readout in psychiatry with topline results now expected in December 2026. We also are excited about the growing value of our earlier stage pipeline as seen through the recent announcement of positive Phase 1 data for our novel, non-opioid pain programs, XEN1701 and XEN1120, which we are planning to progress to Phase 2 proof-of-concept studies in acute pain in the first half of 2027.”
Business Highlights and Anticipated Milestones
Azetukalner Development Program
Azetukalner is a novel, potent KV7 potassium channel opener in late-stage development for multiple potential indications, including two in epilepsy – focal seizures (FS) and primary generalized tonic-clonic seizures (PGTCS) – as well as neuropsychiatric disorders, including major depressive disorder (MDD) and bipolar depression (BPD).
Epilepsy Programs
•
Xenon submitted its New Drug Application (NDA) for azetukalner in FS to the U.S. Food & Drug Administration (FDA) in September 2026.
•
Xenon presented six abstracts at the 16th European Epilepsy Congress (EEC) in September 2026, including an encore oral presentation of the X-TOLE2 topline efficacy and safety data, as well as a new poster highlighting azetukalner’s differentiated KV7 mechanism of action and rational polytherapy potential.
•
The Company expects multiple new azetukalner and epilepsy data presentations at the American Epilepsy Society (AES) 2026 Annual Meeting, taking place December 4-8, 2026, in Denver, Colorado.
•
The Company expects to initiate X-FIRST, a new Phase 3b/4 study, which is designed to evaluate azetukalner as a first adjunctive antiseizure medication (ASM) in patients with FS. X-FIRST is a U.S.-based multicenter, open-label study with a 52-week treatment period, where azetukalner would be added into the treatment regimens of patients who are already taking an SV2A modulator or sodium channel blocker, and are less refractory relative to the X-TOLE and X-TOLE2 study populations with a lower baseline seizure frequency. The study is planned for initiation in Q1 2027.
•
The Phase 3 X-TOLE3 study of azetukalner in FS continues to enroll and is intended to support regulatory submissions outside of the U.S. X-TOLE3 enrollment outside of Japan is expected to complete in 2026.
•
The Phase 3 X-ACKT study of azetukalner in PGTCS continues to enroll and is intended to support regulatory submissions for an additional epilepsy indication.
Neuropsychiatry Programs
•
Xenon completed enrollment in the Phase 3 X-NOVA2 study in MDD with approximately 360 total patients; the topline data readout is now expected in December 2026.
•
The Company announced in September that it had initiated a voluntary pause on new patient enrollment in the ongoing Phase 3 clinical studies of azetukalner in MDD and BPD. Patients currently enrolled in the ongoing studies, including both open label extension studies, remain active.
Clinical Programs for Pain
Xenon continues to expand its portfolio of potent, selective ion channel modulators using the Company’s strong heritage in human genetics, deep understanding of ion channel biology, and expertise in novel chemistries. This includes clinical-stage candidates targeting NaV1.7 and KV7, which are important novel targets for non-opioid pain therapeutics.
NaV1.7 Programs
•
At the International Association for the Study of Pain (IASP) 2026 World Congress on Pain in October 2026, Xenon presented positive data from its Phase 1 Single Ascending Dose (SAD)/Multiple Ascending Dose (MAD) study in healthy adult participants for XEN1701 targeting NaV1.7. XEN1701 achieved Xenon’s target profile, including desired high levels of NaV1.7 receptor occupancy in both the peripheral and central nervous system, and was generally well-tolerated with no notable autonomic cardiovascular effects. Additionally, new preclinical data in cynomolgus monkeys demonstrated that a NaV1.7 inhibitor provided significant pain reductions in capsaicin-evoked heat pain. Based on the positive Phase 1 results, Xenon expects to initiate a Phase 2 proof-of-concept (POC) study for XEN1701 in acute pain in H1 2027.
•
A Phase 1 SAD/MAD study in healthy adult participants is ongoing for XEN1720, Xenon’s second clinical-stage candidate targeting NaV1.7.
KV7 Program
•
At the IASP 2026 World Congress on Pain, Xenon presented positive data from its Phase 1 SAD/MAD study in healthy adult participants for XEN1120 targeting KV7. XEN1120 achieved plasma levels predictive of analgesic effects based on preclinical modeling and was generally well-tolerated. Additionally, new preclinical data in cynomolgus monkeys demonstrated that a KV7 modulator provided significant pain reductions in capsaicin-evoked heat pain. Based on the positive Phase 1 results, Xenon expects to initiate a Phase 2 POC study for XEN1120 in acute pain in H1 2027.
Early-Stage R&D and Partnered Programs for Epilepsy
Beyond azetukalner, Xenon is committed to advancing additional novel treatment approaches to address significant unmet needs in epilepsy.
•
IND-enabling studies are ongoing for the Company’s oral small molecule NaV1.1 program. Preclinical data suggest that targeting NaV1.1 could potentially address the underlying cause and symptoms of Dravet syndrome.
•
In collaboration with Neurocrine Biosciences, a Phase 1b study is ongoing for NBI-921355, an investigational, selective inhibitor of voltage-gated sodium channels NaV1.2 and NaV1.6 in development for the potential treatment of certain types of epilepsy. Data from the Phase 1b study are expected in 2027.

Q3 2026 Financial Results
•
Cash, cash equivalents and marketable securities were $1,153.5 million as of September 30, 2026, compared to $586.0 million as of December 31, 2025. Based on current operating plans, Xenon anticipates having sufficient cash to fund operations into 2029. As of September 30, 2026, there were 98,275,278 common shares and 1,507,885 pre-funded warrants outstanding.
•
Research and development expenses were $95.6 million for the quarter ended September 30, 2026, compared to $77.1 million for the same period in 2025. The increase in research and development expenses for the period was primarily attributable to the ongoing azetukalner Phase 3 clinical studies in MDD and BPD and manufacturing activities to support potential commercialization and launch readiness of azetukalner, the ongoing XEN1720 Phase 1 clinical study, as well as increased personnel-related costs due to an increase in employee headcount and stock-based compensation expense.
•
General and administrative expenses were $24.4 million for the quarter ended September 30, 2026, compared to $19.3 million for the same period in 2025. The increase in general and administrative expenses for the period was primarily attributable to personnel-related costs due to an increase in employee headcount and stock-based compensation expense and an increase in professional and consulting fees.
•
Other income was $11.5 million for the quarter ended September 30, 2026, compared to $6.1 million for the same period in 2025. The increase in other income for the period was primarily attributable to higher interest income.
•
Net loss was $110.4 million for the quarter ended September 30, 2026, compared to $90.9 million for the same period in 2025. The increase in net loss for the period was primarily attributable to higher research and development expenses driven by the azetukalner and pain programs and higher personnel-related costs, higher general and administrative expenses driven by higher personnel-related costs and professional and consulting fees, partially offset by higher interest income.

About Azetukalner
Azetukalner is a novel, potent KV7 potassium channel opener currently in development for the treatment of epilepsy, major depressive disorder and bipolar depression. It represents the most advanced, clinically validated potassium channel modulator in late-stage clinical development. Azetukalner is designed to open potassium channels in the central nervous system, allowing potassium ions to flow and hyperpolarizing neurons. This process helps reduce excessive neuronal firing, which is a key contributor to several neurologic and psychiatric disorders.
