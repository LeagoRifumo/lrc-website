Fermi Announces New Agreements with Investment-Grade Hyperscaler and Third Quarter 2026 Financial Results
Investment grade hyperscaler commits ~$200 million to advance development of 600 MW of power capacity at Project Matador
DALLAS, November 12, 2026 -- Fermi Inc. (NASDAQ/LSE: FRMI), operating as Fermi America™ (“Fermi” or the “Company”), today announced its third quarter 2026 financial results and multiple new agreements with an investment-grade hyperscaler to advance 600 megawatts (“MW”) of power capacity at Project Matador. A conference call is scheduled for 9 a.m. Eastern Time / 2 p.m. British Time today, November 12, 2026. Accompanying slides and prepared remarks can be found at https://investor.fermiamerica.com. Participation details are included in this release.
“Hyperscalers want sites that are ready with permitted power, and Fermi’s Project Matador is one of few that can meet that demand today,” said Lee McIntire, Fermi Chief Executive Officer. “Our headstart in speed to power is a key reason an investment-grade hyperscaler has signed up for 600 megawatts and committed more than $200 million up front to accelerate construction at Project Matador with an additional $90 million contemplated. We believe this is just the start of converting our strong pipeline of customers. If we successfully turn this pipeline of prospective customers into leases, it would completely fill our 2.2 gigawatts of current capacity. This gives us growing confidence in our path forward and puts us in strong position to evolve from pre-revenue, pre-lease to the next phase of growth.”
Tenant Agreement Updates
Investment-Grade Hyperscaler Agreements. Fermi has entered into agreements with an undisclosed investment-grade hyperscale customer to advance development of an initial 600 MW of power capacity beginning in 2027 at Project Matador. The customer is making financial and development commitments ahead of finalizing a lease, reflecting the importance of securing access to power within its targeted delivery timeline. These financial and development commitments include:
● | $200 million prepayment agreement: Provides for substantial upfront customer funding in connection with the proposed 600 MW development.

● | $15 million early construction and site development agreement: Establishes a framework for advancing site preparation and construction activities as soon as possible to maintain the customer’s desired schedule.

Additionally, Fermi and the customer are finalizing a $90 million structural steel and building materials purchase agreement, which would enable the customer to leverage critical long-lead materials Fermi secured in advance, accelerating the customer’s planned construction of multiple data center facilities on Project Matador.
These agreements demonstrate Fermi’s strategy of investing early in infrastructure readiness to enable faster customer development, attract customer capital earlier in the project lifecycle, and translate our power and infrastructure advantages into long-term revenue opportunities with little or no dilution.
The customer intends to use Fermi’s infrastructure and advance development work to accelerate its deployment at Project Matador. The parties continue to work toward a definitive long-term lease and finalization of the steel purchase agreement. The customer’s identity remains confidential.
TensorWave Lease Termination. Fermi and TensorWave have terminated the Data Center Lease and Services Agreement signed in August 2026. The AMD credit support agreement associated with TensorWave is also terminated.
Texas Tech University Partnership
Fermi and the Texas Tech University System maintain a strong relationship and continue to work together to advance Project Matador, including the conditions under the land lease that precede vertical construction. Earlier this week, Texas Tech University System Chancellor, Brandon Creighton, issued a statement reaffirming the partnership: “Texas Tech is proud of our partnership with Fermi and remains firmly committed to the success of Project Matador.”

About Fermi’s Project Matador
Fermi America is where AI gets power. Fermi America is developing one of the world’s largest advanced energy and AI ecosystems in the Texas Panhandle. The site, called Project Matador, is secured or under long-term lease with nearly $1.7 billion invested in buildout to date. Fermi designed the project to solve the single biggest constraint in AI infrastructure: access to large-scale, reliable power on a timeline that meets customer demand – delivered behind the meter and on a private grid built and operated on site. Subject to entering into binding customer agreements, the project is expected to ramp to approximately 17 GW, with capital deployment matched to commercial commitments.

About Fermi America™
Fermi America™ (Nasdaq & LSE: FRMI) develops next-generation private electric grids that deliver highly redundant power at gigawatt scale to support next-generation intelligence and AI compute. Fermi America™ combines cutting-edge technology with a deep bench of proven world-class multi-disciplinary leaders with a combined 25 GW of experience, to create one of the world’s largest, 17 GW next-gen private grid, helping ensure America’s energy and AI dominance. The behind-the-meter Project Matador campus is expected to integrate the nation’s biggest combined-cycle natural gas project, one of the largest clean, new nuclear power complexes in America, utility grid power, solar power, and battery energy storage, to support hyperscale AI and advanced computing. For additional information visit www.fermiamerica.com.
About the Texas Tech University System
Established in 1996, the Texas Tech University System is one of the top public university systems in the nation, consisting of five universities - Texas Tech University, Texas Tech University Health Sciences Center, Angelo State University, Texas Tech University Health Sciences Center El Paso and Midwestern State University.
Headquartered in Lubbock, Texas, the TTU System is a more than $3 billion enterprise focused on advancing higher education, health care, research and outreach with approximately 21,000 employees and 64,000 students, more than 400,000 alums, a statewide economic impact of $19.2 billion and an endowment valued at $3 billion. In its short history, the TTU System has grown tremendously and is nationally acclaimed, operating at 20 academic locations in 16 cities (15 in Texas, 1 international).
In addition, the TTU System is one of only nine in the nation to offer programs for undergraduate, medical, law, nursing, pharmacy, dental and veterinary education among other academic areas.

Non-GAAP Financial Measures This presentation includes NOI, which is a non-GAAP financial measure. The Company defines NOI as expected fixed lease payments under the lease (base rent and fixed capacity charges, including fixed contractual escalators) less expected non-reimbursable operating expenses of the data center and the power generation assets that serve it, including operations and maintenance, long-term service agreement costs, insurance, property taxes, ground rent, an allocation of campus common area costs and an operating cost contingency. This measure excludes variable power charges passed through to the tenant and the associated variable power costs, and exclude depreciation and amortization, selling, general and administrative expenses, interest expense and capital expenditures. The Company believes this measure is useful to investors in evaluating the expected operating performance of a lease, and management uses them for that purpose. It has limitations as an analytical tool and should not be considered in isolation or as a substitute for measures prepared in accordance with U.S. generally accepted accounting principles ("GAAP"). Operating income is the GAAP measure most directly comparable to NOI. The Company has not provided a reconciliation of this measure to operating income because the facility has not commenced operations and the Company cannot predict without unreasonable effort certain items required to calculate operating income, principally depreciation, which depends on final capitalized project costs and placed-in-service dates, and the allocation of selling, general and administrative expenses. These items could be significant. Other companies, including real estate investment trusts, may calculate similarly titled measures differently.
