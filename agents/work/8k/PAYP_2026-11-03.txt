PayPay Corporation Reports
Second Quarter Ended September 30, 2026 Financial Results
Another strong quarter, with Total Revenue up 28% year-on-year, Profit for the period of ¥18.4 billion and Adjusted EBITDA Margin of 36%, exceeding guidance
Raises guidance for the fiscal year ending March 31, 2027, with Total Revenue expected to reach ¥474 to ¥478 billion and Adjusted EBITDA of ¥159 to ¥163 billion
TOKYO, Japan, November 4 (November 3 EST), 2026 – PayPay Corporation (NASDAQ: PAYP) ("PayPay" or the "Company") today released its unaudited financial results for the second quarter ended September 30, 2026, prepared in accordance with IFRS Accounting Standards, as well as managerial results.
Financial Highlights
Second Quarter ended September 30, 2026
•
Total Revenue - Total Revenue grew 28% year-on-year to ¥118.1 billion, propelled by solid double-digit momentum across both segments—with Payment segment revenue up 27% and Financial Service segment revenue up 38% year-on-year. Our high-frequency payments touchpoint continues to provide a foundation for deepening customer relationships across credit card, banking and securities services.
o
Payment Segment - Total Revenue grew 27% year-on-year to ¥96.7 billion, outpacing the 20% year-on-year growth in Payment Segment GMV (Gross Merchandise Value). This reflected both solid GMV growth and an increase in Take Rate, including payment-related revenue. GMV increased 20% year-on-year to ¥5.56 trillion. This solid growth was achieved despite temporary headwinds from a high prior-year comparison driven by elevated "Hometown Tax Donation(1)" demand in September 2025 ahead of government rule changes, as well as prolonged rainfall during the quarter, particularly in September 2026, that weighed on offline payment activity. Online channel GMV increased 40% year-on-year. PayPay Credit GMV (payments made by linking a PayPay Card to the PayPay app) and PayPay Card GMV (payments made using a physical PayPay Card) increased 25% and 28% year-on-year, respectively. Increased usage of PayPay Credit and PayPay Card continued to support GMV growth, driven by promotional initiatives including the “Super PayPay Festival” and campaigns linked to SoftBank mobile plans. Take Rate increased 0.1 percentage point year-on-year to 1.74%. This increase was supported by a higher share of online payments in GMV, increased interest income from expanding credit card financing balances, and lower point expenses deducted from Total Revenue following the comprehensive review of point reward programs in June 2026. Specific promotional campaigns with municipalities also contributed. Online payments accounted for 19% of combined PayPay Balance and PayPay Credit GMV, up 3 percentage points year-on-year. In addition, credit card financing balances continued to expand, led by revolving, installment payment and cash advances balances, which increased 26% year-on-year on a combined basis.
•
PayPay MTU (Monthly Transacting Users) - Reached 42.22 million, up 9% year-on-year, or 3.32 million users. Registered users increased 6% to 75.6 million. With PayPay MTU growth outpacing PayPay registered user growth, the active rate—calculated as PayPay MTU divided by registered users—increased 1 percentage point year-on-year to 56%.
•
Payment Segment Monthly GMV per MTU - Increased 9% year-on-year to ¥43,746. Broader use of PayPay Credit and PayPay Card alongside PayPay Balance supported higher transaction frequency and a higher average ticket size. Monthly GMV per MTU also continued to trend upward across all customer cohorts grouped by registration month.
1
•
Financial Service Segment - Total Revenue grew 38% year-on-year to ¥22.9 billion, driven primarily by increased interest income. The number of deposit accounts at PayPay Bank reached 10.45 million, while deposit balances increased 16% year-on-year to ¥2.4 trillion. Loan balances increased 37% year-on-year to ¥1.4 trillion, reflecting growth in mortgages, consumer and business loans. Growth in interest-earning assets, including loans and investments, together with higher interest rates following policy rate hikes, drove a 70% year-on-year increase in interest income. PayPay Securities accounts increased 28% year-on-year to 1.88 million.
•
Total Transaction Cost - Total Transaction Cost represented 24% of Total Revenue, compared with 22% in the same period of the previous fiscal year. The increase largely reflected higher costs associated with the rising interest rate environment and higher credit costs associated with the growth in credit card financing balances. At the same time, we continued to improve efficiency in settlement-related costs. Settlement-related costs increased 13% year-on-year on a consolidated basis, well below the 20% growth in Total GMV. This cost efficiency was supported by an improved funding source mix following the "Changes to Other Credit Card Usage on PayPay" implemented in July 2026.
•
Revenue Growth and Operating Leverage Improved Profitability - Revenue grew in both our Payment and Financial Service segments. While Total Transaction Cost increased with the expansion of GMV and financial balances, revenue growth outpaced operating expense growth, resulting in operating leverage. In addition, we curbed the increase in operating expenses through measures including efforts to contain growth in point reward expenses through revision of our point rewards program in June 2026, a shift toward funding sources with relatively lower costs, and continued control of outsourcing and other expenses. These factors contributed to a 55% year-over-year increase in Adjusted EBITDA to ¥42.1 billion and a 6-percentage-point increase in Adjusted EBITDA margin to 36%. Profit for the period was ¥18.4 billion, down 75% year-on-year. The prior-year period included a one-time tax benefit of ¥57.5 billion resulting from the recognition of deferred tax assets at PayPay Corporation. Excluding this benefit from the prior-year period, profit for the period increased year-on-year.
(1)
Hometown Tax Donation (Furusato Nozei): A Japanese tax incentive program that allows taxpayers to donate to local municipalities of their choice. In turn, donors receive tax credits for income and resident taxes (for amounts exceeding ¥2,000) and typically receive local specialty products as thank-you gifts.

(1) Adjusted EBITDA is defined as profit for the period plus income tax expense (benefit), share of loss of investments accounted for using the equity method, depreciation and amortization, loss on disposal of property and equipment and intangible assets, share-based payment expense, amortization of contract cost, listing-related expenses, M&A-related expenses and net interest expense (income) from corporate borrowings and treasury assets. Share of profit (loss) of investments accounted for using the equity method includes share of loss of a joint venture accounted for using the equity method.
(2) Adjusted EBITDA Margin is calculated as Adjusted EBITDA divided by Total Revenue.
(3) Total GMV, or gross merchandise value, is defined as the total of PayPay Balance GMV, PayPay Credit GMV, PayPay Card GMV and PayPay Bank Visa Debit Card GMV, excluding the GMV of cancelled transactions. PayPay Bank Visa Debit Card GMV is defined as payments made using PayPay Bank Visa Debit Card (physical card) and Cardless Visa Debit transaction volume for both personal and corporate use, excluding the GMV of PayPay Debit and ATM withdrawal amounts when using the cash card function, excluding the GMV of any cancelled transactions.
(4) PayPay Balance GMV is defined as payments made using PayPay Balance, PayPay Debit, PayPay Balance Card, other credit card payment linked to the PayPay app and payments made through other payment services and networks such as Alipay+ and HIVEX® via PayPay code payment, excluding top-ups to PayPay Balance with PayPay Card and excluding the GMV of cancelled transactions.
(5) PayPay Credit GMV is defined as payments made using PayPay Credit, top-ups to PayPay Balance made using PayPay Card and GMV made by linking a PayPay Card to the PayPay app without linking a PayPay account, excluding the GMV of cancelled transactions.

(6) PayPay Card GMV is defined as payment made using PayPay Card (physical card), excluding top-ups to PayPay Balance with PayPay Card and excluding the GMV of cancelled transactions.
(7) Payment Segment GMV is defined as the total of PayPay Balance GMV, PayPay Credit GMV and PayPay Card GMV, excluding the GMV of cancelled transactions.
