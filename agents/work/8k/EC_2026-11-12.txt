MESSAGE FROM THE CEO
I assume the responsibility of leading Ecopetrol at a pivotal moment for the company. We are embarking on a new phase focused on strengthening business competitiveness, restoring market confidence and executing a strategy aimed at sustainable value creation. To achieve this, we are building a high-level executive team that will enable us to capture opportunities in exploration, production of oil and gas, and enhance the performance of our core businesses.
Our commitment is clear: to manage capital with discipline, maximize investment profitability and deliver competitive returns. We will work to strengthen free cash flow to support growth and dividends, maintain healthy debt levels, foster an enduring culture of cost restraint, efficiency and discipline, and ensure timely, reliable and transparent financial information.
Operationally, we will focus on growing reserves through value-focused barrels, strengthening exploration activities, and pursuing new growth opportunities in Colombia and abroad. At the same time, we will support efforts to strengthen the country’s energy and natural gas supply security and reliability at competitive prices, including advancing key offshore developments, while attracting investment, fostering strategic partnerships and revitalizing the oil and gas industry under Ecopetrol’s leadership.
We recognize the challenges in our operating environment: geopolitical volatility, rising logistics and financing costs, and natural field decline, require disciplined execution, supported by the strengths of our integrated model, the development of long-term growth projects, our competitive advantage in heavy crude marketing, and close coordination with the National Government to restore the country’s energy sovereignty.
Our commitment is to translate this vision into tangible results. We will act with financial discipline, operational excellence and rigorous capital allocation to strengthen Ecopetrol’s competitiveness, restore market confidence and create value for all our shareholders.
Joaquín Gutiérrez
Ecopetrol S.A. CEO

Highlights
· | Financial Results: In 3Q26, the pricing environment was a key driver of results, helping offset operating pressures arising from lower production and scheduled maintenance at Barrancabermeja Refinery. During the first nine months of the year, Ecopetrol improved its net income by 83% compared to the same period of 2025, driven primarily by a favorable pricing environment that, together with higher refinery throughput, helped offset a higher tax burden (wealth tax and the higher corporate income tax surcharge (15%)) and a lower exchange rate.

Sales Revenue
Sales revenue in 3Q26 totaled COP 35.1 trillion, up +17.5%, equivalent to COP +5.2 trillion compared to 3Q25, reflecting the net effect of:
· | Sales of crude oil, gas and refined products, before including Brava Energía, increased by COP +4.5 trillion, mainly due to the favorable effect of the weighted-average selling price of the sales basket, which reached 105.6 USD/bl, up +36.0 USD/bl from the comparable period. · | The consolidation of Brava Energía’s September results, which contributed COP +1.1 trillion to the Ecopetrol Group’s revenue.

· | A decline in service revenue (COP -0.4 trillion), mainly due to a lower impact from contractual indexation factors on energy tariffs at ISA Brasil.

Sales revenue for the nine months ended September totaled COP 103.9 trillion, up +14.3%, equivalent to COP +13.0 trillion compared to the same period of the previous year, reflecting the net effect of: i) higher sales of crude oil, gas and refined products excluding Brava Energía (COP +11.2 trillion), mainly due to the price, exchange rate and volume factors described for the quarter; ii) the inclusion of Brava Energía’s September revenue (COP +1.0 trillion); and iii) higher revenue from power transmission services and toll roads (COP +0.7 trillion), mainly due to progress on power and toll road projects, the performance of toll road concessions and growth in telecommunications at ISA.

Cost of Sales
Cost of sales increased by +5.4%, equivalent to COP +1.1 trillion, in 3Q26 compared to 3Q25, reflecting the net effect of:
· | Higher purchase costs for crude oil and refined products, associated with higher international benchmark prices (COP +3.7 trillion).

· | Higher purchase volumes, amounting to COP +1.0 trillion, mainly due to: i) higher fuel imports (COP +0.6 trillion, +19.4 mboed) to meet domestic demand during major maintenance at Barrancabermeja Refinery; and ii) increased crude oil purchases and sales with third parties (COP +0.5 trillion, +19.6 mboed).

· | The inclusion of Brava Energía’s September cost of sales of COP + 0.5 trillion.

· | An increase in operating and other costs (COP +0.1 trillion), mainly due to the inflationary impact on rates and services, partially offset by efficiencies captured during the period.

· | These effects were offset by the positive impact of a lower average exchange rate on foreign currency costs (COP -3.1 trillion).

· | Inventory movements (COP -1.1 trillion) due to lower crude oil deliveries and the buildup of refined product inventories to meet future commercial commitments.

Cost of sales for the nine months ending September totaled COP 62.5 trillion, up +1.3%, equivalent to COP +0.8 trillion compared to the same period of the previous year. This change was primarily explained by higher purchase prices for crude oil and refined products (COP +7.1 trillion), the consolidation of Brava’s costs (+COP 0.5 trillion) and increased operating and other costs (COP +0.2 trillion), in line with the factors described for the quarter. These impacts were largely offset by the favorable effect of the lower exchange rate on foreign currency costs (COP -7.0 trillion).
Operating and Exploration Expenses
Operating and exploration expenses in 3Q26 increased by +17.5%, equivalent to COP +0.5 trillion compared to 3Q25, mainly due to: i) recognition of the wealth tax of COP +0.3 trillion; ii) fuel losses of COP +0.2 trillion; and iii) the effect of consolidating Brava’s expenses of COP +0.1 trillion. These effects were partially offset by the business combination gain associated with ISA’s acquisition of Madeira of COP -0.1 trillion.
For the nine-month period, operating and exploration expenses increased by 15.4%, equivalent to COP +1.2 trillion, compared to the same period of 2025, mainly due to: i) recognition of the wealth tax of COP +0.9 trillion; ii) higher expenses associated with fuel losses and repairs to transportation infrastructure of COP +0.4 trillion; iii) increased exploration activity, with asset write-offs of COP +0.3 trillion; and iv) the effect of consolidating Brava of COP +0.1 trillion. These effects were partially offset by lower taxes, mainly those associated with the state of internal disturbance measures applicable in 2025, of COP -0.3 trillion, and by the business combination gain associated with ISA’s acquisition of Madeira and other effects of COP -0.2 trillion.

Income Tax
In 3Q26, the effective tax rate reached 37.7%, compared to 31.2% in 3Q25, and stood at 37.3% for 9M26, compared to 32.1% in 9M25. The increase was mainly due to recognition of the wealth tax, which is not deductible for income tax purposes, and the higher income tax surtax applicable to Ecopetrol S.A. and Hocol S.A., which rose from 0% in 2025 to 15% in 2026, based on the projected year-end Brent price. These effects were partially offset by stronger results at companies operating under special tax regimes, such as Cartagena Refinery.

Update on the customs adjustment proceedings initiated by DIAN concerning VAT on fuel imports
To date, Ecopetrol and Refinería de Cartagena have filed two and six actions, respectively, seeking annulment and restoration of rights against DIAN before the Administrative Courts of Cundinamarca and Bolívar in connection with VAT claims on fuel imports. Litigation has progressed through the procedural stages, and 7 of the 8 cases have been admitted by the respective courts.
In addition, on October 13, 2026, DIAN issued Ruling No. 100202208-1829, partially reconsidering its previous interpretation and confirming that VAT incurred on imports of tangible goods is a tax matter and, therefore, if the taxpayer files an action for annulment and restoration of rights, the tax authority may not pursue enforced collection against the taxpayer or impose precautionary measures, such as asset seizures.
7
Consequently, until the courts issue final decisions on the relief sought in the actions brought by Ecopetrol and Refinería de Cartagena, the disputed amounts (VAT, penalties and interest) are not enforceable, and DIAN may not collect them. Both seizures of the plaintiff Companies’ assets and offsets of the disputed amounts against their tax credit balances would therefore be precluded while the dispute remains before the courts.
Acquisition of Brava Energia S.A.
On August 17, 2026, Ecopetrol Group completed the acquisition of a controlling 51% interest in Brava Energía for total consideration of USD 1,123 million. The acquisition was carried out through two transactions: a voluntary tender offer for approximately 25% of the shares and the purchase of an additional interest of approximately 26% from significant shareholders, pursuant to the agreement signed on April 23, 2026.
For purposes of consolidating results, 100% of its revenue, costs and expenses are included beginning in September, with the portion of earnings attributable to non-controlling interests recognized separately. In the statement of financial position, Brava’s assets and liabilities are consolidated line by line at 100%, with the corresponding 49% non-controlling interest recognized in equity.
Brava’s results are included in the Ecopetrol Group’s Exploration, Development and Production segment, in line with the strategic and operating nature of the acquisition, its integration into the business management model and the applicable IFRS (International Financial Reporting Standards) segment reporting criteria.
On August 5, 2026, Ecopetrol Capital AG secured a short-term credit facility, under which USD 1.2 billion was drawn. The proceeds were subsequently provided through an intercompany loan to Ecopetrol Investimentos do Brasil Ltda. to fund the acquisition of a 51% equity interest in Brava Energia. The main effects of consolidating Brava are described below:
Income statement: Brava’s consolidation in 3Q26 includes one month of operations, corresponding to September, with 100% of its results included, comprising revenue of COP 1.1 trillion, generated mainly by crude oil sales in Brazil and exports, EBITDA of COP 0.6 trillion and a contribution to net income of COP 0.1 trillion.
Statement of financial position: The consolidation of Brava resulted in the inclusion of assets of COP 27.5 trillion and liabilities of COP 19.8 trillion, as well as a non-controlling interest of COP 3.7 trillion. These amounts are provisional and may be adjusted upon completion of the valuation of the assets acquired and liabilities assumed.
This transaction marks a milestone in our portfolio growth and diversification strategy, through which we expect to strengthen the Ecopetrol Group’s presence in Brazil and add a significant reserve and production base to support sustainable long-term value creation. In addition, the integration process is expected to leverage complementary capabilities to accelerate reserve additions, improve operating efficiency and maximize asset performance.
Following the acquisition closing, the integration process began, encompassing a range of short-, medium- and long-term strategic and operating activities centered on the transfer of best practices. In addition to the developments mentioned above, during the quarter we progressed with our analysis of reserve certification supported by robust estimation, control and reporting processes under SEC criteria, as well as support for the development of a reserve progression and project maturation plan. We also advanced initiatives aimed at cost optimization and alignment around a long-term sustainability vision to help strengthen Brava’s operations and value creation.

Efficiencies
The efficiency program contributed COP 4.4 trillion to Ecopetrol Group’s financial and operating results as of 3Q26, of which 64% impacted EBITDA, 20% CapEx and 16% working capital. The main achievements are summarized below:
EBITDA COP 2.8 trillion: (COP 2.2 trillion from the Hydrocarbons business line, COP 0.5 trillion from Corporate and Support areas and COP 0.1 trillion from other areas), with the following actions standing out:
ü | Efficiencies in energy, digital solutions and infrastructure, demand management for regional personnel services (meals, transportation and catering), and downhole and surface maintenance strategies in the Exploration, Development and Production segment, among others, totaling ~COP 0.8 trillion.

ü | Refining optimizations that supported improved fuel oil quality, increased aromatics production at Barrancabermeja Refinery and optimization of the refining slate at Cartagena Refinery, totaling ~COP 0.7 trillion.

ü | Maximization and optimization of transportation and logistics systems totaling ~COP 0.6 trillion.

ü | Maximization of crude oil and refined product margins through commercial strategies totaling ~COP 0.5 trillion.

ü | Optimization of new contracts through goods and services sourcing strategies totaling ~COP 0.2 trillion.

Cost efficiencies in the Exploration, Development and Production segment reduced lifting costs by -2,331 COP/bl (-0.67 USD/bl), helping partially offset the impact of external factors, primarily the exchange rate, as well as inflation and crude oil prices.
CapEx COP 0.9 trillion: efficiencies distributed as follows:
Exploration, Development and Production Segment (64%, COP 0.6 trillion):
· | Synergy levers from project integration, facility cost optimization, design/engineering improvements and equipment mobilization efficiency, mainly at Dina T-Palogrande, Rubiales, Akacias and Chichimene - COP 0.4 trillion.

· | Reuse of surplus materials and design optimizations, mainly at Caño Sur, Castilla and Akacias - COP 0.1 trillion.

· | In directly operated drilling and completion activities, design and engineering initiatives, technology adoption and sourcing strategies enabled cost targets to be exceeded.

Downstream Segment (18%, COP 0.15 trillion): Concentrated in the Fuel Quality Baseline Project (LBCC)[1] project, where design improvements and operating facility optimizations were implemented.
Working Capital COP 0.7 trillion: efficiencies based on inventory optimization, fiscal and tax management through the recovery of fuel VAT via FEPC[2], tax benefits in commercial operations from implementing Plan Vallejo program and the AEO (Authorized Economic Operator) certification, tax benefits for energy efficiency, and optimization of resources for well abandonment.
