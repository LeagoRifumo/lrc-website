IDACORP, Inc. Announces Third Quarter 2026 Results, Increases Earnings Guidance Range
BOISE, Idaho--IDACORP, Inc. (NYSE: IDA) reported third quarter 2026 net income attributable to IDACORP of $160.2 million, or $2.73 per diluted share, compared with $124.4 million, or $2.26 per diluted share, in the third quarter of 2025.
"Strong third quarter results were driven by continued customer growth and additional revenues from current base rates and from large contract customers as they continue to ramp-up operations," said IDACORP President and Chief Executive Officer Lisa Grow. "This has been an exciting year with the installation of battery storage projects and significant progress on development and construction of our transmission and generation projects," Grow added.
IDACORP is increasing its previously reported full-year 2026 earnings guidance to the range of $6.40 to $6.50 per diluted share, with the expectation that Idaho Power will use less than $10 million of additional tax credits available under the Idaho regulatory mechanism in 2026. The earnings guidance assumes normal weather conditions through the end of the year.

Net Income - Third Quarter 2026
IDACORP's net income increased $35.8 million for the third quarter of 2026 compared with the third quarter of 2025, due primarily to higher net income at Idaho Power.
The net increase in retail revenues per MWh, excluding large contract customers, and net of power cost adjustment mechanisms, increased operating income by $38.1 million in the third quarter of 2026 compared with the third quarter of 2025. This benefit was due primarily to an overall increase in Idaho base rates, effective January 1, 2026, from the outcome of the settlement stipulation for Idaho Power's 2025 Idaho general rate case (2025 Settlement Stipulation).
Customer growth, excluding large contract customers, and net of associated power supply costs and power cost adjustment mechanisms, increased operating income by $5.8 million in the third quarter of 2026 compared with the third quarter of 2025, as the number of Idaho Power customers grew by approximately 15,000, or 2.2 percent, during the twelve months ended September 30, 2026. Usage per retail customer, excluding large contract customers, and net of associated power supply costs and power cost adjustment mechanisms, was relatively consistent in the third quarter of 2026 compared with the third quarter of 2025, as a 1 percent increase in usage per residential customer and a 3 percent increase in usage per irrigation customer were mostly offset by a decrease in usage per industrial customer. An increase in the deferral of residential and small commercial customer revenues through the FCA mechanism negatively affected retail revenues by $3.7 million.
Retail revenues from large contract customers, net of associated power supply costs and the PCA mechanism, increased operating income by $11.8 million in the third quarter of 2026 compared with the third quarter of 2025. This benefit was primarily due to the commencement of Idaho Power earning revenue under a large customer contract on June 1, 2026, the overall increase in Idaho base rates effective January 1, 2026, and an increase in usage per large contract customer.
Other O&M expenses increased $22.5 million in the third quarter of 2026 compared with the third quarter of 2025, primarily due to the amortization of previously deferred costs associated with the conversion of generating units at the Jim Bridger and North Valmy plants from coal to natural gas and higher wildfire mitigation costs, including amortization of previously deferred wildfire mitigation program expenses. These increases were substantially offset by corresponding revenues recognized through regulatory recovery mechanisms, including the 2025 Settlement Stipulation and approved Idaho and Oregon customer rates.
Depreciation and amortization expense increased $7.4 million in the third quarter of 2026 compared with the third quarter of 2025, due primarily to an increase in plant-in-service.
Other changes in operating revenues and expenses, net, increased operating income by $10.6 million in the third quarter of 2026 compared with the third quarter of 2025. This was due primarily to a decrease in net power supply expenses that were not accrued for future refund in rates through Idaho Power's power cost adjustment mechanisms. Also contributing to the increase in other changes in operating revenues and expenses, net, was lower property tax expense due to property tax legislative changes in Idaho.
Non-operating expense, net, increased $1.1 million in the third quarter of 2026 compared with the third quarter of 2025. Higher long-term debt balances increased interest expense, while lower interest-bearing cash investment balances decreased interest income. This increase was mostly offset by an increase in Allowance for Funds Used During Construction (AFUDC) income in the third quarter of 2026 compared with the third quarter of 2025, as the average construction work in progress balance was higher.
The decrease in income tax expense for the third quarter of 2026, compared with the third quarter of 2025, was primarily due to plant-related flow-through items, partially offset by statutory taxes on higher pre-tax income and a decrease in additional ADITC amortization. Based on Idaho Power's current expectations of full-year 2026 financial results, Idaho Power recorded no additional ADITC amortization during the third quarter of 2026, compared with $2.5 million of additional ADITC amortization during the third quarter of 2025.
Net Income - Year-To-Date 2026
IDACORP's net income increased $50.9 million for the first nine months of 2026 compared with the first nine months of 2025, due primarily to higher net income at Idaho Power.
The net increase in retail revenues per MWh, excluding large contract customers, and net of power cost adjustment mechanisms, increased operating income by $75.7 million in the first nine months of 2026 compared with the first nine months of 2025. This benefit was due primarily to an overall increase in Idaho base rates, effective January 1, 2026, from the outcome of the 2025 Settlement Stipulation.
Customer growth, excluding large contract customers, and net of associated power supply costs and power cost adjustment mechanisms, increased operating income by $13.9 million in the first nine months of 2026 compared with the first nine months of 2025. Usage per retail customer, excluding large contract customers, and net of associated power supply costs and power cost adjustment mechanisms, decreased operating income by $12.4 million in the first nine months of 2026 compared with the first nine months of 2025. Residential usage per customer decreased most significantly, as more moderate temperatures in the first six months of 2026 compared with the first six months of 2025 led residential customers to use less energy for heating and cooling purposes, slightly offset by less moderate temperatures and higher usage per customer in the third quarter of 2026 compared with the third quarter of 2025. A decrease in the deferral of residential and small commercial customer revenues through the FCA mechanism positively affected retail revenues by $18.2 million.
Retail revenues from large contract customers, net of associated power supply costs and the PCA mechanism, increased operating income by $20.1 million in the first nine months of 2026 compared with the first nine months of 2025. This change was primarily due to the commencement of Idaho Power earning revenue under a large customer contract on June 1, 2026, the overall increase in Idaho base rates effective January 1, 2026, and an increase in usage per large contract customer.
Other O&M expenses increased $47.4 million in the first nine months of 2026 compared with the first nine months of 2025, primarily due to the amortization of previously deferred costs associated with the conversion of generating units at the Jim Bridger and North Valmy plants from coal to natural gas and higher wildfire mitigation costs, including
amortization of previously deferred wildfire mitigation program expenses. These increases were substantially offset by corresponding revenues recognized through regulatory recovery mechanisms, including the 2025 Settlement Stipulation and approved Idaho and Oregon customer rates.
Depreciation and amortization expense increased $18.4 million in the first nine months of 2026 compared with the first nine months of 2025, due primarily to an increase in plant-in-service.
Other changes in operating revenues and expenses, net, increased operating income by $40.0 million in the first nine months of 2026 compared with the first nine months of 2025. This was due primarily to a decrease in net power supply expenses that were not accrued for future refund in rates through Idaho Power's power cost adjustment mechanisms. Also contributing to the increase in other changes in operating revenues and expenses, net, was lower property tax expense due to property tax legislative changes in Idaho.
Non-operating expense, net, increased $5.7 million in the first nine months of 2026 compared with the first nine months of 2025. Higher long-term debt balances increased interest expense, while lower interest-bearing cash investment balances decreased interest income. This increase was partially offset by an increase in AFUDC income in the first nine months of 2026 compared with the first nine months of 2025, as the average construction work in progress balance was higher.
The increase in income tax expense was principally the result of a decrease in additional ADITC amortization and statutory taxes on higher pre-tax income, partially offset by plant-related flow-through items. Based on Idaho Power's current expectations of full-year 2026 financial results, Idaho Power recorded $6.3 million of additional ADITC amortization under its Idaho regulatory settlement stipulation during the first nine months of 2026, compared with $39.0 million of additional ADITC amortization during the same period in 2025.

Background Information
IDACORP, Inc. (NYSE: IDA), Boise, Idaho-based and formed in 1998, is a holding company comprised of Idaho Power, a regulated electric utility; IDACORP Financial, an investor in affordable housing and other real estate tax
credit investments; and Ida-West Energy, an operator of small hydroelectric generation projects. Idaho Power, headquartered in vibrant and fast-growing Boise, Idaho, has been a locally operated energy company since 1916. Today, it serves a 24,000-square-mile service area in Idaho and Oregon. With 17 low-cost hydropower projects at the core of its diverse energy mix, Idaho Power’s residential, business, and agricultural customers pay among the nation's lowest prices for electricity. Its nearly 2,200 employees proudly serve more than 670,000 customers with a culture of safety first, integrity always, and respect for all. To learn more about IDACORP or Idaho Power, visit idacorpinc.com or idahopower.com.
