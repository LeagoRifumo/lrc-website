Ellington Financial Inc. Reports Third Quarter 2026 Results
OLD GREENWICH, Connecticut—November 4, 2026
Ellington Financial Inc. (NYSE: EFC) ("we") today reported financial results for the quarter ended September 30, 2026.
Highlights
•Net income attributable to common stockholders of $40.4 million, or $0.31 per common share, including unallocated Corporate/Other income and expense items.
◦$61.1 million, or $0.47 per common share, from the investment portfolio segment.
◦$12.5 million, or $0.10 per common share, from the Longbridge segment.
•Adjusted Distributable Earnings1 of $58.0 million, or $0.45 per common share, including incentive fee expense of $0.9 million, or $0.01 per common share, and other unallocated Corporate/Other income and expense items.
◦$77.6 million, or $0.60 per common share, from the investment portfolio segment.
◦$8.6 million, or $0.07 per common share, from the Longbridge segment.
•Book value per common share of $13.53 as of September 30, 2026, including the effects of dividends of $0.39 per common share for the quarter.
•Recourse debt-to-equity ratio2 of 2.2:1 as of September 30, 2026. Including all recourse and non-recourse borrowings, which primarily consist of securitization-related liabilities, debt-to-equity ratio of 9.4:12.
◦25% of total recourse borrowings2 are long-term and non-mark-to-market.
◦17% of total recourse borrowings2 are unsecured.
◦Weighted average remaining term of repo borrowings2 is 8.9 months.
•Total unencumbered assets3 of $1.92 billion, consisting of cash and cash equivalents of $269.5 million and other unencumbered assets of $1.66 billion as of September 30, 2026.
•Issued 2,496,140 common shares under the ATM program at an average price of $13.61 per share, during the quarter. Subsequent to quarter end, repurchased 329,045 shares at an average price of $11.44 per share.4
•Issued $150 million of 7.375% senior unsecured notes due 2030 in an add-on offering, bringing the series to $550 million outstanding.

Financial Results
Investment Portfolio Segment
The investment portfolio segment generated net income attributable to common stockholders of $61.1 million in the third quarter.
The total adjusted long portfolio5 increased by 4.5% sequentially, to $4.70 billion as of September 30, 2026. The increase was driven by net purchases of residential transition loans, closed-end second lien loans, Agency-eligible residential mortgage loans, prime jumbo residential mortgage loans, commercial mortgage bridge loans, and a larger portfolio of retained RMBS. These increases were partially offset by the impact of continued securitization activity and a smaller Agency RMBS portfolio.
Key Highlights6:
•Net interest income remained robust, while net gains on hedges more than offset net realized and unrealized losses. Earnings from unconsolidated entities also contributed to the positive results.
•Strong performance across several strategies, led by retained tranches from securitizations of non-QM and Agency-eligible loans, commercial mortgage bridge loans, and equity investments in loan originators, particularly LendSure. Additional positive contributions from retained tranches of closed-end second lien loan securitizations, non-Agency RMBS, CLOs, and corporate debt and equity.
•These contributions were partially offset by negative results in warehoused non-QM whole loans, residential transition loan retained tranches, CMBS, and residential and commercial REO.
•Strong credit performance across our loan businesses, with sequentially lower 90-day delinquency rates and continued low life-to-date realized loan losses.
During the quarter, the net interest margin7 on our investment portfolio increased to 3.55% from 3.36%, as higher asset yields were only partially offset by higher funding costs. We also benefited from modestly higher positive carry on our interest rate swaps, with our weighted average receive rate continuing to exceed our weighted average pay rate.
During the quarter, we acquired a controlling interest in Statebridge Company, LLC ("Statebridge"), a diversified loan servicer. As of September 30, 2026, Statebridge serviced approximately 14,700 loans with an aggregate unpaid principal balance ("UPB") of $1.9 billion, nearly all for third-party clients, comprising approximately 3,500 non-QM and RTL loans with $1.1 billion of UPB, approximately 4,900 qualifying-mortgage loans with $0.6 billion of UPB, and approximately 6,300 consumer installment and commercial mortgage loans with $0.2 billion of UPB.

Longbridge Segment
The Longbridge segment reported net income attributable to common stockholders of $12.5 million, compared with $30.2 million in the second quarter. Adjusted Distributable Earnings were $8.6 million, compared with $28.9 million in the second quarter. The sequential decline primarily reflected narrower origination margins and reduced proprietary reverse mortgage securitization gains.
Longbridge originated $676.3 million of loans during the quarter, up 36% year over year, contributing to a 59% sequential increase in the net Longbridge portfolio5 to $1.0 billion as of September 30, 2026. Much of this increase was reversed upon the October settlement of a proprietary reverse mortgage loan securitization that priced in September.
Key Highlights6:
•Positive contribution from originations, driven by sequentially higher origination volumes and net gains from a proprietary reverse mortgage loan securitization priced during the quarter, partially offset by origination margin compression amid higher interest rates.
•Positive contribution from servicing, reflecting strong tail securitization executions, steady base servicing net income, and gains on MSR-related hedges, partially offset by a mark-to-market loss on the HMBS MSR.
•Significant net gains on "enterprise" interest-rate hedges intended to mitigate the potential impact of higher interest rates on origination profits.
•Net unrealized loss on the retained tranches of consolidated proprietary reverse mortgage loan securitization trusts, driven by revised home price projections.
•Achieved a quarterly record HMBS market share of 31%, ranking as the second-largest issuer in the market, according to Bloomberg.
Corporate/Other Summary
The net loss in Corporate/Other narrowed quarter over quarter, primarily driven by a smaller combined net loss on our unsecured debt and interest rate hedges. Higher interest rates during the third quarter resulted in losses on the fixed receiver interest rate swaps used to hedge the fixed payments on our unsecured notes and preferred equity. These losses exceeded the net gains on our unsecured notes.
Our unsecured notes generated mark-to-market gains as a result of higher interest rates, although this was partially offset by credit spread tightening on our existing notes, reflecting the tighter pricing achieved on our September add-on offering. Related debt issuance costs were fully expensed at issuance. We also recorded a realized loss on the par redemption of our 6% senior notes, which were carried at a discount to par. Finally, results reflected a higher income tax expense.

Longbridge Portfolio
Longbridge originates reverse mortgage loans, including (i) home equity conversion mortgage loans, or "HECMs," which are insured by the FHA, and (ii) "proprietary reverse mortgage loans," which are not FHA-insured. HECMs are eligible for inclusion in GNMA-guaranteed HECM-backed MBS, or "HMBS." Upon securitization, the HECMs remain on our balance sheet under GAAP. We have securitized certain proprietary reverse mortgage loans originated by Longbridge and have retained certain related securitization tranches in compliance with credit risk retention rules. Longbridge has typically retained the MSRs associated with the loans it has originated. Longbridge also originates home equity lines of credit, or "HELOCs," designed for homeowners aged 62 or older.

About Ellington Financial

Reconciliation of Net Income (Loss) to Adjusted Distributable Earnings
We calculate Adjusted Distributable Earnings as U.S. GAAP net income (loss) as adjusted for: (i) realized and unrealized gain (loss) on securities and loans, REO, mortgage servicing rights, financial derivatives (excluding periodic settlements on interest rate swaps), any borrowings carried at fair value, and foreign currency transactions; (ii) Catch-up Amortization Adjustment (as defined below); (iii) non-cash equity compensation expense; (iv) provision for income taxes; (v) certain non-capitalized transaction costs; and (vi) other income or loss items that are of a non-recurring nature. For certain investments in unconsolidated entities, we include the relevant components of net operating income in Adjusted Distributable Earnings. The Catch-up Amortization Adjustment is a quarterly adjustment to premium amortization or discount accretion triggered by changes in actual and projected prepayments on our Agency RMBS (accompanied by a corresponding offsetting adjustment to realized and unrealized gains and losses). The adjustment is calculated as of the beginning of each quarter based on our then-current assumptions about cashflows and prepayments, and can vary significantly from quarter to quarter. Non-capitalized transaction costs include expenses, generally professional fees, incurred in connection with the acquisition of an investment or issuance of long-term debt. We also include in Adjusted Distributable Earnings, for all loans that we originate through Longbridge, any realized and unrealized gains (losses) on such loans up to the point of loan sale or securitization, net of sale or securitization costs; and any realized and unrealized gains (losses) on HECM buyout loans and REO related to Longbridge's servicing activities.
Beginning with the three-month period ended September 30, 2026, Adjusted Distributable Earnings is presented net of incentive fee expense. Adjusted Distributable Earnings presented for prior periods has been conformed to the current-period presentation.
Adjusted Distributable Earnings is a supplemental non-GAAP financial measure. We believe that the presentation of Adjusted Distributable Earnings provides information useful to investors, because: (i) we believe that it is a useful indicator of both current and projected long-term financial performance, in that it excludes the impact of certain current-period earnings components that we believe are less useful in forecasting long-term performance and dividend-paying ability; (ii) we use it to evaluate the effective net yield provided (a) by our investment portfolio, after the effects of financial leverage, and (b) by Longbridge, to reflect the earnings from its reverse mortgage origination and servicing operations; and (iii) we believe that presenting Adjusted Distributable Earnings assists investors in measuring and evaluating our operating performance, and comparing our operating performance to that of our residential mortgage REIT and mortgage originator peers. Please note, however, that: (I) our calculation of Adjusted Distributable Earnings may differ from the calculation of similarly titled non-GAAP financial measures by our peers, with the result that these non-GAAP financial measures might not be directly comparable; and (II) Adjusted Distributable Earnings excludes certain items that may impact the amount of cash that is actually available for distribution.
In addition, because Adjusted Distributable Earnings is an incomplete measure of our financial results and differs from net income (loss) computed in accordance with U.S. GAAP, it should be considered supplementary to, and not as a substitute for, net income (loss) computed in accordance with U.S. GAAP.
Furthermore, Adjusted Distributable Earnings is different from REIT taxable income. As a result, the determination of whether we have met the requirement to distribute at least 90% of our annual REIT taxable income (subject to certain adjustments) to our stockholders, in order to maintain our qualification as a REIT, is not based on whether we distributed 90% of our Adjusted Distributable Earnings.
In setting our dividends, our Board of Directors considers our earnings, liquidity, financial condition, REIT distribution requirements, and financial covenants, along with other factors that the Board of Directors may deem relevant from time to time.
