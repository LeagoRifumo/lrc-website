NOG Announces Third Quarter 2026 Results
Raises 2026 Production Guidance
HIGHLIGHTS
•Record quarterly production of 156,239 Boe per day (48% oil), a 19% increase from the third quarter of 2025 and a 7.3% increase from the second quarter of 2026
•Record natural gas production of 488,391 Mcf per day, a 39% increase from the third quarter of 2025 and a 5.2% increase from the second quarter of 2026
•GAAP net loss of $111.8 million, record Adjusted EBITDA of $444.5 million, and Adjusted Net Income of $142.2 million, meaningfully improved over the second quarter of 2026. See “Non-GAAP Financial Measures” below
•Cash flow from operations of $397.7 million as compared to $321.6 million in the second quarter of 2026.
•Generated $148.6 million of Free Cash Flow, up 25.0% from the third quarter of 2025. See “Non-GAAP Financial Measures” below
•Capital expenditures of $247.3 million, excluding non-budgeted acquisitions and other
•Completed 19 Ground Game transactions adding over 1,060 net acres and an additional 2.9 net wells for $56.5 million, inclusive of associated development costs
•Retired over $100 million of total debt and achieved a net debt-to-annualized Adjusted EBITDA ratio of sub 1.5x
•Issued $500 million of 7.5% Senior Notes due in 2034
•Repurchased 667,989 shares of common stock at an average price of $23.96, including commissions
•Raised 2026 annual production guidance to a range of 147,000 to 150,000 Boepd
MINNEAPOLIS (BUSINESS WIRE) - November 5, 2026 - Northern Oil and Gas, Inc. (NYSE: NOG) (“NOG” or “Company”) today announced the Company’s third quarter results.
MANAGEMENT COMMENTS
“The third quarter was a record for NOG, and the drivers matter as much as the results. We set new highs for total production, natural gas volumes and Adjusted EBITDA, and generated nearly $150 million of Free Cash Flow even as activity increased across our oil-weighted basins. Our assets are self-funding their own growth and still generating substantial excess cash, and we’re putting that cash to work. We cut total debt by more than $100 million, brought leverage below 1.5 times a quarter ahead of our year-end goal, and continued buying back our own shares,” stated Nick O’Grady, Chief Executive Officer.
“With every region performing, we are raising full-year production guidance while holding our capital budget unchanged. Put simply, we expect to deliver more for the same capital. Our shares, like many in our sector, trade at a deep discount to the value of the assets underneath them. We don’t see that as a setback. We see it as an opportunity which we intend to seize upon. This quarter’s records are not a ceiling. We are a company run by investors, for investors, and we intend to keep delivering like one.”
FINANCIAL RESULTS
Oil and natural gas sales for the third quarter were $697.7 million. Third quarter GAAP net loss was $111.8 million or $1.06 per diluted share, driven by non-cash mark-to-market losses on derivatives. Third quarter Adjusted Net Income was $142.2 million or $1.32 per adjusted diluted share. Adjusted EBITDA in the third quarter was $444.5 million, an 11% increase from the second quarter of 2026, driven primarily by increased production volumes. See “Non-GAAP Financial Measures” below.
PRODUCTION
Third quarter 2026 production averaged 156,239 Boe per day, a 19% increase from the third quarter of 2025. Oil represented approximately 48% of total production in the third quarter at an average of 74,837 Bbls per day. During the quarter, NOG added 23.9 net wells to production, compared to 12.7 net wells in the second quarter, excluding major acquisitions. Year to date, the Company has added 53.7 net wells to production.
Well performance continues to be strong across all of NOG’s basins. Appalachian volumes set another production record as the wells from our completed joint development program in West Virginia continue to outperform and our Utica joint development begins to accelerate activity. Consistent with the first half of 2026, in the third quarter NOG’s Uinta Assets continued to outperform versus internal estimates both on legacy production as well as on the 2026 development program.

PRICING
During the third quarter, NOG’s unhedged net realized oil price was $82.83 per Bbl. The Company’s average differential to WTI prices was $2.84, as compared to $3.03 in the second quarter of 2026 and improved by 27% from the third quarter of 2025. NOG’s unhedged net realized gas price in the third quarter was $2.84 per Mcf, representing a 98% realization compared with Henry Hub pricing. Natural gas realizations have improved meaningfully year to date as Waha pricing has normalized and NGL realizations continue to show strength.
HEDGING
In the third quarter, the Company recorded a non-cash unrealized mark-to-market loss on derivatives of approximately $308.0 million, driven by changes to the value of the Company’s derivatives portfolio. Realized hedge losses were $51.4 million as gains on the Company’s natural gas hedges were more than offset by losses on the Company’s crude oil hedges.
OPERATING COSTS
Lease operating costs were $143.5 million in the third quarter of 2026, or $9.98 per Boe, 2% higher on a per unit basis compared to the third quarter of 2025, driven in part by higher processing costs. Production taxes were $48.3 million in the third quarter of 2026, compared to $28.7 million in the third quarter of 2025 due to higher revenues derived by higher oil prices as well as record gas volumes. Third quarter general and administrative (“G&A”) costs totaled $18.0 million or $1.25 per Boe, as compared to $1.17 per Boe in the third quarter of 2025. The increase was primarily driven by higher general corporate costs to support the Company’s growth initiatives. NOG’s adjusted cash G&A costs, which excludes non-cash share-based compensation and acquisition cost amounts of $4.5 million and $0.9 million, respectively, totaled $12.6 million or $0.88 per Boe in the third quarter, down $0.06 per Boe compared to the second quarter of 2026.
CAPITAL EXPENDITURES AND ACQUISITIONS
Capital expenditures for the third quarter were $247.3 million (excluding non-budgeted acquisitions and other). This was comprised of $190.7 million of total drilling and completion (“D&C”) capital on organic assets, and $56.5 million of Ground Game activity, inclusive of associated development costs. Normalized well costs on the Company’s AFE elections improved 2.9% quarter over quarter averaging approximately $739 per lateral foot in the third quarter, as compared to $761 in the second quarter of 2026. NOG’s Williston Basin spending was 33% of the capital expenditures for the third quarter followed by the Permian at 31%, Appalachian at 16%, the Duvernay at 11% and the Uinta at 9%.
LIQUIDITY AND CAPITAL RESOURCES
NOG had total liquidity of $1.6 billion as of September 30, 2026, consisting of $1,577.0 million of committed borrowing availability under its Revolving Credit Facility and $26.4 million of cash on hand. The Company has a weighted average debt maturity of 5.3 years and its earliest maturity is not until 2029. NOG exited the quarter with a net debt to annualized Adjusted EBITDA ratio of 1.47 times.
In August, NOG issued $500 million of 7.5% Senior Notes due 2034 in an oversubscribed offering. Proceeds of the offering were used to pay down a portion of the outstanding balance under the Company’s revolving credit facility and for general corporate purposes.
In November, the Company completed its semi-annual borrowing base redetermination under its reserves-based revolving credit facility (the “Facility”). In connection with the redetermination, the elected commitment and borrowing base remain unchanged at $1.8 billion and $1.975 billion, respectively.
SHAREHOLDER RETURNS
In August 2026, the Company’s board of directors declared a cash dividend on the Company’s common stock in the amount of $0.45 per share. The dividend was paid on October 30, 2026, to stockholders of record as of the close of business on September 29, 2026.
In November 2026, the Company’s board of directors declared a cash dividend on the Company’s common stock in the amount of $0.45 per share. The dividend will be paid on January 29, 2027, to stockholders of record as of the close of business on December 30, 2026.

In July 2026, NOG’s Board of Directors authorized a $150.0 million increase to the Company’s common stock repurchase program, which provides a current total repurchase capacity of approximately $227.2 million as of September 30, 2026.

NOG is raising its annual production guidance for the year to reflect outperformance in the third quarter. The Company is maintaining its Capital Expenditures budget guidance but expects Cap Ex to trend toward the high-end of the range based on current activity levels. Ranges for operating costs, production taxes and cash G&A have been adjusted to reflect costs incurred year to date. Ranges for oil differentials and gas realizations have been adjusted for year to date actuals and expectations for the fourth quarter.

ABOUT NOG
Northern Oil and Gas (NOG) is the largest publicly traded dedicated non-operator in the United States, built on a differentiated strategy of acquiring non-operated minority working interests and mineral rights across the premier basins of North America. By combining deep industry relationships with disciplined capital allocation, NOG has built a scaled, diversified portfolio that generates durable production and strong cash flow for its shareholders. Non-GAAP Financial Measures
Adjusted Net Income, Adjusted EBITDA and Free Cash Flow are non-GAAP measures. NOG defines Adjusted Net Income as income before income taxes, excluding (i) (gain) loss on unsettled commodity derivatives, net of tax, (ii) (gain) loss on extinguishment of debt, net of tax, (iii) contingent consideration (gain) loss, net of tax, (iv) acquisition transaction costs, net of tax, (v) (gain) loss on unsettled interest rate derivatives, net of tax, (vi) (gain) loss on foreign currency transactions and (vii) impairment of long-lived assets, net of tax. NOG defines Adjusted EBITDA as net income before (i) interest expense, (ii) income taxes, (iii) depreciation, depletion, amortization and accretion, (iv) non-cash stock-based compensation expense, (v) (gain) loss on extinguishment of debt, (vi) contingent consideration (gain) loss (vii) acquisition transaction costs, (viii) (gain) loss on unsettled interest rate derivatives, (ix) (gain) loss on unsettled commodity derivatives, (x) (gain) loss on foreign currency transactions, (xi) impairment of long-lived assets, and (xii) other non-cash adjustments. NOG defines Free Cash Flow as cash flows from operations before changes in working capital and other items, less (i) capital expenditures, excluding non-budgeted acquisitions and changes in accrued capital expenditures and other items. A reconciliation of each of these measures to the most directly comparable GAAP measure is included below.
Management believes the use of these non-GAAP financial measures provides useful information to investors to gain an overall understanding of current financial performance. Management believes Adjusted Net Income and Adjusted EBITDA provide useful information to both management and investors by excluding certain expenses and unrealized commodity gains and losses that management believes are not indicative of NOG’s core operating results. Management believes that Free Cash Flow is useful to investors as a measure of a company’s ability to internally fund its budgeted capital expenditures, to service or incur additional debt, and to measure success in creating stockholder value. In addition, these non-GAAP financial measures are used by management for budgeting and forecasting as well as subsequently measuring NOG’s performance, and management believes it is providing investors with financial measures that most closely align to its internal measurement processes. The non-GAAP financial measures included herein may be defined differently than similar measures used by other companies and should not be considered an alternative to, or more meaningful than, the comparable GAAP measures. From time to time NOG provides forward-looking Free Cash Flow estimates or targets; however, NOG is unable to provide a quantitative reconciliation of the forward looking non-GAAP measure to its most directly comparable forward looking GAAP measure because management cannot reliably quantify certain of the necessary components of such forward looking GAAP measure. The reconciling items in future periods could be significant.
