CEO Letter 2 We are pleased to share our 3Q26 results, with record net income of R$425 million, up 26% year over year. We now serve 46.8 million clients, 27.1 million of them active, and we rank among the fastest growing retail banks in the Americas. Since launching the first digital bank in Brazil, Inter has grown consistently for more than 10 years. Today, the combination of a bank's strength with a tech company's speed and innovation drives our flywheel of learning effects, fueling growth and sustainable profitability. We are executing with confidence, built on four pillars: a fortress balance sheet, alignment with the regulator's direction, a growing client franchise, and a technology base advancing with AI. Our 60/30/30 and Rule of 50 continue to lead our ambition to grow with profitability. These results show the strength of our model, and we are well positioned for the next phase of growth. As in 2015, when we built the foundation for our digital model, we are making a bold investment again, this time on AI. More than 700 AI models already run in production across our teams, and in software development, tasks that took ten days now take four. Seven, our multi-agent AI assistant for clients, resolves about 85% of client requests without human support. To accelerate this work, we signed a strategic partnership with SpaceXAI. We have also built a leadership structure for this next cycle. Alexandre Riccio is our new Chief Operating Officer, focused on operational excellence and credit underwriting. Francisco Santos joins as our Chief Growth Officer, focused on growth and monetization in Brazil. Santiago Stel continues as our CFO, focused on profitability and capital allocation. In short, Alexandre makes every turn run better, Francisco makes every turn bigger, and Santiago makes sure each turn is profitable. With robust capital, a strong brand, a talented team and our "BanTech" design, we are well positioned to leapfrog in the years to come. Highlights of the quarter reflect growth with discipline. Our expanded loan portfolio grew 25% year over year, almost 3 times the pace of the Brazilian market, while we managed credit quality with discipline. NPL over 90 days rose to 5.3%, mainly from private payroll loans and credit cards, while early delinquency (NPL 15 to 90 days) fell to 4.2% from 4.5% in 2Q26, as the first improvements are visible in private payroll, where automatic relinkage is now live. Looking ahead, we expect the gap between revenue and expenses to keep widening, driving continuous improvement in our efficiency ratio and further profitability expansion. Our model was built for any cycle, even in a challenging macro scenario. A flywheel only matters if it can run for years, getting stronger each time. We have the right business positioning and the team to turn technology leadership into lasting, profitable growth. Thank you to our clients, employees, partners and shareholders. Your trust and dedication make this journey possible. Banking Strength. Technology at Scale. A Flywheel in Montion Inter&Co Global CEO João Vitor Menin

We are managing credit quality with discipline. NPL over 90 days reached 5.3% on the expanded portfolio. The increase came mainly from private payroll loans fast expansion and from credit cards, as part of our reshaping strategy. Early delinquency (NPL 15 to 90 days) fell to to 4.2% on the expanded portfolio from 4.5%. Early-stage delinquency moves into the 90-day balance later, so this decline is an early sign that delinquency is nearing its peak. Private payroll is the main driver of the increase, as its operating processes are still being optimized. The main cause of missed payments is an employee changing employers, which is why automatic relinkage matters. Relinkage has been live since September and has already reduced NPL formation in this segment. Stage 3 formation, however, remains elevated because our stage-transfer policy keeps loans under observation before they move. Private payroll insurance, now at about 40% penetration, adds further protection to new cohorts. Credit card delinquency is also nearing its peak, although the macro scenario remains challenging for this portfolio. Our reshaping strategy increased the interest-earning portfolio, and credit card interest income grew about 37% year over year, more than offsetting the higher delinquency. All-in cost of risk was 5.9% in 3Q26, in line with 2Q26. The Desenrola refinancing program lowered cost of risk by about 20 bps in the quarter. It also added about 30 bps to Stage 3 formation, through renegotiated loans that had been written off. Note: Definitions are in the Appendix section of this Earnings Release.
