The New York Times Company Reports Third-Quarter 2026 Results
NEW YORK, November 4, 2026 – The New York Times Company (NYSE: NYT) announced today third-quarter 2026 results.
Key Highlights
•Digital-only subscription revenues grew 14.6 percent year-over-year, driven by growth in both digital-only subscribers and digital-only average revenue per user (“ARPU”). The Company added approximately 420,000 net digital-only subscribers compared with the end of the second quarter of 2026, bringing the total number of subscribers to 13.76 million. Digital-only ARPU increased 1.4 percent year-over-year to $9.93.
•Digital advertising revenues increased 12.0 percent year-over-year primarily due to continued marketer demand and growth in advertising supply.
•Affiliate, licensing and other revenues increased 6.2 percent year-over-year.
•Operating costs increased 8.5 percent year-over-year and adjusted operating costs (defined below) increased 8.7 percent year-over-year, largely as a result of higher compensation and benefits expenses, primarily relating to our journalism, higher general and administrative costs, as well as higher marketing and promotion costs.
•Operating profit increased 16.4 percent year-over-year to $121.9 million, while adjusted operating profit (defined below) increased 13.9 percent year-over-year to $149.6 million.
•Operating profit margin increased to 15.9 percent, while adjusted operating profit margin (defined below) increased to 19.5 percent, a year-over-year increase of approximately 90 and 80 basis points, respectively.
•Diluted earnings per share for the quarter was $0.60, a $0.10 increase year-over-year and adjusted diluted earnings per share (defined below) was $0.68, a $0.09 increase year-over-year.
Meredith Kopit Levien, president and chief executive officer, The New York Times Company, said, “Q3 was another quarter of strong execution and sustained momentum across The Times. Our results highlighted the enduring advantages of our strategy and our multi-revenue stream model. Subscriber growth was powered by strong engagement and retention across our portfolio. We grew operating profit and generated healthy free cash flow while continuing to invest in growth areas like video. As the information ecosystem continues to evolve, our essential subscription strategy — which is about human-made journalism and product experiences that people seek out directly and make room for in their lives — provides powerful advantages that we believe will only deepen with time.”

Consolidated Results
Subscription Revenues
Total subscription revenues increased 10.7 percent to $547.5 million compared with $494.6 million in the third quarter of 2025. Subscription revenues from digital-only products increased 14.6 percent to $421.3 million due to higher average digital-only subscribers and higher digital-only ARPU. Print subscription revenues decreased 0.7 percent to $126.3 million, primarily due to lower single-copy revenues.
Subscribers and Net Additions
The Company ended the third quarter of 2026 with approximately 13.76 million subscribers to its print and digital products, including approximately 13.22 million digital-only subscribers.
Compared with the end of the second quarter of 2026, there was a net increase of approximately 420,000 digital-only subscribers. Compared with the end of the third quarter of 2025, there was a net increase of approximately 1,460,000 digital-only subscribers.
Average Revenue Per User
Digital-only average revenue per user or “ARPU,” a metric we calculate to track the revenue generation of our digital-only subscriber base, represents the average revenue per digital-only subscriber over a 28-day billing cycle during the applicable quarter. For more information, please refer to the Supplemental Subscriber and ARPU Information in the exhibits.
Digital-only ARPU was $9.93 for the third quarter of 2026, an increase of 1.4 percent compared with the third quarter of 2025 driven primarily by subscribers transitioning from promotional to higher prices and price increases on certain tenured subscribers.
Advertising Revenues
Total advertising revenues increased 7.8 percent to $142.6 million compared with $132.3 million in the third quarter of 2025. Digital advertising revenues increased 12.0 percent to $109.9 million, primarily due to continued marketer demand and growth in advertising supply. Print advertising revenues decreased 4.3 percent to $32.7 million.
Affiliate, Licensing and Other Revenues
Affiliate, licensing and other revenues increased 6.2 percent to $78.4 million compared with $73.9 million in the third quarter of 2025, primarily as a result of higher licensing and higher Wirecutter affiliate referral revenues, which more than offset a shift in the timing of a marketing promotion by one of our partners.
Total Revenues
In the aggregate, subscription; advertising; and affiliate, licensing and other revenues for the third quarter of 2026 increased 9.7 percent to $768.5 million from $700.8 million in the third quarter of 2025.

Operating Costs
Total operating costs increased 8.5 percent in the third quarter of 2026 to $646.6 million compared with $596.0 million in the third quarter of 2025. Operating costs in the third quarters of 2026 and 2025 included, as special items, Generative AI Litigation Costs of $3.8 million and $2.4 million, respectively. Adjusted operating costs increased 8.7 percent to $618.9 million from $569.4 million in the third quarter of 2025.
Cost of revenue increased 7.0 percent to $373.5 million compared with $349.1 million in the third quarter of 2025, primarily due to higher compensation and benefits expenses relating to our journalism, higher digital content delivery costs and higher subscriber servicing costs.
Sales and marketing costs increased 12.5 percent to $89.5 million compared with $79.6 million in the third quarter of 2025, primarily due to higher marketing and promotion expenses and higher advertising compensation and benefits expenses.
Product development costs increased 7.9 percent to $72.3 million compared with $67.0 million in the third quarter of 2025, primarily due to higher compensation and benefits expenses and higher technology-related expenses.
General and administrative costs increased 12.7 percent to $86.4 million compared with $76.6 million in the third quarter of 2025, primarily due to higher expenses from professional fees and one-time promotional expenses related to the 175th anniversary of the Company, partially offset by lower compensation and benefits expenses.

Consolidated Other Data
Interest Income and Other, net
Interest income and other, net in the third quarter of 2026 was $11.6 million compared with $7.9 million in the third quarter of 2025. The prior year included, as a special item, a $3.5 million charge related to an impairment of a non-marketable equity investment.
Income Taxes
The Company had income tax expense of $32.5 million in the third quarter of 2026 compared with $26.4 million in the third quarter of 2025. The increase in income tax expense in the third quarter of 2026 was primarily due to higher pre-tax income in the quarter. The effective income tax rate was 25.0 percent in the third quarter of 2026 compared with 24.4 percent in the third quarter of 2025.
Earnings Per Share
Diluted EPS in the third quarter of 2026 was $0.60 compared with $0.50 in the third quarter of 2025. The increase in diluted EPS was primarily driven by higher operating profit. Adjusted diluted EPS was $0.68 in the third quarter of 2026 compared with $0.59 in the third quarter of 2025.
Liquidity
As of September 30, 2026, the Company had cash and marketable securities of $1.26 billion, an increase of $96.9 million from $1.17 billion as of December 31, 2025.
The Company has a $400 million unsecured revolving line of credit. As of September 30, 2026, there were no outstanding borrowings under this credit facility, and the Company did not have other outstanding debt.
Net cash provided by operating activities in the first nine months of 2026 was $462.2 million compared with $420.3 million in the same period of 2025. Free cash flow in the first nine months of 2026 was $435.8 million compared with $392.9 million in the same period of 2025. Net cash provided by operating activities in the first nine months of 2025 included net proceeds of approximately $33 million in connection with the lease and subsequent sale of approximately four acres of excess land at our printing and distribution facility in College Point, N.Y., which was finalized in February 2025.
The One Big Beautiful Bill Act (“OBBBA”) resulted in lower cash tax payments of $65 million for the fiscal year ended December 31, 2025. The Company expects lower cash tax payments of approximately $60 million in total for the fiscal year ending December 31, 2026, and does not expect the majority of this cash flow benefit to recur beyond fiscal 2026.
Share Repurchases
During the quarter ended September 30, 2026, the Company repurchased 1,158,652 shares of its Class A Common Stock for an aggregate amount of approximately $79.1 million. As of October 30, 2026, approximately $146.0 million remains available and authorized for repurchases.
Capital Expenditures
Capital expenditures totaled approximately $4 million in the third quarter of 2026 compared with approximately $8 million in the third quarter of 2025.

About The New York Times Company
The New York Times Company (NYSE: NYT) is a trusted source of quality, independent journalism whose mission is to seek the truth and help people understand the world. RECONCILIATION OF NON-GAAP FINANCIAL MEASURES
In this release, the Company has referred to non-GAAP financial information with respect to adjusted diluted EPS, defined as diluted EPS excluding amortization of acquired intangible assets, severance, non-operating retirement costs and special items; adjusted operating profit, defined as operating profit before depreciation, amortization, severance, multiemployer pension plan withdrawal costs and special items; adjusted operating profit margin, defined as adjusted operating profit divided by revenues; adjusted operating costs, defined as operating costs before depreciation, amortization, severance, multiemployer pension plan withdrawal costs and special items; and free cash flow, defined as net cash provided by operating activities less capital expenditures. The Company has included these non-GAAP financial measures because management reviews them on a regular basis and uses them to evaluate and manage the performance of the Company’s operations. Management believes that, for the reasons outlined below, these non-GAAP financial measures provide useful information to investors as a supplement to reported diluted earnings/(loss) per share, operating profit/(loss) and operating costs. However, these measures should be evaluated only in conjunction with the comparable GAAP financial measures and should not be viewed as alternative or superior measures of GAAP results.
Adjusted diluted EPS provides useful information in evaluating the Company’s period-to-period performance because it eliminates items that the Company does not consider to be indicative of earnings from ongoing operating activities. Adjusted operating profit and adjusted operating profit margin are useful in evaluating the ongoing performance of the Company’s business as they exclude the significant non-cash impact of depreciation and amortization, as well as items not indicative of ongoing operating activities. Total operating costs include depreciation, amortization, severance, multiemployer pension plan withdrawal costs and special items. Adjusted operating costs provide investors with helpful supplemental information on the Company’s underlying operating costs that is used by management in its financial and operational decision-making.
Management considers special items, which may include impairment charges, pension settlement charges, acquisition-related costs, and beginning in 2024, Generative AI Litigation Costs, as well as other items that arise from time to time, to be outside the ordinary course of our operations. Management believes that excluding these items provides a better understanding of the underlying trends in the Company’s operating performance and allows more accurate comparisons of the Company’s operating results to historical performance. Management determined to report Generative AI Litigation Costs as a special item and thus exclude them beginning in 2024 because, unlike other litigation expenses which are not excluded, the Generative AI Litigation Costs arise from discrete, complex and unusual proceedings and do not, in management’s view, reflect the Company’s ongoing business operational performance. In addition, management excludes severance costs, which may fluctuate significantly from quarter to quarter, because it believes these costs do not necessarily reflect expected future operating costs and do not contribute to a meaningful comparison of the Company’s operating results to historical performance.
The Company considers free cash flow as providing useful information to management and investors about the amount of cash that is available to be used to strengthen the Company’s balance sheet, for strategic opportunities, including investing in the Company’s business and strategic acquisitions, and/or for the return of capital to stockholders in the form of dividends and stock repurchases.
Non-operating retirement costs include (i) interest cost, expected return on plan assets, amortization of actuarial gains and loss components and amortization of prior service credits of single-employer pension expense, (ii) interest cost, amortization of actuarial gains and loss components of other postretirement benefits and (iii) all multiemployer pension plan withdrawal costs. These non-operating retirement costs are primarily tied to financial market performance including changes in market interest rates and investment performance. Management considers non-operating retirement costs to be outside the performance of the business and believes that presenting adjusted diluted EPS excluding non-operating retirement costs and presenting adjusted operating results excluding multiemployer pension plan withdrawal costs, in addition to the Company’s GAAP diluted EPS and GAAP operating results, provide increased transparency and a better understanding of the underlying trends in the Company’s operating business performance.
