BUY, HOLD or SELL: how Leago Rifumo Capital rates a company

Every LRC rating comes from the same written rules: six-month upside, a 50-point checklist and a red-flag test. Here is exactly how it works.

Prayer Nkuna, Analyst22 October 2026 · 2 min read

A rating is only useful if you know how it was made. At Leago Rifumo Capital every company goes through the same five steps, and the rating at the end follows written rules — not a feeling.

The five steps

1. Understand the business — what it sells, who pays, why customers stay and what could break it. 2. Check the numbers — four years of growth, margins, cash flow, debt and returns. 3. Value it three ways — forward P/E, a five-year discounted cash flow and EV/EBITDA against listed peers, blended 50 / 20 / 30. 4. Score it — a 50-point quality checklist, averaged to a score out of 5. 5. Time it and manage the risk — technical review, review levels and position sizing.

The rating rules (six-month horizon)

| Rating | Rule | |---|---| | BUY | Upside of 7.5% or more and a score of at least 3.5 and no critical item scored 2 or lower | | SELL | Downside of 5% or more or a score below 2.5 | | HOLD | Everything in between |

The critical items are the ones that can sink a company on their own: disruption, debt, leadership and accounting.

Why a great company can be a SELL

Price matters. A business can be excellent and still be priced for more than it can deliver over the next six months. That is why our October screen found that the strongest-trending US shares were often the most expensive — and why a great chart is not a BUY.
